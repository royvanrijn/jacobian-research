## Result

The orbit-103 descent obstruction is useful as a controlled rank-jump
laboratory, but this first deep bounded pass does **not** meet the proposed
rank-8--12 success condition.

The generic arithmetic group over `QQ(k)(r)` is generated by the two known
sections `Q_plus,Q_minus`.  The third geometric section is anti-invariant over
`QQ(sqrt(-3))`; no rational specialization of `k` was treated as making that
direction rational.  Instead the search looked for points independent of the
specialized `Q_plus,Q_minus`.

`H(r)<=500` prefix produced the following certified lower-bound distribution:

For every row, all seven displayed rational points lie on the exact integral

bound.  The PARI `ellrank` intervals stored with the records are diagnostic
only.  In particular, the rank claims use the points returned by PARI, not its
reported upper bound.

Sage's bundled PARI 2.17 hit a reproducible `small_norm (precision too low)`
error on 617 candidates.  A bounded fallback to system GP 2.15.4 with a
256-MB stack completed all 617.  The same exact transport and finite-quotient
gate is applied regardless of which backend returns the candidate points.

## Search design

The exact family is

```text
y^2 = x^3 - 27*(r^2-4)^2*A4(k,r)*x
            + 54*(r^2-4)^3*B6(k,r),
```

with `A4,B6` in
[`E6A1_RHO19_ORBIT103_WEIERSTRASS_2026-09-02.md`](E6A1_RHO19_ORBIT103_WEIERSTRASS_2026-09-02.md).
For `k=ka/kb` and `r=ra/rb`, the scaling

```text
u = kb*rb^2,   x -> u^2*x,   y -> u^3*y
```

gives the integral model used for point discovery and certification.  The
same bidegree homogenization is evaluated over finite fields, including the
points at infinity of both parameter lines.

The first stage scores rational `k` by averaging the exact local Nagao
contribution over all good `r` in `P1(F_p)`.  It exhausts the projective
height box `H(k)<=3000`, or `10,944,752` primitive rational parameters after
the implemented scan conventions, and quotients the exact involution
`(k,r)->(-k,-r)`.  The eight retained `k` values are

```text
1, 641/1101, 2117/1183, 539/2448,
1099/838, 2070/1327, 1201/2825, 2744/2507.
```

The weakest of three disjoint standardized selection blocks is used, so a
single favorable collection of primes cannot determine the ranking.  The
selection primes are `19..199`, distributed round-robin among the blocks.
The leading surface signal is `3.0540` at `k=1`; the next is `2.3324`.

For each retained `k`, the second stage exhausts `H(r)<=5000`, or
`30,401,832` rational parameters per `k`, and retains 100 trace leaders.  A
separate coefficient lane exhausts `H(r)<=500` at `k=1` and retains its top
1000.  All finalists are rescored using three disjoint blocks from the
independent primes `211..401`.  Bad reductions are omitted from exact local
evaluation and mean-imputed only inside the standardized ranking machinery.

This use of local trace averages follows the Nagao heuristic framework.  The
relation between these averages and surface rank is discussed by Rosen and
Silverman, [*On the rank of an elliptic surface*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002220050238),
and by Kim, [*The Sato--Tate conjecture and Nagao's conjecture*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02775).
Here the finite prime blocks are only a candidate-ranking device; no limiting
formula is asserted.

## Exact promotion gate

Each selected integral curve is globally minimalized, and the exact images of
`Q_plus,Q_minus` seed the point backend.  Returned points are transported back
to the original short integral model and checked by exact substitution.  A
candidate point is retained only when the full ordered point set has a
finite-quotient independence certificate.  The default certificate uses
relations modulo 3 and good reduction primes at most 500.  Modulo 3 was
chosen because modulo 2 can collapse the images of the two known points on
some fibres.

The exact certificate is always built against `Q_plus,Q_minus`.  No formula,
point, or finite-field image for the known `sqrt(-3)` direction enters the
search or the rank lower bounds.

## Interpretation and boundary

This pass proves that orbit 103 can cheaply produce specializations with five
new independent rational directions.  It also gives a visibly nontrivial
selected tail through rank seven: 110 of the 1000 selected fibres certify rank
at least six.  That is promising discovery behavior, but it is
not yet evidence that the family has a fatter rank-8--12 tail than R17:

- no completed candidate certifies rank at least eight;
- the 1000 fibres are a Nagao-selected prefix, not a random sample or a
  complete rank census of all `H(r)<=500` fibres;
- the high-denominator `H(r)<=5000` leaders were not promoted within a useful
  exact point-search time budget.

Accordingly this is a negative result for the stated success condition, not a
negative theorem about higher-rank specializations.  It neither bounds the
rank of an uncompleted fibre nor excludes rank-8--12 fibres elsewhere in the
declared boxes.
