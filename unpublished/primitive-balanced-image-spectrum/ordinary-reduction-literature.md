# Ordinary-reduction literature

This note compares the ordinary-prime criterion in *The primitive balanced-degree Image spectrum in two pairs* with the closest published and preprint theorems examined for this submission. The claim is an extension of specified theorem hypotheses. It leaves priority for the general statement open and makes no assertion about the smallest extension of the base field.

## The comparison

Let \(X\) have dimension \(2e\) and let the CM field \(E\) have degree \(2e\). The criterion assumes a unital embedding \(E\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}^{0}(X_{\bar K})\) that is stable under a polarization's Rosati involution and on which Rosati acts as complex conjugation. The relative rank on first cohomology is two. Each pair of complex embeddings has Hodge multiplicities \((2,0)\), \((0,2)\) or \((1,1)\). If at most two pairs have multiplicities \((1,1)\), there is a finite extension of \(K\) over which ordinary primes have density one.

**Cantoral Farfán, Li, Mantovan, Pries and Tang.** Assumption 2.1 requires the full geometric endomorphism algebra to equal the CM field \(F\). Assumption 2.5 requires one chosen embedding to have Hodge multiplicity 1 and every other embedding in the chosen CM type to have multiplicity 0. At relative rank two, this means exactly one pair has multiplicities \((1,1)\). Theorem 4.18 gives eventual density one under these assumptions and also determines the density over the original field when \(F/\mathbf Q\) is Galois in relative rank at least two. Theorem 4.19 adds the hypothesis that \(F/\mathbf Q\) is abelian and gives a density-one \(\mu\)-ordinary result over the original base field. Remark 4.21 explains the extension to products of satisfying factors. Overlapping special cases may therefore follow from the existing theorem on factors. The new criterion extends the relative-rank-two signature range to two balanced pairs and permits a Rosati-compatible CM subfield of a larger endomorphism algebra. Its conclusion concerns eventual ordinary density. [arXiv:2508.11174v1, Assumptions 2.1 and 2.5, Theorems 4.18–4.19, and Remarks 4.20–4.21](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11174v1).

The direct antecedent for the relative trace norm is Proposition 3.12 and the ensuing argument in Section 4.1 of the same paper. In relative rank two, at a split residue-degree-one prime and with \(b\) balanced pairs, the unbalanced pairs contribute \(e-b\) to the valuation of the trace norm. A nonordinary balanced pair makes both integral local relative traces divisible by the rational residue prime. The two extra valuations give the lower bound \(e-b+2\). It reaches \(e\), the exponent from the Weil bound, for \(b\leq 2\). This is why the finite-value argument covers a second balanced pair. [arXiv:2508.11174v1, Proposition 3.12 and Sections 4.1 and 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11174v1).

**Katz–Ogus.** Proposition 2.7.2 in Ogus's chapter fixes a bounded integral weight-two normalized Frobenius trace at its maximal value by making torsion and roots of unity rational over a finite extension. Corollary 2.9 uses this observation for elliptic curves and abelian surfaces. The same congruence method applies here to the relative trace norm, once the split-prime valuation makes its weight-zero normalization integral. Equality in the Weil bound forces \(\tau\bar{\tau}=4p\) for the relative trace \(\tau\in E\). At odd split primes this equality is incompatible with divisibility of the traces at both places of any nonordinary balanced pair. The IAS scan's viewer pages 376–377 correspond to printed pages 371–372. Its SHA-256 is 577dcb0d7549ab8200fa9fafb5809f6520d99e524a88d547a99141c2353f3873. [Ogus, Hodge Cycles, Motives, and Shimura Varieties, LNM 900 (1982), Chapter VI, pp. 371–372](https://publications.ias.edu/sites/default/files/Number45.pdf#page=376).

A concrete witness for the difference in hypotheses is \(X=C^4\) for a non-CM elliptic curve \(C\). Its full geometric algebra is \(M_4(\mathbf Q)\). Use the regular representation of the CM field \(\mathbf Q(i,\sqrt 2)\) in the basis \(1,i,\sqrt 2,i\sqrt 2\). The product polarization with weights \(1,1,2,2\) gives the adjoint for the trace form \(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbf Q}(x\bar y)/4\), so Rosati is complex conjugation on the subfield. The induced action on holomorphic one-forms is the complex regular representation and both conjugate pairs are balanced. This example exhibits both hypothesis changes simultaneously. Its ordinary primes already follow from those of \(C\).

**Fité.** Theorem 2 allows a degree-\(g\) Galois subfield of the geometric endomorphism algebra of an abelian \(g\)-fold without requiring equality with the full algebra. Its conclusion is positive density of primes of \(p\)-rank at least \(\lceil g/2\rceil\). The proof in Section 5 uses the relative degree-two Weil bound. Theorems 1, 3 and 4 and Corollary 5 give ordinary-prime results for other specific endomorphism and dimension hypotheses, including threefolds with imaginary quadratic multiplication and fourfolds with imaginary quadratic multiplication of signature \((2,2)\). Proposition 8 isolates the role of representations whose integral trace has weight two. Thus Fité already allows embedded fields in related theorems. The comparison here concerns full ordinarity under a different set of Hodge conditions. [Publ. Mat. 68 (2024), 27–40, Theorems 1–4, Corollary 5 and Proposition 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.10185v3).

**Sawin.** Theorem 2.1 interprets the normalized middle Frobenius coefficient of an abelian surface as a weight-zero character, and counts components on which its trace is constant using algebraic Chebotarev. Corollary 2.2 gives ordinary density one when the algebraic monodromy group is connected. This already covers the surface case of the present criterion and is a precursor of the finite-value argument in higher dimension. Remark 4.20 of the Cantoral Farfán–Li–Mantovan–Pries–Tang preprint also discusses a relationship of its simple-signature result to unpublished work of Sawin on K3-type Hodge structures. [C. R. Math. 354 (2016), 566–568, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.crma.2016.01.025.pdf), [arXiv:2508.11174v1, Remark 4.20](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11174v1).

**Suh.** Theorem 4.1.1 concerns motives attached to parallel weight-two non-CM Hilbert eigenforms. Section 4.1 uses bounds on norms over the coefficient field, together with a trace argument in low degree. Fité's discussion identifies Suh's method as anticipating his \(p\)-rank result. The Hilbert modular hypotheses do not give the CM-subfield and two-balanced-pair criterion considered here. [Compos. Math. 156 (2020), 647–678, author preprint, Theorem 4.1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01182v1), [Fité, Section 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.10185v3).

**Wang and Zhang.** Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 assume that a degree-\(g\) field acts on a \(g\)-fold over \(\mathbf Q\) or over a quadratic field, with the stated restrictions on \(g\). Theorem 1.3 assumes absolute simplicity, excludes Albert type IV for the full geometric endomorphism algebra and imposes a splitting condition at an auxiliary prime in the degree-\(g\) field. Their conclusions give positive density of ordinary primes over the base field. Their proof also uses normalized Frobenius norms, and their theorems supply related results with different restrictions on the endomorphism field and its splitting. [Mathematika 72 (2026), e70081, Theorems 1.1–1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21111v2).

**Noot and Pink.** Noot's density-one result in Theorem 2.2 uses its specific tensor-product monodromy representation. Pink's Theorem 7.1 assumes trivial geometric endomorphism ring and a restriction on the monodromy root system. These provide precedents for density one after base change under monodromy hypotheses. Neither gives the stated criterion from an embedded CM field and its Hodge multiplicities. [Noot, Compos. Math. 97 (1995), 161–171](https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1995__97_1-2_161_0.pdf), [Pink, J. Reine Angew. Math. 495 (1998), 187–237](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~pinkri/ftp/AMT-v3.pdf).

**Tankeev and Thakur.** Tankeev's Theorem 0.12 proves eventual density-one ordinary reduction for absolutely simple Albert type IV threefolds. Thakur's Theorem 1.1 covers certain absolutely simple type IV varieties with connected monodromy and specified noncommutative division endomorphism algebra and Mumford–Tate representation from Definition 1.1. These are precedents for the type IV setting with different dimension and endomorphism hypotheses. In Thakur's class the dimension is \(d\binom dr\), and the largest possible commutative subfield of the division algebra has degree \(2d\). Relative rank two for a degree-\(\dim X\) subfield would require \(\binom dr\le 2\), while the defined range has odd \(d\ge 3\) and \(1\le r\le d-1\). [Tankeev, Izv. Math. 63 (1999), 181–218, Theorem 0.12](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=im&paperid=233&what=fullteng), [Thakur, arXiv:1803.05954v2, Theorem 1.1 and Definition 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1803.05954v2).

**Can and Lin, and Hui.** Theorem 1.1 of Can and Lin proves a \(\mu\)-ordinary density result for K3 surfaces under assumptions on the endomorphism field and the connectedness field. It uses the normalized Frobenius function method and discusses the unpublished K3 result of Sawin. Theorem 1.1 of Hui proves density zero of supersingular primes for non-CM abelian varieties or K3 surfaces. Supersingularity controls the all-slope-\(1/2\) case for abelian varieties. At the split primes used here a nonordinary balanced pair can coexist with ordinary slopes from other pairs, so that density-zero result does not control the present obstruction. [Can and Lin, arXiv:2609.34093v1, Sections 1.2–1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34093v1), [Hui, arXiv:2504.08088v2, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08088v2).

## Cyclotomic factors in the Image proof

For each relevant factor of a cyclic-cover Jacobian, the deck transformation supplies the cyclotomic field and is unitary for the Jacobian polarization. Its adjoint is its inverse, so Rosati restricts to complex conjugation. The signature calculation then applies uniformly to every parameter in the indicated chart.

| Primitive order | CM subfield | Dimension | Balanced conjugate pairs |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 5 | \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_5)\) | 4 | 1 |
| 7 | \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_7)\) | 6 | 2 |
| 9 | \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_9)\) | 6 | 2 |
| 10 | \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_{10})\) | 4 | 1 |
| 11 | \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_{11})\) | 10 | 2 |
| 15 | \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_{15})\) | 8 | 2 |

For order 5 the covering exponents \((4,2,2,2)\) are equivalent to \((1,3,3,3)\) under multiplication by a unit modulo 5, so the family is Moonen's \(M[11]\). It is already treated in Sections 5.1–5.2 of Cantoral Farfán and coauthors. At orders 7, 9, 11 and 15 the displayed cyclotomic actions have two balanced pairs. The order-7 and order-9 exponent data have a double repetition, whereas the corresponding rows \(M[17]\) and \(M[19]\) in Moonen's Table 1 have a triple repetition. There are no rows of order 11 or 15. The order-10 tuple \((5,3,3,9)\) is all odd, while the sole order-10 row \(M[18]\) has mixed parity, which is invariant under multiplying by a unit modulo 10. The uniform application in the Image proof requires the signature and Rosati-compatible field action, including for special fibers where the full endomorphism algebra can differ. These comparisons leave open whether specific fibers or isogenous decompositions can also be handled by earlier theorems. [Moonen, Doc. Math. 15 (2010), 793–819, Theorem 3.6 and Table 1](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/949135/83991_Moonen_DocMath_2010.pdf), [arXiv:2508.11174v1, Corollary 5.1 and Sections 5.1–5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11174v1).
