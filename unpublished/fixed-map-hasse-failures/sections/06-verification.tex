\section{Verification scope and finite data}\label{sec:verification}

The rational classification and counting theorem are written mathematical
arguments. Their arithmetic inputs are the elementary local cube criteria;
the analytic input is the precisely stated one-variable
Selberg--Delange theorem used in \eqref{eq:rational-sd}. Coprimality,
passage to the height polygon, and summation of the valuation strata are
proved above rather than imported from a multivariable counting theorem.

Exact finite checks compare the projective content formula with direct
greatest common divisors, and compare the target height with independent
denominator clearing of its rational coordinates. On all reduced signed
pairs \(|m|,n\le128\), the local criterion agrees with finite unit-cube
residue tests at every prime at most \(127\). These tests cover all
prime divisors of the pairs in that box, but do not replace the unrestricted
Hensel arguments in Section~\ref{sec:local-global}.

A complete enumeration uses the bounds \(|m|\le3B/4\), \(n\le8B/3\)
following from \eqref{eq:rational-height}. After imposing the local
conditions and removing global cubes it gives
\[
\begin{array}{r|r}
B&N_{\Phi,\ell}(B)\\ \hline
1000&617\\
3000&4377\\
10000&41415
\end{array}
\]
A separate direct enumeration agrees through height \(80\).
The checker also verifies the exact polygon areas and the rational
algebra of the two content weights. These are bounded arithmetic audits;
the asymptotic and its constant follow from the proof, not from fitting
the table.

The retained Lean development concerns the specialized fixed-map algebra
and the earlier sufficient integer family \(\mathcal A\), including its
height \(32a\). The rational local converse, the exceptional fibers at
\(a=0,1\), the general rational height, and the rational counting theorem
are not claimed to be formally verified. The Selberg--Delange arguments
and the classical minimal-degree obstruction also remain outside that
formal development. Supporting verification records distinguish these
scopes from a new toolchain replay or external mathematical review.
