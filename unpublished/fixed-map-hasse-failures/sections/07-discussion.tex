\section{Discussion}

Centering the quadratic factor makes the family
\((X^3-a)(X^2+X+1)\) fit the inverse pencil of one fixed map. The local
converse then classifies every Hasse-failing rational parameter on this
line, and the height calculation turns the classification into an exact
asymptotic. The exceptional parameters \(0,1\) have been treated
separately, so the line count leaves no unclassified rational targets.

The sufficient integer family has order \(B/\sqrt{\log B}\), whereas
the complete rational family has order \(B^2/\log B\). Part of this
increase comes from allowing numerator and denominator pairs. The
essential constant calculations are the coprimality correction, the
coupled unit condition at \(3\), and the projective content at \(2,3\).
The negative and positive parameters have slightly different height
regions, which also affects the leading term.

The target line is exceptional: every inverse polynomial on it factors
as a quadratic times a cubic. That factorization supplies the local
points, but does not describe local solubility on the ambient
three-dimensional target. A higher-dimensional Hasse-failing locus in
the same fixed map remains a separate problem.

The present theorem counts targets rather than distinct fiber algebras.
For example, multiplying \(a\) by a rational cube preserves the cubic
field up to isomorphism whenever \(a\) is not a cube. A count of
isomorphism classes would therefore require a different equivalence
relation and a compatible height. Integral source points are another
question: a root of the inverse polynomial over \(\Q_p\) need not
reconstruct to an integral source point without further valuation control.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
The arithmetic polynomial at \(a=19\) is due to Berend and Bilu
\cite{BerendBilu}. The fixed-map construction specializes the quadratic-gauge
prescribed-fiber mechanism of \cite{vanRijnFibers}; the all-degree
coefficient construction is already present in Ulam
\cite[Theorem~5.2]{Ulam}. This attribution does not identify the fixed-map
local classification or height asymptotic as a consequence of that source.
