# Conductor/contact-loss truncation for plane boundary residues

> **Status.** The branchwise truncation theorem below is proved for every
> finite normalization of a reduced curve once its completed conductor
> exponents and contact-loss bounds are certified. It applies to nodes,
> arbitrary numerical-semigroup branches, multibranch singularities,
> determinant-line twists, and cokernels of finite source/target/gauge
> matching maps. The scalar bound is sharp in general. A dependency-sensitive
> refinement and a Newton-support valuation adapter are executable. For the
> archived `(72,108)` Case-1 data, the theorem reduces the former request for
> a full lower Newton tail to a finite ledger, but does not yet certify that
> ledger: the matching map and its band-to-branch valuations remain missing.

## 1. The conductor already is a finite-jet theorem

Let \(A\subset B\) be a finite birational extension of reduced Noetherian
one-dimensional rings, with \(B\) normal, and put

\[
 Q=B/A.
\]

The conductor

\[
 \mathfrak c=(A:_A B)=\operatorname{Ann}_A(Q)       \tag{1.1}
\]

is also an ideal of \(B\). Consequently the quotient map \(B\to Q\) factors
canonically through

\[
 B/\mathfrak cB\longrightarrow Q.                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Indeed, if \(b\in\mathfrak cB=\mathfrak c\), then \(b\in A\), so its class
in \(B/A\) vanishes. Equivalently, \(\mathfrak cQ=0\). Tensoring by an
invertible \(A\)-module \(\mathcal L\) preserves this annihilation:

\[
 \mathfrak c(Q\otimes_A\mathcal L)=0.              \tag{1.3}
\]

Thus determinant-line twisting does not enlarge the conductor jet. A change
of local trivialization multiplies by a unit and leaves the orders unchanged.

Complete at a finite conductor point and normalize. After a residue-field
extension, the completed normalization has the branch form

\[
 \widehat B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^s K_i[[t_i]],
 \qquad
 \mathfrak c\widehat B=
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^s t_i^{c_i}K_i[[t_i]].            \tag{1.4}
\]

Every supported branch has \(c_i\ge1\). Equations (1.2)--(1.4) show that a
regular normalization section contributes to \(Q\) using only coefficients
of orders \(0,\ldots,c_i-1\) on branch \(i\).

Examples recover the familiar descriptions.

- For a node, the two branch exponents are \(c_1=c_2=1\); the quotient
  records the difference of the two constant terms.
- For \(A=k[t^2,t^3]\subset k[t]\), \(c=2\), and \(Q\) is generated by the
  gap monomial \(t\).
- For a numerical-semigroup curve \(k[t^S]\subset k[t]\), \(c\) is the
  numerical-semigroup conductor and the gap monomials \(t^g\), \(g\notin S\),
  form a basis of the quotient.

This factorization is not by itself a Newton truncation theorem:
differentiation and polar factors can move an omitted high-order input into
the conductor window.

## 2. Contact loss

On \(K[[t]]\), write \(\operatorname{ord}(0)=+\infty\). Let \(\Psi\) be an
expression in one or more regular input series. Say that \(\Psi\) has
**contact loss at most** \(\lambda\) if

\[
 \operatorname{ord}\bigl(\Psi(f)-\Psi(g)\bigr)
 \ge
 \min_j\operatorname{ord}(f_j-g_j)-\lambda.        \tag{2.1}
\]

This is a property of the actual expression on the selected chart, not
something inferred from its coarse Newton polygon.

The following operations give a compositional certificate.

1. Addition and multiplication of regular expressions take the maximum of
   incoming losses.
2. Multiplication or division by a certified unit does not change the loss.
3. Applying \(d\) total derivatives adds at most \(d\).
4. Multiplication by \(t^{-\ell}\) adds \(\ell\), provided the outputs are
   regular.
5. A separately proved cancellation or coordinate substitution can be
   recorded as an additional nonnegative loss. It must not be guessed from
   an expected final pole order.

For multiplication, use

\[
 f_1f_2-g_1g_2=(f_1-g_1)f_2+g_1(f_2-g_2).         \tag{2.2}
\]

For a derivative,
\(\operatorname{ord}(h^{(d)})\ge\operatorname{ord}(h)-d\). For unit
inversion,

\[
 f^{-1}-g^{-1}=\frac{g-f}{fg}.                    \tag{2.3}
\]

These estimates are characteristic-independent. Coefficients can vanish in
positive characteristic, but that can only improve the lower bound.

## 3. Truncation theorems

> **Theorem 3.1 (scalar conductor/contact-loss truncation).** In (1.4),
> suppose the \(i\)-th branch expression producing a section of
> \(Q\otimes\mathcal L\) has contact loss at most \(\lambda_i\). If two sets
> of inputs satisfy
>
> \[
>  f_j\equiv g_j\pmod {t_i^{c_i+\lambda_i}}
>  \quad\text{on every branch }i,                  \tag{3.1}
> \]
>
> then their resulting sections of \(Q\otimes\mathcal L\) are equal.

**Proof.** Equation (2.1) and (3.1) give

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{t_i}\bigl(\Psi(f)-\Psi(g)\bigr)\ge c_i.
\]

The difference lies in
\(\bigoplus_i t_i^{c_i}K_i[[t_i]]
=\mathfrak c\widehat B\). It is zero in \(Q\) by (1.2), and remains zero
after the invertible twist by (1.3). This holds branch by branch.
\(\square\)

The safe scalar ledger is

\[
 \lambda_i=d_i+\ell_i+\epsilon_i,\qquad
 \boxed{n_i\ge c_i+d_i+\ell_i+\epsilon_i}.         \tag{3.2}
\]

Here \(d_i\) is derivative loss, \(\ell_i\) is polar loss, \(\epsilon_i\)
is any further certified loss, and knowing the input modulo \(t_i^{n_i}\)
means knowing coefficients \(0,\ldots,n_i-1\).

### Dependency-sensitive form

The scalar minimum is safe but can be wasteful when inputs occur through
different expression paths. For output \(\alpha\), let
\(\lambda_{i,\alpha,j}\) be the loss along paths from input \(j\) on branch
\(i\). Structural induction on the expression tree gives

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_{t_i}
 \bigl(\Psi_\alpha(f)-\Psi_\alpha(g)\bigr)
 \ge
 \min_{j\leadsto\alpha}
 \left(
  \operatorname{ord}_{t_i}(f_j-g_j)
  -\lambda_{i,\alpha,j}
 \right).                                         \tag{3.3}
\]

For addition and regular multiplication, operand sensitivities are merged by
taking their maximum for each input. A derivative, pole, or separately
certified loss adds its order to every sensitivity below it; fixed unit
changes add zero.

> **Theorem 3.2 (dependency-sensitive truncation).** The output
> \(\Psi_\alpha\) is fixed in the conductor quotient whenever
>
> \[
>  \boxed{
>  n_{i,j}\ge c_i+\lambda_{i,\alpha,j}
>  \quad\text{for every dependency }j\leadsto\alpha.
>  }                                               \tag{3.4}
> \]
>
> Inputs absent from a certified complete expression graph require no jets
> for that output.

The proof is (3.3) followed by the conductor factorization (1.2), exactly as
in Theorem 3.1. Formula (3.4) can strictly improve the scalar compression: a
short \(P\)-jet is not compared with a large loss occurring only on a
\(Q\)-path.

### Sharpness

Over characteristic zero, the scalar bound cannot be lowered uniformly. Put
\(\lambda=d+\ell\) and compare \(0\) with \(t^{c+\lambda-1}\). Applying
\(d\) derivatives and multiplying by \(t^{-\ell}\) produces a nonzero
multiple of \(t^{c-1}\), which is not forced into the conductor. Thus
\(c+\lambda-1\) input jets do not suffice in general. Extra cancellation can
improve a particular row only when separately proved.

## 4. Matching cokernels and the residue class

Fix bases for finite coherent source, target, and gauge-jet modules and let

\[
 \Phi:\mathcal J_{\rm src}\oplus\mathcal J_{\rm tgt}
       \oplus\mathcal J_{\rm gauge}
 \longrightarrow Q\otimes\mathcal L,
 \qquad M=\operatorname{coker}\Phi.                \tag{4.1}
\]

Suppose every matrix entry of \(\Phi\), and a determinant residue
\(\rho\in Q\otimes\mathcal L\), is produced by certified expression trees.

> **Corollary 4.1 (cokernel invariance).** Inputs satisfying either the
> scalar bounds (3.2) or all detailed bounds (3.4) give the same matrix
> \(\Phi\), the same presented module \(M\), and the same distinguished
> class \([\rho]\in M\). Consequently they give the same question
>
> \[
>  [\rho]\in H_Z^0(M)=0:_M I_Z^\infty.             \tag{4.2}
> \]

The fixed-basis hypothesis matters. If the allowed source or gauge module
changes with omitted coefficients, an identification must first be
constructed. The theorem does not manufacture \(\Phi\), prove that \(\rho\)
is supported on \(Z\), or show that (4.2) vanishes. Those are separate module
and saturation calculations.

## 5. Generality and base change

The proof uses only the finite conductor quotient and filtered contact
orders. It covers:

- nodes and arbitrary multibranch reduced curve singularities;
- cusps and arbitrary numerical-semigroup branches, symmetric or not;
- finite products of conductor points;
- vector-valued residues and matrices of matching conditions;
- invertible determinant-line twists; and
- any characteristic, although the sharpness example is over characteristic
  zero.

For a family over a coefficient ring \(R\), the same statement holds on a
stratum where normalization and the conductor quotient commute with base
change and the branch exponents \(c_i\) are fixed. A sufficient convenient
hypothesis is a chosen simultaneous normalization with a presented conductor
quotient finite locally free over \(R\). Without such a hypothesis, conductor
exponents can jump under specialization. One must then stratify the
coefficient base and compile a separate ledger on each stratum.

## 6. Exact consequence for the Case-1 gap

For a Case-1 boundary presentation, the former alternative was: derive every
omitted lower Newton band, or prove that none changes the alternate-chart
residue. Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 make the second option finite and decidable.
For every preimage of the conductor support, the compiler needs:

1. the completed conductor exponent \(c_i\);
2. immutable bases and an expression tree for every entry of \(\Phi\) and
   \(\rho\), giving \(\lambda_{i,\alpha,j}\); and
3. the band-to-normal-parameter valuation map giving \(n_{i,j}\).

If every dependency passes (3.4), Corollary 4.1 proves that the archived
truncation defines the same boundary module and residue as the full Newton
tail. If one fails, only coefficients below its displayed deficit need be
recovered.

For the archived `(72,108)` Case-1 package, the Laurent endpoints
`P through z^-5` and `Q through z^-4` are not themselves the normal-branch
orders \(n_{i,j}\). Moreover the matching map (4.1) has not been compiled.
It would be circular to infer the detailed ledger from the visible pole table.
The concrete conductor-ledger application therefore remains **uncertified**.
However, the separate exact Newton continuation in
[`CASE1_FULL_BAND_CONTINUATION.md`](CASE1_FULL_BAND_CONTINUATION.md) now
recovers the complete tails directly. It bypasses, rather than proves, this
particular conductor truncation audit. This does not reopen those coefficient
systems, which are independently empty by the quotient-first determinantal
certificate.

## 7. Executable certificate

The shared evaluator is
[`scripts/conductor_jet_truncation.py`](../scripts/conductor_jet_truncation.py).

The legacy scalar schema records

```text
name
conductor_exponent
differential_order
pole_order
additional_contact_loss
available_jet_order
conductor_certificate
expression_certificate
valuation_certificate
```

and returns `not_declared`, `uncertified`, `passes`, or `insufficient`.

The preferred `conductor_jet_sensitivity` schema records named inputs, named
matching/residue outputs, and a complete expression tree using

```text
constant, input, add, multiply, unit_change, invert_unit,
derivative, pole, certified_loss
```

It emits every inequality in (3.4), its margin and exact deficit, the maximum
deficit, and inputs certified unused. Scalar and dependency-sensitive ledgers
are mutually exclusive. Only `passes` is a truncation theorem;
`insufficient` is a band-recovery instruction, not a Keller obstruction.

Available normal-jet orders need not be entered by hand. Given normal
valuations \(v_i(x_k)=w_{i,k}\), a trivialization shift \(s_i\), and a
certified finite valuation frontier of omitted Newton exponent vectors \(a\),
the adapter computes

\[
 n_{i,j}=
 \min_{a\text{ in the certified frontier}}
 \left(s_i+\sum_k a_k w_{i,k}\right).              \tag{7.1}
\]

The support-completeness certificate must prove that every omitted monomial
has normal order at least the displayed minimum. A coordinatewise antichain
proves this automatically only when the valuation is nonnegative on the
omitted-support cone; on a Laurent chart with a negative weight, the
certificate needs a separate lower-bound argument. This condition makes
(7.1) safe even if leading omitted coefficients later cancel. A negative
value is rejected because the input remains polar after the declared
trivialization. JSON accepts a branch `normal_valuation` plus per-input
`first_omitted_exponents` and this support-completeness certificate.

Run the exact node, cusp, numerical-semigroup, sharpness, scalar/detailed
parser, asymmetric `P/Q`, Newton-support valuation, deficit, and
matching-cokernel regressions with

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_conductor_jet_truncation.py
```

The checker writes no artifact.
