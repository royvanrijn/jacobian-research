## 1. The labelled seed

Normalize the six roots to

\[
(0,1,r_3,r_4,r_5,r_6)
=\left(0,1,\frac{19}{5},\frac{143}{25},
              \frac{168}{25},\frac{41}{5}\right).
\]

The two labelled affine abscissae are

\[
x_1(T)=-\frac{95}{23}+\frac{37}{23}T,
\qquad
x_2(T)=\frac{583}{115}+\frac{13}{23}T.
\]

Let \(I\) be the ideal generated by

\[
M,E_{2,1},E_{1,1},E_{0,1},E_{2,2},E_{1,2},E_{0,2}
\]

in the eight projected incidence coordinates
\((c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,x_{01},x_{11},x_{02},x_{12})\).  The residuals are always
evaluated by the monic-square and ordinate recursions; their universal
degree-40, 50 and 60 expansions are not part of this certificate.

All root differences, the root discriminant, \(D\),
\((1-x_{11}^2)(1-x_{12}^2)\), the difference of the two displayed affine
lines, and every recursion denominator are nonzero at the seed.  Hence any
saturation by their product has the same localization and completion at the
seed as \(I\).  The arithmetic square root is treated on the etale double
cover \(w^2=D\), where \(D=(528/25)^2\) at the selected lift.

## 2. Recovered root-coordinate surface

Put

\[
e=r_3-r_4+1,\qquad d=r_3-r_5-r_6+1.
\]

On \(ed\ne0\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{01}&=\frac{r_3}{e},
&x_{11}&=\frac{r_5-r_6}{e},\\
x_{02}&=\frac{r_3-r_5r_6}{d},
&x_{12}&=-\frac{r_4}{d}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Besides the Mestre equation \(M=0\), impose the sparse cubic relation

\[
H:=\frac{r_3}{e}+r_5+r_6-r_4-
       \frac{r_3-r_5r_6}{d}=0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

For a polynomial presentation, introduce \(i_e=e^{-1}\) and \(i_d=d^{-1}\)
and replace (2.2) by

\[
r_3i_e+r_5+r_6-r_4-(r_3-r_5r_6)i_d=0.
\]

The complete intersection \((M,H)\) has an extraneous component away from
the selected branch.  A convenient separator is

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q={}&r_3^3r_4-2r_3^2r_4^2+r_3r_4^3-r_3^3r_5+r_3^2r_4r_5
 +r_3r_4^2r_5-r_4^3r_5\\
&+r_3^2r_5^2-2r_3r_4r_5^2+r_4^2r_5^2-r_3^3+2r_3^2r_4
-2r_3r_4^2+r_4^3\\
&-r_3^2r_5+r_4^2r_5+2r_3r_5^2-2r_4r_5^2+2r_3r_4-2r_4^2\\
&-r_3r_5+r_4r_5+r_5^2-r_3+r_4-r_5.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

At the seed, \(Q=629592/15625\ne0\).  The exact working chart is therefore

\[
\mathcal S=V(M,H)\cap D(edQ).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The formulas (2.1) recover the two labelled abscissae exactly.  Rebuilding
the Mestre remainder and reducing recursively by

\[
(M,ei_e-1,di_d-1,H,Qi_Q-1)
\]

gives zero for all six section residuals over \(\mathbb Q\).  The same exact
normal-form calculation succeeds modulo 17, 29 and 31.  The full component,
seed and etale-coordinate checks agree at 17 and 31.  At 29 the selected
Jacobian minor vanishes because the numerator in (3.1) has one factor of 29,
so that prime is lucky for the component identity but unlucky for this local
coordinate certificate.  Modulo 37 the section identities still reduce to
zero, but (2.3) vanishes at the reduced seed, so 37 is unlucky for this
particular component separator even though it remains a good prime for the
original tangent calculation.

On the double cover \(w^2=D\), both cubic ordinates are recovered without a
new elimination.  For either line write

\[
s=\frac{(1-x_1^2)w}{2},\quad
N_1=4s^2f_4-f_5^2,\quad
N_0=8s^4f_3-N_1f_5.
\]

Then

\[
z_2=\frac{f_5}{2s},\qquad
z_1=\frac{N_1}{8s^3},\qquad
z_0=\frac{N_0}{16s^5}
\]

and \(y=z_0+z_1T+z_2T^2+sT^3\) satisfies \(y^2=R(x(T),T)\)
identically on the declared open chart.

## 3. Exact local scheme

The five equations presenting (2.4), followed by \(x_{02}\) and \(x_{12}\),
have a nonzero seven-by-seven Jacobian determinant in
\((r_3,r_4,r_5,r_6,i_e,i_d,i_Q)\) at the seed:

\[
\det J=
\frac{152748884574215424}{645751953125}\ne0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus \(\mathcal S\) is smooth of dimension two there, and
\((x_{02},x_{12})\) are etale local coordinates.  Its image under the root
coefficient map and (2.1) is a two-dimensional germ contained in the
two-section incidence scheme.

Independently, the seven-by-eight incidence Jacobian at the labelled seed has
exact rank six.  Consequently the incidence local ring has embedding
dimension two.  Since it contains the two-dimensional smooth germ above, its
Krull dimension is also two.  A Noetherian local ring whose dimension equals
its embedding dimension is regular.  Therefore the whole localized and
saturated incidence germ is regular, not merely one component inside a
larger tangent scheme.

### Local-scheme theorem

At the labelled seed, the saturated two-section incidence germ is a single
reduced smooth two-dimensional component.  Its completed local ring is

\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}\cong\mathbb Q[[u,v]],
\qquad (u,v)=(x_{02}-583/115,x_{12}-13/23).
\]

In particular:

- the tangent cone is one affine plane and the associated graded ring is
  \(\mathbb Q[u,v]\);
- the Hilbert--Samuel graded values are \(h(n)=n+1\), while
  \(\operatorname{length}(\mathcal O/\mathfrak m^{n+1})=\binom{n+2}{2}\);
- the Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is one;
- there is one minimal prime and no embedded prime;
- there is no nilpotent thickening and no intersection of tangent curves.

This explains the rank-six Jacobian and all previously vanishing obstruction
jets for the selected pair: they are the tangent and finite truncations of an
actual smooth surface, not evidence for a hidden nonreduced curve.

## 4. What this does and does not settle

The component is not the fixed-root D-square curve.  That curve is a
one-dimensional specialization through the displayed moduli point, and its
second companion is already an exact combination of the visible sections and
the first companion.  The surface (2.4) moves the root moduli and is the
globalization of the formerly unresolved two-dimensional formal germ.

The second rational six-companion point `(0,23,93,128,133,175)` lies on the
same root surface after the relabelling

\[
(r_3,r_4,r_5,r_6)=
\left(\frac{175}{23},\frac{93}{23},
      \frac{128}{23},\frac{133}{23}\right).
\]

There (2.1) gives the exact pair
\(5/3-T/21\) and \(619/69+31T/21\).  This supplies a distant exact point on
the recovered surface, not an independence certificate.

The following remain open and are not promoted by the local theorem:

- normalization or a low-genus model of the full surface (2.4);
- a generic visible-subgroup relation, or two-direction independence, for
  the two recovered sections;
- split-infinity parameterization, generic rank at least 14, a third section,
  or a discriminant/conductor comparison.

At each of the two known rational root points, current finite-reduction tests
gain at most one direction after adjoining the pair.  This is evidence for a
generic dependence relation, but it is not yet its proof.

There is also a sharp split-infinity signal, again not promoted to a theorem.
At the two rational points the leading invariant is respectively

\[
D=(528/25)^2,
\qquad
D=(14400/529)^2.
\]

An exhaustive enumeration of the root-distinct open chart \(edQ\ne0\) gives
only nonzero-square values of \(D\) at all 112, 280, 544 and 744 component
points over \(\mathbb F_{17},\mathbb F_{29},\mathbb F_{31}\) and
\(\mathbb F_{37}\), respectively.  This is evidence that \(D\) may already
be a square in the component function field; pointwise squareness over finite
fields does not prove that assertion.

The precise globalization obstruction is now the following.  In the
degree-eight presentation of the component over
\(\mathbb Q(r_3,r_4)\), one must either reconstruct and verify a rational
square root of \(D\), or prove that the quadratic cover is nontrivial, and
then perform the visible-subgroup relation calculation in that exact field.
The available algebra system does not factor or extract square roots in this
two-transcendental algebraic function-field tower.  Direct covariant normal
forms on the root chart were stopped after they failed to construct the first
two generic Jacobian points within a compact resource bound.  Thus neither
split infinity nor generic dependence is certified here.
