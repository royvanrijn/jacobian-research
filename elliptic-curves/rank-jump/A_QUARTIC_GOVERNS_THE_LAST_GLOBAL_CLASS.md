# A degree-four Galois scheme governs the last global class

The fixed published R17 cubic root curve has an exactly defined
**degree-four étale incidence scheme** Ω. Its rational-point question
settles the last global-pool dimension:

| Galois action on Ω | Étale algebra | dim J[2](Q) | Extra global class |
|---|---|---:|---|
| S4 | quartic field | 17 | absent |
| S3 fixing one letter | Q × non-Galois cubic field | 18 | present |

Both alternatives remain **UNKNOWN** for this curve. We have defined Ω
inside J[2] and certified its representation, but have **not** computed a
coefficient polynomial for its étale algebra or a representative of the
extra class. In both alternatives Ω has two real points and one conjugate
pair; at primes 211 and 229 its Frobenius cycle type is 1+3.

This is a structural reduction of the outstanding gate in
[the common-class native-block theorem](ONE_COMMON_CLASS_FOR_A_LARGE_NATIVE_BLOCK.md),
not another comparison of heuristic invariants. It is also a **fixed
root-curve incidence result**. It does not explain the new unramified
classes in the fresh +10/+11 or historic +12/+14 specializations, which
must largely occur outside this global pool. The missing specialized
class creation remains the principal research question.

## Exact computation from generic sections

Let C be the smooth projective cubic root curve

    x^3 + A(t)x + B(t) = 0,

J its Jacobian, and F the degree-three divisor above t=infinity. The
published model has degrees (8,12,24), squarefree discriminant and smooth
infinity. Thus C has genus ten. Adjunction for the degree-three spectral
curve in O(4) gives K_C=6F. Equivalently its dualizing sheaf is the pullback
of K_P1 tensor O(4)^2. Consequently

    λ = 3F,       h0(λ)=4

is a rational even theta characteristic. The monic equation gives

    π_* O_C = O ⊕ O(-4) ⊕ O(-8),

which proves the h0 assertion and the pole-bounded bases used below.
The affine equation is smooth; at infinity its three distinct roots give
the same weighted pole bounds.

For each of the seventeen generic sections P_i=(x_i(t),y_i(t)), define
D_i on C by y_i(t)=0 and x=x_i(t). The certificate checks that y_i has
six simple roots, that gcd(y_i,3x_i^2+A)=1, and that its leading coefficient
is nonzero. Hence

    div(x-x_i) = 2D_i - 4F,
    g_i = [D_i-2F] in J[2](Q).

The earlier generic-independence certificate shows that these classes
span a seventeen-dimensional G. This input uses only the generic section
polynomials, without exceptional coordinates, fibre parameters, branch
selection or gain labels.

The theta characteristic gives the Galois-invariant quadratic form

    q(v) = h0(λ+v) + h0(λ) modulo 2.

Its polarization is the additive Weil pairing b, and its Arf invariant
is h0(λ) modulo two. This is the Riemann–Mumford theorem; see
[Bruin–Poonen–Stoll, Theorem 5.1](https://www.mathe2.uni-bayreuth.de/stoll/papers/genus3-May2012.pdf).

Here each q(g_i)=0, by an explicit Riemann–Roch calculation. Multiplication
by x-x_i identifies L(D_i+F) with L(5F-D_i). The latter is the space

    a(t)+b(t)x,      deg(a)<=5, deg(b)<=1,
    a+b*x_i = 0 modulo y_i.

It has dimension two. Thus h0(λ+g_i)=2, as independently checked for
all seventeen sections.

For each pair i!=j the certificate checks gcd(y_i,y_j)=1, so D_i and D_j
are disjoint. Multiplication by (x-x_i)(x-x_j) identifies
L(D_i+D_j-F) with L(7F-D_i-D_j). Write

    a(t)+b(t)x,      deg(a)<=7, deg(b)<=3.

Vanishing at D_i and D_j imposes twelve linear conditions, namely
reduction of a+b*x_i modulo y_i and a+b*x_j modulo y_j. The nullity of
this 12-by-12 rational matrix is h0(λ+g_i+g_j). Its parity is b(g_i,g_j),
since both single-section quadratic values are zero.

All **136** pairings are computed this way. The resulting alternating
matrix has rank **16**, with radical generated by the bit mask **27687**.
Bits are zero-based section indices; this is a word in the existing
marked generic basis, not a newly discovered direction. Its quadratic
value is **zero**.

Let H=span(g_2,...,g_17), using the published one-based labels. Its pairing
is nondegenerate. Among its 65536 vectors, q has **32640** zeros, so

    dim H=16,       Arf(q|H)=1.

An independent symplectic elimination verifies the same Arf invariant.
The radical vector r has nonzero g_1 coefficient, hence

    G = H ⊕ <r>.

## Why only four objects remain

Put V=J[2](Qbar) and W=H-perp under b. Because H is rational and
nondegenerate, this is a Galois-stable orthogonal decomposition

    V = H ⊥ W,       dim W=4.

The action on H is trivial. Additivity of Arf invariants gives
Arf(q|W)=1, since Arf(q)=0 and Arf(q|H)=1. The radical r of G lies in W,
is nonzero, rational, and isotropic.

A nonsplit four-dimensional quadratic space over F2 has exactly five
nonzero isotropic vectors. Define the following finite étale subscheme
of J[2]:

\[
 \boxed{\Omega=\{v\in H^\perp:q(v)=0,\quad v\ne0,r\}.}
\tag{1}
\]

It has degree four and is defined over Q. This is a scheme-theoretic
specification using the already defined Jacobian, theta characteristic
and marked generic classes; it is not a supplied quartic equation.

The four vectors in Ω form a basis of W, and their sum is r. In that
basis

    q(z) = sum_{i<j} z_i*z_j,
    b(e_i,e_j)=1 for i!=j.

Thus every permutation of Ω is an isometry fixing r. Conversely every
isometry fixing r permutes Ω and is determined by that permutation.
It follows that

\[
 \boxed{J[2]\simeq\mathbb F_2^{16}\oplus\mathbb F_2[\Omega]
 \quad\text{as Galois modules}.}
\tag{2}
\]

The marked G corresponds to the first summand and the diagonal vector
in the permutation summand. This is a finite torsion-module statement;
it does not assert an abelian-variety decomposition of J.

The certificate enumerates the 120 isometries of the nonsplit quadratic
space and the 24 fixing r. An independent replay conjugates every matrix
to its literal permutation action on the four isotropic vectors.

For any extension k/Q, equation (2) gives the useful exact formula

    dim J[2](k) = 16 + number of Gal(Qbar/k)-orbits on Ω.

## The existing arithmetic observations leave precisely S3 or S4

The [real-topology calculation](BRANCH_FIBRES_BOUND_NATIVE_CLASS_CREATION.md)
finds ten components of C(R). Therefore dim J[2](R)=19. Complex
conjugation fixes a three-dimensional subspace of W, so its permutation
on Ω has three cycles: it is a **single transposition**.

The [Frobenius calculation](ONE_COMMON_CLASS_FOR_A_LARGE_NATIVE_BLOCK.md)
finds odd root-curve point counts at p=211 and 229. Since the sixteen
fixed H directions have even trace, Frobenius on W has odd trace.
In a four-letter permutation module this is the parity of the number
of fixed letters. The only odd case is a **3-cycle**, with one fixed
letter.

Every subgroup of S4 containing a transposition and a 3-cycle is either
a point stabilizer S3 or all of S4. Enumeration checks all thirty
subgroups: four conjugate S3 subgroups and S4 are exactly the surviving
possibilities. Thus the two alternatives in the opening table are
exhaustive, not examples selected from a larger list.

In particular the full J[2] division field has degree **6 or 24**.
At 211 and 229 the finite-field two-torsion dimension is exactly **18**,
not merely at most 18: a 3-cycle on four letters has two orbits.
This still does not choose the rational dimension over Q.

The table makes clear why extending the earlier parity counts would not
by itself answer the question:

| Frobenius cycle type on Ω | dim J[2](Fp) | Root-curve point-count parity |
|---|---:|---|
| 1+1+1+1 | 20 | even |
| 2+1+1 | 19 | even |
| 2+2 | 18 | even |
| 3+1 | 18 | odd |
| 4 | 17 | even |

A double transposition or a 4-cycle would certify the S4 alternative.
The point-count parity alone distinguishes neither of them from a
transposition or the identity.

## A precise incidence witness and the remaining computation

If Ω has its unique rational point h, then

    L=J[2](Q)=G+<h>,       b(h,r)=1,

where L is the full original global cover pool. More generally **every**
class in L outside G pairs nontrivially with r. Conversely a constructed
rational unramified norm-square class with b(h,r)=1 proves L!=G. This
replaces a generic independence guess by one exact pairing witness.
It does not test rational solubility of an elliptic 2-cover.

The next small falsifiable computation is now specified:

1. Construct a coefficient algebra for the four-element scheme (1), or
   compute its Frobenius action directly at one of the already retained
   good primes. Use only the root equation and generic divisor data.
2. An irreducible quartic, a Frobenius 4-cycle, or a double transposition
   proves L=G. A rational point of (1), supplied with its divisor/function
   representative, proves dim L=18 and constructs the needed global class.
3. Failure to find such a representative is UNKNOWN. The existing parity
   counts do not authorize choosing either alternative.

No coefficient construction is claimed to be cheap simply because the
final algebra has degree four. Extracting this small Galois scheme from
a genus-ten Jacobian remains the concrete missing computation. Computing
it would close a restricted carrier gate: L=G makes the retained native
products have only their known singleton generic gains; L/G nonzero
permits the subsequent common-quartic solubility tests but does not solve
them.

For the central specialization question, (1) is constant with respect to
the original elliptic parameter t. Its factorization is therefore **not
a prospective rank predictor on that family**. The specialized cubic
fields still need new unramified S4 covers outside the specialization of
L, as forced by
[the strict class-creation capacity theorem](CLASS_CREATION_REQUIRES_NEW_UNRAMIFIED_COVERS.md).
No matched-panel class groups or CT matrices were recomputed here. The
rational-to-Sha switch remains a separate second-stage control.
