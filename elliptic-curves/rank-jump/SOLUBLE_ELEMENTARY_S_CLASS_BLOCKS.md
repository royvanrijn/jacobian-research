# Soluble half ideals split off elementary S-class factors

The retained half ideals survive localization in a much stronger form than
the previous unit bound established. They certify elementary direct factors
of dimensions **9, 8 and 6** in the three \(S\)-class groups. The selected
factors contain the generic strict half-ideal images, leaving relative
dimensions **8, 6 and 0**, respectively.

For the R17 high fibre, the entire eight-dimensional known strict soluble
block pairs nondegenerately with its own half ideals. For the matched low
control the corresponding six-dimensional block does too, but it is
entirely generic. MW16-05 has a nine-dimensional split factor and one
unresolved pairing direction.

These are exact **incidence** structures exposed retrospectively by known
points. They do not assert that a class-group direct factor is automatically
rationally soluble.

## What is paired

Use the same cubic field \(K\), complete bad-place set \(S\), and strict
subspace as in the [complete-boundary theorem](DERIVATIVE_RECIPROCITY_AND_COMPLETE_BOUNDARY.md).
Put
\[
C=\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{K,S_K}),\quad
U=\operatorname{Sel}_2^S(E)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(C,\mathbf F_2),
\quad V=W\cap U.
\]
The [half-ideal certificates](STRICT_HALF_IDEALS_AND_UNIT_KERNEL.md) give
\((\beta_j)=\mathfrak J_j^2\) for a basis of \(V\). Let \(h_j\) be the
image of \([\mathfrak J_j]\) in \(C[2]\). We evaluate
\[
M_{ij}=\chi_{\beta_i}(h_j).
\]
Unlike the earlier small-prime ideal dictionary, the column ideals here
are proved to have order dividing two.

This matrix is an Artin evaluation of characters on ideal classes.
It is **not** the Cassels–Tate matrix. All the displayed characters come
from rational points, so their elliptic CT pairings vanish, while their
Artin matrix can have full rank and nonzero diagonal.

The bilinear map on \(V\) is independent of its basis: changing both the
character and half-ideal basis changes \(M\) by congruence. It need not be
symmetric; its left and right kernels have different meanings.

## An elementary direct-factor certificate

Suppose some \(d\) half ideals \(h_j\) and \(d\) characters have an invertible
evaluation matrix. Let \(H\) be the subgroup generated by those ideals,
and let
\[
F:C\longrightarrow\mathbf F_2^d
\]
be evaluation by the selected characters. Since each \(h_j\) has order
dividing two, invertibility proves
\[
H\simeq(\mathbf Z/2)^d,\qquad F|_H\text{ is an isomorphism}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{C=H\oplus\ker F.}
\]
Indeed, \(c\mapsto(F|_H)^{-1}F(c)\) is a retraction onto \(H\).
This also proves \(H\cap2C=0\). Thus these classes neither vanish upon
localization nor merely survive inside a subgroup divisible by two.

A nonzero Artin matrix alone would not imply this conclusion for arbitrary
ideals. The exact identities \(\mathfrak J_j^2=(\beta_j)\) are essential.

Write \(c_S=\dim C/2\) and \(\epsilon=c_S-k\ge0\), separately on each curve.

## The unresolved MW16-05 direction

In the ten-character order retained in the artifacts, the left kernel
has coefficient mask **896**, or full point mask **29384098**.
It is a nonzero known rational strict character annihilating all ten
half-ideal columns.

The right kernel has coefficient mask **637**, or full point mask
**20890849**. This is a product of half ideals annihilated by every one
of the ten retained characters. It is not the left-kernel character.

The right-kernel ideal product might be zero in \(C\), nonzero but in
\(2C\), or detectable by an uncomputed character outside \(V\).
The calculation does not distinguish these alternatives. In particular,
a zero column combination is not a principal-ideal certificate.

This kernel also differs from the character missed by the earlier
prime-norm-\(\le97\) dictionary (mask 74). These are two different
evaluation domains, not contradictory ranks for the same matrix.

## Factorization-free Artin calculation

For each retained \(\mathfrak J_j\), one ideal reduction gives a smaller
integral ideal \(\mathfrak R_j\) and an exact principal multiplier
\[
\mathfrak J_j=(a_j)\mathfrak R_j.
\]
The multiplier is retained and its identity checked exactly. This uses
[PARI's documented extended-ideal reduction](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html-stable/General_number_fields.html#idealred);
no principal-ideal search or full class-group computation is needed.

Remove the factors of \(\mathfrak R_j\) at primes over \(S\), obtaining
\(\mathfrak Q_j\). Strict characters vanish on those factors and on all
principal ideals, so they have the same evaluation on
\(\mathfrak J_j,\mathfrak R_j,\mathfrak Q_j\).

All 24 resulting ideals have cyclic residue rings. In the recorded
integral basis, beginning with 1, their HNF has the form
\[
\begin{pmatrix}N&b&c\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus the quotient is \(\mathbf Z/N\), with the other two basis elements
mapping to \(-b,-c\). Exact multiplication verifies this ring map.
The norm \(N\) is odd and prime to the cubic discriminant.

If an integral representative \(\beta\) has unit residue \(t\bmod N\),
then
\[
(-1)^{\chi_\beta(\mathfrak Q)}
 =\left(\frac{t}{N}\right).
\]
To see this, factor the ideal formally into prime powers. The cyclic
quotient forces each contributing residue field to be \(\mathbf F_p\),
and its exponent is \(v_p(N)\). The product of Frobenius signs is
therefore exactly the ordinary Jacobi symbol. Computing that symbol
does **not** require the factorization used in this proof.

The [first protocol](HALF_IDEAL_ARTIN_PROTOCOL.json) fixed all 24 ideals,
one reduction each and 30 seconds per curve. It completed 181 of the
200 entries. Nineteen entries across four columns had nonunit residues
and were retained as UNKNOWN. Its completed-column ranks were 8, 7 and 5.

The gcds exposed by those failed residue tests were the primes
191, 53, 59 and 29. The separate
[completion protocol](HALF_IDEAL_ARTIN_COMPLETION_PROTOCOL.json) froze
exactly those 19 entries and four primes. For \(N=p^eM\), it computes
\[
(-1)^{\chi_\beta(\mathfrak Q)}
 =\left(\frac{t}{M}\right)
  (-1)^{e\,\chi_\beta(\mathfrak p)},
\]
where \(\mathfrak p\) is the unique prime above \(p\) dividing
\(\mathfrak Q\). Each local bit is checked both by PARI's local-power
routine and by the separate rational-point signature implementation.
All 19 repairs passed. The large cofactor \(M\) was never factored.
No alternative ideal reductions or new ideals were tried.

## Ranked interpretation and next experiment

1. **Incidence, strongest established block:** the high fibres contain
   large elementary \(S\)-class direct factors whose duals include many
   rational exceptional classes after generic correction. For R17 the
   entire known strict soluble block splits off. This is more specific
   than a large class-group order or a collection of local supports.
2. **Incidence, useful prospective target:** an elementary \(S\)-class
   factor and its dual characters are defined by the equation before
   points are supplied. Their relative contribution must be measured
   against the generic strict subspace. The present construction of the
   factors uses exceptional points, so these values cannot enter a
   prospective selector.
3. **Solubility, missing theorem:** nothing here proves that the dual of
   an independently obtained elementary factor consists of rational
   classes. A common construction or CT calculation must establish that.
   The Artin matrix does not replace either.
4. **Weak explanations:** disappearance under localization and a unit-only
   origin cannot account for the displayed split factors. Full Artin
   nondegeneracy alone is neither sufficient to identify high gain
   (the low control has it) nor necessary for the observed high gain
   (MW16-05 has a one-dimensional kernel on its known strict space).
5. **Visibility:** the calculation contains no point-height or chart
   exposure information. It supplies no reason to change the search
   budget or chart ranking.

The next falsifiable test should directly probe the missing solubility
implication: compare strict characters that remain in the same cubic
arithmetic block under the already retained fixed-cubic deformations,
but whose rationality changes as witnessed by nonzero CT pairings.
First compute their common strict subspace at the union of the retained
bad places. If that subspace contains an obstructed class, the unchanged
class-field data cannot by itself explain simultaneous rationality.
If it contains none, retain that negative endpoint; do not enlarge the
deformation or parameter set.
