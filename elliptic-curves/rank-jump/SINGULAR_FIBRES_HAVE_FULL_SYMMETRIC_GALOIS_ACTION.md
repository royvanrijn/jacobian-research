# Singular fibres have full symmetric Galois action

The published original R17 fibration has **Galois group S24 on its
24 singular parameter values**. This strengthens the
[degree-24 irreducibility certificate](PARAMETER_COVERS_HAVE_ROOM_BUT_NO_BLOCK_CERTIFICATE.md):
there is no rational partition of the singular fibres into nontrivial
blocks, and no proper composition of its degree-24 j-map into two rational
maps over Q of degrees greater than one.

This closes a specific possible block mechanism: the original family is
not hiding such a rational parameter composition whose fibres group the
singular places. It does not explain which smooth rational fibres gain
rank. The obstruction is common to every specialization of this family.

## Coefficient-only experiment

The [protocol](SINGULAR_FIBRE_GALOIS_PROTOCOL.json) uses only the already
frozen original coefficients and the three-prime modular certificate.
It adds no primes, parameter tests, curve searches, or exceptional points.
Both arithmetic implementations complete within the ten-second cap.

Let D(u) be the primitive integer polynomial obtained from
Delta(u)=-16(4A(u)^3+27B(u)^2) by removing its integer content.
The earlier independent verifier proves that D is irreducible over Q,
using full-degree squarefree reductions at 167, 181 and 191. In particular,
its Galois group G is transitive on 24 roots.

At 181 the irreducible factor degrees are

    (1, 6, 17).

The unramified Frobenius therefore has this cycle type. Its sixth power
has type (1,1,1,1,1,1,1,17), a single 17-cycle fixing seven points.

Any nontrivial block system for a transitive degree-24 group has block
size s and number of blocks m=24/s as follows:

| s | m |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 12 |
| 3 | 8 |
| 4 | 6 |
| 6 | 4 |
| 8 | 3 |
| 12 | 2 |

Both s and m are below 17. An element of order 17 cannot act nontrivially
on the m blocks; if it fixes them, it cannot act nontrivially within any
block either. This contradicts the displayed 17-cycle. Thus G is primitive.

Jordan's prime-cycle theorem now gives A24 contained in G, since the
17-cycle fixes at least three points. The original Frobenius is odd
(its 6-cycle is odd and its 17-cycle is even), so

    G = S24.

The theorem and the Frobenius interpretation are stated in
[Gareth A. Jones, *Primitive permutation groups containing a cycle*,
Theorem 1.1 and Comment 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.5169).
No enumeration of subgroups or construction of the splitting field is
needed. The theorem is a mathematical dependency, not an algorithmically
enumerated claim.

## No rational grouping of singular fibres

Full S24 has no invariant partition except singletons and the entire
24-element set. It also acts transitively on the k-element subsets.
Consequently, the field generated by the coefficients of a monic factor
whose roots are a specified k-element subset has exactly this degree:

| Number of selected singular values k | Field degree over Q |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 24 |
| 2 | 276 |
| 3 | 2,024 |
| 4 | 10,626 |
| 12 | 2,704,156 |

Indeed, the stabilizer of that monic factor is exactly the setwise
stabilizer S_k times S_(24-k). A larger field defining the same subset
must contain this coefficient field. These degrees concern **selected
subsets of singular parameter values**, not arbitrary descent fields or
fields generated by exceptional points.

There is also no nonidentity rational Möbius transformation preserving
the singular divisor. Its permutation of the roots would commute with
the entire Galois action S24. The centralizer of this natural S24 action
is trivial, so the Möbius transformation fixes all 24 roots and hence is
the identity. This is a statement about rational base symmetries of this
fibration, not about all automorphisms or alternative fibrations of the K3.

The obstruction even survives any finite solvable Galois extension of
the constant field. If L is the splitting field and F/Q is such an
extension, Gal(L/(L intersect F)) is normal in S24 with solvable quotient.
It contains the commutator A24, so the action over F is still primitive
and transitive. In particular, a tower of quadratic constant extensions
does not split the singular polynomial or provide a nontrivial block
system. This assertion does not concern quadratic extensions Q(u,sqrt(d(u)))
of the **parameter function field**.

## The j-map cannot have a proper rational composition

For the retained model, exact polynomial arithmetic gives

    j(u) = (-48 A(u))^3 / Delta(u),
    degree(numerator) = degree(denominator) = 24,
    gcd(numerator, denominator) = 1.

Delta is squarefree and infinity is not a pole. The reduced pole divisor
of j therefore consists precisely of the 24 singular values, each once.

Suppose j=J composed with h, where J,h belong to Q(u), and both have degree
greater than one. Pullback of pole divisors gives

    (j)_infinity = h^*((J)_infinity).

Every coefficient on the left is one. Thus every pole of J is simple,
and h is unramified at every point lying above these poles. Each pole
of J has exactly deg(h) distinct preimages. Group the 24 poles of j
according to their h-value. This is a Galois-invariant partition into
deg(J) blocks of size deg(h), contradicting primitivity.

Hence such a composition over Q does not exist. More specifically, this
excludes presenting E, even up to quadratic twist, as a pullback from an
elliptic family whose j-map has degree greater than one by a rational
parameter map of degree greater than one. Twisting does not change j.

The qualifications matter:

- A degree-one outer J is excluded from this statement. Writing any j-map
  as a pullback of a degree-one j-parameter is not a nontrivial composition
  of rational maps.
- A composition over Qbar is not tested or excluded by this argument.
- The Galois group proved here is the constant-field group of the singular
  locations. It is not a computation of the monodromy group of the j-map.
- A new cover of the original parameter line is still possible. The result
  concerns descending the original j-map through an intermediate rational
  parameter, not adjoining new functions to it.

As a regression control, 1/(u^4-4u^2+1) has a reduced four-point pole
divisor but decomposes as 1/(h(u)^2-3), h=u^2-2. Its opposite-pair partition
is compatible with the imprimitive D4 action. The test does not mistake
squarefree poles or transitivity alone for a composition obstruction.

## Relation to incidence, solubility and visibility

This is a **structural incidence exclusion** for two proposals:
rationally grouping the original singular places, or obtaining this
original family by a proper rational composition of its j-map. Neither
proposal can explain a multi-direction block here in that form.

It is not a **solubility** test. The S24 action is fixed before choosing
u0 and gives no answer about rational points on the specialized 2-covers.
In particular, S24 here must not be conflated with the S3 Galois group
of the cubic x^3+A(u0)x+B(u0). The former permutes singular parameter
values, while the latter permutes elliptic 2-torsion x-coordinates at a
fixed fibre. The discriminant of D as a polynomial in u is a constant;
it is different from the varying value Delta(u0) in the previous
discriminant-cover experiment.

Nor is this a **visibility** test: it does not use charts, heights of
points, or point-search outcomes.

## Ranked implications and next missing certificate

1. **Still viable:** a cover branched at smooth parameter values can carry
   several rational sections. S24 places no new obstruction on such a
   cover. The earlier bounds of 20 or 22 new geometric directions remain
   capacity bounds, with actual rational section spaces unknown.
2. **Excluded here:** nontrivial rational block systems on the 24 singular
   values; rational base Möbius symmetries preserving them; a proper
   rational composition of this j-map. Passing to solvable constant fields
   does not rescue the block-system proposal.
3. **Weak as a predictor:** the singular-fibre Galois group and the j-map
   obstruction are identical across all high/low controls of this family.
   They cannot discriminate their specialization ranks.
4. **Missing computation:** a rational section-space lower bound for one
   point-independent smooth-branch twist, followed by an exact quotient
   specialization map. More branch-group counting will not supply this.
5. **For Agent 1:** there is no new score or selection rule. A future block
   construction should exhibit its rational sections and specialization
   condition; it cannot rely on a rational grouping of these singular
   places. No current search change follows from this exclusion.

The unresolved chain remains a point-independent specialization condition
forcing a large soluble rational block, then independence modulo the
original MW17 subgroup. This result narrows where that condition can live;
it does not assert that the chain is closed.
