# Binary GVC nonfree-factorization tomography and consecutive-residue rigidity

## 1. Status and scope

This note carries the prime-power packet experiment one level deeper.  A
primitive projected return packet determines a semigroup of all its scaled
fiber states.  That semigroup need not be free: one state may have several
factorizations into Hilbert atoms.  We compute the Hilbert basis of every
such normalized profile through radial span six and ask whether inequivalent
factorizations can remain invisible to the factorial and marked packet data.

The outcome is negative in the tested range.  Through span four the exact
side-refined factorial map has a nonzero relation lattice in thirteen
profiles, but every one becomes injective on Hilbert atoms after adjoining
the \(C_2,C_3\) marked-side histograms.  Through spans five and six, adjoining
\(C_4\) makes every Hilbert-atom packet signature injective.  Thus no
primitive factorization relation in these profiles is an all-signature
collision.

More importantly, the pattern has a uniform proof.  On radial span \(s\),
put \(q=\lceil s/2\rceil\).  The complete marked-side histograms for
\(C_q\) and \(C_{q+1}\) reconstruct every state when \(s\) is odd.  When
\(s\) is even, their entire kernel is generated by a conformal sum of known
safe four-point beta swaps.  Consequently the nonfree factorization
semigroup is injective modulo safe circuits in every span once one fixed
marked packet has been promoted.

This closes the abstract nonfree-factorization ambiguity, but it is not a
proof of unrestricted \(\operatorname{GVC}(2)\): it does not prove that a
prime-dependent Hall--jet shell inherits one fixed profile with these
markings.  Nor is a factorial-only collision below a GVC counterexample.

## 2. The scaled-fiber semigroup

Let \(R,B\subseteq\{0,\ldots,s\}\) be the marked operator and polynomial
levels of a normalized primitive packet.  Fix color counts \(c_R,c_B>0\)
and total level \(w\).  Its scaled-fiber semigroup is

\[
 \mathsf S(R,B;c_R,c_B,w)=
 \left\{
 (N,x^R,x^B)\in\mathbb N^{1+|R|+|B|}:
 \begin{array}{l}
 |x^R|=c_RN,\\
 |x^B|=c_BN,\\
 \sum_{i\in R}i x^R_i+
 \sum_{j\in B}j x^B_j=wN
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Normaliz computes the Hilbert basis \(H=\{h_1,\ldots,h_t\}\) of (2.1).
For

\[
 h=(N,x^R,x^B),
\]

put \(\bar w=s(c_R+c_B)-w\).  The repeated-ray factorial weight is

\[
 W_h(k)=
 \frac{(kc_RN)!}{\prod_i(kx^R_i)!}
 \frac{(kc_BN)!}{\prod_j(kx^B_j)!}
 (kwN)!(k\bar wN)!.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Its side-refined signed slope vector has entries

\[
\begin{aligned}
 v_h^R&=[c_RN]-\sum_{i:x^R_i>0}[x^R_i],\\
 v_h^B&=[c_BN]-\sum_{j:x^B_j>0}[x^B_j],\\
 v_h^\rho&=[wN]_x+[\bar wN]_y.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let \(M_H\) be the matrix whose columns contain both the atom \(h_i\) and
the vector (2.3).  Then

\[
 z\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}M_H
\tag{2.4}
\]

is exactly an equality of two Hilbert-atom factorizations which gives the
same semigroup state and the same side-refined factorial product for every
dilation \(k\).  In particular, every Legendre valuation and factorial unit
of that product agrees at every prime and prime power.  The
[factorial-trace separation theorem](FACTORIAL_TRACE_INDEPENDENCE.md)
shows that unequal signed vectors cannot cancel in a fixed finite trace.

For each atom we additionally retain its radial vector, its two positive
multiplicity partitions, and the marked group-ring histograms

\[
 \left(\sum_{i\equiv a\bmod q}x^R_i\right)_{a\in C_q},
 \qquad
 \left(\sum_{j\equiv a\bmod q}x^B_j\right)_{a\in C_q}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Fourier evaluation of (2.5) gives every \(C_q\)-character trace.  A
factorization signature is the multiset of its atom signatures; exact marked
levels are not inserted into the key.

## 3. Complete span-four factorial census

There are 426 raw two-color Graver relations through radial span four.  After
translation, common dilation, reversal, color exchange, and side exchange,
90 remain.  The unresolved support-at-least-five filter gives 65 distinct
scaled profiles.

For every profile the computation obtains its complete Hilbert basis and the
exact rational rank of \(M_H\).  The result is:

| factorial layer | profiles |
|---|---:|
| \(\ker M_H=0\) | 52 |
| nonzero kernel, entirely internal reversal | 1 |
| nonreversal same-vector kernel | 12 |

The largest two same-vector profiles have 34 and 56 Hilbert atoms.  The
calculation records exact integer kernel bases for them.  For the eleven
collision profiles having at most twenty atoms, the Lawrence lifting gives
the complete factorial-compatible Graver basis: 308 unoriented primitive
moves.  Of these, packet partitions separate 15, \(C_2\) separates 207, and
\(C_3\) separates the remaining 86.

Full Graver enumeration is unnecessary for the two larger profiles.  On all
65 Hilbert bases the atom signature itself becomes injective as follows:

| first injective atom layer | profiles |
|---|---:|
| packet partitions | 35 |
| \(C_2\) | 14 |
| \(C_3\) | 16 |
| unresolved | 0 |

If atom signatures are injective, two different factorization multisets
cannot have the same packet signature.  Thus this last check separates every
factorization relation, including the unenumerated Graver moves in the two
large lattices.

### 3.1 The first factorial-only square

The first nonreversal relation occurs on

\[
 R=\{0,4\},\qquad B=\{0,1,2,4\},\qquad
 (c_R,c_B)=(1,3),\qquad (w,\bar w)=(8,8).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Four \(N=1\) Hilbert atoms satisfy

\[
 \bigl(R_4B_0^2B_4\bigr)
 \bigl(R_0B_2^2B_4\bigr)
 =
 \bigl(R_4B_0B_2^2\bigr)
 \bigl(R_0B_0B_4^2\bigr).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Every atom in (3.2) has operator partition \((1)\), polynomial partition
\((2,1)\), radial vector \((8,8)\), and repeated weight

\[
 \binom{3k}{k}(8k)!^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently (3.2) is invisible to every valuation and factorial-unit probe.
It is the lifted centered-triple curvature

\[
 X=\frac{B_0B_4}{B_2^2}
\]

appearing over both operator marks.  It is \(C_2\)-blind because all three
polynomial levels are even.  The \(C_3\) polynomial histograms on its two
sides are, however,

\[
 \{(2,1,0),(0,1,2)\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \{(1,0,2),(1,2,0)\},
\tag{3.4}
\]

so \(C_3\) separates it.  This is a concrete factorial-tomography collision,
but not an all-packet collision and not a GVC lift.

## 5. Consecutive residues close every fixed marked span

For an integer vector \(u=(u_0,\ldots,u_s)\), let
\(\mathcal H_n(u)\) be its complete residue histogram modulo \(n\).

> **Theorem 5.1 (consecutive-residue reconstruction).**  Let \(q\geq2\).
> On levels \(0,\ldots,2q-1\), the map
> \[
>  u\longmapsto\bigl(\mathcal H_q(u),\mathcal H_{q+1}(u)\bigr)
> \tag{5.1}
> \]
> is injective over \(\mathbb Z\).  On levels \(0,\ldots,2q\), its kernel is
> generated by
> \[
>  \kappa_q=
>  (\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{q},0,
>   \underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{q}).
> \tag{5.2}
> \]

### Proof

Make a bipartite graph with left vertices \(L_0,\ldots,L_{q-1}\), right
vertices \(T_0,\ldots,T_q\), and one edge

\[
 e_i=\bigl(L_{i\bmod q},T_{i\bmod(q+1)}\bigr)
 \qquad(0\leq i\leq2q).
\tag{5.3}
\]

The matrix in (5.1) is the vertex--edge incidence matrix of this graph.
Multiplying the right-vertex rows by \(-1\) turns it into the oriented
incidence matrix without changing its column kernel.  The first \(2q\)
edges form the path

\[
 T_q-L_0-T_0-L_1-T_1-\cdots-L_{q-1}-T_{q-1}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Indeed \(e_i=L_iT_i\) for \(0\leq i<q\), while
\(e_{q+j}=L_jT_{j-1}\) for \(0\leq j<q\), with \(T_{-1}=T_q\).
The columns of the incidence matrix of a forest are independent, proving
the first assertion.  The final edge \(e_{2q}=L_0T_{q-1}\) creates exactly
one cycle.  Its signed edge vector is (5.2), while the path edge
\(e_q=L_0T_q\) has coefficient zero.  This proves the second assertion.
\(\square\)

> **Corollary 5.2 (all-span return rigidity modulo beta swaps).**  Fix a
> radial span \(s\geq3\) and put \(q=\lceil s/2\rceil\).  Suppose two
> two-colour states have the same total level and the same
> \(C_q,C_{q+1}\) histograms separately on the operator and polynomial
> sides.
>
> If \(s\) is odd, the states are equal.  If \(s=2q\) is even, their
> difference is a conformal sum of the \(q\) four-point returns
> \[
>  R_iB_{i+q+1}=R_{i+q+1}B_i,
>  \qquad 0\leq i<q.
> \tag{5.5}
> \]
> Consequently no unresolved \(C_q,C_{q+1}\)-blind Graver-primitive return
> survives: the only possible kernel decomposes into known safe beta
> circuits.

For odd \(s=2q-1\), Theorem 5.1 gives equality on each colour.  For even
\(s=2q\), write the two colour differences as \(a\kappa_q\) and
\(b\kappa_q\).  The weighted level of \(\kappa_q\) is

\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{2q}i(\kappa_q)_i=-q(q+1).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Equality of total levels gives \(b=-a\).  After orienting \(a\), the combined
difference is \(\lvert a\rvert\) copies of every relation in (5.5), with no
sign cancellation.  This is a conformal decomposition.

> **Corollary 5.3 (nonfree factorization quotient is injective).**  Give
> each scaled-fibre atom its radial vector and its four marked-side
> histograms for \(C_q,C_{q+1}\), with \(q=\lceil s/2\rceil\).  Two
> factorizations with the same multiset of these atom signatures differ
> only by atomwise applications of the safe beta relations (5.5).

Indeed equal multisets pair the atom occurrences signature by signature.
Each paired atom has equal colour counts from the trivial character, equal
total level from its radial vector, and equal consecutive histograms.
Corollary 5.2 makes the pair equal or safely equivalent.  Applying this to
every pair proves the claim.

Thus no Hilbert or Graver basis is needed for the all-span quotient theorem.
The computations in Sections 3--4 remain valuable independent checks with
the smaller orders \(C_2,C_3,C_4\), and they locate the first
factorial-only ambiguity explicitly.

### 5.1 The span-six specialization

There is a short structural explanation at the primitive-return level.  For
an integer vector \(u=(u_0,\ldots,u_6)\), let \(\mathcal H_q(u)\) be its
residue histogram modulo \(q\).

> **Proposition 5.4 (small-order span-six reconstruction).**
> If
>
> \[
>  \mathcal H_2(u)=\mathcal H_3(u)=\mathcal H_4(u)=0,
> \]
>
> then
>
> \[
>  u=a(1,1,1,0,-1,-1,-1)
> \tag{5.7}
> \]
>
> for some integer \(a\).  On levels \(0,\ldots,5\), necessarily \(a=0\).
> If two colored states through span six have the same color counts, total
> level, and \(C_2,C_3,C_4\) histograms on each color, their difference is a
> conformal sum of the three support-four returns
>
> \[
>  R_iB_{i+4}=R_{i+4}B_i,\qquad i=0,1,2.
> \tag{5.8}
> \]
>
> Consequently no support-at-least-five Graver-primitive return relation
> through span six is invisible to all \(C_2,C_3,C_4\) character data.

Indeed the \(C_4\) equations give

\[
 u_3=0,qquad u_4=-u_0,qquad u_5=-u_1,qquad u_6=-u_2.
\]

The residue-one and residue-two \(C_3\) equations then give
\(u_1=u_0\) and \(u_2=u_1\), proving (5.7).  If level six is absent,
\(u_6=0\) forces \(a=0\).

For two colors, write their differences as \(a\kappa\) and \(b\kappa\),
where \(\kappa\) is the vector in (5.7).  Its weighted level is \(-12\).
Equality of the total levels therefore gives \(b=-a\).  After orienting the
sign of \(a\), the combined difference is exactly \(\lvert a\rvert\) copies
of the three relations (5.8), with no sign cancellation.  Thus every nonzero
invisible relation is a conformal sum of the already-safe support-four
pieces and is not Graver primitive.

Proposition 5.4 is unbounded in the multiplicities.  The Hilbert computations
in Section 4 additionally verify the second-order statement for every
nonfree profile selected by the primitive census.

## 6. What moved, and what remains

The proposed adelic/Graver programme passes its first serious test:

1. exact factorial slopes dispose of 52 of the 65 span-four profiles;
2. the residual same-vector lattices are real, with the first one given by
   (3.2), so scalar tomography alone is insufficient;
3. marked packet partitions and \(C_2,C_3,C_4\) separate every Hilbert atom
   through span six; and
4. Theorem 5.1 and Corollaries 5.2--5.3 prove, at every span, that two
   consecutive marked residue groups reconstruct the packet modulo
   already-safe beta circuits.

This closes the **nonfree-factorization gap in the fixed marked projected
model at every radial span**.  It does not close affine-carry promotion
\((\mathrm{SC})\).  A Hall--jet initial shell may still select different
profiles at different primes before any fixed character packet is inherited.
The next theorem must therefore be a signature-compatible promotion
statement: after fixing the bounded low digit, a primitive return move must
be inherited with one common high-digit quotient, or its safe circuit
decomposition must be inherited as a triangular block.  The exact
\(m=p+r\) transform shows that the high quotient is empty or singleton; the
remaining issue is cancellation among the singleton-marked low bridges.
The subsequent
[affine singleton-localization theorem](BINARY_GVC_AFFINE_SINGLETON_LOCALIZATION.md)
proves the state-level reduction exactly: every such fibre difference
conformally decomposes in one fixed Graver basis; an exposed singleton has a
finite prime-independent signed correction alphabet, while every unbounded
branch repeats one fixed primitive move with positive density; the full long
radial-carry interval is one bounded-order derivative row.  The subsequent
[translation-tangent theorem](BINARY_GVC_TRANSLATION_TANGENT_RIGIDITY.md)
shows that the coefficient-blind **linear-to-toric inheritance** formulation
is false even for two free module translates with a common factorial weight.
The corrected remaining problem is Hall-specific: the full Cartesian
translation/derivative tower must expose a flat scale-compatible twist or a
nonflat curvature block over one common high quotient.  Its primitive
one-direction tangent and every prime-power-character case with sufficiently
large underlying prime are now proved; exceptional small primes, mixed-prime
torsion, and the genuinely two-dimensional case remain.

At span seven the small \(C_2,C_3,C_4\) histogram kernel has a second
generator, but Theorem 5.1 with \(q=4\) says that \(C_4,C_5\) reconstruct
all eight levels exactly.  A complete span-seven Hilbert-atom census is
therefore unnecessary for the quotient theorem, although it could remain a
useful small-character regression.

  --verify-consecutive-residues
