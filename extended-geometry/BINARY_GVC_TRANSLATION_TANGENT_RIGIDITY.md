# Binary GVC translation-tangent rigidity

## 1. Scope and outcome

The finite Hilbert-module normal form does not, by itself, imply the proposed
linear-to-toric Hall inheritance statement.  Section 2 gives an all-order
counterexample with a common factorial weight and two free module translates.
It is an abstract trace counterexample, not a binary GVC pair.

The rank-one Cartesian structure nevertheless supplies an additional exact
row which is absent from a general module.  For the translated monomial

\[
 (z+Y)^d=\sum_{j=0}^d\binom dj z^{d-j}Y^j,
\]

Section 3 determines the complete tangent kernel of all moving diagonal
periods.  At a primitive slope \(\gcd(d,r)=1\), that kernel is one-dimensional
and is generated by the flat torus direction \(j-r\).  Section 4 uses this to
prove that, for every sufficiently large underlying prime \(q\) and every
\(a\geq1\), two \(q^a\)-order Taylor twists with the same complete
diagonal-period sequence are already scalar--torus equivalent on that slope.

This closes the large-prime-power, one-direction, monomial Taylor branch of the
translation-curvature target.  It does not prove affine-carry promotion,
handle the finitely many exceptional underlying primes, classify mixed-prime
composite character order, or triangularize a general two-dimensional Taylor
down-set.
Consequently no unrestricted \(\operatorname {GVC}(2)\) theorem or binary
counterexample is claimed.

## 2. A module-only inheritance theorem is false

Let

\[
 f_1=z+z^{-1},\qquad f_2=z^2+z^{-2},\qquad R_N=(N!)^2.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Take two disjoint translates of the free semigroup \(\mathbb N\), and give
their scale-\(N\) states the weights

\[
 W_{1,N}=R_N\operatorname {CT}(f_1^N),\qquad
 W_{2,N}=-R_N\operatorname {CT}(f_2^N).
\tag{2.2}
\]

> **Proposition 2.1 (factorially weighted module inheritance fails
> abstractly).**  The complete shell \(W_{1,N}+W_{2,N}\) vanishes for every
> \(N\geq1\), but neither module translate has an identically zero row and
> neither translate contains a nontrivial Graver move.  Moreover the
> cancellation is nonterminal for a fixed Laurent multiplier.

### Proof

The dilation \(z\mapsto z^2\) preserves constant terms, so

\[
 \operatorname {CT}(f_1^N)=\operatorname {CT}(f_2^N)
\]

at every order.  For even \(N\) the common value is
\(\binom N{N/2}\ne0\), proving that neither translate vanishes.  Each
translate has one free generator, so its factorization matrix is \([1]\) and
its integer kernel, hence its Graver basis, is zero.  Finally, for odd \(N\),

\[
 \operatorname {CT}(z^{-1}f_1^N)
 -\operatorname {CT}(z^{-1}f_2^N)
 =\binom N{(N-1)/2}\ne0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the pure cancellation does not survive a fixed multiplier.  \(\square\)

The common factor \(R_N\) shows that merely adjoining a radial factorial does
not repair the implication.  Proposition 2.1 does not satisfy the full
rank-one Cartesian Hall/translation hypotheses.  Its role is logical: the
remaining theorem cannot be deduced from Dickson--Gordan finiteness, Graver
decomposition, and factorial weighting alone.  Any valid promotion lemma must
use the Taylor down-set and its adjacent-channel derivative relations.

## 3. The complete translated-binomial tangent kernel

Fix \(d\geq2\) and \(1\leq r<d\).  For
\(b=(b_0,\ldots,b_d)\) over a characteristic-zero field define

\[
 T_N(b)=\sum_{j=0}^d b_j\binom dj
 \binom{d(N-1)}{rN-j},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where an out-of-range binomial coefficient is zero.  Equivalently, if

\[
 B(t)=\sum_{j=0}^d b_j\binom dj t^j,
\]

then

\[
 T_N(b)=[t^{rN}]B(t)(1+t)^{d(N-1)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

> **Theorem 3.1 (primitive translation-tangent rigidity).**  If
> \(\gcd(d,r)=1\), then
> \[
>  T_N(b)=0\quad(N\geq1)
>  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
>  b_j=c(j-r)\quad(0\leq j\leq d)
> \tag{3.3}
> \]
> for one scalar \(c\).

### Proof

Put \(s=d-r\), \(x=N-1\), and \(k=j-r\).  For all sufficiently large
\(N\), divide (3.1) by the nonzero central term
\(\binom{dx}{rx}\).  The term indexed by \(k\) becomes

\[
 R_k(x)=
 \frac{(rx)!(sx)!}{(rx-k)!(sx+k)!}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

For \(k>0\) and \(\ell>0\), respectively,

\[
 R_k(x)=\frac{\prod_{a=0}^{k-1}(rx-a)}
                 {\prod_{a=1}^{k}(sx+a)},
 \qquad
 R_{-\ell}(x)=\frac{\prod_{a=0}^{\ell-1}(sx-a)}
                 {\prod_{a=1}^{\ell}(rx+a)}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Hence all solutions of (3.1) give a rational-function relation among
\(R_{-r},\ldots,R_s\).  The positive functions have possible poles
\(-a/s\), \(1\leq a\leq s\), and the negative functions have possible poles
\(-b/r\), \(1\leq b\leq r\).  Since \(\gcd(r,s)=1\), the two lists meet only
at \(-1\).  Every displayed pole is simple and its numerator is nonzero
there.

At \(-(s-1)/s\), only \(R_{s-1},R_s\) among the not-yet-determined positive
tail can contribute.  Descending through
\(-(s-2)/s),\ldots,-1/s\) determines every positive coefficient from the
coefficient of \(R_s\).  The negative poles similarly determine every
negative coefficient from that of \(R_{-r}\).  The common pole \(-1\)
relates those two extreme coefficients, and the constant term is then
determined at any regular point.  The relation space therefore has dimension
at most one.

It is nonzero.  For \(H(t)=(1+t)^d\), the choice \(b_j=j-r\) gives

\[
 B(t)=tH'(t)-rH(t).
\]

Consequently

\[
 [t^{rN}]B(t)H(t)^{N-1}
 =\frac1N[t^{rN}]t\frac d{dt}H(t)^N
  -r[t^{rN}]H(t)^N=0.
\]

This proves (3.3).  \(\square\)

The generator \(j-r\) is exactly the infinitesimal form of
\(h(t)\mapsto C h(\eta t)\) subject to preservation of the moving coefficient
\([t^{rN}]h(t)^N\).  If \(g=\gcd(d,r)>1\), the Laurent polynomial
\(t^{-r}(1+t)^d\) is a \(g\)-th power and additional residue-subsequence
directions occur.  The primitive hypothesis is therefore structural, not a
proof artifact.

## 4. Large prime-power torsion twists are flat

Let \(q\) be prime, \(a\geq1\), let \(\zeta\) be a primitive \(q^a\)-th
root of unity, and for
\(u=(u_0,\ldots,u_d)\in(\mathbb Z/q^a)^{d+1}\) put

\[
 h_u(t)=\sum_{j=0}^d\zeta^{u_j}\binom dj t^j,
 \qquad
 A_{u,r}(N)=[t^{rN}]h_u(t)^N.
\tag{4.1}
\]

> **Corollary 4.1 (eventual prime-power-character rigidity).**  Fix
> primitive \((d,r)\).  There is a finite set of rational primes
> \(E_{d,r}\) such that, for every prime \(q\notin E_{d,r}\), every
> \(a\geq1\), and every pair of \(q^a\)-order twists,
> \[
>  A_{u,r}(N)=A_{v,r}(N)\quad(N\geq1)
> \tag{4.2}
> \]
> implies
> \[
>  v_j-u_j=c(j-r)\pmod {q^a}
> \tag{4.3}
> \]
> for one \(c\in\mathbb Z/q^a\).  Thus
> \[
>  h_v(t)=\zeta^{-cr}h_u(\zeta^c t),
> \tag{4.4}
> \]
> and the collision is the flat scalar--torus symmetry.

### Proof

By Theorem 3.1 the infinite matrix in (3.1) has rank \(d\).  Choose
positive rows \(N_1,\ldots,N_d\) and a nonzero \(d\)-minor \(\Delta\).
Put in \(E_{d,r}\) every prime dividing \(\Delta\prod_iN_i\), together with
the finitely many primes at most \(\max_iN_i\).

In \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]\), set \(\pi=1-\zeta\).  Its residue field at
\((\pi)\) is \(\mathbb F_q\).  We prove (4.3) one \(q\)-adic digit at a
time.  Suppose, after removing a flat scalar--torus twist already found, that

\[
 v_j-u_j=q^k b_j\pmod {q^a}
 \qquad(0\leq k<a).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Put \(\delta_k=\zeta^{q^k}-1\).  Then

\[
 \zeta^{q^k b}=(1+\delta_k)^b
 \equiv1+b\delta_k\pmod {\delta_k^2}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Expand (4.2) for a fixed \(N_i\), subtract, divide by \(\delta_k\), and
reduce modulo \(\pi\).  The common factor
\(\zeta^{\sum_j u_jn_j}\) reduces to one.  Marking one of the \(N_i\)
selected Taylor channels gives

\[
 0=N_i\sum_{j=0}^d b_j\binom dj
       \binom{d(N_i-1)}{rN_i-j}
   =N_iT_{N_i}(b)\qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_q.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The chosen primes make every \(N_i\) and \(\Delta\) invertible, so the
reduced row matrix has rank \(d\).  Its kernel is the reduction of the
one-dimensional characteristic-zero kernel in Theorem 3.1.  Hence
\(b_j=c_k(j-r)\pmod q\).  Removing the flat twist with exponent
\(q^kc_k(j-r)\) makes every difference divisible by \(q^{k+1}\).  Starting
at \(k=0\) and iterating through \(k=a-1\) proves (4.3).  Equation (4.4) is
immediate, and its scalar and torus factors cancel on the coefficient of
\(t^{rN}\).  \(\square\)

Corollary 4.1 is an exact all-degree and all-prime-power statement;
“sufficiently large” concerns the underlying prime, not its exponent, the
Taylor degree, or the number of moment rows in the premise.  Small underlying
primes and mixed-prime composite torsion still require additional
cyclotomic-adic rows.  Those rows are the natural analogue of factorial-unit
tomography in the Hall shell.

## 5. Corrected final target

Proposition 2.1 retires the module-only formulation of the final lemma.  A
valid statement must retain all of the following hypotheses:

1. the packet comes from one rank-one Cartesian operator/polynomial support;
2. its channel coefficients form a connected Taylor down-set and obey the
   adjacent-channel derivative identities;
3. the inherited rows include the complete translated diagonal-period tower,
   not only a specialized scalar shell; and
4. factorial normalization is performed over one common high-digit quotient.

Under these hypotheses, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 4.1 prove the primitive
one-direction monomial tangent and large-prime-power torsion cases.  The
unresolved promotion theorem is now narrower:

> A nonflat small-prime or mixed-prime-torsion collision on a two-dimensional Taylor
> down-set must be exposed by a higher cyclotomic-adic derivative row with the
> same high-digit quotient, or else realize an actual nonterminal rank-one
> Cartesian Hall packet.

That statement, rather than a coefficient-blind Hilbert-module idempotent, is
the remaining route to unrestricted binary GVC.
