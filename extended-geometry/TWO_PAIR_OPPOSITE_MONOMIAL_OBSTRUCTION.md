# Opposite monomial corrections never propagate the two-pair seed

## 1. Statement

Let
\[
 A_d=R^{d-4}F,\qquad d\geq5,
\]
where \(F\) is the quartic two-pair Image-Mathieu counterexample.  For
\(1\leq s\leq d\), put
\[
 K_{d,s}(a,b)
 =A_d+aZ^sT^{d-s}+bW^sT^{d-s}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

> **Theorem 1.1.** If
> \[
>  \mathcal E_2(K_{d,s}(a,b)^m)=0\qquad(m\geq1),
>  \tag{1.2}
> \]
> then \(a=b=0\).

This is an obstruction to propagating the known counterexample, not a
classification of \(V_d\).  It excludes one opposite pair at a time; it
does not exclude sums involving several phase pairs or independent height
profiles.

Most cases follow from the
[primitive-prefix obstruction](TWO_PAIR_PRIMITIVE_PREFIX_OBSTRUCTION.md).
Odd height \(d-s\) is Theorem 1.5 there.  For even height, phases
\(s\geq3\) are Theorem 1.6 and phase \(s=2\) is Theorem 1.8.  It remains
only to close
\[
 K_h(a,b)
 =R^{2h-3}F+aZT^{2h}+bWT^{2h},
 \qquad d=2h+1,\quad h\geq2.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

## 2. Uniform phase-one elimination

On the Hopf sphere, remove the common radial factor and write
\[
 q=p+axt^{2h}+byt^{2h},
 \qquad y=\frac{1-t^2}{2x},
 \tag{2.1}
\]
where
\[
 p=\frac{1-t^2}{2x}
   +\frac{1-3t^2}{2}
   -\frac{3t^2}{2}x
   -\frac{t^2}{2}x^2.
 \tag{2.2}
\]
The full contraction is the angular constant term multiplied by the
nonzero radial factorial \((dm+1)!\).

The second angular moment solves
\[
 a=
 \frac{3b(2h+1)(4h+1)(4h+3)}
 {2(2h+5)
  \bigl(4bh^2+8bh+3b+16h^2+16h+3\bigr)}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]
If the denominator multiplying \(a\) in the original second-moment
equation vanishes, its remaining term is nonzero for \(b\ne0\), so no
branch was lost.  In particular \(b=0\) forces \(a=0\).

Assume \(b\ne0\) and substitute (2.3).  After deleting factors nonzero for
\(h\geq2\), the third- and fourth-moment numerators are respectively a
quadratic \(H_h(b)\) and a cubic \(L_h(b)\).  Their exact coefficient
polynomials are recorded in the generated certificate.  Exact elimination
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Res}_b(H_h,L_h)
={}&36(4h+5)h^2(2h+1)^2(2h+3)^2(2h+7)^2\\
 &\cdot(4h+1)^2(4h+7)^2(4h+3)^6P_{31}(h),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]
where \(P_{31}\in\mathbb Z[h]\) has degree \(31\).  All displayed linear
factors are nonzero for \(h\geq2\).

The reduction of the descending coefficient vector of \(P_{31}\) modulo
\(29\) is
\[
\begin{split}
(26,20,11,10,16,1,17,3,4,0,8,21,9,25,8,14,\\
 28,10,5,14,24,1,28,15,22,10,25,5,27,15,16,19).
\end{split}
\tag{2.5}
\]
Its values on the \(29\) residue classes are
\[
\begin{split}
(19,22,21,20,6,24,16,3,20,6,5,8,27,26,14,\\
 21,19,11,3,4,9,13,18,16,6,2,23,18,25),
\end{split}
\tag{2.6}
\]
so it has no root in \(\mathbb F_{29}\).  An integral root would reduce
to a root modulo \(29\); hence \(P_{31}(h)\ne0\) for every integer \(h\).
Thus (2.4) is nonzero for \(h\geq2\), the two residual moment equations
have no common solution, and Theorem 1.1 follows.

## 3. Consequences and remaining frontier

1. Every one-pair opposite monomial correction of radial propagation is
   rigid in every balanced degree \(d>4\).
2. The degree-five conclusion is no longer exceptional: it is the first
   member \(h=2\) of the uniform phase-one elimination.
3. A successful primitive continuation must use at least two different
   absolute phases, a nonmonomial height profile, or an operation not
   expressible as addition of a single opposite pair.
4. The result does not obstruct the existing finite-prefix primitive
   perturbations, because those deliberately fail a later pure moment.

There is also a local strengthening for the first multi-pair sector.  In
degree five put
\[
 H=a_2Z^2T^3+a_4Z^4T+b_2W^2T^3+b_4W^4T.
 \tag{3.1}
\]
Every linearized moment of \(RF+\varepsilon H\) vanishes by height parity.
Let \(I_2\) be the ideal generated by the coefficients of
\(\varepsilon^2\) in moments \(2,\ldots,10\).

> **Proposition 3.1.** Over characteristic zero,
> \[
>  \sqrt{I_2}=(a_2,a_4,b_2,b_4).
> \tag{3.2}
> \]

Indeed, exact sparse contraction gives nine quadratic forms.  Their
grevlex Gröbner basis reduces
\[
 a_2^3,\quad a_4^3,\quad b_2^3,\quad b_4^3
 \tag{3.3}
\]
to zero.  Thus their projective common zero set is empty.  Consequently
there is no nonconstant all-moment-zero formal deformation whose
correction remains inside this odd-height subspace at every order.  This
restricted-sector statement alone would not exclude using even-height
terms at second order to cancel the quadratic moments.

The same Gröbner basis gives the sharper intrinsic description
\[
 \operatorname{Hilb}\bigl(k[a_2,a_4,b_2,b_4]/I_2\bigr)=(1,4,1).
 \tag{3.4}
\]
Thus the obstruction algebra has length six and one-dimensional socle in
degree two.  The multiplication pairing from degree one to that socle is
nondegenerate: an integral Gram matrix recorded in the certificate has
determinant congruent to \(9\pmod {11}\).  Hence the quadratic obstruction
algebra is a compressed Artin--Gorenstein algebra.  Up to a linear change
of the four correction variables, its nine-dimensional space of
quadratic relations is the apolar hyperplane of one nondegenerate
quadratic form.

That possible second-order cancellation can also be eliminated.  Let
\[
 \mathcal C_5=
 \left\{
 \sum_{s=1}^5
 \left(a_sZ^sT^{5-s}+b_sW^sT^{5-s}\right)
 \right\}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

> **Theorem 3.2.** The point \(RF\) is formally isolated in
> \(RF+\mathcal C_5\) under the equations of all pure moments.

The linearized moments \(2,\ldots,7\) have rank six on the even-height
variables
\[
 a_1,a_3,a_5,b_1,b_3,b_5.
 \tag{3.6}
\]
Their \(6\times6\) coefficient matrix is invertible, while height parity
kills the four odd-height variables.  Therefore the first nonzero
coefficient of any hypothetical formal arc must lie in the odd-height
space (3.1).

At twice that parameter order, moments \(2,\ldots,7\) uniquely solve the
six even-height correction coefficients.  Substitution into moments
\(8,\ldots,11\) leaves four quadratic forms in
\((a_2,a_4,b_2,b_4)\).  Their exact Gröbner basis contains
\[
 a_2^5,\quad a_4^5,\quad b_2^5,\quad b_4^5.
 \tag{3.7}
\]
Hence the projected quadratic cone is supported only at the origin,
contradicting the choice of a nonzero leading coefficient.  This proves
formal isolation.

The four projected quadrics form a homogeneous complete intersection:
\[
 \operatorname{Hilb}
 \frac{k[a_2,a_4,b_2,b_4]}{J_{\mathrm{proj}}}
 =(1,4,6,4,1),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{length}=16.
 \tag{3.8}
\]
This theorem remains local.  It does not rule out an isolated finite
multiweight solution away from \(RF\), nor corrections outside
\(\mathcal C_5\).

## 4. Higher-degree formal rigidity

The same elimination pattern persists in the next two degrees.  Define
\[
 \mathcal C_d=
 \left\{
 \sum_{s=1}^d
 \left(a_sZ^sT^{d-s}+b_sW^sT^{d-s}\right)
 \right\}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

> **Theorem 4.1.** For \(d=6,7\), the point \(R^{d-4}F\) is formally
> isolated in \(R^{d-4}F+\mathcal C_d\) under all pure-moment equations.

For \(d=6\), moments \(2,\ldots,7\) give an invertible linear block on
the six even-height variables.  The odd-height kernel has dimension six,
and after linear elimination, moments \(8,\ldots,13\) give six quadrics.
For \(d=7\), the even-height block has dimension eight and pivot orders
\(2,\ldots,9\); the six projected quadrics occur at orders
\(10,\ldots,15\).

In both degrees the projected Gröbner ideal contains the seventh power of
every odd-height variable.  The six quadrics form a complete intersection
with
\[
 \operatorname{Hilb}=(1,6,15,20,15,6,1),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{length}=64.
 \tag{4.2}
\]
The leading-coefficient argument from Theorem 3.2 then proves formal
isolation.

The next degree is accessible by a good-prime certificate.

> **Theorem 4.2.** For \(d=8,9,10,11\), the point \(R^{d-4}F\) is formally
> isolated in \(R^{d-4}F+\mathcal C_d\) under all pure-moment equations.

For \(d=8\), moments \(2,\ldots,9\) give the invertible eight-variable
linear block and moments \(10,\ldots,17\) give the eight projected
quadrics.  For \(d=9\), the ten linear pivots are moments \(2,\ldots,11\)
and the eight projected quadrics are moments \(12,\ldots,19\).  Reducing
either of these first two exact rational systems modulo \(1000003\), Singular computes
quotient dimension \(256\) and reduces the ninth power of every variable
to zero.  Both linear pivot determinants are nonzero at this prime.

For \(d=10\), moments \(2,\ldots,11\) give ten linear pivots and moments
\(12,\ldots,21\) give ten projected quadrics.  At the same prime, the
quotient has dimension \(1024\) and every variable's eleventh power
reduces to zero.  The pivot determinant is again nonzero.

For \(d=11\), moments \(2,\ldots,13\) give twelve linear pivots and
moments \(14,\ldots,23\) give ten projected quadrics.  The same prime
gives quotient dimension \(1024\), zero eleventh-power remainders, and a
nonzero pivot determinant.

Finite special fiber implies that the homogeneous resultant is nonzero
modulo \(1000003\), hence nonzero in characteristic zero.  Therefore the
eight rational quadrics have no projective common zero.  Being eight
quadrics in eight variables, they form a regular sequence with
\[
 \operatorname{Hilb}=(1,8,28,56,70,56,28,8,1),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{length}=256.
 \tag{4.3}
\]
The formal leading-coefficient argument again proves isolation.
In degree ten the corresponding Hilbert vector is
\[
 (1,10,45,120,210,252,210,120,45,10,1),
 \qquad \operatorname{length}=1024.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

The seven verified degrees suggest a precise pattern.  Put
\[
 e_d=2\lceil d/2\rceil,\qquad o_d=2\lfloor d/2\rfloor.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

> **Conjecture 4.3.** For every \(d\geq5\), moments
> \(2,\ldots,e_d+1\) give an invertible linear block on the \(e_d\)
> even-height variables.  After eliminating them, the next \(o_d\)
> moments give a complete intersection of \(o_d\) quadrics on the
> odd-height kernel.  Its Hilbert series is \((1+t)^{o_d}\), and
> \(R^{d-4}F\) is formally isolated in \(R^{d-4}F+\mathcal C_d\).

The linear block and the phase-parity split are plausible targets for a
uniform phase-triangular proof.  The substantive remaining step is to
identify the determinant or resultant of the projected quadratic system
without expanding its rapidly growing Gröbner basis.

## Reproduction

Run

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_two_pair_phase_one_uniform_obstruction.py
python3 scripts/verify_two_pair_primitive_prefix_obstruction.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_two_pair_degree_five_odd_height_quadratic_rigidity.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_two_pair_higher_degree_monomial_formal_rigidity.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_two_pair_degree_eight_monomial_formal_rigidity.py
```

The first checker reconstructs the phase coefficients, performs the
symbolic height integrals, derives the quadratic and cubic residuals,
computes (2.4), and verifies all \(29\) nonzero residues in (2.6).  The
second dependency-free checker replays the other height and phase cases
by direct sparse contraction.  The third checker constructs the nine
quadratic deformation obstructions and verifies the nilpotence
certificates (3.3), Hilbert vector (3.4), and nondegenerate socle pairing.
It then eliminates the even-height second-order variables and verifies
the projected complete-intersection certificate (3.7)--(3.8).
The fourth checker performs the analogous exact Hopf-angular elimination
in degrees six and seven and verifies (4.2).
The fifth checker constructs the exact systems in degrees eight through
eleven and invokes Singular for the good-prime complete-intersection
certificates (4.3)--(4.4).
