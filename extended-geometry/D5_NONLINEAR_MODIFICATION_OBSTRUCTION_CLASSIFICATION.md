# Obstruction classes for nonlinear absolute \(D_5\) modification

## 1. Scope and verdict

Let \(k\) have characteristic zero and contain \(\sqrt5\).  Retain

\[
\begin{aligned}
P&=P_5(a,u)=a^5-5a^3u+5au^2,\\
J&=\partial_aP=5Q,\\
Q&=R_+R_-,\\
C&=a^2-4u,\\
R_\pm&=a^2-\frac{3\pm\sqrt5}{2}u,\\
\Delta(u,v)&=v^2-4u^5.
\end{aligned}
\]

The fundamental ledger is

\[
\Delta(u,P)=CQ^2=CR_+^2R_-^2.                     \tag{1.1}
\]

The
[first \(D_5\) modification audit](ABSOLUTE_DIHEDRAL_D5_MODIFICATION_FRONTIER.md)
closes product and separated Cox fills, every auxiliary-affine map retaining
\(u\), and every thickening which retains the incidence as a zero section.
This note classifies the obstructions which remain for the only surviving
case:

- two or three genuinely active auxiliary coordinates;
- nonlinear feedback into the cover coordinates;
- no polynomial graph carrying the old incidence;
- preservation of the degree-five field only after birational elimination.

The classification is exhaustive as a list of necessary gates, not as a
no-go theorem for every nonlinear polynomial map.  It separates eight ways
a candidate can fail:

| code | obstruction | exact test |
|---|---|---|
| `D5-N0` | arithmetic monodromy | distinguish geometric split \(D_5\) from the rational Dickson normalizer |
| `D5-N1` | field degree | prove stable degree \(5\), not degree \(1\) or \(5e\) |
| `D5-N2` | finite branch | relocate \(\Delta,R_+,R_-\) from finite divisorial ramification |
| `D5-N3` | log-discrepancy ledger | solve the boundary valuation equation |
| `D5-N4` | affine realization | source and target must be smooth factorial affine spaces with constant units |
| `D5-N5` | section/normal module | exclude every polynomial graph of the old incidence |
| `D5-N6` | polynomiality | clear every inverse adjugate numerator, not only its determinant |
| `D5-N7` | complete fibres | certify finite flat rank \(5\) on the regular target open |

The first surviving two-coordinate stratum must pass all eight gates.  A
third coordinate helps only if it is genuinely active in branch relocation
or inverse divisibility; a free or triangular stabilization changes none of
the obstructions.

No absolute Keller map is constructed here.

## 2. The object being classified

For \(r=2\) or \(3\), let

\[
F\colon\mathbb A^{2+r}\longrightarrow\mathbb A^{2+r}
\]

be polynomial with nonzero constant Jacobian.  Write \(L_F\) for the source
function field and \(K_F\) for the field generated by its outputs.  A
**stable \(D_5\) realization** must provide an isomorphism of extensions

\[
(L_F/K_F)\simeq
\left(
k(a,u)(z_1,\ldots,z_r)/
k(u,P)(t_1,\ldots,t_r)
\right).                                           \tag{2.1}
\]

It is not enough that \([L_F:K_F]=5\).  The normal closure and its natural
five-point action must also agree with the original geometric \(D_5\)
extension.

The most controlled construction format is a birational factorization

\[
\beta\circ F
=
(\pi_5\times\operatorname{id})\circ\alpha,          \tag{2.2}
\]

where \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are generically birational polynomial
modifications of affine space.  If (2.2) holds and the inverse divisions
are polynomial, preservation of the function-field extension is automatic.
The obstruction classes below also apply as intrinsic tests when a
candidate is not initially presented in the form (2.2).

## 3. `D5-N0`: arithmetic-monodromy obstruction

The Dickson incidence has geometric \(D_5\) after passing to the split
reflection field.  Over \(\mathbb Q(u,v)\),

\[
\operatorname{Disc}_A(P_5(A,u)-v)
=5^5(4u^5-v^2)^2,
\]

whose square class is \(5\).  The natural degree-five action of \(D_5\) is
contained in \(A_5\), so this rational row does not have that arithmetic
group.

Every proposed arithmetic family must therefore do one of two things:

1. work over the cosine field and prove descent without enlarging the
   arithmetic normal closure; or
2. replace the Dickson row by a rational \(D_5\) generic polynomial and
   recompute all boundary ledgers.

This gate is independent of Kellerization.  Passing all geometric
obstructions does not settle it.

## 4. `D5-N1`: field-degree and auxiliary-degree obstruction

There are three distinct failures.

### 4.1 Primitive collapse

If the target field recovers \(a\), the degree is one.  The affine-linear
mask theorem gives an all-degree instance: retaining \(u\) and making the
other outputs affine-linear in any number of auxiliaries forces precisely
this collapse.

### 4.2 Auxiliary multiplication

If, after the quintic root is chosen, the auxiliary equations have generic
degree \(e>1\), then

\[
[L_F:K_F]=5e                                      \tag{4.1}
\]

unless the equations couple the root and auxiliaries so that the tower does
not split.  Merely observing the quintic factor in one elimination
polynomial does not rule out this failure.

### 4.3 Wrong degree-five action

Even degree five is insufficient.  A different index-five subfield or an
arithmetic normalizer twist can change the normal closure.  The certificate
must identify the recovered subfield with \(k(u,P)\), or independently
certify the natural \(D_5\) permutation action.

The preferred certificate is (2.2).  Otherwise one needs an exact primitive
element, its quintic minimal polynomial, and a normal-closure computation.

## 5. `D5-N2`: finite-branch relocation obstruction

A polynomial map with nonzero constant Jacobian is étale.  Hence a
height-one source valuation with a height-one center on the affine target
cannot have ramification index greater than one: the corresponding local
map of discrete valuation rings is étale, so a target uniformizer has
valuation one rather than \(e>1\).

The original extension has two finite ramification valuations
\(R_+=0\) and \(R_-=0\), both lying over \(\Delta=0\), with index two.
Therefore a stable Keller model cannot retain simultaneously:

- the finite target divisorial center \(\Delta=0\); and
- either finite source divisorial center \(R_\pm=0\).

At least one side of each ramified valuation must be moved to infinity or
to an exceptional center of codimension at least two by the birational
recharts.  This is the intrinsic reason a polynomial graph of the old
incidence cannot survive.

This gives the first geometric prefilter:

> If the proposed affine coordinates leave \(\Delta\) and either \(R_+\) or
> \(R_-\) as corresponding finite prime divisors, reject the ansatz before
> solving its Jacobian equations.

## 6. `D5-N3`: boundary and log-discrepancy obstruction

In the source boundary basis

\[
([C],[R_+],[R_-]),
\]

write

\[
d=(1,2,2),\qquad j=(0,1,1)                         \tag{6.1}
\]

for the pullback of \(\Delta\) and the divisor of \(J/5\).

Consider a branch-supported birational factorization in which the target
exceptional determinant, after pullback to the original incidence chart,
has boundary vector \(md\), and no other divisor meets the generic points
of \(C,R_+,R_-\).  If the source rechart has Jacobian orders

\[
s=(s_C,s_+,s_-),
\]

constant Jacobian forces

\[
j+s=md.                                            \tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
s=(m,\,2m-1,\,2m-1),\qquad m\ge1.
}                                                   \tag{6.3}
\]

This classifies the entire single-branch valuation ledger.

- \(m=0\) would require negative source orders and is impossible for a
  polynomial source modification.
- The first possible target order is \(m=1\).
- Its forced source Jacobian is, up to a constant,

  \[
  CR_+R_-=CQ.                                      \tag{6.4}
  \]

- The two ramification orders must be equal.  An asymmetric one-color
  source correction cannot descend through the single target branch.
- Higher \(m\) only adds the pullback vector \(d\); it does not create a new
  primitive solution.

Equation (6.3) is a necessary divisor condition, not a construction.
Realizing the two Jacobian divisors by affine-space blowdowns and cancelling
their inverse matrices entry by entry are separate gates.

## 7. `D5-N4`: affine-space obstruction

Every proposed intermediate source or target intended to be affine space
must pass:

\[
\text{regularity},\qquad
\operatorname{Cl}=0,\qquad
\mathcal O^\times=k^\times.                        \tag{7.1}
\]

For the natural fills:

| fill | regularity | class group | units | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| \(xy=R_+\) or \(xy=R_-\) | smooth | \(0\) | \(k^\times\) | \(\mathbb A^3\), but one color remains |
| \(xy=R_+R_-\) | isolated singularity | \(\mathbb Z\) | \(k^\times\) | reject |
| separated \(x_+y_+=R_+\), \(x_-y_-=R_-\) | isolated quadric vertex | \(0\) | \(k^\times\) | reject |
| either failed fill times \(\mathbb A^m\) | still singular | unchanged | unchanged | reject |

The separated example is important: class group and units are only
prefilters.  They do not detect the singular vertex created by the
order-two tangency of \(R_+\) and \(R_-\).

Any nonlinear smoothing must be checked again for all three invariants.
Adding linear auxiliary terms may smooth the vertex, but it can also remove
a boundary color or create a graph section, triggering `D5-N3` or `D5-N5`.

## 8. `D5-N5`: graph-section and normal-module obstruction

The zero-section obstruction extends to every polynomial graph.

### Theorem 8.1

Suppose there are polynomial vectors

\[
\sigma(a,u)\in k[a,u]^r,\qquad
\tau(u,v)\in k[u,v]^r
\]

such that

\[
F(a,u,\sigma(a,u))
=\bigl(u,P(a,u),\tau(u,P(a,u))\bigr).              \tag{8.1}
\]

Then \(F\) cannot have nonzero constant Jacobian.

### Proof

The source automorphism

\[
z\longmapsto z+\sigma(a,u)
\]

and the target automorphism

\[
Z\longmapsto Z+\tau(U,V)
\]

both have determinant one.  Conjugating by their inverses reduces (8.1) to
an unchanged zero section.  Along that section the derivative is block
triangular, so

\[
\det DF=-J(a,u)\det M(a,u),
\]

which cannot be a nonzero constant. \(\square\)

Thus complete regular fibres cannot be retained by visibly marking the old
five incidence points with a polynomial auxiliary graph.  The marking must
exist only rationally, with its poles absorbed by a nontrivial affine
modification.

At first order this is a normal-module obstruction.  A rechart which
preserves the ideal of the incidence graph acts invertibly on its normal
module, while the inverse derivative-unit chart has determinant \(J^{-1}\)
there.  An invertible polynomial normal change cannot supply that factor.

## 9. `D5-N6`: normalization and adjugate-divisibility obstruction

Moving the old section into the target branch is the first way around
`D5-N5`.  Such embeddings have a rigid form.

### Proposition 9.1

Let \(A\) be a factorial \(k\)-algebra with \(A^\times=k^\times\), and let
\(U,V\in A\) satisfy

\[
V^2=4U^5.                                          \tag{9.1}
\]

After a harmless constant extension and rescaling, there is \(s\in A\)
such that

\[
\boxed{U=s^2,\qquad V=\pm2s^5.}                    \tag{9.2}
\]

Indeed, for every prime \(p\) of the UFD \(A\),
\(2\nu_p(V)=5\nu_p(U)\).  Coprimality of \(2\) and \(5\) forces
\(\nu_p(U)\) to be even and \(\nu_p(V)\) to be divisible by five.  Units
account for the constant rescaling.

Therefore every polynomial incidence into \(\Delta=0\) factors through
the normalization parameter \(s\).  Away from \(s=0\), the base-coordinate
differential has rank one.  At the cusp \(s=0\), it has rank zero.  An
ambient completion must obtain the missing tangent directions from the
auxiliary outputs:

- one missing direction on the smooth branch;
- two missing directions at the cusp.

For two auxiliaries, a cusp-centered chart uses all available first-order
normal directions.  For three auxiliaries, the extra direction must still
participate in the inverse divisibility or field reconstruction; a passive
direction is only a stabilization of a failed lower-dimensional chart.

Determinant matching is not polynomiality.  If a generically birational
target blowdown has mask matrix \(B\), its inverse contains

\[
\frac{\operatorname{adj}B}{\det B}.                \tag{9.3}
\]

Every entry of the pulled-back adjugate numerator must be divisible by the
pulled-back denominator.  Equality of determinant divisors checks only the
product of the invariant factors and can miss an uncancelled pole in one
matrix entry.

The minimal diagonal realization demonstrates the distinction.  Give the
source mask multiplier \(CQ\) and the target mask multiplier \(\Delta\).
The divisor equation (6.4) is exact, but after pullback

\[
\frac{CQ\,z}{\Delta(u,P)}
=\frac{z}{Q},                                      \tag{9.4}
\]

so the inverse retains both ramification poles.  Coupled adjugate
numerators, not diagonal scaling, are mandatory.

The
[canonical two-mask blowdown audit](D5_TWO_MASK_BLOWDOWN_OBSTRUCTIONS.md)
uses the matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}V&2U\\2U^4&V\end{pmatrix}
\]

of determinant \(\Delta\).  It closes every constant-linear assembly and,
more strongly, every polynomial automorphic base rechart whose determinant
condition is

\[
q^2-4p^5=\lambda(V^2-4U^5).
\]

For each mask variable this identity gives a polynomial map from
\(\mathbb A^1\) to a smooth generic genus-two curve; hence \(p,q\) cannot
depend on the masks.  Exact division separately rejects all \(72\)
coordinate assignments from the unchanged and two normalized
ramification-incidence charts.  Thus the word **nonautomorphic** in the
remaining stratum is forced, not merely a search preference.

The first nonautomorphic normalized-cusp chart

\[
p=V^2+\Delta S,\qquad q=2V^5+\Delta T
\]

also fails in all degrees.  Every possible contraction determinant for its
fourth coordinate is divisible by \(\Delta\), whereas the required
log-crepant quotient is congruent to
\(4V^5(T-5SV^3)\) modulo \(\Delta\).  The next chart must therefore move
tangentially along the normalized cusp modulo \(\Delta\), rather than give
both transverse corrections a common branch factor.

The affine-normal tangential repair

\[
h=V+S,\qquad
p=h^2+A(U,V,S)\Delta T,\qquad
q=2h^5+B(U,V,S)\Delta T
\]

also has no nondegenerate log-crepant solution.  The rechart Jacobian has
\(T\)-degree at most one; the \(T^5\) coefficient forces \(A=0\), then the
\(T^2\) coefficient forces \(B=0\).  Viewed only at this affine-normal
stage, the apparent next branches are nonlinear dependence on the same
normal coordinate or two independent nonlinear tangent/normal directions.

The later
[all-degree obstruction](DIHEDRAL_ALL_DEGREE_AFFINE_COMPLETION_OBSTRUCTIONS.md)
closes the first alternative completely, not merely in the affine-normal
class.  For \(D_5\), nonresonant one-normal degrees contradict the
Jacobian degree bound, while the only resonant pairs
\((\deg_Tp,\deg_Tq)=(2d,5d)\) have incompatible infinity valuations unless
\(q^2=4p^5\), when the base bracket vanishes.  Consequently the `D5-N6`
search should now reject every candidate retaining two coordinates and
using only one active normal variable before attempting adjugate division
or fibre elimination.  Only two coupled normal directions or a different
blowdown proceed to `D5-N7`.

## 10. `D5-N7`: complete-regular-fibre obstruction

Polynomiality and constant Jacobian still do not prove that the prescribed
fibres survive.  On the regular open

\[
B^\circ=D(\Delta),
\]

a valid candidate must be finite flat of rank five after the intended
birational target rechart.

An exact certificate should establish all of the following.

1. **Generic rank.**  The source coordinate ring localized at \(\Delta\)
   is a rank-five module over the localized target ring.
2. **Root recovery.**  A primitive element satisfies a quintic equivalent
   to \(P_5(A,u)-v\), rather than a polynomial with an additional factor.
3. **Auxiliary uniqueness.**  Once a quintic root is fixed, every auxiliary
   coordinate is recovered uniquely in the localized ring.
4. **No localization on the source fibre.**  Reconstruction denominators
   must be units pulled back from \(B^\circ\); a factor vanishing at only
   some of the five roots deletes sheets.
5. **No vertical components.**  Saturation by \(\Delta\) must remove all
   components not dominating the target, and the remaining algebra must
   have constant rank five.
6. **Correct normal closure.**  The five sheets must carry the natural
   \(D_5\) action.

A bounded point search cannot certify these statements.  The appropriate
certificate is an exact Gröbner basis or a displayed free module basis over
the localized target ring.

## 11. Two versus three auxiliary coordinates

The classification leaves the following precise strata.

### Two auxiliaries

Every survivor must:

1. move the branch and both ramification valuations;
2. realize the primitive ledger \(CQ\) against \(\Delta\), or a higher
   solution of (6.3);
3. move the old incidence graph into a normalized branch-incidence center;
4. use both auxiliaries in nonlinear base feedback;
5. satisfy every adjugate divisibility;
6. admit a rank-five localized module certificate.

This is the minimal and most rigid search.

### Three auxiliaries

The same six conditions apply.  In addition, reject immediately if a
polynomial source or target automorphism makes the third coordinate:

- free;
- triangular over a two-auxiliary core; or
- an independent map of generic degree \(e>1\).

The first two reduce to the two-auxiliary obstruction; the third multiplies
the field degree.  A useful third coordinate must enter the branch-center
embedding, the coupled adjugate numerators, or the rational reconstruction
of the old \(D_5\) field.

## 12. Search compiler

For any proposed nonlinear ansatz, apply the gates in this order:

1. **Field prefilter:** compute the generic degree and eliminate candidates
   which expose \(a\) or multiply degree five.
2. **Branch-center prefilter:** track the centers of
   \(\Delta,R_+,R_-\); reject retained finite ramification.
3. **Valuation prefilter:** compute the three boundary orders and enforce
   (6.3).
4. **Graph prefilter:** search for a polynomial source/target incidence
   graph; its existence is an all-degree contradiction.
5. **Affine prefilter:** compute the singular locus, class group, and unit
   lattice of every modification space.
6. **Divisibility prefilter:** divide every pulled-back adjugate numerator
   by the proposed exceptional determinant.
7. **Jacobian equations:** only now solve the remaining coefficient system.
8. **Fibre certificate:** saturate by \(\Delta\) and prove finite flat rank
   five with the correct normal closure.

The order matters.  The first six gates are cheaper and structurally
stronger than a raw constant-Jacobian Gröbner system.

## 13. Remaining open stratum

After classification, only one qualitative mechanism remains:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{a nonautomorphic, nonlinear, branch-incidence rechart}\\
\text{which moves the old graph, realizes the primitive log ledger,}\\
\text{clears every adjugate numerator, and is finite flat of rank five.}
\end{gathered}}
\]

Finding it would give an absolute degree-five Keller map with nontrivial
generic extension, hence a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture.
Conversely, excluding this stratum uniformly for \(D_n\) would be a genuine
affine-completion theorem.

## 14. Reproduction

Run

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_d5_nonlinear_obstruction_classification.py
```

The checker verifies the boundary vectors and all solutions of the
single-branch valuation equation, the minimal diagonal pole, the normalized
branch incidence and its rank drop at the cusp, and the graph-section block
determinant for two and three auxiliary coordinates.
