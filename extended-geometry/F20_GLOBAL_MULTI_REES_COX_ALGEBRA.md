# F20 global multi-Rees/Cox algebra: the normalization-first gate

## Status

This note replaces the phrase “missing global Cox algebra” in the compact
(F_{20}) packet calculation by an exact algebraic frontier.

There are two different algebras and they must not be conflated.

1. The ordinary multi-Rees algebra of incidence ideals on the nonnormal root
   hypersurface has a global saturated presentation, but it cannot realize
   the compact divisor (D_d).
2. The required object is the divisorial Cox algebra on a regular controlled
   transform of the normalization.  Its local value-one generator is absent
   from both the incidence ring and its affine normalization; it is an
   exceptional Cox variable.

For the natural incidence ideals, the ((3,1,1)) piece does not contain
(P_X).  Its exact cyclic residue has length (57), projects to a base scheme
of length (33), is killed by (dqr) but by no proper squarefree divisor of
(dqr), and has reduced support exactly the eight already-certified finite
collision centers.  These statements are exact.  They do **not** obstruct a
normalized divisorial Cox algebra, an affine completion, or a Keller map.

## 1. A general multi-Rees saturation theorem

Let (A) be a domain, let

\[
 I_i=(a_i,b_i)\subset A,\qquad a_i\ne0,
\]

and define

\[
 \Phi:A[U_1,V_1,\ldots,U_n,V_n]\longrightarrow
 A[T_1,\ldots,T_n],
 \quad U_i\mapsto a_iT_i,\quad V_i\mapsto b_iT_i.
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (global two-generator multi-Rees presentation)

Put (a=\prod_i a_i).  Then

\[
 \ker\Phi=
 (b_iU_i-a_iV_i:1\le i\le n):a^\infty.       \tag{1.1}
\]

The image is the multi-Rees algebra

\[
 \mathcal R_A(I_1,\ldots,I_n)
 =\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbf N^n}
 I_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots I_n^{\alpha_n}T^\alpha, \tag{1.2}
\]

so its multidegree-(\alpha) coefficient module is exactly
(I_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots I_n^{\alpha_n}).

**Proof.**  After localizing at (a), every (a_i) is a unit and the displayed
linear relation solves
(V_i=(b_i/a_i)U_i).  Thus the localized kernel is generated by the (n)
linear relations.  Since (A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]) is a domain, the original
kernel is their contraction from (A_a), which is precisely saturation by
(a).  The homogeneous image in multidegree (\alpha) is the indicated
product ideal.  \(\square\)

This theorem is useful computationally: the symmetric algebra gives the
linear front end, and saturation records all torsion and cross-relations.
It also makes the section gate exact.  A proposed function (f\in A) occurs
in degree (\alpha) only if

\[
 f\in I_1^{\alpha_1}\cdots I_n^{\alpha_n}.          \tag{1.3}
\]

Equality of normalized divisorial valuations does not imply (1.3) in a
nonnormal ring.

## 2. The natural F20 incidence ideals

Work in

\[
 R=\mathbf Q[s,t,X]/(P),
\]

where (P) is the corrected Lecacheux polynomial and

\[
 \operatorname{Disc}_X(P)=d^3q^2r^2/256.
\]

The exact factorization check finds (P) irreducible.  Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 h_d&=2X-s-2,\\
 h_q&=2(s-1)X-(2s^2t+2s^2+3s-4),\\
 h_r&=\operatorname{Sres}_2(P,P_X)/(s^2+4).
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{2.1}
\]

The quadratic subresultant is divisible by (s^2+4=d), and (h_r) is a
primitive polynomial of (X)-degree two with (115) terms.  At the exact
sample (s=2,t=7/4),

\[
 h_r=-\frac{35721}{64}(X^2+8X-2),                  \tag{2.2}
\]

recovering the repeated quadratic on (r=0).

The natural incidence ideals are

\[
 I_d=(d,h_d),\qquad I_q=(q,h_q),\qquad I_r=(r,h_r). \tag{2.3}
\]

Theorem 1.1 gives their global multi-Rees kernel over the polynomial
presentation:

\[
\begin{split}
 (&P, h_dU_d-dV_d, h_qU_q-qV_q, h_rU_r-rV_r)\\
 &\hspace{42mm}:(dqr)^\infty.                       \tag{2.4}
\end{split}
\]

Formula (2.4) is a presentation of the natural incidence algebra.  It is not
yet the Cox algebra of the normalized colored resolution.

## 3. The value-semigroup gap

Consider either conjugate triple center.  Over
(s_0=2i, t_0=-3/4+i/2), the ramified root is (X_0=1+i).  The exact
normalization expansion used in the color atlas is

\[
 s=s_0+\tau^4,qquad
 t=t_0+z\tau^4,qquad
 X=X_0+\tau^2(b+\tau Y),qquad b^2+i=0.             \tag{3.1}
\]

It gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccccccc}
f&s-s_0&t-t_0&X-X_0&d&q&r&h_d&P_X\\ \hline
\nu_\tau(f)&4&4&2&4&4&4&2&7.
\end{array}                                        \tag{3.2}
\]

Every element of the maximal ideal of the original local incidence ring is
a polynomial in the first three local coordinates in (3.2).  Hence every
nonzero such element has value at least two; cancellation can raise a value
but cannot produce value one.  Consequently the value semigroup of the
incidence ring has a gap at one.

On the other hand, the compact packet column has

\[
 D_d(E_{1,\mathrm{ram}})=1.                         \tag{3.3}
\]

The valuation of any finitely generated integral ideal is the minimum of the
valuations of a set of generators, so (3.2)--(3.3) prove:

### Corollary 3.1 (normalization-first Cox gate)

No ordinary integral ideal of the nonnormal root-incidence ring realizes the
compact (D_d) column.  In particular (I_d) has order two, not one, at the
triple-(E_1) ramified color.  Any global realization of
(3D_d+D_q+D_r) must pass through normalization and then to a controlled
transform on which the exceptional valuation has a primitive Cox variable.
The global normalization computation below shows that normalization itself
still supplies no value-one regular function.

More generally, if a desired colored Cox coefficient is a gap in the local
value semigroup of a proposed base ring, no ordinary multi-Rees algebra of
integral ideals on that ring can realize the column.  This is a cheap gate
that precedes Rees elimination.

## 4. The exact natural (3,1,1) residue

Although (2.3) does not realize the compact columns, it is the canonical
pre-normalization control.  Put

\[
 J=I_d^3I_qI_r\subset R.                            \tag{4.1}
\]

The product has sixteen displayed generators.  Exact Singular reduction
gives a Gröbner basis of size (34) and

\[
 \operatorname{NF}_J(P_X)=P_X\ne0.                 \tag{4.2}
\]

Thus (P_X\notin J).  Let

\[
 C=(J:P_X).                                         \tag{4.3}
\]

The exact quotient calculation gives

\[
 \dim R/C=0,qquad \operatorname{length}_{\mathbf Q}(R/C)=57. \tag{4.4}
\]

Eliminating (X) produces an eight-element Gröbner basis on the base and a
zero-dimensional scheme of length (33).  Among squarefree boundary
monomials, the annihilator test is sharp:

\[
 dqr\in C,qquad
 1,d,q,r,dq,dr,qr\notin C.                          \tag{4.5}
\]

Equivalently, (dqrP_X\in J), but no proper squarefree boundary factor
annihilates the residue class of (P_X).

The radical of the eliminated base ideal has degree eight and equals the
intersection of the five rational/Galois-stable center ideals

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(s-1,2t+1),\quad (s-11,2t+1),\\
 &(s-4t-3,16t^2+24t+13),\\
 &(12s-7,t-2),\\
 &(s-4t^2+5,8t^3+16t^2+2t-7).                     \tag{4.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

Their geometric degrees are (1,1,2,1,3).  Hence the reduced support is
exactly the (q)-node, the (r)-cusp, the two conjugate triple centers, the
transverse (q)-(r) point, and the three (q)-(r) tangencies—no new
boundary center appears.

The length-(57) class is therefore a finite incidence/conductor residue,
not a new divisorial obstruction.  Part of it records the missing
exceptional Cox generator exposed by Section 3.

## 5. The correct global Cox target

Let (\widetilde X) denote a future certified regular normalization carrying
the sixty-three colors.  The honest three-column object is the divisorial
algebra

\[
 \mathcal C=
 \bigoplus_{a,b,c\ge0}
 H^0\!\left(\widetilde X,
 \mathcal O_{\widetilde X}(-aD_d-bD_q-cD_r)\right)
 U^aV^bW^c.                                         \tag{5.1}
\]

Here the grading uses the Rees/vanishing-ideal convention
(\mathcal O(-D)\subset\mathcal O); reversing all divisor signs gives the
usual Cox-ring convention.

The subsequent
[`F20_NORMALIZED_COX_CONDUCTOR_FRONTIER.md`](F20_NORMALIZED_COX_CONDUCTOR_FRONTIER.md)
computes the normalization and conductor exactly.  Its two nontrivial module
generators both have value two at triple (E_1), the residue splits into five
primary collision packets, and the natural degree-((3,1,1)) product still
fails after normalization.  Thus the next construction is finite and
explicit:

1. construct the controlled transform and its primitive exceptional Cox
   variables at the triple and cusp packets;
2. construct the simultaneous divisorial ideals for (D_d,D_q,D_r) and
   their multiplication maps;
3. distribute the certified anti-invariant total derivative residue among
   the three individual conductor sections;
4. retest whether the degree-((3,1,1)) module contains (P_X);
5. only then run inverse-adjugate polynomiality and affine-space recognition.

The abstract sixty-three-row identity
(3D_d+D_q+D_r=\operatorname{div}(P_X)) remains valid.  What fails is its
naive realization by ideals on the pre-normalization incidence ring.
