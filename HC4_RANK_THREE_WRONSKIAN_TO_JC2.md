# Global Wronskian unit obstruction for moving rank-three `[4]`

## Status

This note completes the **linearly-dependent top-kernel** subbranch of the
remaining generic-rank `[4]` stratum.  It supersedes the earlier interpretation
of the final determinant square as merely constructing a plane Keller pair.
The polynomial-unit argument is stronger: genuine projective motion is
impossible.

> **Theorem HC4RSD70 — Wronskian unit obstruction.**
> Assume the rank-three `[4]` packet has been reduced to
> \[
> A=yP(x,w)+zQ(x,w)+R(x,w),\qquad J(P,Q)=0.
> \]
> Then the top Hessian-kernel line is constant.  Consequently this entire
> subbranch falls under the fixed-kernel theorem `HC4RSD65` and hence reduces
> to `HC2` or to the exact `JC2` cotangent endpoint.

Thus a genuinely moving rank-three `[4]` packet, if one exists, must have an
associated four-variable quasi-translation with **no linear invariant**.

## 1. A single polynomial generator

Since \(J(P,Q)=0\), the polynomial Lüroth/generative-polynomial theorem gives
a closed polynomial \(h\in K[x,w]\) and \(p,q\in K[t]\) with

\[
P=p(h),\qquad Q=q(h).
\]

Divide \((p',q')\) by its gcd and write the primitive pair

\[
(a(h),b(h)),\qquad \gcd(a,b)=1.
\]

The top Hessian kernel is generated by

\[
D=b(h)\partial_y-a(h)\partial_z.                  \tag{1.1}
\]

Its projective motion is measured by

\[
\Theta(h)=a(h)b'(h)-b(h)a'(h).                    \tag{1.2}
\]

Hence \(\Theta=0\) is exactly the fixed-kernel case.

Choose \(\alpha,\beta\in K[h]\) with

\[
b\alpha-a\beta=1.
\]

Then

\[
u=ay+bz,\qquad t=\alpha y+\beta z             \tag{1.3}
\]

is a polynomial passive coordinate change of determinant \(-1\), satisfying

\[
Du=0,\qquad Dt=1.
\]

## 2. The top coefficient integrates globally

The coefficient of \(s^3\) in the Hessian pencil is the rank-three cofactor
quadratic form on the top kernel.  Hence

\[
D^2\psi=0.
\]

Because \(D=\partial_t\) in (1.3), polynomial integration gives

\[
\psi=tC(x,w,u)+E(x,w,u).                            \tag{2.1}
\]

Put

\[
\tau=h_x\partial_w-h_w\partial_x.                  \tag{2.2}
\]

Since every coefficient of (1.3) lies in \(K[h]\),

\[
\tau h=\tau u=\tau t=0.                            \tag{2.3}
\]

## 3. Generic determinant identities

At a generic active point where \(dh\ne0\), freeze determinant-one active and
passive affine frames.  Ordinary Hessian congruence then applies; no nonlinear
coordinate invariance is assumed.

The universal generic-jet computation gives

\[
[s^2]\det(S+sT)
=3\Theta\Lambda\,t\,C_u^2+\text{terms independent of }t, \tag{3.1}
\]

where \(\Lambda\ne0\) is the generic rank-three transverse scalar.  If
\(\Theta\ne0\), equation (3.1) forces

\[
C_u=0,
\]

and the remaining part of the same coefficient forces

\[
\tau C=0.
\]

Closedness of \(h\) therefore gives

\[
C=H(h).                                             \tag{3.2}
\]

The next coefficient is

\[
[s]\det(S+sT)
=-\Lambda E_{uu}\bigl(\Theta E_u+B(h)\bigr)^2.     \tag{3.3}
\]

Since \(\det S\ne0\), this forces

\[
E_{uu}=0,
\qquad
E=uL(x,w)+M(x,w).                                   \tag{3.4}
\]

Finally the constant term becomes

\[
\boxed{
\det S=(\tau L)^2\bigl(\Theta(h)L+B(h)\bigr)^2.
}                                                    \tag{3.5}
\]

The companion checker verifies (3.1), (3.3), and (3.5) with arbitrary second
jets of \(h\) and \(R\).

## 4. The polynomial-unit argument

Let \(\det S=\delta\in K^*\).  Equation (3.5) implies, after absorbing the
constant square/sign over the algebraic closure if necessary,

\[
(\tau L)\bigl(\Theta(h)L+B(h)\bigr)=c\in K^*.      \tag{4.1}
\]

Both factors lie in the polynomial ring \(K[x,w]\).  The only units of this
ring are nonzero constants.  Hence

\[
\tau L=\alpha\in K^*,
\qquad
\Theta(h)L+B(h)=\beta\in K^*.                      \tag{4.2}
\]

Apply \(\tau\) to the second identity.  Because \(\tau h=0\),

\[
0=\tau(\Theta L+B)
 =\Theta(h)\,\tau L
 =\Theta(h)\alpha.
\]

Since \(\alpha\ne0\),

\[
\boxed{\Theta=0.}                                  \tag{4.3}
\]

This contradicts genuine projective motion.  Therefore the top kernel line is
constant.

Notice that the previously written polynomial

\[
G=\frac{\Theta(h)}2L^2+B(h)L
\]

and identity \(J(h,G)=c\) are true consequences of (4.1), but they are much
weaker than (4.2)--(4.3).  There is no live moving `JC2` subcase here.

## 5. Consequences

The linearly-dependent rank-three `[4]` branch is now completely closed:

1. if the associated quasi-translation has a linear invariant, the reduction
   above applies and forces the kernel line to be fixed;
2. `HC4RSD65` then reduces the fixed-kernel packet to `HC2` or to the exact
   `JC2` cotangent endpoint.

Therefore the **only** unresolved relative-nilpotent `HC4` stratum is a
rank-three `[4]` packet whose associated singular-Hessian quasi-translation
has four linearly independent components.

## 6. External algebraic input

The only non-HC4 input is the standard polynomial Lüroth/generative-polynomial
fact that algebraically dependent bivariate polynomials lie in one polynomial
subalgebra \(K[h]\), with \(h\) chosen closed.
