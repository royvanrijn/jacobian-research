\section{Introduction}

A polynomial map with nonzero constant Jacobian is \'etale, so every one of
its fibers is a finite \'etale scheme.  The converse realization problem is
much less formal: which finite \'etale algebras can occur as a \emph{full}
fiber, with no generic inverse sheet lost at infinity?  The answer is as sharp
as possible.  Apart from the classical obstruction in geometric degree two,
there is no restriction at all.

The main theorem proves that every nonzero finite \'etale algebra of rank
$N\neq2$ is a Keller fiber.  Rank one is trivial.  For every $N\geq3$, a
squarefree presentation $A\simeq K[T]/(P)$ is converted directly into a
three-variable Jacobian-one map whose distinguished full fiber is
$\Spec A$.  The formulas are uniform in $P$; once an admissible translation
parameter is fixed, they commute with extension of the ground field and have
coordinate degree at most $6N+2$.

The mechanism is a quadratic gauge built around the marked variable
$S=x/(1+xy)$.  Its target coordinates are designed so that the generic inverse
problem reduces to one polynomial equation
\[
 E_{\Pi,B,C}(S)=0.
\]
At the distinguished target this equation is exactly $P(a+S)=0$.  Two facts
then make the rational chart global on the whole scheme fiber.  First, the
unit first target coordinate forces both chart factors to be units.  Second,
separability makes $E'(S)$ invertible in every commutative test algebra by a
B\'ezout identity.  This gives mutually inverse formulas between roots of $E$
and source points, naturally in the test algebra.  Thus the argument identifies
the complete fiber functor, including nilpotents and all base changes, rather
than only its geometric points.

The proof has four logically separate layers:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a coefficientwise all-degree calculation gives the displayed polynomial
      map, constant Jacobian, generic inverse equation, and degree bound;
\item the functor-of-points reconstruction proves that a separable inverse
      polynomial represents the entire source fiber;
\item translation identifies $K[S]/(P(a+S))$ with $K[T]/(P)$, while an
      admissible $a$ always exists because $P'P'''$ is nonzero;
\item monogenicity passes from squarefree polynomials to arbitrary finite
      \'etale algebras, and the Campbell--Razar--Wright theorem excludes rank
      two.
\end{enumerate}

The arithmetic consequences require no further geometry.  Any connectedness,
Galois action, local factorization, or local--global obstruction already
present in a finite \'etale algebra survives unchanged in its Keller fiber.
This yields the optimal rank-five failure of the Hasse principle and an
infinite family of such failures inside one fixed map.

The construction through the polynomial-presentation stage is formalized in
Lean.  Starting from a squarefree $P$ of degree at least three, Lean chooses an
admissible translation, constructs the actual all-degree
$\operatorname{MvPolynomial}(\operatorname{Fin}3,K)$ map and normalized target,
proves its Jacobian is one and its coordinate degrees are at most $6N+2$, and
identifies its literal fiber functor naturally with maps from $K[T]/(P)$ over
every commutative test algebra.  Exact SymPy and Singular certificates provide
independent expansions.  Monogenicity, the generic-function-field degree
argument, the historical degree-two theorem, and the arithmetic applications
remain explicitly separated mathematical inputs.
