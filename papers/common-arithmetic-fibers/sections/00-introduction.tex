\section{Introduction}

A polynomial map with nonzero constant Jacobian is \'etale.  Its fibers are
therefore finite \'etale schemes, possibly empty.  This raises a simple reverse
question: given a finite \'etale algebra $A/K$, can we build a Keller map with
$\Spec A$ as one of its fibers?

There is one important word in that question.  The fiber should be \emph{full}:
its length must equal the geometric degree of the map.  Otherwise some generic
inverse sheets may have escaped to infinity, and the special fiber records
only part of the inverse problem.  Our first and main result answers this
prescribed-algebra question: every finite \'etale $K$-algebra of rank at least
three occurs as a full fiber of an explicit Keller map in three variables.
Together with the elementary fact that every Keller fiber is finite \'etale,
this gives an exact fixed-degree classification: for $N\geq3$, the full fibers
of geometric-degree-$N$ Keller maps in $\A^3_K$ are precisely the rank-$N$
finite \'etale $K$-schemes.  The degree-two case is empty by the known
degree-two exclusion, while the identity realizes rank one.

For rank $N\geq3$, the construction starts from a squarefree presentation
\[
 A\simeq K[T]/(P).
\]
Choose $a\in K$ with $P'(a)P'''(a)\neq0$ and set
\[
 G(S)=P(a+S)-P(a).
\]
The polynomial $G$ is inserted into one explicit three-variable
quadratic-gauge map.  A fixed linear change in the target makes its Jacobian
one.  The resulting coordinate degrees are bounded by $6N+2$.

The family begins with the announced counterexample itself.  The seed
\[
 G(S)=S+S^3
\]
gives Alp\"oge's announced map coordinate for coordinate, with no conjugation
or output rescaling.  The higher-degree construction is therefore an extension
of the announced map rather than a separate template that happens to share
its Jacobian.

The displayed map is fairly large, but its inverse problem is deliberately
small.  It is organized around the marked coordinate
\[
 S=\frac{x}{1+xy},
\]
and reduces to a single polynomial equation
\[
 E_{\Pi,B,C}(S)=0.
\]
The general localized quadratic-gauge fiber theorem comes first.  At an
arbitrary target with nonzero first coordinate, it identifies the whole fiber
as
\[
 \operatorname{Spec}\left((K[S]/(E))[1/\overline{E'}]\right).
\]
This is the natural answer even when $E$ has repeated factors: the source map
is \'etale, so only roots at which $E'$ is invertible can occur.  At the target
used for realization, the finite-\'etale (squarefree) corollary applies:
$E=P(a+S)$ is squarefree, hence $E'$ is already a unit modulo $E$ and the
localization disappears.

This local description extends to an exact global boundary theorem.  The
Jelonek set of the quadratic-gauge map is precisely
\[
 V\!\left(\operatorname{Disc}_S(E_{\Pi,B,C})\right).
\]
Over $\Pi\neq0$, a root block of multiplicity $m$ accounts for exactly $m$
missing affine sheets.  On $\Pi=0$, two roots return to the divisor $q=0$ and
one root returns to $t=0$, while the remaining $N-3$ roots escape.  This
explains intrinsically why the prescribed squarefree fiber is full: its target
lies outside the nonproperness hypersurface.  It also detects a necessary
degree-three caveat---the raw resultant adds an extraneous factor $\Pi$ even
though a general target on that plane still has all three sheets.

The generic inverse problem is as large as possible in a second sense.
For every admissible degree-$N$ seed, not just a generic choice of its
coefficients, the geometric and arithmetic Galois groups of
$E_{\Pi,B,C}$ over the target function field are both $S_N$.  The proof
restricts to a line with fixed $\Pi$ and $B$: a birational parametrization of
the discriminant shows that the resulting polynomial is generically Morse,
so its simple finite branch transpositions generate $S_N$.  Consequently the
algebra prescribed at the distinguished target lies on a cover with maximal
generic monodromy.  Over a Hilbertian field, the same fixed map has infinitely
many connected full fibers with splitting-field group $S_N$.

The reconstruction is stronger than a count of geometric points.  Its formulas
are natural in the test algebra, so they identify the entire fiber functor,
including nilpotents and every scalar extension.  After translating back from
$S$ to $T$, the literal fiber is the original algebra $K[T]/(P)$.  Nothing
arithmetic has been replaced by a numerical proxy.

The proof splits into six steps.  First, a coefficientwise calculation builds
the all-degree map and proves its constant Jacobian and degree bound.  Second,
the root-to-source formulas prove the general localized quadratic-gauge fiber
theorem; its finite-\'etale (squarefree) corollary identifies $K[S]/(E)$ with
the whole scheme fiber.  Third, the exact discriminant and Newton polygons
compute the nonproperness hypersurface, every sheet loss, and the valuations
of the missing branches.  Fourth, the discriminant parametrization and the
Morse-polynomial theorem give full symmetric generic monodromy.  Fifth,
translation turns the inverse polynomial into the prescribed $P$ and proves
the prescribed-algebra realization theorem.  Finally, monogenicity handles
arbitrary finite \'etale algebras.  Separately, the Campbell--Razar--Wright
theorem rules out geometric degree two and yields the attainable-rank
corollary.

Once the prescribed-algebra realization theorem is proved, the arithmetic
applications are immediate.  An irreducible polynomial gives a connected
fiber; a product gives the same field decomposition; local points, real
signature, and Galois action are inherited from the original algebra.  In
particular, the classical
Berend--Bilu quintic produces the smallest possible Hasse-principle failure,
and a second construction places infinitely many such failures inside one
fixed Keller map.

The constructive part is formalized in Lean.  Starting from squarefree $P$ of
degree at least three, Lean chooses an admissible translation, constructs the
actual $\operatorname{MvPolynomial}(\operatorname{Fin}3,K)$ map and target,
proves the Jacobian-one identity and the $6N+2$ bound, and identifies the
literal fiber naturally with maps from $K[T]/(P)$ into every commutative test
algebra, proves that the representing quotient is finite \'etale, and verifies
the exact specialization $G(S)=S+S^3$.  The formalization promotes $\Pi$ and
$B$ to independent parameters and proves that the full inverse equation is
irreducible of degree $N$ over the iterated presentation $K(\Pi,B)(C)$; its
root quotient also has finrank $N$.  It further proves algebraic independence
of the three displayed coordinates and constructs the induced injective
pullback on rational function fields, and proves that the supplied-translation
map and target commute coefficientwise with extension of the ground field,
together with the tensor-product base change of the representing quotient.
The remaining inputs---the explicit
identification of that pullback-field extension with the formal inverse-root
extension and hence with $\gdeg(F_G)$, exact nonproperness and boundary
accounting, monogenicity, the generic-monodromy theorem, the degree-two
theorem, and the nonarchimedean number-theoretic applications---are kept
separate and proved in the paper rather than presented as formalized results.
For the explicit quintic, Lean additionally proves that its representing
quotient is finite \'etale, that the literal fiber has no rational point, and
that it has both a real point and a three-adic point.
