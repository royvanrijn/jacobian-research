\section{Introduction and main results}

A polynomial map with nonzero constant Jacobian is \'etale.  Its fibers are
therefore finite \'etale schemes, possibly empty.  This raises a simple reverse
question: given a finite \'etale algebra \(A/K\), can we build a Keller map with
\(\Spec A\) as one of its fibers?

There is one important word in that question.  The fiber should be
\emph{full}: its length must equal the geometric degree of the map.  Otherwise
some generic inverse sheets may have escaped to infinity, and the special
fiber records only part of the inverse problem.  Our main result answers the
prescribed-algebra question.  Every finite \'etale \(K\)-algebra of rank at
least three occurs as a full Keller fiber in three variables.  The
construction is explicit once a squarefree presentation
\(A\simeq K[T]/(P)\) and an admissible translation parameter are supplied.
For an abstract algebra, monogenicity supplies such a presentation
existentially, but not canonically.

For precision, let \(K\) be a field of characteristic zero.  A polynomial map
\[
 F:\A^m_K\longrightarrow\A^m_K
\]
is a \emph{Keller map} if \(\det DF\in K^*\), and its geometric degree is
\[
 \gdeg(F)=[K(x_1,\ldots,x_m):K(F_1,\ldots,F_m)].
\]
Such a map is \'etale and hence quasi-finite, so every fiber over a
\(K\)-point is a finite \'etale \(K\)-scheme, possibly empty.

\begin{definition}[Keller fibers and fullness]\label{def:keller-fiber}
If \(F:\A^m_K\to\A^m_K\) is a Keller map and \(y\in\A^m(K)\), then
\(F^{-1}(y)\) is a \emph{Keller fiber}.  It is a \emph{full Keller fiber} if
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_K F^{-1}(y)=\gdeg(F).
\]
Here \(\operatorname{rank}_K X=\dim_K\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)\) for a finite
\(K\)-scheme \(X\).  Thus ``Keller fiber'' means any ordinary fiber; the
adjective ``full'' says that the special fiber contains every generic inverse
sheet.  A shorter fiber can occur when sheets escape through the
nonproperness boundary.
\end{definition}

Producing Keller maps in each generic degree is now shared terrain.
Gallagher gives explicit all-degree families, and Migus explicitly takes the
available all-degree constructions as input in his classification of generic
degrees for real Keller maps with non-dense image
\cite{Gallagher,Migus}.  Shaska develops the mixed-sign grading and quotient
geometry of the announced cubic map \cite{Shaska}.  These results clarify why
large families should exist.  They do not address the reversed quantifier
order here: begin with an arbitrary finite \'etale algebra, then construct a
map whose \emph{complete} fiber represents that algebra.

For rank \(N\geq3\), choose a squarefree presentation
\[
 A\simeq K[T]/(P)
\]
and \(a\in K\) with \(P'(a)P'''(a)\neq0\), and put
\(G(S)=P(a+S)-P(a)\).  The proof then has five steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Design the scalar inverse equation
\[
 E_{\Pi,B,C}(S)
 =G_\Pi(S)-\frac{g_1}{2}(BS^2+C).
\]
\item Arrange the exact derivative identity
\[
 E_{\Pi,B,C}'(S)=g_1D,\qquad D=1-SQ+\Pi S^2.
\]
\item Use the unit \(D\) to reconstruct the source rationally from a simple
root \(S\); the complete formula is boxed in
Section~\ref{sec:gauge}.
\item Choose the quadratic gauge so that this reconstruction polynomializes
in \((x,y,z)\), while the source and target Jacobian factors
\(D^{-1}\) and \(-2D\) cancel.
\item At the selected target, specialize \(E\) to \(P(a+S)\).
Squarefreeness makes \(E'\) invertible modulo \(E\), so the localization
disappears and the full fiber is \(\Spec K[T]/(P)\).
\end{enumerate}
A fixed linear change in the target makes the Jacobian one, and all
coordinate degrees are at most \(6N+2\).  The detailed reconstruction is
natural over arbitrary commutative test algebras; it is this derivative-unit
argument, rather than the graded quotient alone, that identifies the exact
scheme fiber.

Together with the elementary fact that every Keller fiber is finite \'etale,
the realization theorem gives an exact fixed-degree classification.  For
\(N\geq1\), a finite \(K\)-scheme of rank \(N\) is a full Keller fiber of a
geometric-degree-\(N\) map in \(\A^3_K\) exactly when it is finite \'etale
and \(N\neq2\).

The same coordinates identify the actual function-field extension.  On the
dense chart \(1+xy\neq0\), the source field is
\[
 K(x,y,z)=K(\Pi,B,S),
\]
and \(S\) has minimal polynomial \(E_{\Pi,B,C}\) over
\(K(\Pi,B,C)\).  Since that polynomial is irreducible of degree \(N\), the
displayed map has geometric degree \(N\).  This comparison is what turns the
literal rank-\(N\) fiber into a full Keller fiber.

The generic inverse problem is also as large as possible in its Galois
theory.  For every admissible degree-\(N\) seed, not just a generic choice of
its coefficients, the geometric and arithmetic Galois groups of
\(E_{\Pi,B,C}\) are both \(S_N\).  The proof fixes \(\Pi\), parametrizes the
repeated-root curve, and shows that all but finitely many vertical slices are
Morse.  The classical Morse-polynomial theorem gives \(S_N\) on one slice,
and the finite-\'etale ordered-root cover embeds that group into the generic
geometric group.  Over a Hilbertian field, the same fixed map consequently has
infinitely many connected full Keller fibers with splitting-field group
\(S_N\).  Since the natural action of \(S_N\) is primitive, there is no
proper intermediate generic function field.  The chain rule then turns this
into a structural consequence: every realization map remains compositionally
indecomposable after every characteristic-zero extension and every
stabilization by identity variables.

The same inverse equation also detects precisely where affine sheets are
lost.  The logically separate calculation is placed in
Appendix~\ref{app:nonproperness}.  Over an algebraic closure, the reduced
nonproperness locus is
\[
 V_{\mathrm{red}}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Disc}_S E_{\Pi,B,C}\right).
\]
This statement is set-theoretic, or equivalently an equality of reduced
closed subschemes; it does not identify the generally nonreduced
discriminant scheme with a scheme structure on the nonproperness locus.  The
slice \(\Pi=0\) is treated separately by direct source charts and a Newton
polygon calculation.

The reconstruction is stronger than a count of geometric points.  Its
formulas are natural in the test algebra, so they identify the whole fiber
functor, including nilpotents and every scalar extension.  After translating
back from \(S\) to \(T\), the literal fiber is the original algebra
\(K[T]/(P)\).  Nothing arithmetic has been replaced by a numerical proxy.

The statements are separated according to their dependencies.  The
polynomial realization theorem starts from the supplied pair \((P,a)\) and
records the map, its determinant, geometric degree, and exact fiber; the
immediately following effective corollary records the degree bound and
naturality.  Monogenicity then gives the finite-\'etale realization
corollary.  Symmetric monodromy is a separate theorem about the generic
inverse equation, followed by the stable-atomicity corollary.  The rank
classification combines realization with the
classical degree-two obstruction; its scalar-extension and descent argument
is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:degree-two}.  The nonproperness calculation,
which is logically independent of this hierarchy, is collected in
Appendix~\ref{app:nonproperness}; the coefficientwise pullback calculation is
kept separately in Appendix~\ref{app:gauge-identities}.

Arithmetic consequences remain literal transfers: field decomposition,
Galois action, signatures, local points, and rational points are preserved
because the whole finite \'etale algebra is preserved.  Concrete arithmetic
applications are kept in separate certificates rather than developed in this
paper.

The polynomial-presentation construction and its passage to abstract finite
\'etale algebras in characteristic zero have been formalized in Lean.
Starting from a squarefree polynomial of degree at least three, the
formalization chooses an admissible translation, constructs the Jacobian-one
map and target, proves geometric degree \(N\) and the \(6N+2\) coordinate
bound, and identifies the full Keller fiber naturally with
\(\Spec K[T]/(P)\), including its rank and finite \'etaleness.  It also
noncomputably constructs a squarefree polynomial presentation of every
abstract finite \'etale \(K\)-algebra and transports the full natural fiber
certificate across that presentation.
Appendix~\ref{app:lean-correspondence} records the correspondence between
these paper statements and their Lean declarations.
