\section{Introduction and main result}

A polynomial self-map of affine space with nonzero constant Jacobian is
\'etale, so every one of its fibers is a finite \'etale scheme, possibly
empty.  We study the reverse problem: which finite \'etale algebras can occur
as entire fibers of Keller maps?

The relevant condition is fullness.  Let \(K\) be a field and let
\[
 F:\A^m_K\longrightarrow\A^m_K
\]
be a Keller map.  Its geometric degree is
\[
 \gdeg(F)=[K(x_1,\ldots,x_m):K(F_1,\ldots,F_m)].
\]
For \(y\in\A^m(K)\), we call \(F^{-1}(y)\) a \emph{full Keller fiber} if
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_K F^{-1}(y)=\gdeg(F).
\]
Thus the fiber contains every generic inverse sheet; no boundary calculation
is needed once the two ranks have been identified.

Our main theorem begins with concrete presentation data.

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial-presentation theorem]\label{thm:main-intro}
Assume that \(\operatorname{char}K\neq2\).  Let \(P\in K[T]\) be separable
of degree \(N\geq3\), and let \(a\in K\) satisfy
\[
 P^{[1]}(a)P^{[3]}(a)\neq0,
\]
where \(P^{[j]}\) denotes the \(j\)-th Hasse derivative.  There is an
explicitly given polynomial map
\[
 \widetilde F_{P,a}:\A^3_K\longrightarrow\A^3_K
\]
and an explicitly given target \(y_{P,a}\in\A^3(K)\) such that
\[
 \det D\widetilde F_{P,a}=1,\qquad
 \gdeg(\widetilde F_{P,a})=N,\qquad
 \widetilde F_{P,a}^{-1}(y_{P,a})
 \simeq\Spec K[T]/(P).
\]
The fiber isomorphism is natural on commutative \(K\)-algebras, every
coordinate of \(\widetilde F_{P,a}\) has degree at most \(6N+2\), and the
supplied \((P,a)\)-construction commutes with extension of the ground field.
\end{theorem}

Equivalently, separability means \(\gcd(P,P')=1\); this is the
derivative-squarefree condition intended throughout the paper.
Theorem~\ref{thm:polynomial-realization} gives the formulas and proof.  Its
mechanism is a compact scalar inverse equation
\[
 E_{\Pi,B,C}(S)=0
\]
whose derivative is the unit used to reconstruct the source.  The same
factor cancels in the Jacobian.  Over the generic target, the reconstruction
identifies the actual source field with the field obtained by adjoining a
root of \(E\).  A linear-in-\(C\) irreducibility argument then proves that
this extension has degree \(N\).  At the distinguished target,
\(E(S)=P(a+S)\); squarefreeness removes the derivative localization and
gives the literal fiber scheme.  This proves fullness through
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_K\widetilde F_{P,a}^{-1}(y_{P,a})
 =N=\gdeg(\widetilde F_{P,a}).
\]

Every finite \'etale algebra over an infinite field is monogenic.  In
characteristic zero an admissible translation always exists.  Consequently
the concrete theorem gives the paper's principal abstract corollary:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:main-intro}
If \(K\) has characteristic zero, every finite \'etale \(K\)-algebra of rank
at least three occurs as a full Keller fiber of a Jacobian-one polynomial
map \(\A^3_K\to\A^3_K\).
\end{corollary}

The word ``explicit'' applies to the supplied polynomial presentation and
admissible translation.  Starting from an abstract finite \'etale algebra,
the primitive element and the admissible translation are chosen
noncanonically; the corresponding Lean construction is noncomputable and no
functorial choice is claimed.

In characteristic zero, the construction realizes every rank \(N\geq3\).
Together with the classical Galois case of the Jacobian theorem, due to
Campbell, Razar, and Wright, it gives the possible nonzero ranks
\[
 1,3,4,\ldots.
\]
This classification is a corollary with an external input, not part of the
formalized construction.

The proof is organized in the same order as the construction.  Section
\ref{sec:gauge} designs the inverse equation and polynomial pullback.
Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} proves root-to-source reconstruction, the
literal fiber theorem, and the function-field comparison.  Section
\ref{sec:prescribing} passes from a separable presentation to an abstract
finite \'etale algebra and records scalar extension.  Section
\ref{sec:example} gives one explicit arithmetic fiber.  The appendices record
the coefficientwise pullback identities and the Lean correspondence.

The wider characteristic-\(\ne2\) supplied-presentation theorem is proved
directly here.  The current Lean development formalizes its
characteristic-zero specialization; its general files still assume
\texttt{CharZero}.  The exact nonproperness locus, symmetric monodromy, Hilbertian
specialization, and stable compositional atomicity are separate geometric
and monodromy results.  They are not used here.
