\begin{abstract}
Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero.  A fiber of a polynomial map with
nonzero constant Jacobian is finite and \'etale, but the converse question is
not automatic: which finite \'etale $K$-algebras can occur as a \emph{full}
fiber, containing all generic inverse sheets?  We prove that every nonzero
rank occurs except rank two.  Every algebra of rank at least three is already
realized by a map $\A^3_K\to\A^3_K$.

The construction is explicit.  Start with a squarefree polynomial $P$ of
degree $N\geq3$, translate the variable so that $P'(a)P'''(a)\neq0$, and feed
$G(S)=P(a+S)-P(a)$ into a universal quadratic-gauge formula.  After a fixed
linear normalization, the resulting map has Jacobian one and coordinate
degree at most $6N+2$.  At one distinguished target its inverse equation is
exactly $P(a+S)=0$, and the full fiber is naturally isomorphic to
$\Spec K[T]/(P)$.

The useful point is that the construction remembers the algebra, not just the
number of geometric points.  Connectedness, field decomposition, Galois
action, real signature, local points, and scalar extension therefore pass to
the Keller fiber unchanged.  Over $\Q$ this gives an explicit rank-five fiber
with points over $\R$ and every $\Q_p$ but no rational point; rank five is
minimal, and one fixed Keller map contains infinitely many such fibers.

The constructive part of the argument is formalized in Lean: the automatic
choice of $a$, the actual three-variable polynomial map, its Jacobian-one
identity, the $6N+2$ degree bound, and the natural equivalence between the
literal map fiber and $K[T]/(P)$.  Independent SymPy and Singular calculations
check the same identities from separate implementations.
\end{abstract}
