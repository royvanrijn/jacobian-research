\begin{abstract}
A Keller map can only have finite \'etale fibers.  We ask the reverse
question: given a finite \'etale algebra, can it be made into the whole
fiber of such a map?  Over a field \(K\) of characteristic zero, the answer
is yes in every rank at least three.  More precisely, for every separable
polynomial \(P\in K[T]\) of degree \(N\geq3\), we construct a polynomial map
\(F_P:\A^3_K\to\A^3_K\) with \(\det DF_P=1\), geometric degree \(N\), and a
distinguished full fiber naturally isomorphic to \(\Spec K[T]/(P)\).

The construction becomes simple when read backwards.  A one-variable
inverse equation supplies a marked root; its derivative reconstructs the
source and, at the same time, is exactly the factor that cancels from the
Jacobian.  A translation inserts the prescribed polynomial into this
equation.  The characteristic-zero theorem---including the automatic
translation, function-field comparison, and abstract finite-\'etale
corollary---is formalized in Lean.  Over a field of characteristic different
from \(2\), the same formulas also give an explicit supplied-presentation
result whenever the first and third Hasse coefficients at the chosen
translation are nonzero.  That broader result commutes with scalar extension
but is not yet formalized end to end.  Choices made from an abstract finite
\'etale algebra are noncanonical.
\end{abstract}
