\begin{abstract}
Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero.  We determine exactly which
nonzero finite \'etale $K$-algebras occur as coordinate algebras of full fibers
of polynomial maps with nonzero constant Jacobian: every rank occurs except
rank two, and every realizable algebra already occurs for a map
$\A^3_K\to\A^3_K$.  Here full means that the fiber length equals the geometric
degree of the map.

The realization is explicit and effective.  Given a squarefree polynomial
$P$ of degree $N\geq3$, choose $a\in K$ with $P'(a)P'''(a)\neq0$.  A universal
quadratic-gauge formula, followed by a fixed linear output normalization,
gives a Jacobian-one map of coordinate degree at most $6N+2$ whose
distinguished fiber is naturally represented by $K[T]/(P)$.  Such an $a$
always exists.

Consequently every number field of degree at least three occurs as a connected
Keller fiber, and all arithmetic information carried by an arbitrary finite
\'etale algebra transfers unchanged.  Over $\Q$ we exhibit an explicit
rank-five fiber with points over $\R$ and every $\Q_p$ but no rational point,
and prove that rank five is minimal.  One fixed Keller map carries infinitely
many such fibers.  Lean formalizes the existence of the admissible translation,
the source-equation reconstruction over every commutative test algebra, its
naturality, and representation by the original quotient $K[T]/(P)$.  The
displayed all-degree map identities are proved coefficientwise and independently
checked by exact symbolic computation in representative degrees.
\end{abstract}
