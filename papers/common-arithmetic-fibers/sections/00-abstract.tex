\begin{abstract}
Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero.  We determine exactly which
nonzero finite \'etale $K$-algebras occur as coordinate algebras of full fibers
of polynomial maps with nonzero constant Jacobian: every rank occurs except
rank two, and every realizable algebra already occurs for a map
$\A^3_K\to\A^3_K$.  Here full means that the fiber length equals the geometric
degree of the map.

The realization is explicit and effective.  Given a squarefree polynomial
$P$ of degree $N\geq3$, choose $a\in K$ with $P'(a)P'''(a)\neq0$.  A universal
quadratic-gauge formula, followed by a fixed linear output normalization,
gives a Jacobian-one map of coordinate degree at most $6N+2$ whose
distinguished fiber is naturally represented by $K[T]/(P)$.  Such an $a$
always exists.

Consequently every number field of degree at least three occurs as a connected
Keller fiber.  More generally, field decomposition, geometric Galois action,
real signature, and every scalar extension of a finite \'etale algebra are
preserved by the realization.  Over $\Q$ we exhibit an explicit rank-five
fiber with points over $\R$ and every $\Q_p$ but no rational point, and prove
that rank five is minimal.  One fixed Keller map carries infinitely many such
fibers.  Lean formalizes the automatic choice of $a$, the actual all-degree
three-variable polynomial map, its determinant-one identity and $6N+2$ degree
bound, and a natural equivalence between the literal map fiber over every
commutative test algebra and the original quotient $K[T]/(P)$.  Independent
exact SymPy and Singular calculations audit the universal identities and
expanded maps.
\end{abstract}
