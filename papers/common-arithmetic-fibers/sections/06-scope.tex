\section{Consequences, verification, provenance, and literature boundary}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a realization theorem, not merely a source of
counterexamples.  Some immediate applications are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every squarefree polynomial of degree at least three is, after one
      translation of its variable, the exact inverse polynomial of a full
      Jacobian-one Keller fiber.
\item A degree-$N$ input has an explicit realization in $\A^3$ of coordinate
      degree at most $6N+2$.
\item Every intersective polynomial of degree at least three transfers to a
      Hasse-failing Keller fiber without stabilization or auxiliary factors.
\item Any real signature, splitting field, or collection of local
      factorization patterns already carried by a finite \'etale algebra is
      carried by the corresponding Keller fiber, compatibly with extension of
      the ground field.
\item Connected fibers require no additional geometric argument: start with
      an irreducible polynomial.
\end{enumerate}

The fourth item is deliberately a transfer statement.  It does not solve an
inverse Galois problem or assert that arbitrary independently prescribed
local algebras globalize; it says that every arithmetic realization which
already exists is preserved exactly by the Keller construction.

The realization is not unique.  Separate boundary calculations in the
accompanying research repository show that the same finite \'etale algebra
can occur in stably inequivalent weighted, cancellation, and quadratic-gauge
Keller maps.  That stable-classification problem is logically independent of
the present theorem and is best treated in a sequel, tentatively titled
\emph{One Fiber, Many Keller Maps}.

\paragraph{Literature boundary.}
A dated search completed on 24 July 2026 found no earlier statement that every
finite \'etale algebra of rank at least three is a full fiber of a polynomial
Keller map, nor the resulting rank classification.  This is necessarily a
qualified priority statement: bibliographic searches can miss unpublished
work, differently phrased results, and sources not indexed electronically.
The appropriate claim is therefore ``to our knowledge'', not an absolute
firstness assertion.  A reproducible search ledger is included with the
paper sources.

The closest results located have different scopes.  Gallagher gives explicit
Keller maps in every geometric degree and complete split rational fibers
\cite{Gallagher}; this realizes $K^N$, rather than an arbitrary finite
\'etale $K$-algebra.  Miranda--Neto proves radicality and maximal-ideal
decomposition properties for fibers already arising from a Keller map
\cite{Miranda}; this is not a prescribed-fiber realization theorem.
Lipton and Markakis study rational images and Hilbert irreducibility
\cite{LiptonMarkakis}, while Shaska studies graded Keller maps and arithmetic
thinness phenomena \cite{Shaska}.  None of these sources states the
finite-\'etale realization theorem proved here.

\paragraph{Independent exact verification.}
The structural checker
\[
 \texttt{scripts/verify\_universal\_quadratic\_gauge.py}
\]
verifies the source-chart reciprocal and Jacobian identities, the marked-line
Jacobian cancellation, the generic $k$-th coefficient identities, an
independent six-coefficient bridge, the all-degree termwise coordinate bound,
and the target-preserving determinant-one normalization.  These are symbolic
identities, not numerical sampling.

A separate Singular script
\[
 \texttt{scripts/verify\_universal\_quadratic\_gauge.sing}
\]
works over the rational function field in six algebraically independent
coefficients.  It expands the complete degree-six three-variable map, obtains
Jacobian determinant exactly $-2$, and checks the coordinate-degree profile
$(7,38,36)$.  This provides a CAS implementation independent of both Lean and
SymPy.

The concrete checker
\[
 \texttt{scripts/verify\_finite\_etale\_keller\_fibers.py}
\]
independently constructs the quadratic-gauge map from translated seeds of
degrees three, four, and five.  For each seed it expands the three-variable
Jacobian exactly, checks the determinant-one normalization and coordinate-degree
bounds, recovers the prescribed inverse polynomial, and verifies both directions
of the source reconstruction in the quotient ring using an explicit inverse of
$E'$.  It separately audits the scaling, determinant, target, and fiber of the
integral degree-five map and the inverse equations in the fixed-map infinite
family.

These computations are independent regression and structural certificates,
not a proof by testing finitely many degrees.  The universal paper proof is the
coefficientwise calculation in Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge} and
Subsection~\ref{subsec:gauge-identities}; the corresponding arbitrary-degree
map, determinant, and effective-degree theorems are also formalized in Lean.

\paragraph{Lean formalization.}
The repository contains a Lean project at
\[
 \texttt{formal/finite-etale-keller}.
\]
It contains no \texttt{sorry} and introduces no project-specific axioms.
Starting from a squarefree polynomial $P$ with $\deg P\geq3$, the final formal
construction performs the following steps.

First, Lean proves that an admissible translation parameter exists and chooses
one internally.  It forms the rooted translated seed
\[
 G(S)=P(a+S)-P(a)
\]
and packages the complete finite coefficient sums as a single actual map
\[
 F_G:\operatorname{MvPolynomial}(\operatorname{Fin}3,K)^3.
\]
The files \texttt{GaugeAssembly.lean},
\texttt{GaugeInverseAssembly.lean}, and
\texttt{GeneralGaugeMap.lean} prove the coefficientwise assembly and exact
evaluation formulas uniformly in the degree.

Second, \texttt{GeneralGaugeJacobian.lean} directly expands the full
three-by-three Jacobian, including every finite coefficient sum, and proves
\[
 \det DF_G=-2.
\]
The fixed output normalization is then proved to have determinant one.
\texttt{GeneralGaugeDegree.lean} proves the bounds
\[
 \deg\Pi\leq7,\qquad \deg B\leq6N+2,\qquad \deg C\leq6N,
\]
and \texttt{GeneralGaugeRealizationDegree.lean} transports them to the final
automatically chosen map in terms of the original degree $N=\deg P$.

Third, \texttt{GeneralGaugeInverse.lean} defines the full generic inverse
polynomial, its marked polynomial $\beta$, and the exact derivative
factorization.  \texttt{GeneralGaugeDisplayedFiber.lean} proves that the
actual evaluated $B$ and $C$ coordinates are precisely the marked source
relations.  \texttt{GeneralGaugeRawFiber.lean} begins with a literal triple
satisfying the three actual polynomial equations; from $tq=\pi$ with unit
$\pi$, it constructs the global chart unit over an arbitrary commutative test
ring and identifies the literal map fiber with the represented source fiber.

Fourth, \texttt{GeneralGaugeNormalization.lean} proves that the zero second
coordinate fiber is unchanged by the determinant-one output normalization.
\texttt{GeneralGaugeRealization.lean} proves that the inverse polynomial at
the chosen target is exactly $P(a+S)$, composes with
\[
 K[S]/(P(a+S))\simeq K[T]/(P(T)),
\]
and removes the supplied translation parameter through the automatic choice.
For every commutative test $K$-algebra $A$, the final theorem gives a natural
equivalence
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{K\text{-alg}}(K[T]/(P),A)
 \simeq
 \{u\in A^3:\widetilde F_G(u)=y_{P,a}\}.
\]
The corresponding declarations are
\texttt{automaticRealizationMap\_certificate} and
\texttt{automaticJacobianOneFiberRepresentingEquiv\_natural}.  The latter's
axiom report contains only Lean's standard \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}; the Jacobian and degree
certificates use no additional axioms.

The remaining formal boundary is now outside the constructive map and fiber
calculation.  Lean does not yet formalize the primitive linear-in-$C$
irreducibility argument which identifies the generic function-field degree,
monogenicity of arbitrary finite \'etale products over an infinite field, or
the Campbell--Razar--Wright degree-two theorem.  The local-number-theoretic,
Chebotarev, Dirichlet, and prime-counting inputs in the arithmetic applications
also remain paper proofs.  These inputs are listed claim by claim in the
companion verification matrix; none is represented as machine-checked.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
The recent three-dimensional counterexample was publicly attributed to
Alp\"oge and Fable.  Gallagher independently developed a weighted all-degree
viewpoint and an exact public atlas of complete rational fibers
\cite{Gallagher}.  The proof here instead uses the root-engineered
quadratic-gauge construction and the finite-\'etale realization theorem.
Berend and Bilu are credited for the classical intersective quintic.  The
rank-two exclusion is the Galois case proved over $\mathbb C$ by Campbell and
subsequently treated algebraically by Razar and independently Wright
\cite{Campbell,Razar,Wright}; Lemma~\ref{lem:degree-two} includes the descent
argument needed for an arbitrary characteristic-zero ground field.
