\section{Consequences, checks, and scope}

The main theorem is a prescribed-fiber realization result.  Given a finite
\'etale algebra, it builds a Keller map whose full fiber is that algebra.  The
classification by rank is a useful corollary, but it is not the main point: two
finite \'etale algebras of the same rank can have completely different field
decompositions, Galois actions, signatures, and local behavior, and the
construction distinguishes all of that data.

Several consequences are immediate.  A squarefree polynomial of degree at
least three becomes an exact inverse polynomial after one translation; an
irreducible polynomial gives a connected fiber; and a degree-$N$ input is
realized in $\A^3$ with coordinate degree at most $6N+2$.  Moreover, every
map produced by the construction has geometric and arithmetic generic
monodromy $S_N$.  Over a Hilbertian field, each such fixed map has infinitely
many connected full fibers with splitting-field group $S_N$.

The fullness condition also has an intrinsic geometric interpretation for
this family.  Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-nonproperness} computes the exact
nonproperness hypersurface, and Corollary~\ref{cor:intrinsic-fullness} shows
that every prescribed target lies in its complement.  Thus fullness is not
only a length equality checked at the selected target: it is equivalent there
to membership in the maximal finite-\'etale locus.  The partition formula
\eqref{eq:partition-sheet-loss} and the special table
\eqref{eq:pi-zero-fiber-table} describe exactly how fullness fails on every
boundary stratum.

More importantly, the construction does not forget the arithmetic structure.
If the original algebra has a particular real signature, splitting field,
Galois action, or collection of local factorizations, the fiber has the same
data.  The same applies to local and rational points.  Thus an intersective
polynomial with no rational root gives a Hasse-failing Keller fiber directly,
without adding auxiliary factors or extra variables.

This is a transfer statement, not an inverse Galois theorem.  It does not claim
that arbitrary local specifications can always be globalized.  It says that
once a finite \'etale algebra with the desired arithmetic properties exists,
the Keller construction preserves it exactly.  Nor does the monodromy theorem
claim that one fixed seed realizes every rank-$N$ finite \'etale algebra: the
prescribed algebra is used to choose the seed.  Its precise strengthening is
that every resulting cover, including one built for a highly special algebra,
has maximal generic monodromy.

The realization is also far from unique.  Other constructions in the
accompanying repository place the same finite \'etale algebra inside weighted,
cancellation, and quadratic-gauge Keller maps that need not be stably
equivalent.  That is a different question, and I leave it for a separate
paper.

\paragraph{Relation to earlier work.}
To my knowledge, the following prescribed-algebra statement has not appeared
before: every finite \'etale $K$-algebra of rank at least three occurs as a
\emph{full scheme fiber} of an explicit Keller map $\A^3_K\to\A^3_K$.  The
rank set $\{1,3,4,\ldots\}$ follows from this theorem together with the known
degree-two obstruction.  I searched for earlier results up to 24 July 2026,
but this priority statement is necessarily qualified: searches can miss
unpublished work or a theorem stated in different language.

The closest constructions have a different scope.  Gallagher gives explicit
Keller maps in every geometric degree together with complete split rational
fibers \cite{Gallagher}; these realize $K^N$, rather than an arbitrary finite
\'etale $K$-algebra.  Miranda--Neto studies radicality and maximal-ideal
decompositions of fibers that already come from Keller maps \cite{Miranda}.
Lipton and Markakis connect rational images with Hilbert irreducibility
\cite{LiptonMarkakis}, and Shaska studies graded Keller maps and arithmetic
thinness \cite{Shaska}.  None of these results states the prescribed-algebra
realization theorem used here.  The monodromy argument uses the classical
theorem that a Morse polynomial has symmetric Galois group over the
value-function field \cite[Theorem~4.4.5]{Serre}.
The global boundary argument uses Jelonek's fiber-multiplicity criterion and
is consistent with his theorem that the nonproperness locus of a dominant
complex polynomial self-map is a uniruled hypersurface
\cite{Jelonek1993}.  Jelonek and Laso\'n later bounded the degrees of
polynomial curves covering such a locus \cite{JelonekLason}; in the present
family the curves and missing source arcs are explicit.

\paragraph{Independent computer checks.}
The formulas are uniform in the degree, but expanding them by hand is not a
pleasant way to find a sign error.  I therefore checked the construction in
three independent ways.

The SymPy script
\[
 \texttt{scripts/verify\_universal\_quadratic\_gauge.py}
\]
verifies the source-chart identity, the marked-line formulas, the Jacobian
cancellation, every generic coefficient contribution, and the degree bound.
These are symbolic identities with arbitrary coefficients, not numerical
tests at a few sample points.

The Singular script
\[
 \texttt{scripts/verify\_universal\_quadratic\_gauge.sing}
\]
starts again over a rational function field in six independent coefficients.
It expands the complete degree-six map, obtains determinant exactly $-2$, and
checks the degree profile $(7,38,36)$.  This gives a separate CAS
implementation of the same construction.

Finally,
\[
 \texttt{scripts/verify\_finite\_etale\_keller\_fibers.py}
\]
builds translated seeds in degrees three, four, and five, expands the actual
maps, reconstructs the prescribed quotient rings, and checks the explicit
rank-five Hasse example and the fixed-map family.  These bounded examples are
regression tests.  The proof for arbitrary degree is the coefficientwise
argument in the general localized quadratic-gauge fiber theorem,
Theorem~\ref{thm:localized-gauge}, and
Subsection~\ref{subsec:gauge-identities}.

The additional checker
\[
 \texttt{scripts/verify\_quadratic\_gauge\_nonproperness.py}
\]
independently verifies the degree-three and degree-four discriminant and
resultant factors, the exact \(\Pi\)-adic discriminant order through degree
ten, the uniform Newton-polygon formula through degree sixty-four, the
\(q=0\) and \(t=0\) special-fiber charts, and all displayed boundary
valuations.  The pre-existing normalization checker
\[
 \texttt{scripts/verify\_quadratic\_weighted\_stable\_separation.py}
\]
derives the same all-degree boundary ledger from the finite normalization.

\paragraph{Lean formalization.}
The Lean project lives in
\[
 \texttt{formal/finite-etale-keller}.
\]
It contains no \texttt{sorry} and introduces no project-specific axioms.  The
formal development covers the constructive core of the paper.  Given a
squarefree polynomial $P$ of degree at least three, it:
\begin{enumerate}
\item proves that an admissible translation exists and chooses one;
\item constructs the complete all-degree map as an actual
      $\operatorname{MvPolynomial}(\operatorname{Fin}3,K)$ expression;
\item proves that its normalized Jacobian is one and that all coordinate
      degrees are at most $6\deg P+2$;
\item proves that the inverse polynomial at the selected target is
      $P(a+S)$;
\item identifies the literal map fiber naturally with
      $\operatorname{Hom}_{K\text{-alg}}(K[T]/(P),A)$ for every commutative
      test algebra $A$, while recording that the representing quotient has
      $K$-dimension $\deg P$ and is finite \'etale over $K$; and
\item identifies the actual source function field over $K(\Pi,B)(C)$ with
      the inverse-root quotient and proves geometric degree $\deg P$.
\end{enumerate}
It further proves monogenicity for every abstract finite \'etale algebra over
a characteristic-zero field, noncomputably selects a squarefree polynomial
presentation of degree equal to its rank, and composes that presentation with
the preceding construction.  Thus for rank at least three it identifies the
literal Keller fiber naturally with
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{K\text{-alg}}(A,-)\) for the original abstract algebra
\(A\).
It also checks that the seed $G(S)=S+S^3$ gives the announced map
\eqref{eq:announced-counterexample} coordinate for coordinate.  It proves
that the three map coordinates are algebraically independent and constructs
the induced injective pullback on rational function fields.  Promoting $\Pi$
and $B$ to independent parameters, it also proves irreducibility and degree
$\deg G$ of the full inverse equation over the iterated presentation
$K(\Pi,B)(C)$, and proves that its root quotient has that finrank.  For the
optimal quintic example it goes further: the exact denominator-free map
\eqref{eq:explicit-hasse-map}, at the exact target $(1,0,-38)$, has a literal
fiber naturally represented over every commutative rational test algebra by
\[
 \Q[T]/\bigl((T^3-19)(T^2+T+1)\bigr),
\]
and Lean proves that this quotient is finite \'etale and has rank five.
It also proves that the literal fiber has no rational point and constructs
points over the reals and every $p$-adic field.  The nonarchimedean proof
formalizes the two generic residue classes and the direct Hensel witnesses at
$2$, $3$, and $19$.

\quad\text{and}\quad
\small\texttt{generalGaugeFullyGenericInverseAdjoinRoot\_finrank},
\end{center}
and the natural fiber equivalence is

\end{center}
The corresponding concrete quintic certificate is

\end{center}
Their axiom reports contain only Lean's standard logical principles
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

Lean does not currently cover every theorem used in the final classification.
Lean proves algebraic independence, the injective function-field pullback,
the explicit comparison with the inverse-root extension over
$K(\Pi,B)(C)$, and the resulting geometric degree.  It also proves that the
complete map and target attached to a supplied translation parameter commute
coefficientwise with extension of the ground field, and proves the
tensor-product base change of the representing polynomial quotient.  Exact
nonproperness and boundary-sheet accounting, the
symmetric-monodromy and Hilbertian-specialization arguments, and the
Campbell--Razar--Wright degree-two theorem remain ordinary mathematical
proofs.
For the quintic example, Lean now verifies the exact literal fiber, its rank,
finite \'etaleness, the absence of rational points, and the existence of
points over the reals and every nonarchimedean completion.  Chebotarev,
Dirichlet, and prime-counting arguments elsewhere in the arithmetic
applications remain outside the formal development.  Lean does prove the
finite-\(G\)-set fixed-point lemma used immediately after Chebotarev in the
paper's degree-four argument.  For the alternative degree-four
barrier, Lean verifies the tensor-product local-point identity, the
finite-\'etale field decomposition and component count, the complete
rank-at-most-four local-sheet inequality, the strict diagonal tensor
surplus, and the positive first/second-moment deduction.  The analytic
extraction of the first prime moment from Dedekind zeta is the only remaining
ordinary-proof input to that alternative route.

\paragraph{Provenance and AI assistance.}
The recent three-dimensional counterexample was announced by Alp\"oge, who
credited Akhil Mathew for suggesting the problem and Fable for producing the
example \cite{Alpoge}.  Gallagher independently developed a weighted
all-degree viewpoint and a public atlas of complete rational fibers
\cite{Gallagher}.  The present proof uses a root-engineered quadratic gauge
that contains the announced map as the seed $S+S^3$.  Berend and Bilu supplied
the classical intersective quintic, while Campbell, Razar, and Wright supplied
the degree-two obstruction \cite{Campbell,Razar,Wright}.

I used generative AI tools during exploration, symbolic-checker development,
proof review, literature search, Lean formalization, and manuscript editing.
They were useful as fast but fallible collaborators.  I checked the stated
mathematical claims, rewrote the exposition, and take responsibility for the
paper.
