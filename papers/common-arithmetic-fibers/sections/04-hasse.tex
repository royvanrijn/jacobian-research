\section{The minimal Hasse failure}\label{sec:hasse}

We first separate the arithmetic statement from Keller maps.  A finite
\'etale \(K\)-scheme \(X\) over a number field is a \emph{Hasse failure} if
it has a point over every completion of \(K\) but no \(K\)-point.  The
degree-four obstruction is the following elementary fact about finite
Galois sets, followed by one use of Chebotarev.

\begin{lemma}[The degree-four fixed-point lemma]
\label{lem:degree-four-fixed-point}
Let \(G\) be a finite group acting on a finite set \(\Omega\).  If
\[
 |\Omega|\leq4,
 \qquad
 \Omega^g\neq\varnothing\quad\text{for every }g\in G,
\]
then
\[
 \boxed{\Omega^G\neq\varnothing.}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that \(\Omega^G\) is empty.  Then the action has no orbit of size one.
Since \(|\Omega|\leq4\), either \(\Omega\) is transitive, or
\(|\Omega|=4\) and it is the disjoint union of two orbits of size two.

In the transitive case, Burnside's lemma gives
\[
 \frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|\Omega^g|=1.
\]
The identity fixes at least two points.  If every other element fixed at
least one point, the average would be strictly larger than one.  Hence some
element is a derangement.

In the remaining case, let \(H_1,H_2\) be the kernels of the actions on the
two two-point orbits.  Both have index two.  There is an element outside
\(H_1\cup H_2\): this is immediate if \(H_1=H_2\), while if they are
distinct one may choose
\(h_i\in H_i\setminus H_{3-i}\), and then \(h_1h_2\) lies in neither.
Such an element swaps both orbits and is again a derangement.  Both cases
contradict the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Chebotarev input.}
We use the Chebotarev density theorem in the following standard form: if
\(L/K\) is a finite Galois extension and \(C\subset\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\)
is a conjugacy class, then infinitely many finite places \(v\) of \(K\),
unramified in \(L\), have Frobenius class \(C\)
\cite[Chapter~VII, \S13]{Neukirch}.

\begin{corollary}[Degree-four local--global theorem]
\label{cor:degree-four-local-global}
Let \(K\) be any number field.  A finite \'etale \(K\)-scheme \(X\) of
degree at most four which has a point over \(K_v\) for all but finitely many
finite places \(v\) has a \(K\)-point.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(L/K\) be a finite Galois extension splitting \(X\), put
\[
 G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K),
 \qquad
 \Omega=X(\overline K),
\]
and enlarge the exceptional set to include the places ramified in \(L\).
A \(K\)-point of \(X\) is exactly a point of \(\Omega^G\).

Fix \(g\in G\).  Chebotarev supplies a finite place \(v\) outside the
exceptional set whose Frobenius class is the conjugacy class of \(g\).
By hypothesis \(X(K_v)\neq\varnothing\).  After choosing a place of \(L\)
above \(v\), a \(K_v\)-point gives a geometric point fixed by the
decomposition group, hence by its Frobenius element.  Fixed-point
nonemptiness is invariant under conjugacy, so \(\Omega^g\neq\varnothing\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:degree-four-fixed-point} now gives
\(\Omega^G\neq\varnothing\), as required.
\end{proof}

We use the classical polynomial of Berend and Bilu,
\begin{equation}
 P_5(T)=(T^3-19)(T^2+T+1).
 \label{eq:BB}
\end{equation}
It appeared in their study of polynomials with roots modulo every integer
\cite{BerendBilu}.

\begin{theorem}[Minimal finite-\'etale Hasse failure over \(\Q\)]
\label{thm:finite-etale-hasse}
The scheme
\[
 X_5=\Spec\Q[T]/\bigl((T^3-19)(T^2+T+1)\bigr)
\]
is finite \'etale of degree five and satisfies
\[
 X_5(\Q)=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 X_5(\Q_v)\neq\varnothing
 \quad\text{for every place }v.
\]
Rank five is minimal for finite-\'etale Hasse failures over \(\Q\).  In fact,
no finite \'etale \(\Q\)-scheme of degree at most four can fail the
local--global principle if it is soluble at all but finitely many finite
places.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 g(T)=T^3-19,\qquad h(T)=T^2+T+1.
\]
The following table is the complete nonarchimedean local-solubility audit.
In a generic row, the displayed residue root is simple and hence lifts by
Hensel's lemma.  For a finite base-case audit we remove \(2,3,19\) from the
generic rows and give a direct witness at each of them.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
prime $p$ & factor & residue or approximate root & Hensel check\\
\hline
$p\notin\{2,3,19\}$, $p\equiv2\pmod3$
 & $g$ & $a^3=19$ in $\mathbb F_p$
 & $g'(a)=3a^2\neq0$\\
$p\notin\{2,3,19\}$, $p\equiv1\pmod3$
 & $h$ & $\zeta\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, $\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)=3$
 & $h'(\zeta)=2\zeta+1\neq0$\\
$p=2$ & $g$ & $a=1$ & $g(1)=-18$, $g'(1)=3\in\mathbb Z_2^\times$\\
$p=3$ & $g$ & $a=-2$ &
 $v_3(g(-2))=3>2=2v_3(g'(-2))$\\
$p=19$ & $h$ & $a=7$ &
 $h(7)=57$, $h'(7)=15\in\mathbb Z_{19}^\times$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here are the justifications behind the two generic rows.  If
$p\equiv2\pmod3$, then $\gcd(3,p-1)=1$, so cubing is an automorphism of
$\mathbb F_p^\times$; since $p\neq19$, it gives a nonzero $a$ with
$a^3=19$, and $p\neq3$ makes $3a^2$ nonzero.  If $p\equiv1\pmod3$, the
cyclic group $\mathbb F_p^\times$ contains an element $\zeta$ of order three.
Then $\zeta\neq1$ and
\[
 0=\zeta^3-1=(\zeta-1)h(\zeta).
\]
The root is simple because the two roots of $h$ can coincide only when its
discriminant $-3$ vanishes, which is excluded.  The rows $p=2$ and $p=19$
use ordinary simple-root Hensel.  The $p=3$ row uses the strong form of
Hensel's lemma: $g(-2)=-27$ and $g'(-2)=12$, so
$v_3(g(-2))>2v_3(g'(-2))$.

This covers every finite prime, since every prime outside
$\{2,3,19\}$ is congruent to $1$ or $2$ modulo $3$.  At the real place,
$g(2)=-11<0<8=g(3)$, so the intermediate value theorem supplies a real
root.

There is no rational root: the rational-root theorem excludes a root of
$g$ (none of $\pm1,\pm19$ is a root), while $h$ has discriminant $-3$.
Moreover, \(P_5=gh\) is squarefree: both factors are squarefree and they are
coprime, since a root of \(h\) has cube \(1\) and therefore cannot be a root
of \(g\).  Thus \(X_5\) is finite \'etale of degree five, and its local and
global points are exactly those just audited.  The final assertion is
Corollary~\ref{cor:degree-four-local-global} with \(K=\Q\).
\end{proof}

Now call a full Keller fiber over \(\Q\) a \emph{Keller Hasse failure} if it
has points over every completion but no rational point, and let
\(d_{\mathrm{HP}}\) be the least possible rank.

\begin{corollary}[Optimal Keller Hasse failure]\label{thm:hasse}
There is an explicit Keller map \(F:\A^3_\Q\to\A^3_\Q\) of geometric degree
five and a rational target \(y\) such that \(F^{-1}(y)\) is a full Keller
fiber of rank five and
\[
 F^{-1}(y)(\Q)=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 F^{-1}(y)(\Q_v)\neq\varnothing
 \quad\text{for every place }v.
\]
No Keller Hasse failure over \(\Q\) has rank below five.  Hence
\[
 \boxed{d_{\mathrm{HP}}=5}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:polynomial-realization} applies to \(P_5\) with \(a=0\).
To keep the resulting full Keller fiber completely explicit, we record a
denominator-free target rescaling.  Put
\[
 G(T)=P_5(T)-P_5(0)=T^5+T^4+T^3-19T^2-19T.
\]
Thus
\[
 (g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4,g_5)=(-19,-19,1,1,1).
\]
With $t=1+xy$ and
\[
 q=t^2z-19y^2(1+3t),
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:localized-gauge} gives the gauge map
\begin{align*}
 \Pi={}&tq,\\
 B_0={}&y-\frac3{19}xq+2tq
       -\frac4{19}t^2x^2q^4-\frac5{19}t^2x^3q^5,\\
 C_0={}&x(5-3t)+\frac1{19}x^3z
       +\frac2{19}(xq)^4+\frac3{19}(xq)^5,
\end{align*}
with $\det D(\Pi,B_0,C_0)=-2$.  Apply the target linear automorphism
\[
 L(\Pi,B_0,C_0)=(\Pi,19B_0,19C_0).
\]
The resulting denominator-free map $F=L\circ F_G$ is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 F_1={}&tq,\\
 F_2={}&19y-3xq+38tq-4t^2x^2q^4-5t^2x^3q^5,\\
 F_3={}&19x(5-3t)+x^3z+2(xq)^4+3(xq)^5.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:explicit-hasse-map}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 \boxed{\det DF=\det(L)(-2)=19^2(-2)=-722.}
\]
The normalized target $(1,0,-2)$ is sent to
\[
 y=(1,0,-38).
\]
At that target the inverse equation is
\[
 G(T)-\frac{-19}{2}(-2)=G(T)-19=P_5(T).
\]
The finite-\'etale (squarefree) fiber corollary,
Corollary~\ref{cor:squarefree-gauge}, therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{F^{-1}(1,0,-38)\simeq
 \Spec\Q[T]/\bigl((T^3-19)(T^2+T+1)\bigr).}
 \label{eq:explicit-hasse-fiber}
\end{equation}
The arithmetic assertions now follow from
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-etale-hasse}.  Conversely, every Keller Hasse failure
is a finite \'etale scheme, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:degree-four-local-global} excludes a
Keller Hasse failure of rank at most four.  The map, target, determinant, and
quotient algebra are therefore all explicit, while minimality is inherited
from the finite-\'etale statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The moment alternative]\label{rem:degree-four-alternatives}
There is also a zeta-moment route.  If \(\nu_v(A)\) counts local embeddings,
\(c(A)\) counts connected components, and \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{pr}}\) denotes
Dirichlet prime mean, Dedekind zeta gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm{pr}}\nu_v(A)=c(A),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm{pr}}\nu_v(A)^2=c(A\otimes_KA).
\]
The resulting first/second-moment contradiction proves the same degree-four
theorem and explains the \(2+3\) threshold.  It is not used in the paper
proof.
\end{remark}
