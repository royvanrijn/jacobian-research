# Selected-field generic images for multibase quadratic Gaussian fibres

For this architecture, the full actual generic image is a rank-one
connection over the finite field generated by the selected algebraic
base branch. The proof permits arbitrary polynomial quadratic fibres
and every characteristic-zero coefficient field. The bounded internal
controls support explicit identities; they do not prove the unrestricted
theorem. Independent replay, formal verification and external review
are not claimed.

## 1. Statement and precise selected field

Let K be a characteristic-zero field. Use n independent Witt pairs
(Z_i,W_i) with E[Z^alpha W^beta]=delta_(alpha,beta)alpha!, and s ordinary
Gaussian variables Y of fixed nonsingular symmetric covariance C,
independent of the Witt variables. Let

    P=W^T h(Z)+Y^T A(Z)Y+u(Z)^T Y+k(Z),              (M1)

where h is an arbitrary polynomial n-vector, A an arbitrary symmetric
polynomial s by s matrix, u a polynomial s-vector, and k a polynomial.
Empty n or s is permitted. In particular A need not be constant.

Let F=K(t), and let zeta in tK[[t]]^n be the unique root zeta=t h(zeta).
Put

    J=I-t Dh(zeta), D=I-2t C A(zeta),
    S=D^-1 C=(C^-1-2t A(zeta))^-1,
    R=t k(zeta)+t^2 u(zeta)^T S u(zeta)/2,
    f=exp(R)/(detJ sqrt(detD)), L=F(zeta_1,...,zeta_n). (M2)

The square root has constant coefficient one, and S is symmetric even
though D need not be symmetric. The formula uses matrix multiplication
in the displayed order. Empty determinants are one.

For Phi[Q]=E[Qexp(tP)], V=Phi[K[Z,W,Y]], M=K[t]V, the claim is

    L/F is finite separable,
    F M=L f inside K((t)).                           (M3)

Therefore the full ACTUAL generic rank equals [L:F]. Any F-basis of L
chosen from the monomials zeta^alpha gives a faithful frame
Phi[Z^alpha]=zeta^alpha f of ORIGINAL base markers. All original marked
periods are f times elements of L. This is a generic-image theorem:
it does not determine the global K[t]-lattice, literal image, degree
filtration, or unit-cyclic differential rank.

## 2. Algebraicity of the selected branch

Existence and uniqueness of zeta follows by t-adic iteration of
zeta=t h(zeta). Every coefficient is obtained by finite polynomial
operations over K. The selected J has constant matrix I, so detJ is
nonzero in the embedded field L subset K((t)).

Here is a complete field argument that does not assume every component
of the full root system is zero-dimensional. L is finitely generated
over F. Suppose it has positive transcendence degree. Choose a finite
transcendence basis T_1,...,T_q. The F-derivation partial_T1 of
F(T_1,...,T_q) extends to L: L is a finite algebraic extension of that
rational field and is separable in characteristic zero. Explicitly,
for an algebraic generator a with minimal polynomial m, its derivative
is forced by delta(a)=-(delta m)(a)/m'(a); separability makes m'(a)
nonzero, and successive finite extensions give the extension.

Apply this derivation to zeta=t h(zeta). Since it kills F,

    J delta(zeta)=0.

Invertibility of J forces delta(zeta)=0. The zeta_i generate L over F,
so delta is zero on L, contradicting delta(T_1)=1. Thus L/F is algebraic,
and finite because it is finitely generated. It is separable in
characteristic zero.

The selected maximal ideal is the kernel of evaluation
F[Z]->L, Z_i->zeta_i. Indeed F[zeta] is a finite-dimensional integral
domain over F, hence is already the field L. Its monomials span L;
extracting a finite basis gives the claimed original marked frame.

It is essential to use this selected field rather than the whole
quotient by Z-t h(Z). For example take two bases and

    h_1=z_1 z_2, h_2=z_2^2.

The selected root is (0,0), with J=I and L=F. But the full equations
z_1(1-tz_2)=z_2(1-tz_2)=0 also contain the entire line z_2=1/t.
Those extra solutions have no part in the selected actual period.

## 3. Exact original source, including variable A and covariance C

For source vectors alpha,beta,xi, let zeta_beta=t h(zeta_beta)+beta,
with zeta_beta(0,beta)=beta, and evaluate J_beta,D_beta,S_beta at this
root as in (M2). Then exact circular Wick contraction and ordinary
Gaussian completion give

    E exp(tP+alpha^T Z+beta^T W+xi^T Y)
      = exp(alpha^T zeta_beta+t k(zeta_beta)
             +(t u(zeta_beta)+xi)^T
                       S_beta(t u(zeta_beta)+xi)/2)
          /(detJ_beta sqrt(detD_beta)).              (M4)

This is the retained
[multibase contraction](GAUSSIAN_MULTIBASE_QUADRATIC_FIBRE_PRIMITIVITY.md#1-a-self-contained-multibase-wick-contraction),
Section 1, with a general constant ordinary covariance. The same proof
applies directly: the independent circular residues are invariant under
Z->Z-t h(Z), and have inverse Jacobian detJ_beta^-1. Ordinary Wick
completion for any symmetric nonsingular C gives

    E_Y exp(tY^T A(z)Y+ell^T Y)
      =det(I-2t C A(z))^-1/2
         exp(ell^T (C^-1-2t A(z))^-1 ell/2).

Here is a direct verification over K. If G(t,ell) denotes the ordinary
source with A(z) fixed, Gaussian Stein gives
D partial_ell G=C ell G. Thus G=g(t)exp(ell^T S ell/2).
Its time equation at ell=0 gives g'/g=tr(A S), while
partial_t log(detD)=-2tr(A S); initial value g(0)=1 gives the displayed
square root. This uses no positive real covariance or analytic integral.
All operations in (M4) are coefficientwise finite at each t-order and
fixed marker multi-degree. At t=0 it is exp(alpha^T beta+xi^T C xi/2),
the ORIGINAL Gaussian source.

In particular this is an equality of the original scalar source, not
a different solution of a scalar annihilator. It obeys the original
Stein identities on every polynomial-time amplitude Q:

    Phi[(partial_Zi-W_i+t partial_Zi P)Q]=0,
    Phi[(partial_Wi-Z_i+t h_i(Z))Q]=0,
    Phi[(partial_Yj-(C^-1Y)_j+t(2A(Z)Y+u(Z))_j)Q]=0. (M5)

Here each operator acts on Q, with its displayed multiplier. These
identities follow coefficientwise from Gaussian integration by parts;
(M4) computes that same original functional.

First beta-derivatives of the root form J_beta^-1. Differentiating
the implicit equations at any higher fixed beta-order expresses the
new derivative as J_beta^-1 times a finite polynomial in lower
derivatives and derivatives of h. At zero source every such root jet
therefore belongs to L. Matrix inverse jets of S_beta, determinant
jets and variable-A jets are rational in the same data. Differentiating
the square root introduces no extra field extension AFTER division
by its undifferentiated value; its logarithmic derivatives are rational
in detD_beta and its derivatives. The same finite-jet statement holds
for the exponential, whose derivatives divided by itself are finite
polynomials in its exponent jets.

It follows from (M4) that Phi[Q]/f lies in L for EVERY original Q.
Conversely Phi[Z^alpha]=zeta^alpha f spans Lf over F by Section 2.
Multiplication by the nonzero actual series f is faithful in K((t)).
This proves both inclusions in (M3), without any tensor-product
specialization or generic-rank assumption.

## 4. Finite algebraic kernel formula

An explicit normalized source algorithm is available. Work temporarily
in rational functions of independent z and t. Put

    J(z)=I-t Dh(z), D(z)=I-2t C A(z),
    S(z)=D(z)^-1 C, m(z)=t S(z)u(z),
    Rhat(z)=t k(z)+t^2 u(z)^T S(z)u(z)/2.

For each W-source direction j define

    X_j=sum_l (J(z)^-1)_(lj) partial_zl,
    b_j=X_j Rhat-tr(J^-1 X_j J)-tr(D^-1 X_j D)/2,
    B_j=X_j+b_j.                                    (M6)

Let H_gamma(m,S) be the finite multivariate Gaussian moment polynomial
partial_xi^gamma exp(m^T xi+xi^T S xi/2)|_(xi=0). Then

    Phi[Z^alpha W^beta Y^gamma]/f
      = [B_1^beta1 ... B_n^betan
                   (z^alpha H_gamma(m,S))] evaluated at zeta. (M7)

The coordinate vector fields X_j commute: their commutator kills each
coordinate beta_i=z_i-t h_i(z), and invertibility of J then makes that
commutator zero. The B_j commute
as well, since b_j=X_j log(exp(Rhat)/(detJ sqrt(detD))). Thus the
ordering in (M7) is immaterial. This logarithmic notation denotes its
displayed rational differential; no logarithm is adjoined to the field.

For each Q, compute its finite linear combination in (M7) and evaluate
in the SELECTED field L. The normalized element is zero exactly when
Phi[Q]=0. This is a finite algebraic characterization, once the
selected field has been presented. It does not supply a uniform
algorithm for selecting a maximal component from an arbitrary
reducible polynomial quotient, nor does it claim that the kernel is
a spatial ideal.

## 5. Differential module and scope

The unique extension delta of partial_t to finite separable L obeys

    delta(zeta)=J^-1 h(zeta),
    tau=delta R-tr(J^-1 delta J)-tr(D^-1 delta D)/2,
    delta(f)=tau f.                                 (M8)

Thus FM is the restriction to F of the rank-one L-connection Lf.
Any original monomial frame gives a rational matrix by reducing
delta(zeta^alpha)+tau zeta^alpha in its F-basis.

Over C, every original marked period is convergent in a neighbourhood
of t=0. Indeed the holomorphic implicit-function theorem applies to
zeta-t h(zeta)=0 because J(0)=I. The determinants have value one,
R is holomorphic there, and the selected square root is holomorphic.
Every normalized source jet in Section 3 is holomorphic there as well.
This is local convergence, not a global entire-function assertion.
It does not make the entire generic connection regular at zero: other
conjugate source branches can escape or have poles there.

There is also an exact algebraic splitting statement for the generic
connection. Take a finite normal extension E/F containing all conjugates
of L, and then a finite extension adjoining square roots of the conjugate
determinants detD. Separability gives

    L tensor_F E = direct_sum_(sigma:L->E) E.

The unique extension of the derivation respects these idempotents, so
the restriction-of-scalars connection splits into rank-one summands
with connection coefficients sigma(tau). In each summand multiplication
by sigma(detJ) sqrt(sigma(detD)) removes the logarithmic determinant
terms, leaving the exponential connection with coefficient delta(sigma R).
Thus after this finite algebraic base change the full marked generic
module is a direct sum of rank-one exponential connections. Repeated
conjugate exponents cause repeated summands and introduce no extension.
The distinguished unit can still fail to be cyclic over F.

No extra degree is contributed by a determinant square root or the
exponential: they are shared by all marked periods as the one
nonzero factor f. The unit alone need not generate the whole
differential module. The one-base example h=1+z^2,A=u=k=0 has unit
rank one and full rank two, as proved in
[the scalar-base field-image note](GAUSSIAN_W_AFFINE_C_ZERO_GENERIC_IMAGE.md#5-unit-rank-can-be-strictly-smaller-than-full-marked-rank).

## 6. Imported global finite-image criterion

The [existing multibase theorem](GAUSSIAN_MULTIBASE_QUADRATIC_FIBRE_PRIMITIVITY.md#finite-actual-images-and-exact-safety-criterion)
already covers arbitrary polynomial A(Z), all n,s, and primitive finite
vacua. With the covariance C used here its conclusion is precisely

    M finite over K[t]
      iff zeta in K[t]^n and detJ=detD=1.             (M9)

In that case R is polynomial, f=exp(R), and M=fK[t]. If P is nonconstant,
its literal original image V is also M; for a constant P=lambda the
exception is V=K exp(lambda t). Safety holds iff (M9) holds and R=0,
and then f=1. This is an imported global theorem, beyond the generic
image assertion (M3).

For completeness the covariance change is harmless over an arbitrary K.
Over a finite constant-field extension choose B with C=BB^T and set
Y=BX. The standard-covariance theorem applies to A'=B^T A B and
u'=B^T u. Its determinant equals det(I-2tCA), and its exponent is
the R in (M2). Coefficient-field extension is faithful, so polynomial
branch conditions, finite generation and safety descend to K.

The safe case consequently has L=F, f=1, and generic image F. Other
algebraic or positive-dimensional components of the root equations
need not disappear. If h(0)=0, for example, the selected root is zero
and L=F even when h has arbitrarily large degree. In this branch
(M9) reduces to nilpotence of Dh(0) and C A(0). Writing u_0=u(0) and
k_0=k(0), safety further reduces to

    k_0=0,
    u_0^T (C A(0))^j C u_0=0 for 0<=j<s.            (M10)

The empty s=0 condition is vacuous. These equalities follow by expanding
the finite inverse of I-2t C A(0) in R. Selected-field collapse by itself
does not imply finite generation: both determinant conditions in (M9)
are still necessary. No classification of global lattices in the
nonfinite cases, or of literal original degree filtrations, is asserted.
