## 1. Exact source identities,including several phases

Let R be ANY commutative Q-algebra,C a fixed nonsingular rational symmetric
covariance,and P_1,...,P_s in R[X]. Write T=(t_1,...,t_s),

    Phi[A]=E_C[A exp(sum_j t_j P_j)],
    nabla_i=partial_i-(C^-1 X)_i+sum_j t_j partial_i P_j,
    H=R[T,X]/sum_i nabla_i R[T,X].                    (1)

The time Weyl actions are partial_t_j[A]=[partial_t_j A+P_j A].
The spatial operators commute,and their quotient is the EXACT integration
source,not just the cyclic annihilator of one selected scalar period.
Define the commuting polynomial-amplitude operators

    L_i=(C^-1 X)_i-partial_i-sum_j t_j partial_i P_j.

For every polynomial weight rho and every A in R[T,X],integration by
parts is the exact source equality

    [(partial_i rho)A]=[rho L_i A],
    [(partial^gamma rho)A]=[rho L^gamma A].            (2)

Indeed nabla_i(rho A)=(partial_i rho)A-rho L_i A lies in
the defining relation space. This argument works before applying Phi,
without a domain,convergence,purity,safety or image-kernel premise.
The spatial differentiation is applied to rho only on the left of(2).
The L_i act on EVERYTHING to their right on the right of(2).

For D=deg_X rho and c_alpha=[X^alpha]rho,the finite Taylor identity is

    c_alpha=(1/alpha!) sum_(|beta|<=D-|alpha|)
                 (-X)^beta/beta! * partial^(alpha+beta)rho.

Together with(2),this yields the retained finite recovery operator

    [c_alpha A]=[rho T_(rho,alpha)(A)],
    T_(rho,alpha)(A)=(1/alpha!)
      sum_(|beta|<=D-|alpha|) (-1)^|beta|/beta!
                      L^(alpha+beta)(X^beta A).        (3)

The notation retains rho only to specify D and the requested coefficient;
the displayed operator otherwise depends on the phase and covariance.
The sum is finite. When |alpha|=D,the sharper identity is simply

    [c_alpha A]=[rho L^alpha A/alpha!].                (4)

These are actual UNLOCALIZED source identities. Applying Phi gives the
same identities in R[[T]],so no exact image-kernel computation is needed.

## 2. Every amplitude ideal saturates to its coefficient content

For an arbitrary spatial ideal J in R[X],let I(J) be the ideal of R
generated by all coefficients of all elements of J. Let H_J be the R[T]
span of classes of elements of J,and M_J its actual image. Put M=Phi(H).
Both H_J and M_J are stable under every time Weyl action,since J is an
ideal and the phases are polynomial. Then

    H_J=I(J) H, M_J=I(J) M.                           (5)

The forward inclusion follows by expanding coefficients. For the reverse,
take rho in J and a coefficient c_alpha. Formula(3) writes every
[c_alpha A] as a class represented by rho times a polynomial in T,X,
which belongs to J[T]. Finite sums prove(5). No Noetherian hypothesis or
finite generating set for J is required:each expression uses only finitely
many chosen elements and coefficients. For a principal ideal J=(rho),
I(J) is exactly the finitely generated coefficient ideal I_rho.

In particular,over a FIELD and rho!=0,

    H_(rho)=H, M_(rho)=M.                             (6)

The same holds over any R when rho is PRIMITIVE,I_rho=R. An individual
coefficient need not be a unit;finite content witnesses suffice. For
example rho=a+(1-a)X over Q[a] is primitive although neither displayed
coefficient is a unit. Its exact recovery is

    [A]=[rho*(A+L((1-X)A))].                          (7)

Do not replace(6) by D_T[rho]=H or D_T Phi[rho]=M. Multiplication by ALL
original spatial amplitudes is part of H_(rho). For P=Z in a Witt pair,
rho=Z has scalar period0,but Phi[ZW]=1 and the full weighted image is
K[t]. A zero chosen scalar period does not mean its amplitude ideal has
zero image.

## 3. Exact scalar normalization over arbitrary rings

For one phase define

    N(P)={a in R : a M subset R[t]},
    N(P,rho)={a in R : a M_(rho) subset R[t]}.

These are ideals of R,and(5) gives the exact ORDINARY equality

    N(P,rho)=(N(P):I_rho).                            (8)

In particular the rho-weighted original amplitude family is safe iff
I_rho is contained in N(P). Primitive weights preserve safety and the
entire normalization ideal over EVERY R. This retains coefficient torsion;
it is not a radical replacement or a field-point criterion.

Primitivity cannot be dropped over rings. In
R=Q[a,e]/(a*e,e^2),take P=aX^2 and rho=e. Then every rho-weighted
positive moment vanishes because e*a=0,whereas the original unit period
(1-2at)^(-1/2) is not polynomial:its reduction modulo(e) is not polynomial
over the domain Q[a]. Here N(P)=(e),I_rho=(e),and(8) is all of R.
Nilpotent weight content by itself is not enough;the annihilation
relation e*a=0 is essential. Normalization of the weighted measure by
E[rho] is not attempted when that scalar is not a unit.

## 4. Original marker filtration and affine cutoff costs

For one phase put d_P=deg P and delta=max(1,d_P-1). Let F_d be the R[t]

same span with the extra factor rho;the degree d excludes rho. Each L_i

    deg_t T_(rho,alpha)(A)<=D,

    I_rho F_d subset F^rho_(d+(delta+1)D),
    F_d^rho subset F_(d+D).                          (10)

    F_d subset F^rho_(d+delta D),

## 5. Strong radicals are weight invariant;purity is not

For a field K,write lambda_rho(A)=E_C[rho A],without requiring a nonzero
mass. With the classical definition

    sr(ker lambda)={P:for EVERY A,lambda(A P^m)=0 for m>>0},

the full original-image equality gives,for EVERY rho!=0,

    sr(ker lambda_rho)=sr(ker E_C).                    (13)

This is equality of actual Gaussian safety loci in ALL dimensions/degrees.
The all-positive-purity loci,or weak radicals,need not agree. The published
[van den Essen--Schoone,JPAA229(2025),107847](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2024.107847)
Definitions1--4 distinguish radical,strong radical and weak radical. Its
Lemma1 and full proof show that a nonzero univariate finite-tail functional
has an MZ kernel exactly when its mass is nonzero. That theorem does NOT
say finite-tail moments are all zero,and it is not a multivariate Gaussian
purity theorem. No new MZ conjecture is imported here.

For an explicit positive-measure boundary,use two independent standard
real Gaussians and Z=(X+iY)/sqrt(2),W=conjugate Z,so E[ZW]=1. Put

    P=Z, rho=(1+Z)(1+W)=|1+Z|^2,
    lambda^rho(A)=E[rho A]/2.

The mass is2,and the normalized unit period is

    f_rho(t)=lambda^rho(exp(tZ))=1+t/2.                (14)
