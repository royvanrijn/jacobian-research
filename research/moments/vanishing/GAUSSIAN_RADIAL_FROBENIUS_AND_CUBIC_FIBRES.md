# Radial moment obstructions and the relative cubic Gaussian source

Let \(K\) have characteristic zero, let \(Z,W\) be a Witt Gaussian pair,
and let \(Y\) be an independent standard Gaussian. Use \(S=ZW\), so the
balanced original monomials are \(S^iY^j\) and \(E[S^i]=i!\). Write
\(\Phi_P[Q]=E[Qe^{tP}]\) for the period of an original marker and
\(M_P=K[t]\{\Phi_P[Q]\}\).

## 1. A finite list of markers detects tails at prime orders

Suppose the boundary is constant and write
\[
 P=c+R_0,qquad R_0=S^d\bigl(H(Y)+S B(S,Y)\bigr),\qquad
 d\ge1,\quad H\ne0,\quad \operatorname{ord}_0 H\le d.       \tag{1}
\]
At least one of the original markers
\[
 S^sY^j,\qquad 0\le s<d,\quad 0\le j\le\deg H,              \tag{2}
\]
has infinitely many nonzero marked moments against \(R_0\). In particular
\(M_P\) fails to be finitely generated over \(K[t]\), and
\(P\) has no safe constant shift. Every strictly one-sided angular
addition preserves this conclusion. For \(d=1\), if \(H(0)\ne0\) the
single marker \(H(Y)\) suffices. If \(H\) has a simple root at zero,
the original unit marker suffices and \(e^{-ct}\Phi_P[1]\) is nonpolynomial.

### Reduction at a good prime

Take the integral domain over \(\mathbb Z\) generated by the coefficients,
inverting the leading and lowest nonzero coefficients of \(H\). Any
characteristic-zero coefficient field admits such a finitely generated
subdomain. Its spectrum maps dominantly to \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\).
By [Chevalley's theorem](sources/stacks-054K-radial-reductions.html), this
constructible image contains an open subset. In particular, there are
reductions to residue fields at arbitrarily large odd primes \(\ell\).
We work at one such prime, with \(\ell>d\). No coefficient of a Taylor
series divided by its factorial is reduced in this argument.

For \(i\ge\ell\), \(E[S^i]=0\) in residue characteristic \(\ell\). For
\(j\ge\ell\), \(E[Y^j]=0\) there as well. An odd exponent gives zero,
and an even exponent of at least \(\ell+1\) contributes the factor
\(\ell\) in its odd double factorial. Put
\[
 n=\left\lfloor\frac{\ell-1}{d}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 s=\ell-1-dn.
\]
Only the first radial layer contributes to the following equality in
the residue field:
\[
 E[S^sY^j R_0^n]=(\ell-1)!\,E_Y[Y^j H^n].                   \tag{3}
\]
All other terms have \(S\)-exponent at least \(\ell\). This choice of
\(s\) works at every sufficiently large good prime.

Define \(L_\ell(Q)=E_Y[QH^n]\) on the residue polynomial ring. The
ordinary Wick pairing on polynomials modulo \(Y^\ell\) is nondegenerate.
For a polynomial with lowest nonzero term \(aY^k\), \(k<\ell\), pairing
with \(Y^{\ell-1-k}\) gives \(a(\ell-2)!!\ne0\). Thus \(L_\ell\ne0\),
since the lowest degree of \(H^n\) is \(n\operatorname{ord}_0 H\le dn\).

Ordinary Gaussian integration by parts is an identity of integral
moments, so it holds in the residue field too. Apply it to \(QH^{n+1}\).
It gives
\[
 L_\ell\bigl(HQ'+((n+1)H'-YH)Q\bigr)=0.                    \tag{4}
\]
For \(Q=Y^j\) the top term inside \(L_\ell\) is
\(-\operatorname{lc}(H)Y^{j+\deg H+1}\). All other terms have smaller
degree. Thus if \(L_\ell\) vanished on \(1,Y,\ldots,Y^{\deg H}\),
induction would make it zero on every polynomial. This contradicts
the nondegenerate pairing. By (3), at least one marker in (2) has
nonzero residue moment at each good prime. The set of markers is finite
and the orders \(n\) grow without bound, so at least one original
marker has infinitely many nonzero characteristic-zero moments.

For \(d=1\) we have \(n=\ell-1\), \(s=0\). With \(H(0)\ne0\), take the
marker \(H\) and use \(H^\ell=H(0)^\ell\bmod Y^\ell\), giving the
nonzero residue \((\ell-1)!H(0)^\ell\). For \(H=h_1Y+O(Y^2)\),
\(h_1\ne0\), take the unit marker. The resulting residue is
\((\ell-1)!(\ell-2)!!h_1^{\ell-1}\ne0\).

### Consequences for the actual image

By the [invariant boundary reduction](GAUSSIAN_INVARIANT_CORE_BOUNDARY_REDUCTION.md),
a finite full image for constant boundary \(c\) must equal \(e^{ct}K[t]\).
Equation (3) contradicts this for (1). The
[angular invariance theorem](GAUSSIAN_ANGULAR_CORE_FULL_IMAGE_INVARIANCE.md)
preserves that full image for strict one-sided additions. In fact
every marker in (2), and \(H(Y)\) itself, has weight zero, so its literal
period is preserved. The same boundary theorem fixes the only possible
safe shift to \(c\). With a simple root and \(d=1\), the residue
calculation excludes a polynomial unit under the boundary normalization
as well. For \(H(0)\ne0\), (3) with the indicated nonconstant marker
only tests the marked image. This distinction leaves purity of the
unit a separate question there.

For the remaining sextic shape
\(c+aS^3+A_2(Y)S^2+H_4(Y)S\) of constant boundary, \(a\ne0\), this
excludes finite images whenever \(H_4(0)\) or \(H_4'(0)\) is nonzero.
The same proof works at arbitrary radial and ordinary degree and
imposes no restriction on the multiplicities of other roots.

## 2. Constant-boundary cubic radial cores

Take \(R_0=C_3(Y)S^3+A(Y)S^2+H(Y)S\), with \(C_3\ne0\) of degree at
most one and arbitrary polynomials \(A,H\). **Every such core with
constant boundary has nonfinite full original image.** There is no
ordinary degree bound on \(A,H\). Write \(e=\operatorname{ord}_0 C_3\),
so \(e=0\) or \(1\). Section 1 handles nonzero \(H\) of order zero or one,
and it handles \(H=0\) if \(A=0\) or \(\operatorname{ord}_0 A\le2\).

Use the same prime reductions as above, also preserving the nonzero
coefficients of the weighted terms used below. Suppose a positive weight on \((S,Y)\) is minimal
on a face of the support of \(R_0\). Let \((i,j)\) lie on the corresponding
face for its \(n\)-th power and assume both exponents are at most
\(\ell-1\). The marker \(S^{\ell-1-i}Y^{\ell-1-j}\) eliminates every term
with exponent exceeding \((i,j)\) in either coordinate. The only
remaining term is exactly at \((i,j)\): a strict coordinate decrease
would have smaller weight. Its moment is its coefficient times
\((\ell-1)!(\ell-2)!!\), a unit modulo \(\ell\). We choose \(n\) and
\((i,j)\) such that both marker exponents stay bounded as \(\ell\) grows.

For \(H\ne0\) of order \(m\ge3\), write \(j=\operatorname{ord}_0 A\),
with \(j=\infty\) if \(A=0\). The only points that can support the
minimum of a positive weight are \((1,m),(2,j),(3,e)\), omitting an
absent point. For a two-point face use the table. The counts listed
give a term with both exponents \(E=Lq\), where
\(q=\lfloor(\ell-1)/L\rfloor\). Set \(n\) equal to the sum of the counts.
The marker has both exponents \(\ell-1-E<L\), and the binomial
coefficient is a unit since \(n<\ell\). The same last row applies when
\(H=0\) and \(j\ge3\).

| Hypothesis | Points in order | Positive weight on \((S,Y)\) | Counts | \(L\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| \(m+e<2j\), including \(A=0\) | \((1,m),(3,e)\) | \((m-e,2)\) | \((3-e)q,(m-1)q\) | \(3m-e\) |
| \(m+e>2j\), \(j\le2\) | \((1,m),(2,j)\) | \((m-j,1)\) | \((2-j)q,(m-1)q\) | \(2m-j\) |
| \(m+e>2j\), \(j\ge3\), or \(H=0,\ j\ge3\) | \((2,j),(3,e)\) | \((j-e,1)\) | \((3-e)q,(j-2)q\) | \(3j-2e\) |

If \(m+e=2j\), then \(j\ge2\). Set \(w=j-e\ge1\).
The face is \(SY^m f(S/Y^w)\), where
\(f\) is quadratic with nonzero constant and
leading coefficients. Write \(f=a_0+a_1 T+a_2 T^2\) and
\(f^n=\sum_k c_kT^k\) in the residue field. Its coefficients satisfy
\[
 a_0 k c_k=\sum_{i=1}^2 a_i\big((n+1)i-k\big)c_{k-i},         \tag{6}
\]
by \(f(f^n)'=n f'f^n\). Two consecutive zero coefficients force
every later coefficient to vanish until the multiplier \(k\) first
hits the characteristic. The support point for \(c_k\) is
\((n+k,mn-wk)\).

Take \(n=\lfloor (w+1)(\ell-1)/(m+w)\rfloor-1\) and
\(k_0=\lceil(mn-(\ell-1))/w\rceil\). For all sufficiently large primes
the two indices \(k_0,k_0+1\) lie between zero and \(2n\). The first
\(Y\)-slack is less than \(w\), the second less than \(2w\). The first
\(S\)-slack is
\(\lfloor ((w+1)(\ell-1)-(m+w)n)/w\rfloor\).
It is bounded and at least two, and the second is one smaller.
Because \(m+w\ge2(w+1)\), one has \(2n<\ell\). Vanishing of both
coefficients would force \(c_{2n}=0\) by (6), a contradiction. At
\(j=2,e=0\) the two markers are \(S^3,S^2Y^2\).

### A double ordinary root and a critical-section shift

For \(m=2\), put \(J(S,Y)=R_0/S\). Its \(Y\)-derivative has a simple
root at the origin because \(H''(0)\ne0\). Let \(y_*(S)\in SK[[S]]\)
be the unique solution of \(J_Y(S,y_*(S))=0\). If the critical value
\(J(S,y_*(S))\) is nonzero, write \(q\ge1\) for its order and choose
a polynomial \(y_0(S)\) agreeing with \(y_*(S)\bmod S^{q+1}\). In the
coordinate \(U=Y-y_0(S)\), the lowest weight \((2,q)\) terms of the
phase are precisely \(h_2 S U^2\) and \(c_q S^{q+1}\). Both coefficients
are nonzero. The linear \(U\)-term in \(J\) has order at least \(q+1\)
in \(S\), which leaves it strictly above the face. Higher powers of
\(U\) also lie strictly above it.

This polynomial coordinate change only serves to choose original
markers. In characteristic \(\ell\), the two ideals
\((S^\ell,U^\ell)\) and \((S^\ell,Y^\ell)\) are equal, by Frobenius.
Their top class is the same, since
\(S^{\ell-1}U^{\ell-1}=S^{\ell-1}Y^{\ell-1}\) modulo these ideals.
The moments therefore detect the top class in the new coordinates
with the same nonzero factor. Choose \((q+1)k\) copies of \(SU^2\) and
\(k\) copies of \(S^{q+1}\), with
\(k=\lfloor (\ell-1)/(2(q+1))\rfloor\). The resulting exponents both
equal \(E=2(q+1)k\). The marker \(S^{\ell-1-E}U^{\ell-1-E}\), a
polynomial in the original variables, has bounded exponents and detects
the nonzero binomial coefficient at order \((q+2)k<\ell\).

### The exact square and its shifted Gamma moment

If \(J(S,y_*(S))=0\), then \(J,J_Y\) share a root over \(K((S))\).
They have a common factor over \(K(S)\) by the Bézout identity. Since
the characteristic is zero, \(J\) has a repeated factor over \(K(S)\)
in \(Y\). A primitive polynomial for that repeated factor has square
dividing \(J\) in \(K[S,Y]\) by Gauss's lemma. It has positive degree in
\(S\), since its square would otherwise divide \(C_3(Y)\) of degree at
most one. As \(J\) has \(S\)-degree two, its repeated factor is linear
in \(S\). Its leading coefficient must be constant because its square
divides \(C_3(Y)\). After making it monic in \(S\), we have
\[
 R_0=C_3(Y)S(S+B(Y))^2,\qquad B=A/(2C_3)\in K[Y].           \tag{5}
\]
The order condition \(\operatorname{ord}_0 H=2\) and \(H=C_3 B^2\)
force \(e=0\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_0 B=1\). This eliminates the
exact square case when \(e=1\).

Put \(T=S+B(Y)\) so that \(R_0=C_3 T^2(T-B)\). For \(n=(\ell-1)/2\),
only its lowest \(T\)-layer contributes modulo \((S^\ell,Y^\ell)\),
since \(T^\ell=0\) there. The original Gamma functional on the top
shifted power is
\[
 E_S[T^{\ell-1}]=(\ell-1)!\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{B^j}{j!}.
\]
This is the integral binomial identity with coefficients reduced at
the good prime. Hence the marked moment for \(Y^j\) is
\((\ell-1)!(-1)^n L(Y^j)\), where \(D_0=C_3B\),
\(L(Q)=E_Y[Q D_0^n \mathcal E_\ell(B)]\) and
\(\mathcal E_\ell(v)=\sum_{k<\ell}v^k/k!\). The product inside this
functional has lowest degree \(n<\ell\) with nonzero coefficient.
Thus \(L\) is nonzero by the same truncated Wick pairing as before.

Apply the ordinary Stein identity to \(Q D_0^{n+1}\mathcal E_\ell(B)\).
The last term in the derivative of the truncated exponential is
killed by \(D_0^{n+1}\), since \(D_0(0)=B(0)=0\) and \(Y^\ell=0\)
in the pairing.
The resulting recurrence is
\[
 L\bigl(D_0Q'+((n+1)D_0'+D_0(B'-Y))Q\bigr)=0.            \tag{6a}
\]
The polynomial \(B'-Y\) is nonzero because its constant coefficient
is \(B'(0)\ne0\). With \(D=\deg(D_0(B'-Y))\), the last product for
\(Q=Y^j\) has strict highest degree \(D+j\). Preserve its leading
coefficient at the good primes. If \(L\) vanished on all
\(1,\ldots,Y^{D-1}\), (6a) would make it vanish on every monomial.
One of these original markers therefore detects a nonzero residue
moment at each sufficiently large good prime.

In each exclusion the marker list is finite, so the nonzero residue
moments give a fixed original marker with an infinite tail. Constant
boundary and strict one-sided additions are handled as in Section 1.

### Safety for cubic radial cores and through original degree seven

For \(P_0=C_3(Y)S^3+A(Y)S^2+H(Y)S+G_0(Y)\) with \(\deg C_3\le1\)
and arbitrary \(A,H,G_0\), safety forces zero core, including arbitrary
strictly one-sided additions. Indeed safety implies finite full image
and zero boundary by the invariant boundary theorem, so \(G_0=0\).
The constant-boundary exclusion above handles \(C_3\ne0\). The
[quadratic radial theorem](GAUSSIAN_QUADRATIC_RADIAL_UNRESTRICTED_FINITE_IMAGES.md)
handles \(C_3=0\). Conversely strict angular balance makes zero core
safe with primitive unit. This includes every invariant core of
original degree at most seven, because \(S\) has degree two. Thus
safety implies purity throughout that degree range, retaining every
original marker. For the full finite-image problem in original degree six,
the remaining case has \(a\ne0\), affine boundary \(c+bY\) with
\(b\ne0\), and \(3\le\deg H\le4\) by the
[quadratic ordinary-fibre theorem](GAUSSIAN_VARIABLE_RADIAL_QUADRATIC_FIBRE_FINITE_IMAGES.md).

## 3. The full relative source of a cubic radial term

The endpoint mechanism has the classical incomplete Airy/Scorer model.
For a fixed endpoint x, integration by parts gives
I_(xi,xi)+xi I=-exp(x^3/3+xi x) for
I=integral_x^decay exp(U^3/3+xi U)dU. At x=0 along the negative real
ray this is I=-pi Hi(-xi), with Hi the classical Scorer function; see
[Gil--Segura--Temme, equations(1.3)-(1.4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0109187v1).
The inhomogeneous Airy equation and the extra endpoint solution are
therefore established background. The full connection below additionally
tracks the moving endpoint, scalar gauge and original Gaussian seed.

Let \(P=aS^3+A(Y)S^2+H(Y)S+G_0(Y)\), with \(a\ne0\) constant, and
allow a scalar gauge \(-\psi(t)\). Set \(r=1/t\) and
\(G=tG_0-Y^2/2-\psi\). The spatial exponential phase and original measure
are \(t(aS^3+AS^2+HS)-S+G\) and \(dS\,dY\) up to a constant.
In the relative source the primitive for \(D_S\) must vanish at \(S=0\).
In particular the Wick functional descends on the original algebraic
relative source by coefficientwise Gamma and Gaussian integration by
parts. Rational-time amplitudes have a finite Laurent principal part,
so they also give actual formal functional values.

At fixed \(Y,t\), the relative \(S\)-cohomology is free with basis
\(1,S,S^2\) times \(dS\) over \(K(t)[Y]\). The leading coefficient
of the twisted \(S\)-derivative is \(3a t\). A relative primitive
of degree \(j\ge1\) gives a relation whose leading degree is \(j+2\).
This reduces every higher power and leaves the three displayed powers
independent. Absolute primitives can include constants, and give the
rank-two absolute quotient. No spatial coefficient is inverted.

Pass to the cubic time cover with \(\kappa^3=3a/r\). Define
\[
 C=\frac{A}{3a},\qquad U=\kappa(S+C),\qquad x=\kappa C,
 \qquad \xi=\frac{H-r-A^2/(3a)}{r\kappa},
 \qquad \Lambda=G+\frac{2A^3/(27a^2)-A(H-r)/(3a)}{r}.
\]
The phase becomes \(\Lambda+U^3/3+\xi U\), and its value at \(U=x\)
is \(G\). With \(v_0=[dU]\), \(v_1=[U\,dU]\) and the endpoint class
\(e_B=[-(U^2+\xi)dU]\), the full \(Y,t\) connection is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \nabla e_B &= dG\,e_B,\\
 \nabla v_0 &= d\Lambda\,v_0+d\xi\,v_1-dx\,e_B,\\
 \nabla v_1 &= d\Lambda\,v_1-\xi\,d\xi\,v_0-(x\,dx+d\xi)e_B.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, for the twisted derivative in \(U\), the class of the derivative
of \(T(U)\) is \(-T(x)e_B\). In particular \([U^2dU]=-\xi v_0-e_B\).
Differentiating in \(Y,t\) with \(S\) fixed changes \(U\) by an affine
vector field in (U\), say \(W\). Its contribution on \(f(U)dU\) is
the twisted exact derivative of \(Wf\), whose class is
\(-f(x)dx\, e_B\), since \(W(x)=dx\). Evaluation at the endpoint
intertwines the connection with \(d+dG\). These identities give (7),
including the term \(-d\xi\, e_B\) in the last row. The original
unit fibre seed is \(\kappa^{-1}v_0\), with scalar order \(1/3\) in \(r\).
The absolute quotient is the Airy connection after pullback by \(\xi\)
and scalar twist by \(\Lambda\). Its two moving actions on the square
root cover are \(\Lambda\pm(2/3)\xi\sqrt{-\xi}\).

## 4. Uniform descent within each polynomial fibre

The following estimate holds for any polynomial \(L(U)\) of degree
\(d\ge1\) with leading coefficient of absolute value one and any
finite endpoint \(x\). Lift a leftward phase ray from \(w_0=L(x)\),
using finite detours of radius at most one about the critical values.
The lift starts at \(x\) and ends at a decay end. If \(x\) is critical,
choose one ramified inverse branch of the outgoing phase segment.
Then the lifted chain \(\gamma\) can be chosen with
\[
 \int_\gamma |e^{L(U)-L(x)}|\,|dU|\le C_d,                     \tag{8}
\]
uniformly in the other coefficients, the endpoint and critical point
collisions. The estimate allows the usual polynomial weights with a
polynomial loss in the coefficients and \(|x|\).

The curve-length input is the classical Cauchy--Crofton/Bezout bound.
Guth, [Section6, Example2, printed p.1974](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-009-0710-2),
gives exactly length<=pi D in a unit disk for a real algebraic curve
of degree D; translation and scaling give length<=pi D rho. The
fixed-degree inverse-phase arcs below are an application of that bound,
followed by exponential summation. This geometric estimate alone supplies
neither a complete global relative frame nor control of moving Y-chains.

To prove it, break the horizontal phase ray into pieces of unit length.
Choose phase detours on the boundary of a union of tiny discs about
the finitely many critical values, with radii chosen small enough to
keep the total excursions in real part bounded by a constant depending
only on \(d\). There are at most \(d-1\) such discs and each detour is a
bounded concatenation of circular arcs. Each path piece at distance
\(j\) to the left has phase in a disc of fixed radius about \(w_0-j\)
and \(\Re(L-w_0)\le -j+O_d(1)\). For any fixed disc radius \(B\),
factor \(L(U)-w\) at its centre \(w\). The inverse image of that disc
lies in the union of the \(d\) discs of radius \(B^{1/d}\) about its roots.
The inverse of each phase line or circle is a real algebraic curve
of bounded degree. The [curve-length estimate](../../../results/gaussian-boundary/analytic_core.tex)
bounds the length in those discs, independently of the centres.
The inverse lift has bounded multiplicity along each phase segment,
including its integrable ramified start. Summing a constant times
\(e^{-j}\) proves (6). The usual root bound in terms of the coefficients
of \(L(U)-w\) supplies at most a polynomial factor in \(j\) and the
input data for a polynomial weight. This still sums against \(e^{-j}\).

For the cubic normalization one may also use this argument with
leading coefficient \(1/3\), changing only the constant. An incomplete
Airy contour through the endpoint therefore has original absolute
seed integral at most \(C |r|^{1/3}e^{\Re G}\) in a single fibre.
The same estimate relative to the saddle action applies to each of the
two descending arms of an absolute thimble starting at a saddle.
The contour terminology agrees with [DLMF §9.14](https://dlmf.nist.gov/9.14).
The proof of (8) uses no Airy asymptotic expansion.

The single-fibre estimate leaves the global cubic comparison open.
A full relative period can involve varying \(Y\), where two moving
Airy actions meet and where the \(Y\)-chains escape. A fibrewise
bound alone gives no uniform length estimate for those base chains
and no proof of completeness in the moderate solution space. The
remaining cubic problem for invariant sextics has nonconstant affine
boundary \(G_0=c+bY\) with \(b\ne0\). The arithmetic proof decides the
constant-boundary case and therefore suffices for safety in that degree.
