## 1. Output variables and split Gaussian contraction

Let A be any commutative Q-algebra. Use independent output variables
x=(x_1,...,x_n) and circular Gaussian pairs (Z_i,W_i), with

    E(Z^a W^b)=a! if a=b, and0 otherwise.

This is the split Gaussian functional on A[x,Z,W]. After a fixed finite
constant field extension, it is ordinary Wick expectation in 2n independent
standard Gaussian coordinates: Z_i=(Y_i+i V_i)/sqrt(2),
W_i=(Y_i-i V_i)/sqrt(2). The substitution is invertible and preserves
ordinary degree. Its faithful scalar extension loses no identity or
nilpotent coefficient.

For any symbol lambda in A[Z] and polynomial h in A[U],

    E[lambda(Z) h(x+W)]=lambda(partial_x)h(x).             (1)

This is the monomial Taylor identity E[Z^a h(x+W)]=partial_x^a h(x).
The original x is never set to a chosen point in (1).

The polynomial contraction is classical. We reread Zhao,0907.3990v2,
Sections4.1--4.2: equation4.4 and the polynomial expansion/image statements
give the map Ecal(xi^a h(U))=partial_U^a h(U). The latest listed revision
is18 January2010; the HTML body date is not a new version. The retained
Dvorsky lift already uses the product lambda(xi)P(U). No novelty is claimed
for that product construction. The new synthesis below keeps all Gaussian
markers, cutoff degrees, ordinary ideals and coefficient-family consequences.

## 2. A direct polynomial phase for arbitrary constant-coefficient GVC

Let Lambda=lambda(partial_U), with coefficients in A independent of U, and
P in A[U]. Put

    R_x(Z,W)=lambda(Z) P(x+W).                             (2)

For every m>=0 and original multiplier Q in A[U], (1) gives

    E[Q(x+W) R_x^m]=Lambda^m(Q P^m)(x).                   (3)

In particular pure moment identities agree, as polynomial identities in x.
To control **every** Gaussian multiplier, let a,b be multi-indices. Expanding
W=U-x gives the exact reverse identity

    E[Z^a W^b R_x^m]
      =sum_(j<=b) binom(b,j)(-x)^(b-j)
           [partial_U^a Lambda^m(U^j P(U)^m)]_(U=x).       (4)

The derivatives act on the bracketed polynomial in U; they do not act on
the displayed exterior x factors. Constant coefficients are essential in
commuting partial_U^a with Lambda^m.

Every original multiplier in (4) has degree |j|<=|b|<=|a|+|b|. Conversely
Q(x+W) has Gaussian degree at most deg Q. Finite A[x]-linear combinations
then prove, over **every** A:

* Pure GVC nullity is exactly pure Gaussian nullity of R_x over A[x].
* All-original-multiplier eventuality is exactly all-Gaussian-amplitude
  eventuality over A[x].
* For each real C>=0, the original bound m>C*deg Q holds iff the Gaussian
  bound m>C*deg M holds for every Gaussian amplitude M.

No division by x, phase coefficients, a factorial that is not a unit, or a
parameter is used. Over a field K one may equivalently work over K(x);
faithfulness of K[x]->K(x) and finite monomial expansions preserve zeros.
The least failure-multiplier total degree, when failure occurs, is the same
in the two models: either direction turns a bad multiplier into a finite
sum of images of multipliers of no larger degree, at least one of which
must have an infinite nonzero tail.

The Gaussian phase has degree at most r+D if deg lambda<=r and deg P<=D,
in 2n Gaussian coordinates. This does not preserve operator order or
original dimension as an ordinary-Laplacian transfer. Lambda remains the
original operator of order r. Variable-coefficient operators are excluded.

### Full ordinary cutoff ideals agree coefficientwise

Let A be a polynomial coefficient algebra and N>=0 an integer. Let J_N^GVC
be generated by all x coefficients of Lambda^m(U^j P^m) for m>N|j|.
Let J_N^Gauss be generated by all x coefficients of
E[Z^a W^b R_x^m] for m>N(|a|+|b|). Then

    J_N^GVC=J_N^Gauss.                                   (5)

For one inclusion use (4): every contributing original marker is already
forbidden at that m, and output differentiation preserves an ideal generated
by output coefficients. For the other expand (x+W)^j in (3); every Gaussian
marker has degree at most |j|. These are literal coefficient-ideal
inclusions, with no radical replacement. The same argument handles any
specified finite pure prefix. Nilpotents and embedded components are retained.

### The earlier Laurent encoding remains a different differential model

The [Gaussian--Laurent source](CONSTANT_COEFFICIENT_OPERATOR_DECISION.md)
used coefficients Lambda^m(QP^m)/(m!)^2. Exponentiating (2) instead uses
Lambda^m(QP^m)/m!. Their supports, cutoff bounds and ordinary coefficient
ideals agree, since factorials are Q-units. Their actual differential
modules, rationality and singularities need not agree. For Lambda=partial_x,
P=x and Q=1 the original output is m!, so the direct series is 1/(1-t)
whereas the earlier normalized diagonal is exp(t). No rationality,
algebraicity or regularity transfer between these two time normalizations
is claimed. This note supplies a simpler source for support questions,
not a repair of a faulty Laurent formula.

## 3. Full SIC has the same cutoff and ordinary-ideal preservation

For f,g in A[xi,U], allow arbitrary nonseparable dependence. The retained
[translation identity](ALGEBRAIC_SPECIALIZATION_AND_FINITE_FAMILY_DECISION.md)
is

    Ecal(g f^m)(x)=E[g(Z,x+W) f(Z,x+W)^m].                (6)

Set S_x=f(Z,x+W). Conversely

    Z^a W^b=sum_(j<=b) binom(b,j)(-x)^(b-j) Z^a(x+W)^j.   (7)

Original marker xi^a U^j has degree |a|+|j|<=|a|+|b|,
and translating an original marker does not increase its Gaussian degree.
Equations (6)--(7) therefore preserve pure nullity, all-multiplier safety,
**every prescribed C cutoff**, and the least bad-marker total degree.
They also give exactly the analogue of (5) for the full SIC output-coefficient
cutoff ideal, over arbitrary commutative Q-algebras. No rank-one or
separability condition is introduced. If deg f<=D, then deg_(Z,W) S_x<=D;
for bidegree bounds (r,s) one may use D=r+s.

## 4. Closed safe field loci and common cutoffs for GVC and SIC

Let C_G(q,d) be a nonnegative integer supplied theoretically by the
[Gaussian field theorem](FIBERWISE_POLYNOMIAL_HOM_AND_GAUSSIAN_FIELD_UNIFORMITY.md)
for pure-safe phases in q Gaussian variables of degree at most d. It works
over every characteristic-zero field and reduced Q-algebra. Then

    C_GVC(n,r,D)=C_G(2n,r+D),
    C_SIC(n,D)=C_G(2n,D)                                  (8)

are sufficient common integer cutoffs for **all pure-null safe inputs**
in those bounded families. Original marker degree is unchanged. This covers
varying lower-order symbols, zero symbols, all matrix ranks, nonhomogeneous
phases and the full nonseparable SIC coefficient space.

Consequently the pure-null safe loci in the respective finite coefficient
spaces are exactly their closed prescribed-cutoff loci Z_C. Their radical
cutoff ideals stabilize. The full ordinary ideals still need not stabilize.
These assertions also hold relative to any specified coefficient family.

For rational families, compute the ordinary cutoff ideal at the sufficient
integer in (8) by the retained MVLINEARIDEAL20261115 algorithm. Its radical
is the exact field-safe ideal. Search the integers0,...,C and compare
radicals to find the least sufficient integer coefficient. These are
theoretical algorithms, with no computed universal numerical C here.

The same equations represent pure safety on reduced Q-algebras: work over
all prime residue fields and use reducedness coefficientwise in the output
polynomials. Thus the three models now all have closed reduced safe schemes
and fixed-size common field bounds. This is not a positive GVC or SIC
theorem: retained pure-null failures remain outside those safe loci.

### A finite output grid is now valid, with a proved size

Choose finite generators of the split-Gaussian radical safe ideal in the
needed q,d coefficient space, and let M bound their coefficient degrees.
The phase-coefficient maps in (2) or (6) are polynomials in x of degree
at most D. Each substituted safe equation has x-degree at most D*M in
each variable. Hence the fixed grid

    x in {0,1,...,D*M}^n                                  (9)

detects pure safety: an original GVC/SIC input is pure-null and safe iff
every scalar Gaussian phase on that grid is pure-null and safe. The grid
is theoretically computable from the Gaussian ideal and may be enormous;
no grid is enumerated here. A constant field extension converting split
to standard Gaussians is harmless. This does not validate an arbitrary
single output point such as x=0.

More generally, for polynomials h_l(x) over any Q-algebra, with individual
degrees bounded by d_i, their coefficient ideal equals the ideal generated
by h_l(a) on the grid product_i{0,...,d_i}. One inclusion is evaluation;
the other is the multivariate Lagrange formula, whose denominators are
nonzero rational integers. Thus a supplied **ordinary prescribed-cutoff**
ideal has a finite grid realization even with nilpotents. For that statement
the generator degrees and grid can depend on the prescribed cutoff. The
fixed radical-safe grid (9) represents reduced targets, not all nilpotent
thickenings.

## 5. Every genuine counterexample creates a scheme-thickness obstruction

Let K be a characteristic-zero field. In any one of the three models take
a fixed pure-null failure f and a fixed bad marker g of degree delta>=1.
Scale the phase by e; for GVC scale P and keep Lambda fixed. Every marked
m-th output coefficient is multiplied by e^m.

For a prescribed integer N, the complete ordinary forbidden coefficient
ideal on this K[e] line is

    I_N=(e^mu_N),
    mu_N=min{m:some monomial marker of degree d>=1 has
                     m>N*d and a nonzero original output}. (10)

Pure marker degree0 contributes nothing. The fixed failure makes the set
nonempty for every N. Thus mu_N is finite and mu_N>=N+1; the exponents
are unbounded, so the ordinary ideals do not stabilize, although every
radical is(e). Over K[e]/e^L every such phase is safe, with the crude bound
m>(L-1)deg Q. Taking an arbitrarily late nonzero original marked moment
with m<L forces arbitrarily large required slopes as L increases.

In particular no coefficient-independent cutoff over **all nonreduced
algebras** exists for any fixed-size family containing a retained failure.
More precisely, over any commutative K-algebra A the phase epsilon*f is
safe exactly when epsilon is nilpotent. The reverse implication follows
from its powers. For the forward implication choose one nonzero original
marked moment beyond the asserted eventual cutoff. A nonzero output
coefficient belongs to K and is a unit in A, forcing epsilon^m=0.
Thus the entire scaling-line safety functor is the filtered union of all
nilpotent thickenings,not a single closed subscheme.
No closed subscheme of the coefficient line represents its entire safety
functor: it would have to contain all thickenings Spec K[e]/e^L, forcing
its defining ideal to lie in intersection_L(e^L)=0, yet nonzero field
points on the line fail. A reduced closed safe scheme is therefore the
appropriate field/reduced result, not the full nonreduced answer.

### Exact GVC thickness from the retained Dvorsky seed

Keep P=(T+C)(AD+BT), Lambda=partial_T(partial_A partial_D-partial_B partial_C)

equation (10) is therefore exactly

    I_N=(e^(N+1)) for every integer N>=0.                 (11)

Over K[e]/e^L the least integer slope is exactly L-1, for every L>=1.
This is a new coefficient line attached to the retained third-order GVC
seed. It does not change the Gaussian quartic line's earlier I_N=(e^N)
or its sharp Artin slope L.

The retained SIC2C4 phase F has bidegree(4,4), total degree8 and rank5.
