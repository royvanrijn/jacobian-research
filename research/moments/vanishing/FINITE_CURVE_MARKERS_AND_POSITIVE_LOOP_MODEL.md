# Finite curve markers and a positive polynomial-loop model

15 November 2026. Internal written proofs with selected exact algebraic
controls; no novelty, independent specialist replay, formal verification or
external review is claimed. See the [checker](verify_finite_curve_markers.py),
[receipt](finite_curve_marker_verification.json) and
[source audit](finite_curve_marker_source_audit.json).

There is an all-degree marked-tail theorem for the entire thin-image
architecture, on both **full** exceptional quotients. If a nonzero pure phase
is P=p(h), where h is the normalized regular real seed and d=deg p, then
one of the fixed markers h,...,h^(d-1) has infinitely many nonzero marked
moments. Thus finding such a pure phase would already disprove MZ; its
marker problem is solved conditionally, without a further search.

Existence of that phase remains **OPEN** for Haar measure. Positivity alone
cannot exclude it: an explicit cubic polynomial admits all vanishing positive
moments for a positive full-support interval measure constructed below. That
measure is abstract; no realization as an exceptional Haar pushforward is
asserted. Both exceptional MZ decisions remain **OPEN**.

## 1. A finite-module obstruction to eventual marked safety

Let A be a commutative unital complex algebra and Lambda:A->C linear.
Assume its bilinear pairing

    (a,b) -> Lambda(ab)

is nondegenerate. Fix P in A. Suppose A is generated by q_1,...,q_r as
a C[P]-module. Then the following are equivalent:

    for every i,Lambda(q_i P^m)=0 for all sufficiently large m;
    for every Q in A,Lambda(Q P^m)=0 for all sufficiently large m;
    P is nilpotent.                                             (1)

Proof. The second assertion implies the first. If the first holds, choose
a common M after all the finitely many cutoffs. Every a in A has an expression

    a=sum_i q_i R_i(P), R_i in C[T].

Expanding the finite polynomials gives Lambda(P^M a)=0 for every a, since
each exponent is at least M. Nondegeneracy gives P^M=0. Nilpotence implies
the second assertion immediately. This proof does not assume pure nullity.

In particular, for nonnilpotent P, at least one of the finitely many q_i
has Lambda(q_i P^m) nonzero for infinitely many integers m. This does not
claim nonvanishing for every m or provide a cutoff or sign.

The same argument can be applied inside a subalgebra B subset A if the
restricted pairing on B is nondegenerate and B is finite over C[P]. A bad
marker obtained in B is a valid marker in A. Nondegeneracy on the ambient
algebra alone would not justify applying the argument inside B.

The finiteness hypothesis is essential. With A=C[z,z^-1] and Lambda the
constant term, the pairing is nondegenerate. P=z is nonnilpotent, all its
positive pure moments vanish, and each fixed Laurent-polynomial marked tail
eventually vanishes. This A is **not** finite over C[z]: finitely many Laurent
generators have a common lower exponent bound, preserved by multiplication
by polynomials in z. This example also explains why (1) does not contradict
the torus MZ theorem.

## 2. A bounded list of markers for every real-seed pure phase

Let h be a nonconstant real-valued regular function on a connected compact
core M carrying positive smooth probability measure of full support. Its
image is a nondegenerate compact interval I. The induced functional ell on
C[h] has nondegenerate pairing: for nonzero q in C[T],

    ell(q(h) bar(q)(h))=integral_M |q(h)|^2 >0.                  (2)

Indeed the image measure has full support I and a nonzero polynomial cannot
vanish on an interval. This argument requires neither a density formula nor
a uniform interval law.

For a nonconstant polynomial p of degree d,

    C[h]=direct_sum_(j=0)^(d-1) C[p(h)] h^j.                    (3)

Existence follows by repeatedly reducing h^d using p(h); independence
follows because the nonzero summands R_j(p(h))h^j have leading degrees
congruent to distinct j modulo d. Thus this is a free module of rank d.

Suppose P=p(h) is nonzero and

    ell(P^m)=0 for every m>=1.                                 (4)

Apply (1) to C[h], using (2)-(3). It is a domain and P is nonnilpotent.
At least one basis marker h^j has an infinite nonzero tail. By (4), the
marker j=0 cannot do so. Therefore

    some j in {1,...,d-1} has
    ell(h^j P^m) !=0 for infinitely many m.                    (5)

In particular d=1 cannot occur. The existential choice of one **fixed** j
in (5) is crucial; it is not a marker varying with m. Its bound is the degree
on the normalized curve, not an original matrix-coordinate degree bound.

An equivalent one-variable conclusion is useful. For any positive probability
measure of full support on a nondegenerate compact real interval, its
integration kernel in C[T] is a Mathieu subspace if and only if it has no
nonzero pure polynomial. One direction is (5). For the other direction,
eventual pure nullity of q implies pure nullity of q^N for sufficiently large
N, hence q^N=0 and q=0. The MZ conclusion is then vacuous.

## 3. Consequences for both complete exceptional quotient rings

The [normalization theorem](COMPLEX_HEIGHT_IMAGES_AND_CAUCHY_RIGIDITY.md)
proves that every nonconstant thin-image phase on C[SL_2/Gamma], for
Gamma=2O or 2I, is P=p(h) with h a **regular self-adjoint** function in
that same ring. Here thin means that its compact complex image has real
dimension at most one, equivalently that P and P^dagger are algebraically
dependent. The hypotheses are normality, unirationality, constant units and
the usual compact conjugation; they hold on these full quotient rings.
The compact core is connected and Haar has positive full support.

Consequently (5) applies to **every** nonzero pure thin-image phase on
either full quotient. Its real seed is obtained in the normalized image
curve and is regular in the original quotient ring; it need not already be
a polynomial in P and P^dagger. All its powers in (5) are therefore legitimate
original markers. No torus-invariance or finite-support assumption is used.
The same reasoning applies to the full original T-invariant subalgebras;
normalization embeds the seed there too. Their global triangle conditions
remain necessary for a proposed seed or phase.

This closes **conditional marked safety** in the thin-image architecture:

    nonzero + pure + thin image => an actual MZ failure.        (6)

It does not prove that any such phase exists. The earlier Cauchy argument
requires its UNSYMMETRIZED image p(I) to separate the plane. The existence
problem for a separating real-seed phase remains open for both Haar models.

Equivalently, any nonzero pure phase that is eventually safe for every
original marker must have image with interior. Known safe pure phases are
consistent with this conclusion. The full quotient is not finite over
C[P]; (1) cannot simply be applied to every two-dimensional-image phase.
Both exceptional MZ questions and the general rank-two case remain open.

For the auxiliary Hopf height tau, the earlier SYMMETRIZED image criterion
is still the relevant even-moment test. A thin tau does not imply that its
associated Hopf phase has thin image. Statement (6) must be applied to
the phase itself, not inferred merely from the height.

## 4. Positivity alone admits a pure polynomial loop

Set I=[-1,1] and

    p(t)=t^2-1/2 + i*(t^3-t).                                  (7)

The curve gamma=p(I) is a Jordan loop with only the endpoints identified.
Indeed equality of real parts gives s=+t or -t; in the latter case equality
of imaginary parts requires t^3-t=0. The only distinct pair in I is t=+1,-1.
Also p'(t)=2t+i*(3t^2-1) never vanishes for real t. Thus the quotient of I
identifying endpoints maps homeomorphically onto a simple closed curve.

Write u=x+1/2. The two boundary arcs satisfy

    0<=u<=1, y=+/-sqrt(u)*(1-u).

The bounded Jordan domain D is

    0<u<1, |y|<sqrt(u)*(1-u), x=u-1/2.                         (8)

It contains 0. Its area is

    integral_0^1 2*sqrt(u)*(1-u) du=8/15.                      (9)

By the Riemann mapping theorem choose a conformal phi:disk->D with phi(0)=0.
Caratheodory's Jordan-boundary theorem extends phi to a homeomorphism of
the closures. Push normalized circle measure forward through phi and pull
it back through the endpoint quotient p. Since individual boundary points
have zero circle mass, the common endpoint has zero mass; this defines a
positive nonatomic probability measure mu of full support on I. These
standard conformal facts are checked against
[Beliaev's primary notes, Theorems 2.3.1 and 2.6.6](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~bishop/classes/math627.S25/Beliaev_notes.pdf);
the selected passages and cached source hash are in the source audit.

For every integer m>=1, the mean-value property gives exactly

    integral_I p(t)^m dmu(t)
      =integral_circle phi(zeta)^m |d zeta|/(2*pi)
      =phi(0)^m=0.                                           (10)

The boundary equality follows by taking radii tending to one; phi is
continuous on the closed disk, so convergence is uniform. Thus (7) is a
nonzero cubic pure polynomial for a positive full-support interval measure.
No finite moment computation is used to infer (10).

By (5), at least one of the two fixed markers t,t^2 has infinitely many
nonzero marked moments for this same measure. We do not identify which
marker or claim nonvanishing at every order.

This is an **abstract positive interval model**, not an exceptional Haar
counterexample. In particular mu was defined by a Riemann map, not by the
pushforward of any exhibited regular real function on SU(2)/Gamma or its
sphere quotient. No polynomial density, algebraic density, or Haar realization
is asserted. The model only disproves the proposed general argument
"positive interval measure forbids all nonzero pure complex polynomials."

## 5. The remaining global gate

For the thin-image route, the task is now purely an existence or exclusion
problem: can a regular real exceptional seed h and a complex polynomial p
produce the exact pure Cauchy germ 1/s on the exterior of p(h(M))? If yes,
the finite list (5) already supplies a fixed infinite marked tail. If no,
every nonzero pure phase has image with interior and the full marked problem
must be resolved there. A degree/support/window sweep is not a global answer.

The abstract loop construction shows why the special Haar pushforward law,
and the complete original regularity constraints, must enter any exclusion.
There is no unweighted transfer from an arbitrary interval measure. Retain
the full S_K problem, all global triangle conditions and the separate
height/phase distinction. Both exceptional outcomes remain OPEN; the
universal nullcone classification remains separately closed internally.
