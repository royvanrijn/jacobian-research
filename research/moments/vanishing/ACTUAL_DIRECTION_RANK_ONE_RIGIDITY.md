verifies the finite coefficient criterion, representative full identities,

## 2. Complete rank-one criterion for a moving polynomial phase

Let K=C(t), Phi in K[x], and d=deg_x Phi>=2. Define the vector-space quotient
and actual connection

    H=K[x] / D_Phi(K[x]),      D_Phi(q)=q_x+Phi_x*q,
    nabla[f]=[f_t+Phi_t*f].                                  (1)

The seed v=[1] is nonzero and H has basis 1,x,...,x^(d-2). Indeed a nonzero
D_Phi(q) has degree deg q+d-1, so leading reduction gives existence and
no nonzero exact expression can be supported below degree d-1.

**Theorem.** The cyclic submodule generated by v has rank one if and only
if, after a finite algebraic base extension,

    Phi(t,x)=P(alpha(t)*x+beta(t))+gamma(t),                   (2)

where P is a fixed complex polynomial and alpha is nonzero. In that case

    nabla v=(gamma'-alpha'/alpha)*v.                         (3)

This is an equivalence for the constant seed on the polynomial affine-line
quotient. It does not assert the same criterion for a nonconstant amplitude,
extra punctures, rational-in-x phases or multivariate phases.

### Proof and explicit coefficient test

Rank one means Phi_t-lambda=D_Phi(q) for some lambda in K and q in K[x].
Since deg Phi_t<=d, the leading-degree observation forces deg q<=1.
Write q=A(t)x+B(t). The relation is exactly

    Phi_t=(Ax+B)*Phi_x+(A+lambda).                           (4)

Writing c_d,c_(d-1) for the top two coefficients of Phi, it forces

    A=c_d'/(d*c_d),
    B=(c_(d-1)'-(d-1)*A*c_(d-1))/(d*c_d).                    (5)

Thus rank one has a finite exact test: compute (5) and ask whether
Phi_t-(Ax+B)Phi_x-A is independent of x. No full Krylov matrix is needed.
If it is independent, that residual is lambda; otherwise rank is at least
two, not necessarily maximal.

Choose alpha=c_d^(1/d) and beta=c_(d-1)/(d*alpha^(d-1)) in a finite
extension. Then alpha'/alpha=A and beta'/alpha=B. At fixed
y=alpha*x+beta, equation (4) says that the derivative of
Phi(t,(y-beta)/alpha) is A+lambda, independent of y. Its positive-degree
y coefficients are therefore constants in C. Its constant coefficient
gamma is algebraic over K, and gamma'=A+lambda. This gives (2)--(3).
Conversely substitute (2), with
q=(alpha'/alpha)x+beta'/alpha, into D_Phi(q); the difference from Phi_t
is gamma'-alpha'/alpha. Rank cannot increase or decrease upon a faithful
algebraic field extension, so this proves the equivalence over K. A scalar
normalization added to nabla changes lambda but not the seed rank.

## 3. A direction error can change the rank by an arbitrarily large amount

Take P_d(x)=x^d+x, d>=3. In the scaling family Phi=sP_d(x), the quotient
has rank d-1 and

    nabla_s[x^k]=((d-1)/d)*[x^(k+1)]-(k+1)/(d*s)*[x^k]
       for 0<=k<=d-3.                                     (6)

This follows by reducing D_Phi(x^(k+1)). The first d-1 seed iterates are
triangular in the monomial basis, with leading coefficients ((d-1)/d)^j
for 0<=j<=d-2. Their determinant is

    ((d-1)/d)^((d-1)(d-2)/2) !=0.

So the scaling seed has full rank d-1 in every degree, without invoking
the candidate's root-span bridge.

For the translation family Phi=P_d(x-t), however,

    nabla_t[1]=[-P_d'(x-t)]=[-D_Phi(1)]=0.

Its actual seed rank is one and its periods of dx are constant. Every
frozen translated phase still has scaling rank d-1. Thus a frozen-phase
rank or inverse-root calculation can overestimate the actual rank by an
unbounded amount when applied in the wrong direction.

The rank-one part of the fixed-scale problem also has an unconditional
algebraic answer: q(P,dx)=1 if and only if P is affine-equivalent to a
power in every degree, including decomposable P. Depress P and apply (5)
to Phi=sP: A=1/(ds),B=0, and (4) forces every intermediate nonconstant
coefficient of P to vanish. Conversely a power plus a constant gives
lambda=P(0)-1/(ds). This does not import the candidate's higher-rank
classification or prove its stationary-phase bridge.

## 4. A Gaussian initial phase rules out higher-degree rank-one families

Suppose every coefficient of Phi is regular at zero and

    Phi(0,x)=-x^2/2.                                      (7)

If its actual seed rank is one, then its generic degree is exactly two.
For suppose d>=3 and use (2). Its leading coefficient vanishes at zero,
so val(alpha)=a>0. A bounded beta would make the quadratic coefficient
alpha^2 P''(beta)/2 vanish, contradicting (7). Thus val(beta)=b<0.
The nonzero quadratic initial coefficient requires

    2a+(d-2)b=0.

But the linear coefficient alpha*P'(beta) then has valuation

    a+(d-1)b=d*b/2<0,

contradicting regularity. The added scalar gamma cannot change either
coefficient. Puiseux valuations make this argument valid over the finite
algebraic extension as well.

Consequently, if in addition the formal Gaussian series

    I(t)=E_x exp(Phi(t,x)+x^2/2)

is identically one, the entire rank-one locus is exactly

    Phi=-x^2/2+C(t)*x-C(t)^2/2,      C(0)=0.                (8)

To prove this last assertion write Phi=A x^2+B x+C0 with A(0)=-1/2.
The formal quadratic Gaussian identity gives

    I=exp(C0-B^2/(4A))/sqrt(-2A).

I=1 implies exp(2C0-B^2/(2A))=-2A. Rational-exponential rigidity forces
A=-1/2 and C0=-B^2/2. Here that rigidity is elementary: a rational
derivative has zero finite residues, whereas the logarithmic derivative
of a nonconstant rational function has a nonzero residue at some zero or
pole. With the value one at zero this leaves only a constant rational
function and a zero exponent. This proves (8). Conversely (8) has I=1
by the linear Gaussian generating formula. It is a formal coefficientwise
identity, without convergence assumptions.

## 5. The normalized constant-b family: rank one is completely closed

The [Gaussian shear normal form](GAUSSIAN_SHEAR_NORMAL_FORM_AND_TRANSFERS.md)
reduces every nonzero constant-b linear-T family to

    P=W*(a+cT)+G(Z,T),       a,c constants, c!=0,           (9)

with the complete transformed polynomial G retained. Its exact formal
pure series and phase are

    I=E_T exp(t*G(t*(a+cT),T)),
    Phi=-T^2/2+t*G(t*(a+cT),T).                           (10)

There is no Jacobian factor here, because the circular fixed point is
g=t*(a+cT). This is a polynomial phase satisfying (7).

**Corollary.** Among (9) whose actual constant-seed module has rank one,
all positive pure moments vanish if and only if G=0. Such members are
GMC-safe with sharp uniform cutoff m>deg Q in the normalized coordinates.

Indeed (8) makes F=t*G(t*(a+cT),T) linear in T. Write
G=sum_i Z^i*g_i(T). Its t^(i+1) coefficient is (a+cT)^i*g_i(T), so every
i>=2 term must vanish by degree, g_1 is a constant k, and g_0=pT+q.
Thus

    F=t*(pT+q)+k*t^2*(a+cT),
    linear coefficient B(t)=p*t+k*c*t^2.

The identity constant(F)=-B(t)^2/2 has t^4 coefficient -k^2*c^2/2,
forcing k=0; its t^2 coefficient then forces p=0, and t forces q=0.
Conversely G=0 gives P=W*(a+cT), strictly negative circular weight.
The cutoff is sharp already at a=0,c=1: for Q=Z^(2d) and m=2d,
E(QP^m)=(2d)!*(2d-1)!! is nonzero for every d>=1. Under an earlier
nonlinear normalizing shear, transport the bound with its map degree;
do not retain coefficient one without checking multiplier degrees.

Thus any nontrivial pure-null G in the remaining constant-b problem
would have to generate rank at least two and admit a rational trivial
quotient of its cyclic submodule. This is the actual remaining obstruction.

## 6. Rank is not the same question as a trivial quotient

A rank lower bound alone cannot exclude a constant period. For example
the abstract differential module with independent v0,v1 and

    nabla v0=v1,       nabla v1=v1/t

has cyclic seed rank two but admits the rational horizontal quotient
J(v0)=1,J(v1)=0. Its seed relation is nabla^2 v0-(1/t)nabla v0=0,
whose zeroth coefficient is zero. This is an abstract module example,
not a claimed Gaussian counterexample. It shows why even a correct
fixed-seed rank theorem is not an irreducibility or no-quotient theorem.

The remaining work is to exclude or classify these higher-rank quotients
for the actual Gaussian phase and full amplitude, not to calculate larger
frozen-phase ranks. The new rank-one closure and direction test are
algebraic; prior analytic-review gaps, general mixed-sign classification,
repeated-root branches and least cubic prefixes remain separate.
