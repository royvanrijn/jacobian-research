## Results and scope

Let X have N independent standard real Gaussian coordinates, extended
complex-linearly to polynomial moments. Write q(x)=sum(x_i^2)/2. Norms below
are Hermitian norms on complex affine space; q is still the holomorphic
Gaussian quadratic, not the Hermitian squared norm.

**Coercive cubic exclusion (G3COERCIVE20261114).** Suppose deg P<=3 and
there are c,R>0 such that

    ||grad P(x)|| >= c*||x|| whenever ||x||>=R.       (1)

Some positive pure Gaussian moment of P is nonzero. No claim that mere
properness of P, an isolated affine critical locus, or real coercivity
suffices is made. Condition (1) is required on all complex directions.

For a ternary cubic P=F3+F2+F1+F0 whose projective leading curve has only
ordinary nodes, a sufficient algebraic condition for (1) is

    F2(v)!=0 at every projective singular point [v] of F3. (2)

Here an ordinary node means rank Hess F3(v)=2, with kernel C*v. There
is no finite numerical moment bound asserted for this entire nodal locus.

**Smooth leading exclusion and finite bound (GSMOOTHFINITE20261114).**
Let deg P=D>=3. Suppose the homogeneous leading form F_D satisfies

    grad F_D(v)=0 implies v=0.                      (3)

Equivalently its projective hypersurface is smooth. Set

    mu=(D-1)^N, L=N*(D-2), B=L+2,
    K(N,D)=B*mu*(mu+1)/2+mu.                        (4)

Some moment E(P^m) with 1<=m<=K(N,D) is nonzero. The bound is sufficient;
it is not asserted optimal. In particular smooth-leading ternary cubics
are excluded by their first **188** pure moments, with arbitrary lower
quadratic, linear and constant coefficients. This is a global class of
cubics outside the earlier W-linear frame theorem.

The root-of-unity construction gives a uniform smooth-leading cubic lower
bound2N in N variables. In three variables the subsequent
[certified smooth witness](SMOOTH_CUBIC_FOURTEENTH_MOMENT_WITNESS.md)
gives homogeneous lower14. With lower terms allowed, the subsequent
[certified odd cubic](ODD_CUBIC_TWENTIETH_MOMENT_WITNESS.md) has its
first19 moments zero and20th nonzero, giving the current smooth-leading range

    20 <= optimal prefix <= 94.                    (5)

The two theorems above retain their own class scopes. The separate
[full cubic theorem](TERNARY_CUBIC_GMC_CLOSURE.md) now closes the singular
leading cases and all ternary degree<=3 GMC internally.

## Formal moments really imply an analytic constant period

Work over K=C(t), with polynomial twisted quotient

    H=K[x_1,...,x_N]/sum_i D_i(K[x]),
    D_i=partial_i+t*P_i-x_i,
    nabla[u]=[partial_t u+P*u].                     (6)

This is a vector-space quotient, not a quotient ring by the ordinary
Jacobian ideal. Its generic dimension is finite by holonomicity; under the
tameness hypotheses below it is the sum of affine Milnor numbers.

Formal Wick integration by parts makes

    J_t(u)=E(u*exp(tP))

a well-defined K-linear horizontal functional H -> C((t)). This does
not assert convergence of the original real exponential integral. If all
positive pure moments vanish, J_t(1)=1. On the rational differential
submodule generated by [1],nabla[1],... its values are 1,0,0,..., hence
are rational. At an ordinary t-point extend this linear functional to H;
horizontal continuation preserves its restriction to the seed submodule.
The perfect rapid-decay period pairing and solution representation of
[Hien--Roucairol, Sections 2 and 3](https://arxiv.org/html/0704.1739v1)
then give a flat rapid-decay cycle whose actual period of

    exp(tP-q) dx_1...dx_N                          (7)

is the nonzero constant 1. A normalization constant is immaterial. The
argument requires neither a cyclic seed nor convergence of the Wick series.
It is the same seed-submodule bridge used in the
[earlier one-variable reduction](MIXED_PERIOD_AT_INFINITY.md).

## Uniform tameness and the Gaussian amplitude

Under (1), for large |t| and 0<=lambda<=1,

    ||grad(P-lambda*q/t)|| >= (c-1/|t|)*||x||       (8)

outside the same compact set. Thus all these gradients are proper and all
their critical points remain uniformly bounded. The affine critical sets
are finite: a compact complex affine algebraic set has no positive-dimensional
components. The gradient lower bound is stronger than the usual tameness
criterion. [Sabbah, Section 8, printed page 21](https://perso.pages.math.cnrs.fr/users/claude.sabbah/articles/sabbah_bbases.pdf)
identifies that criterion with a sufficient condition for no vanishing
cycles at infinity. The uniform inequality (8), or its normalized-gradient
flow, gives the same control for this deformation. No escaping critical
points or extra infinity homology may be added to the finite critical blocks.

Choose a generic argument theta for t, so rays from distinct critical values
of P do not meet. Use local vanishing homology at each isolated affine
critical point of P and attach its descending P-tails. Coincident critical
values retain the separate local Milnor homology groups. This is the
Milnor-type decomposition of [Pham, Part I, Sections 1--5](https://numdam.org/item/AST_1985__130__11_0.pdf), not a supposition that P is Morse.

Here is why keeping exp(-q) as an amplitude on these fixed P-cycles is
legitimate. Parameterize a descending tail by

    P(x)=P(a)-exp(-i*theta)*r, r>=r0,
    dx/dr=-exp(-i*theta)*conj(grad P)/||grad P||^2. (9)

In the cubic coercive case, (1) implies d||x||^2/dr<=2/c, hence
||x||^2<=C*(1+r). Therefore |exp(-q)|<=exp(C*(1+r)). Since P has degree
at most three, |P(x)|<=C*(1+||x||^3), and on a tail ||x||>=c1*r^(1/3)
for large r. The real differential of the vector field in (9) is
O(1/||x||), using Hess P=O(1+||x||) and (1). Its transport Jacobian on
an (N-1)-cycle is bounded by exp(C*r^(2/3)); the radial speed contributes
at most a polynomial factor. Thus exp(-|t|r) dominates both amplitude
and tail volume uniformly for sufficiently large |t| on a smaller sector.
These cycles are rapid decay for (7). The uniform tame deformation (8)
identifies their full vanishing homology with that for (7); this is not
just an injective collection of selected cycles.

For (3), compactness of the unit sphere instead gives

    ||grad P(x)|| >= c*||x||^(D-1)                 (10)

outside a compact set. This also gives (8) with the stronger leading power.
Along (9), ||x||^D<=C*(1+r), while the polynomial degree bound gives the
reverse inequality up to constants for large r. The vector-field differential
is O(||x||^(-D))=O(1/r), so tail volumes grow at most polynomially and
the Gaussian amplitude grows at most exp(C*r^(2/D)). For D>2 this is
subexponential in r. Again the fixed P-cycles form a full rapid-decay basis
for (7) for large t. This proves the required tail and infinity control
in every degree covered by (3).

## Degenerate finite critical points: the missing Morse hypothesis

A degenerate isolated critical point must not be replaced by a generic
nondegenerate test. The needed local result is explicitly available in
[Pham, Part II, Section 1.1, printed pages 23--25](https://numdam.org/item/AST_1985__130__11_0.pdf).
For an analytic amplitude on a local vanishing thimble, the fiber density
has convergent power-log expansions r^(alpha+k)*(log r)^j, with alpha>-1;
Laplace transformation gives inverse powers t^(-alpha-k-1) times polynomials
in log t. Pham states the integrability inequality in the footnote on
printed page 25. It applies to the analytic germ exp(-q) dx. Thus every
nonzero local zero-action asymptotic block decays to zero, even when its
Hessian is singular. A compact subanalytic thimble and dominated convergence
also show this decay directly; the power-log statement supplies the formal
block information needed to separate different actions.

After restoring the critical value, each finite block is multiplied by
exp(t*P(a)). The tame finite-critical decomposition supplies all blocks.
Group equal critical values before comparing them; distinct exponential
blocks are independent in the sectorial stationary-phase decomposition.
The zero-exponential block has only strictly decaying power-log terms and
cannot contain a nonzero constant. A constant solution has only its nonzero
zero-exponential block. It therefore cannot be a period of (7), contradicting
the preceding formal bridge. This proves both exclusions. In this use, the
fixed local phase is P, not a parameter-dependent split Morse phase, so
one does not take a nonuniform Morse limit as critical points coalesce.

## The nodal algebraic criterion

Suppose (2) holds but (1) fails. Take x_k unbounded with
||grad P(x_k)||/||x_k|| ->0. A projective subsequence tends to a singular
point [v] of F3; otherwise the quadratic leading gradient is already too
large. Fix a complement to C*v and write x_k=s_k*v+y_k, y_k=o(s_k).
Put H=Hess F3(v). Exact expansion gives

    grad P(s*v+y)=s*H*y+grad F3(y)
                  +s*grad F2(v)+grad F2(y)+grad F1. (11)

H is injective on the chosen complement. Dividing (11) by s and using
y=o(s) first forces y to remain bounded: its quadratic error is
O(||y||^2/|s|)=o(||y||), and cannot balance H*y. A subsequence tends to
y0 satisfying H*y0+grad F2(v)=0. Pair with v using the complex bilinear
pairing. Euler's identity and H*v=0 give 2*F2(v)=0, contradicting (2).
Thus (1) holds and the coercive cubic exclusion applies.

Examples outside the prior W-linear frame are

    F3=z*(x^2+y^2)+x^3, F2=x^2+y^2+z^2;
    F3=x*y*z,           F2=x^2+y^2+z^2.

Their singular points are respectively (0:0:1), and the three coordinate
points; all have Hessian rank two and F2 nonzero. The checker exhausts
all three affine projective patches in each example. For xyz with F2=0,
the gradient vanishes along the coordinate axes, so this coercivity test
correctly fails. This boundary does not assert that xyz is moment-null.

## Finite bound for smooth leading form

Under (3), the N degree-(D-1) partials of F_D are a homogeneous regular
sequence. Their Jacobian quotient has Hilbert series

    (1+s+...+s^(D-2))^N,

so it has dimension mu=(D-1)^N and a homogeneous basis with largest degree
L=N*(D-2). The leading-degree filtered twisted Koszul complex has precisely
that regular sequence as symbol complex. Hence these representatives lift
to a basis of the vector quotient (6). This is not ordinary ideal reduction
of t*grad P-x: the derivative terms in D_i must be retained.

If an input of degree M has leading decomposition NF+sum_i (F_D)_i*h_i,
then in (6)

    [(F_D)_i*h_i]=-[partial_i(P-F_D)*h_i]
                  +(1/t)*[x_i*h_i-partial_i h_i]. (12)

Every new polynomial term has degree at most M-1 for D>=3. At each step
there is at most one extra inverse t. Once degree is below D-1 there
are no Jacobian relations to reduce. Reducing P times a basis element,
whose degree is at most D+L, therefore uses at most

    (D+L)-(D-2)=L+2=B

successive degree decreases. The connection matrix C(t) has entries in
C[t^(-1)] of inverse degree at most B; write C=A/H with H=t^B and
numerator degree at most B. Coefficients depending on F_D are constants
in t. On any smooth leading form one chooses its actual homogeneous
Jacobian basis; this does not discard exceptional coefficient charts.

Let v_j be the column of nabla^j[1]. With H^j*v_j=N_j,

    N_(j+1)=H*N_j'-j*H'*N_j+A*N_j,
    deg N_j<=j*B.                                 (13)

If the cyclic seed rank is s<=mu, cofactors of N_0,...,N_s give a scalar
operator sum_(j=0)^s a_j(t)*partial_t^j annihilating I(t). After multiplying
the j-th cofactor by H^j, every a_j has degree at most

    A_s=B*s*(s+1)/2.                              (14)

Its zeroth coefficient is nonzero: otherwise the minimal seed equation
would have the constant solution, which the period argument excludes.
Pick a nonzero rank minor for the actual seed rank, not a generically
nonzero minor imposed on the whole parameter space.

If the first K positive moments vanished, I=1+O(t^(K+1)). In the scalar
equation the derivative-error terms have valuation at least K+1-s.
For K=A_s+s this is greater than deg a_0, impossible since a_0!=0.
Using s<=mu gives (4), in particular B=5, mu=8 and K=188 for N=D=3.
The coarser bound remains valid although specific computed matrices have
smaller inverse degree. D=2 is deliberately excluded from (12)'s strict
reduction argument; use the separate [quadratic theorem](QUADRATIC_GAUSSIAN.md).

The subsequent [certified algebraic smooth cubic](SMOOTH_CUBIC_FOURTEENTH_MOMENT_WITNESS.md)
has moments1 through13 zero and14th nonzero, improving the ternary lower
bound to14. The root-of-unity construction below remains valid evidence
for its separate all-N lower bound and sixth-moment example.

## Exact prefix traps and the all-N lower bound

Let Y=X^3 for a standard real Gaussian. Odd cumulants vanish. Its even
cumulant kappa_(2r) counts connected Wick pairings on 2r labeled vertices
with three half-edges at each vertex. Every weight is one, and a connected
trivalent multigraph exists for every positive r (two vertices with three
parallel edges for r=1; a cycle plus a perfect matching for r>=2).
Consequently kappa_(2r)>0. This is a combinatorial proof in all r;
the finite receipt merely checks its first five even cases.

Choose c_j with c_j^2=zeta^j, j=0,...,N-1, where zeta is a primitive
N-th root, and put P_N=sum_j c_j*X_j^3. Its leading form is smooth.
Independence adds cumulants, while sum_j c_j^(2r) vanishes for 1<=r<N
and equals N for r=N. Thus

    E(P_N^m)=0 for 1<=m<2N,
    E(P_N^(2N))=N*kappa_(2N)(X^3)>0.              (15)

For N=3 take omega^2+omega+1=0 and P=x^3+omega*y^3+omega^2*z^3.
Direct exact Wick expansion gives five zeros and

    E(P^6)=96,665,400.

This is a finite-prefix false positive, not an all-order counterexample.
