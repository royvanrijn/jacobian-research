# Whole Gaussian integration, polynomial Hom and effective marker cutoffs

16 November 2026. Internal proof and implemented pointwise producer. The
registry is authoritative; independent, formal and external assurance remain
false. This closes the general **pointwise source/Hom implementation** gate
over QQ and simple number fields, in arbitrary dimension and degree. An
additional finite actual-jet certificate makes the **decision** independent
of completeness of the CAS integration relations. It does not complete the
broader Gaussian classification or coefficient-family work.

The [producer](gaussian_source_hom_producer.py) and
[controls](verify_gaussian_source_hom_producer.py) provide the implementation
and literal finite example inputs. The
[receipt](gaussian_source_hom_producer_verification.json) records an earlier
successful internal run. Its generated example bundles are not distributed;
the control script's `--generate` mode creates them, and its default replay
mode requires those generated files first.

## 1. Exact source and the direction of the decision

For a characteristic-zero field K and P in K[X_1,...,X_n], put

    I_P=(D_i+X_i-t P_i, D_t-P) in D_(X,t).

This is the exact rank-one polynomial exponential connection with generator
u: D_i acts on amplitudes by partial_i-X_i+t P_i, and D_t by partial_t+P.
Commutation of these connections and reduction of derivatives give
D_(X,t)/I_P=K[t,X]u, not just a module annihilating a chosen scalar period.
It is an algebraic exponential connection, hence holonomic. Its full top
integration over X is

    H_P=K[t,X]/sum_i(partial_i-X_i+t P_i)K[t,X].            (1)

We compute (1) without inverting t or deleting any amplitude. The retained
[completion/Hom theorem](GAUSSIAN_FORMAL_COMPLETION_AND_POLYNOMIAL_HOM.md)
identifies Hom_Dt(H_P,K[[t]]) with K Phi_P, where

    Phi_P([Q])=E[Q exp(tP)].

Thus Hom_Dt(H_P,K[t]) has dimension zero or one, and

    all-original-marker eventual safety
      iff Hom_Dt(H_P,K[t]) is nonzero.                    (2)

In the nonzero case evaluation at the original unit amplitude at t=0 is
nonzero; divide by that scalar. All generator images then equal their
actual Gaussian periods. No actual-period image kernel is computed.

A cyclic source generated by [1] may omit amplitudes. An arbitrary larger
source may add polynomial quotients: D/[D(D-1)] has polynomial solution1,
although the exact constant-phase source D/(D-1) has none. The present
producer uses the whole exact source. More general covers retaining the
actual map may eventually replace this requirement with a separately
proved finite-jet selection criterion; no such replacement is needed here.

## 2. Full integration presentation, retaining its maps

[Oaku's integration paper](https://www.lab.twcu.ac.jp/oaku/Oaku2013.pdf),
physical pp14-15, gives the whole integration module before Definition4
and distinguishes it from the cyclic integration ideal. The full module
uses the integration b-function, its **maximal integer root** B, every
monomial X^alpha with |alpha|<=B, and all relations obtained from the
adapted Groebner basis. Algorithm3 step5 subsequently extracts a cyclic
ideal; we retain the module **before** that extraction.

Singular4.3.2 `dmodapp.lib:integralModule` implements Fourier transform,
`restrictionModuleEngine` and `restrictionModuleOutput`. We instrument the
installed library to export its integration b-function coefficient
vector, adapted basis, maximal integer root, signed amplitude basis, and
relation matrix **before prune**. Its existing integration computations are
unchanged; an additional initial-membership lift constructs the recurrence
below, and unprojected operators are retained for the original IBP witnesses.
Preloading the original library avoids recursive dependencies replacing the
instrumented procedures. The original and instrumented libraries are hashed.

The output is D_t^r/N with the explicit generator maps

    e_alpha -> [X^alpha],  |alpha|<=B.                     (3)

Singular's inverse Fourier transform D_i -> -X_i gives a raw signed list,
but its `coeffs` module construction ignores the scalar signs of these
monomials. Accordingly the actual coefficient-module maps are the positive
monomials in (3). We retain **both** lists, and check their normalization
against the original Gaussian IBP identities below. All monomials are
retained, including generators whose actual formal period is zero. We
check the entire monomial set, its binomial cardinality, maximal integer
root, and ring variable order.
Completeness of the integration relations and the spanning statement (3)
use the classical integration algorithm and this CAS implementation. They
are **not** independently certified by finite membership identities alone.

For example P=X^4 yields two module generators, [1] and [X]. The first
has a second-order time relation and the second a different first-order
relation. P=X_1 X_2 yields three retained generators. Neither case is
replaced by the unit's scalar annihilator.

## 3. Polynomial Hom needs only ordinary scalar indicial polynomials

Let M=D_t^r/N be a holonomic presentation with relation columns N_j.
For each i, compute syzygies of [N_1,...,N_c,e_i]. A nonzero last entry
gives an annihilator A_i of e_i and explicit LEFT coefficients W_ij with

    A_i e_i=sum_j W_ij N_j.                              (4)

Since D_t e_i is a holonomic cyclic submodule, its annihilator is nonzero;
the exact syzygy algorithm terminates and finds one. We need neither the
whole annihilator nor a minimal operator. A separate exact Weyl-arithmetic
routine verifies (4), retaining the original relation columns and witnesses.

Write a nonzero A=sum c_ab t^a D_t^b and let w=max(a-b). Define

    c_A(z)=sum_(a-b=w) c_ab (z)_b.                        (5)

The falling factorials in (5) have distinct degrees, so c_A is nonzero.
If f is a nonzero polynomial of degree m, the coefficient of t^(m+w)
in Af is its leading coefficient times c_A(m). When m+w<0, all those
falling factorials vanish and the same conclusion c_A(m)=0 still holds.
Every polynomial solution therefore has degree among the nonnegative
integer roots of c_A. If there are no such roots it is zero.

Over QQ these roots are computed exactly. In a simple number field
QQ[a]/(g), take Res_a(g,c_A(z)), a nonzero rational norm polynomial.
Its nonnegative integer roots give a valid finite bound (indeed the same
rational roots: a rational specialization has zero norm iff its value in
the field is zero). Retain (5), the norm, roots and degree bound.

Use every monomial t^k up to the bound for the unknown image of each e_i,
then impose **all** original relation columns. Exact field row reduction
computes the whole Hom space. It is complete because (4)-(5) bound every
possible map, not because a finite ansatz happened to find a solution.
Cross-relations between generator values are essential.

This is the classical one-variable infinity-indicial method, also recalled
at the start of Section2 of
[Oaku--Takayama--Tsai2001](https://www.lab.twcu.ac.jp/oaku/OakuTakayamaTsai2001.pdf).
Proposition2.3/Algorithm2.4 provide the more general b-function approach.
We do not implement general Tsai--Walther Hom/duality. Singular's `polSol`
attempted to combine extension fields and failed on our number-field
control; the explicit method (4)-(5) avoids that implementation limitation.
No novelty is claimed for scalar indicial degree bounds or polynomial Hom.

## 4. A checked recurrence gives numerical bounds for EVERY marker

Let b be the computed integration b-function. Its coefficient-vector
construction places b(sum X_i D_i) in the initial ideal of the Fourier
source. Lift this membership to the initial generators. Retain only the
weight-homogeneous part of each coefficient having degree opposite to
its corresponding adapted generator. Replacing initial generators by
the full adapted generators gives an operator with leading part b.
Inverse Fourier transform yields

    L=b(Theta)+R in I_P,  Theta=-sum_i D_i X_i,
    R=sum c_(u,k,v,j) X^u t^k D_X^v D_t^j,
    |v|-|u|>=1.                                         (6)

We retain an explicit LEFT expression of L in the **original** generators
of I_P and verify it by exact multivariate Weyl normal ordering. We also
reconstruct b(Theta), check the remainder, and check every degree drop.
Thus the cutoff step has checked operator identities, not only CAS zero
flags. The adapted-basis and b-function computations remain CAS inputs.

Put F_alpha=E[X^alpha exp(tP)]. Integration by parts in (6) gives

    b(|alpha|) F_alpha
      = -sum c (-1)^|v| (alpha+u)_v
                    t^k D_t^j F_(alpha+u-v).             (7)

Terms with alpha+u not >=v vanish. Every surviving term lowers original
marker degree by r=|v|-|u|. All integer roots of b are <=B. Once (2)
holds, the normalized finite Hom gives the actual F_alpha for every
|alpha|<=B.

Choose the computable rational numbers

    C=max(0, max_R (k-j)/(|v|-|u|)),
    E=max(0, max_(|alpha|<=B) (deg_t F_alpha-C|alpha|)).

Induction in (7) proves deg_t F_alpha<=C|alpha|+E. Hence for EVERY original
polynomial marker Q,

    E[Q P^m]=0 for every integer m>C deg(Q)+E.             (8)

Zero periods are assigned degree-1, harmless for these sufficient bounds.
If the normalized unit period is1, all positive pure moments vanish.
Replace C by max(C,deg F_alpha/|alpha| for 0<|alpha|<=B); the intercept
is then unnecessary. The producer reports this actual pure test only
after a nonzero whole-source polynomial Hom has been found. A zero Hom
does **not** decide pure nullity: pure unsafe phases exist.

For P=(X_1+iX_2)^3 the retained run returns safety and the sharp sufficient
bound m>deg(Q)/3. Over QQ[a]/(a^4+1), P=X_1+a^2 X_2 gives m>deg(Q).
These are controls of the general algorithm, not its scope restriction.

## 5. A finite ACTUAL-period certificate removes the completeness dependency

The implementation additionally retains each unprojected relation used
by the integration engine. For a relation column N_j, form its original
operator O_j=sum_i N_ij(t,D_t) X^alpha_i. The inverse-Fourier unprojected
operator K_j differs from O_j by left spatial derivatives:

    K_j-O_j=sum_l D_l Q_lj.

We obtain Q_lj by the exact identity X^a D_l=D_l X^a-a_l X^(a-e_l).
Apply Q_lj to the exponential connection and change its sign to obtain
polynomial amplitudes C_lj. Direct polynomial arithmetic verifies

    O_j(t,partial_t+P,X)1=sum_l nabla_l C_lj.              (9)

It is (9), not a CAS assertion that K_j belongs to I_P, which certifies
the original amplitude relation. For every coordinate annihilator A_i,
combine these primitives with its time-operator witnesses, or simply
replay the resulting polynomial identity:

    A_i(t,partial_t+P)X^alpha_i=sum_l nabla_l B_li.         (10)

Every primitive in (9)-(10) is retained and checked over the exact field.
Together with the original identity (6), these are all-amplitude
certificates even if the presentation has more relations missing than
the classical algorithm promises. Equation (6) proves that the base
monomials cover every class of H_P by induction on marker degree. Equations
(9) prove a surjection of the supplied module onto H_P. Equality of its
kernel with the entire integration relation module need not be certified.

There is a direct finite selector for each ACTUAL base period. For a
nonzero scalar A=sum c_ab t^a D_t^b, put w0=min(a-b) and

    c_origin(z)=sum_(a-b=w0) c_ab (z)_b.                  (11)

If a nonzero formal solution of A has initial order q, then (11) vanishes
at q. This follows from its lowest possible coefficient, with the same
vanishing-falling-factorial convention when q+w0<0. Consequently formal
solutions are determined injectively by their coefficients through the
largest nonnegative integer root R0 of (11). If no such root exists, the
formal solution space is zero. The number-field norm computes these roots
exactly as in Section3.

The infinity bound (5) bounds **every** possible polynomial period. Compute
the actual original coefficients

    [t^k]F_alpha=E[X^alpha P^k]/k!,  0<=k<=R0,

then solve the finite scalar polynomial equations A f=0 with these jet
values. A solution equals the actual formal period: their difference is
a formal solution vanishing beyond the injective jet bound. If none exists,
the actual period is not polynomial. The negative result retains an
explicit left-nullspace functional that vanishes on every polynomial
ansatz column and is nonzero on the actual target jets, together with
the original bad monomial marker. This supplies both positive and
negative certificates. No finite prefix is interpreted without its
certified origin/infinity bounds.

All base periods are polynomial iff every original marker period is
polynomial, by (6)-(7). Thus the original recurrence, (10), exact scalar
indicial bounds and original Wick jets independently certify the safety
decision, **without trusting completeness of the CAS integration relation
matrix**. The full Hom result is retained and required to agree; it is no
longer the sole certificate of a negative decision. The spurious source
D/[D(D-1)] for exp(t) has origin bound1; its polynomial solution1 fails
the actual first-derivative jet. A zero-order-only check would miss this.

This is a finite preservation of the actual map, rather than computing
its full image kernel. The ingredients are classical scalar ODE theory,
Gaussian IBP and the retained integration recurrence. No novelty or
priority is claimed. The coordinating side audit is retained separately
under `cross-programme-connections/`; it prompted the selected-jet
validation layer and is not silently promoted to external assurance.

## 6. Interface and exact limits

The input is `dimension`, sparse `phase_terms`, and optional `field`.
The default field is QQ. A simple number field is specified as
`{"kind":"number_field","minimal_polynomial":"a^2+1"}`; coefficients
are exact rational expressions in a. The minimal polynomial must be
irreducible over QQ. An arbitrary chosen complex root realizes an
isomorphic coefficient field; no numerical root arithmetic is used.

From the repository root, supply `input.json` in the format above; it is
a caller-provided file, and `result.json` is generated. For example,
`{"dimension":1,"phase_terms":[[[4],"1"]]}` specifies the quartic phase.

    .venv/bin/python research/moments/vanishing/gaussian_source_hom_producer.py input.json result.json
    .venv/bin/python research/moments/vanishing/gaussian_source_hom_producer.py input.json result.json --timeout 0

The default stage cap is30seconds; a timeout returns UNKNOWN. Zero removes
the cap. There is no dimension, phase-degree or ansatz-degree cutoff and
no claim of practical efficiency for large sources. CAS errors fail closed.
The result retains the full source, amplitude maps, root threshold,
coordinate witnesses, degree certificates, Hom basis, normalization,
recurrence, original polynomial IBP primitives, origin-jet selectors and
sufficient affine/pure-linear cutoff. The algebraic-part replayer certifies
the actual decision independently of completeness of the CAS integration;
it does not certify that stronger presentation-completeness assertion.

The mathematical route works over effective characteristic-zero fields
with the required exact integration and scalar integer-root operations.
The executed interface supports QQ and simple number fields. It does
not implement transcendental parameter stratification, specialization of
relative Hom, coefficient-ring normalization ideals, all-ring ordinary
ideal producers, least cutoffs or a classification of safe normal forms.
These finite-input computations do not prove coefficient-ring uniformity or a safe-phase classification.

The [Oaku graph polynomial-symbol backend](OAKU_GRAPH_CERTIFICATES_AND_ACTUAL_POLYNOMIAL_DECISION.md)
and its retained torsion witnesses/specialization thresholds stay intact.

continues to credit Sabbah/Maisonobe for the convergent rational-slope
