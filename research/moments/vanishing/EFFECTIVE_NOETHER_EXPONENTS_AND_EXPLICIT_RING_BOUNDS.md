# Effective Noether exponents give explicit ordinary ring bounds

15 November 2026. Internal written deductions, independent review pending.
The [finite-seed theorem](FINITE_SEEDS_AND_FORMAL_OPERATOR_UNIFORMITY.md)
made its nilpotent exponents computable but left them unevaluated. A
classical ordinary radical-power theorem now supplies explicit upper
bounds without computing a seed radical. The
[implemented bound calculator and controls](verify_effective_ring_bounds.py)
evaluate the formulas or retain exact power expressions when the integers
would be too large. This is not an ordinary-ideal or optimal-cutoff solver.
No novelty or formal verification is claimed.

## 1. The classical input and its exact scope

Kollar, *Sharp Effective Nullstellensatz*, JAMS1(4),1988,963--975,
Definition1.4 and Theorem1.5 (printed964--965; retained PDF pages3--4),
bounds an **ordinary power of the radical inside the original homogeneous
ideal**, in a polynomial ring K[x_0,...,x_p]. We use only its case p>=2
and all generator degrees at least3. If the degrees d_1>=...>=d_s>=3,
a sufficient exponent is

    B=product_(i=1)^s d_i                         if s<=p;
    B=(product_(i=1)^(p-1) d_i) d_s              if s>p.       (1)

The original theorem excludes degree2. We do not apply it to the raw
quadratic seed generators. It is an ordinary affine-cone ideal statement,
not just ideal-sheaf containment after saturation. The theorem works over
the rational field directly. The [retained published source](sources/kollar_sharp_effective_nullstellensatz_1988.pdf)
was inspected visually because its mathematical formulas are partly lost
in text extraction. Its projective parameter p is one less than the
number of coefficient variables; the small p=1 case has a different rule
and is excluded from(1).

Here is the useful consequence. Let I=(f_1,...,f_s) be generated by nonzero
homogeneous forms of positive degrees in a polynomial ring in q>=3
variables. Replace a degree1 generator by f_i^3, a degree2 generator by
f_i^2, and keep every generator of degree>=3. Let T be the resulting ideal.
Then

    T subset I,  sqrt(T)=sqrt(I),

and all modified generator degrees are at least3. If d bounds those degrees,
(1) gives

    (sqrt I)^B subset T subset I,
    B<=d^min(s,q-1).                                        (2)

Using an upper bound on s is harmless. These are containments of ordinary
ideals in the regular coefficient ring, before imposing the pure equations.
There is no Cohen--Macaulay hypothesis on the resulting pure quotient or
on any target coefficient algebra.

## 2. Explicit formal-operator bounds from a field seed

Keep the hypotheses and notation of the
[finite-seed criterion](FINITE_SEEDS_AND_FORMAL_OPERATOR_UNIFORMITY.md):
fixed n,D>=1, first k0 pure field tests and common positive field cutoff C,
both independent of operator order. Set

    q=binomial(n+D,D)+binomial(n+Dk0,Dk0),
    s_bound=sum_(j=1)^k0 binomial(n+Dj,n),
    d=max(4,2k0), t=min(s_bound,q-1), nu_bar=d^t.             (3)

The seed generators coming from Lambda^j(P^j) are homogeneous of total
coefficient degree2j. Only the j=1 generators have degree2; square them.
All later generators already have degree at least4. Their modified maximum
degree is d, and their number is at most s_bound. Thus(2) proves

    J0^nu_bar subset A0                                    (4)

in the finite seed ring. The count q is at least4, so no p=1 exception occurs.

For any proved component-codimension bound h, put

    E=(CD+1)(h nu_bar-1), K=max(k0,E), N=C+E.                (5)

These are now explicit integers, not formulas requiring an unknown
nilradical index. The finite-seed theorem gives all its previous ordinary
identities: the first K pure tests generate the full ordinary pure ideal,
only symbol jets through DK matter for purity, and I_N=I_pure in the
infinite polynomial symbol-coefficient ring. They specialize to every
commutative Q-algebra and arbitrary formal constant-coefficient symbols.
First k0 ordinary tests alone give the affine eventual bound m>C deg Q+E.

This does not verify the field hypotheses for an arbitrary input n,D.
The general calculator labels such input conditional. The two specializations
below have their field hypotheses proved in the retained notes.

## 3. Quadratic phases in every dimension

For D=2,k0=n+1,C=3(n+1), use the known two-jet cylinder bound
h=2binomial(n+2,2). The safe explicit choice

    q_n=binomial(n+2,2)+binomial(3n+2,n),
    nu_bar_n=[2(n+1)]^(q_n-1),
    E_n=[6(n+1)+1][2binomial(n+2,2)nu_bar_n-1],
    K_n=max(n+1,E_n), N_n=3(n+1)+E_n                       (6)

follows from(3), even without using a smaller generator count. Pure
quadratic phases over every Q-algebra, with arbitrary finite or formal
constant-coefficient symbols, satisfy m>N_n deg Q. Their full ordinary
pure ideal is generated by the first K_n pure expressions and is a
cylinder over the symbol jet of order2K_n.

For n=1, formula(6) gives nu_bar=16384,K=1277939,N=1277945.
These are conservative sufficient bounds. The sharper univariate
specialization below applies when there is only one spatial variable.
No huge ideal through this prefix is constructed or suggested as a useful
computation; the formula establishes an explicit general bound.

## 4. Univariate phases in every bounded degree

The elementary univariate field proof has k0=1,C=1 in every phase degree
D>=1. Its seed has q=2(D+1) variables and exactly D+1 quadratic output
generators from Lambda P. Squaring them gives D+1 homogeneous degree4
generators. Since D+1<=q-1, use the first case of(1), obtaining

    nu_bar_D=4^(D+1).                                      (7)

The explicit radical classification gives h=D+1. Consequently

    E_D=(D+1)[(D+1)4^(D+1)-1],
    K_D=E_D, N_D=1+E_D.                                   (8)

For every Q-algebra, every polynomial phase of degree<=D and every
formal constant-coefficient symbol in one variable, the first K_D pure
tests characterize all-order purity. The full ordinary ideal depends
only on symbol jet DK_D, and pure inputs obey m>N_D deg Q. For D=2,
K=573,N=574. The sufficient bounds vary with phase degree; no degree-free
ring cutoff or transfer of sharp field slope1 is claimed.

The calculator implements(8) directly. It does not compute the exact
seed nilpotence index, least ordinary prefix or least mixed slope.

## 5. Ternary Gaussian cubics: an explicit ordinary prefix and cutoff

This application uses the retained
[pure-only94 theorem](RANK_ONE_TWO_BRANCH_GLOBAL94.md) and
[sharp field mixed slope3](TERNARY_CUBIC_GMC_CLOSURE.md), with their
internal analytic review boundary unchanged. In the rational ring of
twenty ternary-cubic coefficients, let A94=(E(P),...,E(P^94)). Each
generator is homogeneous of degree equal to its moment index. Replace
the degree1 generator by its cube and the degree2 generator by its square;
keep the other generators. The maximum degree is94. Formula(2), with
q=20 and s=94, gives

    (sqrt A94)^B subset A94, B=94^19.                       (9)

No cofactor expansion or universal radical calculation is needed.

For a marker of degree delta, coefficient derivatives and symbolic
containment with C=3,D=3,h=20 put each moment in A94 whenever

    m>3 delta+E, E=10(20B-1)=200B-10.                     (10)

This is the same finite-seed argument as before: all points of V(A94)
are pure and have the common field cutoff3. Therefore

    I_pure=(E(P),...,E(P^K)), K=200*94^19-10,
    I_N=I_pure for N=200*94^19-7.                          (11)

Every all-order pure-null ternary cubic over every Q-algebra has this
explicit sufficient mixed cutoff. The first94 ordinary tests alone imply
the affine eventual bound(10), while the large prefix K suffices for
all-order ordinary purity. The field gate94 and least-prefix intervals
are unchanged. This does not identify I_pure with A94, improve the
field gate, compute sharp ring bounds, or expand the huge ideal in(11).
The proof bypasses general Gaussian holonomic field stratification.

## 7. Implemented calculation and checks

The calculator exposes conditional seed bounds and the quadratic,
univariate and ternary specializations. It returns exact base/exponent
data and formulas; it evaluates the power only below a fixed bit budget.
It performs no Groebner, primary-decomposition or period computation.

Exact controls check the homogeneous degrees of small seed outputs,
the degree2 repair, parameter counts, source-dimension exception, known
coordinate-power nilpotence examples, all formula specializations, and
the distinction between projective sheaves and cone ideals. For instance
(u^3,v^3) has empty projective zero set in P1 but ordinary radical exponent5,
so a projective bound1 cannot be substituted for an ordinary one.
The universal proof is the written theorem argument, not an inference
from those controls. Numerical sufficient general bounds are now explicit;
exact indices, optimal cutoffs, expanded ordinary ideals, broader positive
classifications and independent review remain open.
