# Exact full images of W(1+Z)^m Y: rank 2m and a global lattice

Let K be any characteristic-zero field, with one Witt Gaussian pair Z,W
and an independent ordinary Gaussian Y. For an integer m>=1 put

    P_m=W(1+Z)^mY, Phi_m[A]=E[A exp(tP_m)],
    V_m=Phi_m[K[Z,W,Y]], M_m=K[t] V_m,
    R=K[t,t^-1], x=t^2, vartheta=x partial_x=(t/2)partial_t.

The literal original image V_m and its complete spatial kernel are
described in the [literal-kernel theorem](GAUSSIAN_BINOMIAL_MIXED_LITERAL_KERNEL.md).
The present result classifies the polynomial-time image M_m and its
generic actual space; no source-rank upper bound is promoted without
an independent lower bound for actual Wick periods.

For every m>=2, write p=2m-2 and define

    f_m=Phi_m[1]=sum_(q>=0) a_q t^(2q),
    a_q=(2q-1)!!binom(2mq,2q)
       =(2mq)!/[2^q q!(2(m-1)q)!],

    H_m(Z)=(1-(m-1)Z)*((1+Z)^(m-1)-1)/((m-1)Z),
    k_m=Phi_m[H_m]=sum_(q>=0) a_q t^(2q)/(2q+1).

The apparent denominator Z in H_m cancels, so H_m is an explicit
original polynomial of degree m-1 with H_m(0)=1. The frame

    B_m=(1, f_m, vartheta f_m, ..., vartheta^(p-1)f_m, k_m)             (1)

is independent over K(t), and is an R-basis of the full image after
inverting t. In particular the actual generic rank is exactly 2m.
The exact global classification is

    M_m[1/t]=R B_m,
    M_m=(R B_m) intersection K[[t]].                                  (2)

Membership in (2) is a finite principal-part test on Laurent coordinates.
The unit cyclic module has exact rank 2m-2. The primitive k_m adds a
nonsplit rank-one extension; the independent constant line supplies
the remaining dimension. These are statements about actual original
images, not only a spatial quotient or an alternate Gaussian trace.

For m=1, the exact frame is B_1=(1,f_1), the actual generic rank is two,
the unit cyclic rank is two, and (2) holds with that frame.

Over C there is also the precise analytic boundary

    (C(t) M_m) intersection C({t})=C(t),
    M_m intersection C{t}=C[t],                                      (3)

where C({t}) is the field of convergent Laurent germs at zero. Thus
every nonpolynomial element of M_m is divergent, while the literal
image has no nonzero convergent element at all.

## 1. An actual Laurent model and its upper bound

Condition on Y and let z=t(1+z)^mY be the unique formal root with z(0)=0.
Define

    u=(1+z)^-1, q(u)=u^(m-1)(1-u), q(u)=tY,
    J=1-tm(1+z)^(m-1)Y=mu-(m-1),
    F_n=E_Y[u(t,Y)^n/J], n in Z.                                    (4)

The selected root u(0)=1 and J(0)=1 are units, and every coefficient
is polynomial in Y. For n<=0, F_n is the original period
Phi_m[(1+Z)^(-n)]. Positive n are also unambiguous actual conditional
periods: rational base amplitudes regular at Z=0 are coefficientwise
legitimate, since only finitely many base coefficients contract at a
fixed t order. Their exact coefficients are

    F_n=sum_(q>=0)(2q-1)!!binom(2mq-n,2q)t^(2q),                      (5)

using generalized binomial coefficients if necessary.

All original monomials reduce into this Laurent model. Put

    H_(b,j)(Y)=(Y-partial_Y)^bY^j.

Ordinary Y Stein integration, iterated in b, gives the finite amplitude

    Z^aW^bY^j  -> (u^-1-1)^a (u^m/t)^b H_(b,j)(q(u)/t).              (6)

Its period is the corresponding Laurent-polynomial combination of F_n.
Indeed t Phi_m[c F W^bY^j]=Phi_m[F W^(b-1)Y^(j+1)]
-j Phi_m[F W^(b-1)Y^(j-1)], with c=(1+Z)^m. Iteration uses c(0)=1;
it does not introduce an alternate localized functional.

The corresponding phase and volume are

    phi=-u^(2m-2)(1-u)^2/(2t^2), eta=-u^(m-2)du/t.                    (7)

The only puncture is u=0. Its multiplicity in q(u) does not create
additional punctures. The twisted Laurent rank bound can be proved
without importing a cohomology dimension formula. Gaussian Stein on
the selected formal branch gives, for every integer s,

    m F_(s+2m)=(2m-1)F_(s+2m-1)-(m-1)F_(s+2m-2)
                                      +(s+m-1)t^2F_s.                (8)

For example apply the total-u derivative of u^n exp(phi) and use
q'(u)=-u^(m-2)J. Dividing by the unit-coordinate Jacobian converts it
to the ordinary Gaussian identity E[(partial_Y-Y)u^n]=0. Taking
n=s+m-1 gives (8), coefficient by coefficient.

The window

    F_(1-m),F_(2-m),...,F_m                                         (9)

spans all F_n over R. For indices above m, solve (8) for its highest
index. For indices below 1-m, solve for F_s: its coefficient s+m-1
is nonzero. The one resonance at s=1-m remains within (9). Thus the
R-module L_m generated by (9) has at most 2m generators and contains
every original period.

The exact connection also has coefficients in R:

    F_n'=-F_n/t
          +(F_(n+2m-2)-2F_(n+2m-1)+F_(n+2m))/t^3.                   (10)

To justify the derivative directly, u and J depend only on tY, so
t partial_t(u^n/J)=Y partial_Y(u^n/J). Gaussian Stein gives
E[Y partial_Y A]=E[(Y^2-1)A]. Substitute Y=q(u)/t to obtain (10).
There is no contour or moving-source comparison assumption here.
Equations (8),(10) show that L_m is derivative-stable.

## 2. A closed polynomial marker for the primitive

For r=m-1>=1,

    H_m(Z)=(1-rZ)*((1+Z)^r-1)/(rZ)

is polynomial. For every integer n>=0 the exact finite identity is

    [Z^n]H_m(Z)(1+Z)^(mn)=binom(mn,n)/(n+1).                         (11)

Indeed the left side is

    (binom(mn+r,n+1)-binom(mn,n+1))/r
                      -binom(mn+r,n)+binom(mn,n).

Use mn+r-n=r(n+1): the first and third terms cancel; the other two
give binom(mn,n)/(n+1). Direct original Wick contraction then proves
the asserted series for k_m and

    (t k_m)'=f_m, k_m'=(f_m-k_m)/t.                                 (12)

For example H_2=1-Z, H_3=1-3Z/2-Z^2, and
H_4=1-2Z-8Z^2/3-Z^3. This primitive is an actual original marker.

The other distinguished frame element is an actual polynomial-time
period:

    Phi_m[1-tm(1+Z)^(m-1)Y]=1.                                      (13)

The coefficient cancellation in (13) is
m binom(mn-1,n-1)=binom(mn,n) for n>=1. Also every vartheta^j f_m
belongs to M_m, since partial_t Phi_m[A]=Phi_m[P_m A]. Thus the whole
frame (1) lies in M_m and in L_m.

## 3. The unit operator and its exact order

Assume m>=2. Put p=2m-2 and

    C_m=2*m^(2m)/(m-1)^(2m-2),
    Z_m={j/(2m):1<=j<=2m-1, j!=m},
    D_m={j/[2(m-1)]:0<=j<=2m-3, j!=m-1},
    N(q)=product_(z in Z_m)(q-z),
    D(q)=product_(d in D_m)(q-d).

Then deg N=p, deg D=p-1, and the exact factorial ratio is

    a_q/a_(q-1)=C_m N(q)/D(q), q>=1.                               (14)

The sets Z_m and D_m are disjoint, as are their translates modulo
integers. Indeed a common nonzero fractional root would imply
j(m-1)=km, forcing the excluded half-integer. Z_m is contained in
(0,1), and D_m in [0,1); both omit 1/2. The root 0 belongs to D_m.

Consequently f_m obeys the homogeneous order-p equation

    [D(vartheta)-C_m x N(vartheta+1)]f_m=0.                          (15)

There is no constant correction because D(0)=0. Its coefficient of
vartheta^p is -C_m x. For the ordinary derivative partial_t, its leading
coefficient is -C_m t^2(t/2)^p, also a unit of R. This proves the upper bound p for
the unit cyclic rank and makes the R-span of its first p derivatives
stable under differentiation. The next section proves exact minimality.

## 4. Extreme coefficient poles prove actual independence

We prove the entire frame (1) independent at once. Since all frame
series are even, a relation over K(t), after clearing denominators,
splits into its even and odd coefficient parts. A nontrivial relation
would therefore give one with coefficients in K[x].

For l>=0 define the rational shift quotient

    A_l(q)=a_(q-l)/a_q
           =C_m^(-l) product_(i=0)^(l-1) D(q-i)/N(q-i), A_0=1.       (16)

The equality is used initially at sufficiently large integer q, so
there are no negative factorials or undefined sequence terms. A
polynomial-x relation among the frame elements gives, at all large q,

    sum_l A_l(q)[P_l(q-l)+c_l/(2(q-l)+1)]=0,                         (17)

where deg P_l<=p-1 and the sums are finite. The polynomial multiple of
the constant frame element has vanished from those sufficiently high
coefficients. Equation (17) is a rational-function identity: a nonzero
rational function cannot vanish at infinitely many distinct integers
in a characteristic-zero field.

For each l, the k_m summand has a possible simple pole at q=l-1/2.
Every A_i is finite and nonzero at every half-integer: both fractional
root sets in (16) omit 1/2. No other summand has a pole at that point.
Hence (17) forces every c_l=0 individually.

Now let L>=1 be the largest shift with P_L nonzero. A_L has simple,
uncanceled poles at

    q=L-1+z, z in Z_m.

All smaller-shift poles lie below L-1, in their disjoint rational
fraction intervals, so none reaches these points. No numerator D
factor cancels them, by the disjoint fractional root sets. Therefore
P_L(z-1)=0 for the p distinct z in Z_m. Since deg P_L<=p-1, this forces
P_L=0, a contradiction. Repeating removes every positive shift;
then P_0(q)=0 as well. All nonconstant frame coefficients in the
original relation vanish. The remaining polynomial multiple of 1
must vanish too.

This proves independence over K(t) of all 2m actual frame elements.
It proves the exact minimal order p in (15), without invoking an
irreducibility theorem for hypergeometric equations. Combined with
the 2m-generator upper bound (9), it proves actual generic rank 2m
and faithfulness of the Laurent model. The primitive extension is
nonsplit: a splitting would express k_m as a rational combination
of the unit cyclic frame and the independent constant 1, precisely
the relation just excluded.

## 5. Why the generic frame is also the exact global R-frame

An elementary differential-lattice lemma removes possible hidden
finite-point lattice factors. Let L be a finitely generated R-module
with connection, and N a derivative-stable submodule having the same
generic rank, with L torsion-free. Then L/N is finite torsion. Its
nonzero annihilator ideal I is derivative-stable: for a in I,

    a'v=partial_t(av)-a partial_t(v)=0 for every v in L/N.

But R=K[t,t^-1] has no nonzero proper derivative-stable ideal. Multiply
a nonzero Laurent polynomial in I by a power of t to get a polynomial;
successive derivatives give a nonzero constant because char K=0.
Thus I=R and L=N.

Apply the lemma to L=L_m and N=R B_m. Equations (12),(15) and the
constant frame element show N is derivative-stable. Section 4 gives
its full generic rank 2m, and Section 1 shows L has at most that rank.
The inclusion N subset L therefore becomes equality. In particular
the window (9) and the frame (1) are both R-bases; no unproved claim
about a determinant having only origin factors is needed.

Since R B_m subset M_m[1/t] subset L_m, this proves the first part
of (2). For the second, Gaussian Stein gives the original backward
shift

    B_f(h)=(h-h(0)f_m)/t

preserving V_m and M_m. A centered Hermite expansion writes any
Q-E[Q] as the Gaussian divergence of a polynomial vector field X;
Stein against exp(tP_m) gives B_f(Phi_m[Q])=Phi_m[X dot grad P_m].
For polynomial-time coefficients, use
B_f(a h)=a B_f(h)+h(0)(a-a(0))f_m/t.

If h in R B_m is regular at zero, some t^N h is in the K[t]-span of
B_m, hence in M_m. Repeatedly divide by t while the initial coefficient
is zero; the backward shift preserves membership. This puts h in M_m
and proves the global origin-saturated formula (2).

## 6. The m=1 exception is completely classified

Here f_1=sum_q(2q-1)!!t^(2q), u=1-tY, and J=u. Hence

    F_n=E[(1-tY)^(n-1)], F_0=f_1, F_1=1.

The recurrence (8) becomes F_(s+2)=F_(s+1)+s t^2 F_s, so all original
periods reduce over R to 1,f_1. Direct coefficient comparison gives

    t^3 f_1'+(t^2-1)f_1=-1.                                         (18)

These two series are independent over K(t). For a hypothetical rational
relation, clear denominators and split parity to obtain a polynomial-x
relation. For high q it gives sum_l c_l A_l(q)=0 with

    A_l(q)=product_(i=0)^(l-1) 1/[2(q-i)-1].

The largest shift L>=1 has a simple pole at q=L-1/2 absent from all
smaller shifts, so c_L=0; induction then kills every coefficient.
The scalar polynomial remainder vanishes as well. Thus rank is exactly
two. The unit is cyclic of rank two since (18) generates the constant;
differentiating (18) gives its minimal homogeneous order-two equation.
The same R-frame and origin-saturation arguments prove (2) for m=1.

## 7. Exact analytic part

Work over C. Take any element of the generic space and clear its
rational-time denominators to place it in the Laurent frame span.
Multiplying by a further power of t clears all negative time shifts;
this preserves whether the Laurent germ converges.
For sufficiently high even and odd coefficient indices, its coefficients
have the form a_q times a rational function of q: this follows directly
from (16), the polynomial coefficient q^j of vartheta^j f_m, and
the factor 1/(2q+1) of k_m. For m=1 the same assertion uses the shift
quotient in Section 6.

If either rational coefficient function is nonzero, its ratio at
successive integers tends to one. The factorial coefficient quotient
a_(q+1)/a_q is asymptotic to C_m q for m>=2, and to 2q for m=1.
Thus that subsequence has radius zero. Convergence of the Laurent germ
therefore forces both rational tails to be zero. The resulting series
is a finite Laurent polynomial. Frame independence from Section 4
then forces all nonconstant frame coordinates to vanish. The original
element is rational in t. Conversely every rational
function is a convergent Laurent germ and lies in the constant line
of the generic space. This proves the first part of (3).

For an element of M_m, the frame coordinates lie in R. A rational element
can therefore have only its constant coordinate, which belongs to R;
regularity at zero makes it polynomial. All polynomials do lie in M_m
by (13). This proves the second part of (3). Over every characteristic-
zero field the algebraic counterpart is M_m intersection K(t)=K[t].

## Scope

The unrestricted conclusions follow from exact Wick identities,
Laurent descent, coefficient-shift pole separation, and the elementary
differential-lattice lemma. Finite controls may replay these identities
and verify examples, but no generic-rank or global-image conclusion
is inferred from finitely many moments. This is internal proof work;
no independent replay, formal verification, or external review is claimed.

For m=2 the [quartic refinement](GAUSSIAN_QUARTIC_BASELINE_RANK_FOUR.md)
also gives an explicit low-degree cyclic marker and the exact constant-line
projection of the literal original image.
