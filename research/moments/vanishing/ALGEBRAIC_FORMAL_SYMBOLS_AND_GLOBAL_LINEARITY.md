## 1. Formal unit factors preserve every output-zero condition exactly

Let A be any commutative Q-algebra, mu in A[[xi_1,...,xi_n]], and U another
formal symbol with UNIT constant coefficient. For every polynomial f,

    (U mu)(partial)^m f=U(partial)^m mu(partial)^m f.        (1)

Both U(partial) and its formal inverse act locally finitely on polynomials,
and are inverse A-linear maps. Thus the left side is0 iff mu(partial)^m f=0.
This proves exact invariance of EVERY pure test, EVERY individual mixed
output-zero assertion, every sufficient or least cutoff, and least bad-marker
degree, under multiplication of a symbol by a formal unit. No reducedness,
domain or finite-order hypothesis is needed.

**GVCUNITEXACT20261116.** The assertion also holds literally for ordinary
output-coefficient ideals. On polynomials of degree<=L, the matrix of
U(partial)^m in the monomial basis is degree-triangular with diagonal U(0)^m.
Its inverse is the matrix of U(partial)^(-m), and both use only finitely many
unit coefficients. Hence the ideals generated by the two finite coefficient
vectors in(1) are equal, in the coefficient ring with U(0) inverted.
Taking unions over pure or marked tails gives equality of their full ordinary
ideals after the specified coefficient extension. Nilpotents are retained.
This is not an ordinary equality without inverting a nonunit constant.

## 2. Algebraic exponentials have finite derivative spans

Now let K have characteristic zero and let mu in K[[Z_1,...,Z_n]] be algebraic
over K(Z). Choose its specified formal branch. Write L=K(Z,mu), a finite
separable extension of K(Z). Every partial derivative of mu belongs to L,
by differentiating its minimal polynomial; no nonsingularity at the origin
is required. Consequently every derivative of

    A(v,Z)=exp(v mu(Z))                                     (2)

lies in L(v)*A. A field basis of L proves finite derivative span over K(v,Z).
Thus A is D-finite. The factor v makes its formal exponential well-defined
even if mu has a nonzero constant term. Products of D-finite series have
finite derivative spans by taking products of finite derivative bases.

The classical closure input used below is the primary-diagonal theorem for
D-finite FORMAL power series over a characteristic-zero field, with remaining
variables retained. It is stated as Theorem3.2, and the complete-diagonal
version as Theorem3.1, in Chen--Chyzak--Ma--Zhu,
[arXiv2506.22011v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22011v2).
Only closure is imported, not that paper's quantitative order/degree bounds.
The theorem is the classical Lipshitz diagonal result; this primary 2026
version supplies an explicit proof and precise multivariate statement.

## 3. A proper power-series construction of the full GVC joint source

**GVCALGFORMALSOURCE20261116.** For algebraic formal mu and ANY polynomial
phase P in K[x_1,...,x_n], the joint series

    F(t,s,x)=sum_(m,alpha) mu(partial)^m(x^alpha P(x)^m)
                               *t^m s^alpha/((m!)^2 alpha!) (3)

is D-finite in (t,s), over K(x). The output variables x are INDEPENDENT
parameters throughout; no output evaluation at zero is used.

Here is the complete construction. Besides(2) take

    B(u,W,s,x)=exp(u P(x+W)+s dot(x+W)),
    J(R)=product_i sum_(k>=0) k! R_i^k.                     (4)

B is a rank-one polynomial-exponential connection over K(x). The univariate
factorial series j(r)=sum k!r^k is D-finite, since

    r^2 j''+(3r-1)j'+j=0.                                 (5)

No convergence is asserted or needed. Apply primary diagonals to the
product A(v,Z)B(u,W,s,x)J(R), equating for each i the exponents of
Z_i,W_i,R_i to beta_i and retaining that common exponent in rho_i.
The resulting D-finite power series C(v,u,s,rho,x) has coefficients

    [v^m u^l s^alpha rho^beta]C
      =beta! *[Z^beta]mu(Z)^m/m!
              *[W^beta](x+W)^alpha P(x+W)^l/(l! alpha!).   (6)

Let D>=1 bound degP. For fixed (l,alpha), the W polynomial in(6) has degree
at most Dl+|alpha|. Therefore the coefficient of v^m u^l s^alpha in C is a
POLYNOMIAL in rho of total degree at most Dl+|alpha|, uniformly in m.
In particular C(v,u,s,1,...,1,x) is coefficientwise well-defined.

We also justify preservation of holonomicity at this step. Translate
rho=1+q, coefficientwise in (v,u,s). Every coefficient just described is
a polynomial, so translation commutes with polynomial multiplication and
all differentiations and yields an ordinary formal series in (v,u,s,q).
An annihilating holonomic Weyl ideal is carried to one by the affine
Weyl-algebra automorphism rho->1+q. Thus the translated series is holonomic.
Set each q_i=0. This zero section itself is a primary diagonal: introduce
a dummy variable absent from the series and equate its exponent to the
q_i exponent; only exponent0 survives. The diagonal theorem therefore
preserves D-finiteness. This argument uses no evaluation of an unbounded
rho series at1 and no analytic contour through an algebraic branch.

Finally take the primary diagonal v=u and call its common variable t.
Taylor's formula gives

    sum_beta beta! [Z^beta]mu^m [W^beta]f(x+W)
                   =mu(partial)^m f(x),                    (7)

with f=x^alpha P^m. Equations(6)-(7) identify the result EXACTLY with(3),
including both m! factors and every marker normalization. At each coefficient
only mu's jet through order Dm+|alpha| occurs. All formal operations above
are therefore in actual power-series rings with finite coefficient sums.

This proves joint holonomicity, not merely separate holonomicity of each
fixed marked series. It is not a claim that exp(v lambda) is holonomic for
an arbitrary transcendental formal symbol lambda.

## 4. Every algebraic formal divisor has a linear cutoff under eventuality

**GVCALGFORMALLINEAR20261116.** Let lambda=U mu over a characteristic-zero
field, with U a formal unit and mu algebraic formal. Let P be polynomial
with all positive pure outputs zero. If for EVERY fixed polynomial Q,

    lambda(partial)^m(QP^m)=0 for all sufficiently large m,

then there is one integer C such that these outputs vanish for

    m>C degQ.                                              (8)

This holds in every spatial dimension and polynomial phase degree. The
coefficient C depends on the specified pair; no uniform coefficient depending

    (b(E_s)+R)F=0, R=sum_l c_l t^k_l partial_t^j_l

    r_l=|u_l|-|v_l|>=1.                                    (9)

Let B be the largest nonnegative integer root of b. The coefficients of

transcendental unit may multiply an algebraic symbol: its raw joint source
need not be holonomic, but section1 transfers every zero condition exactly.

implication must have no such algebraic generator for its principal formal
