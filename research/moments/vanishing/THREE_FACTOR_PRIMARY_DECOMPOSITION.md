## 1. Removing precisely the origin component

Set

    S = (I:a^infinity) intersect (I:b^infinity) intersect (I:c^infinity).

For an ideal H=(a,b,c), the intersection on the right equals I:H^infinity.
Indeed, membership in all three saturations gives integers r,s,t with
a^r*g,b^s*g,c^t*g in I. Every sufficiently high-degree monomial in H is
divisible by one of these powers, giving g in I:H^infinity. The converse
is immediate.

Of the ten known associated primes, only the origin contains all of H.
Consequently S removes precisely the origin-primary component. It also
equals I:m^infinity, for m=(a,b,c,d,e,f). In particular R/S has no
origin-associated prime and

    T = S/I = H^0_m(R/I)

is the intrinsic finite-length origin torsion.

The exact coordinate-saturation run computes S and both complete ordinary
Hilbert numerators. Subtracting them over denominator (1-t)^6 gives

    H_T(t) = 9t^18 + 15t^19 + 18t^20 + 8t^21
             + 3(t^22+t^23+...+t^30).

Thus length(T)=77 and its highest nonzero degree is30. This agrees with
the earlier origin socle 5t^21+3t^30; the socle dimension8 was not itself
the torsion length.

## 3. A graded separation lemma

Suppose A is a positively graded ring, I is homogeneous, and E is a
graded submodule of A/I concentrated in degrees at most B. If an ideal
L is generated by homogeneous elements of degrees greater than B, then

    E intersect ((I+L)/I) = 0.

Every element of (I+L)/I has homogeneous pieces of degrees greater than B;
the asserted intersection follows directly. There is no asymptotic
search or inferred stabilization in this lemma.

Apply it first to E=D1, B=33, and

    L1=(b^17,c^17,d^34,e^34,f^17).

All five generators have weight34. Hence

    U1 intersect (I1+L1) = I1.                         (1)

The ideal I1+L1 is (b,c,d,e,f)-primary: it is proper and contains a
positive power of every variable, so its quotient is an Artinian local
Q-algebra. Extending this algebra by the Laurent variable a preserves
primaryness. One elementary proof is that the nilradical is the extension
of its maximal ideal and multiplication by any element with nonzero
Laurent constant term has nonzero determinant over Q[a,a^-1].

Contracting back to R gives exactly the displayed axis-A ideal. It is
therefore (b,c,d,e,f)-primary. The two factor-label permutations yield
axis B and C, with the displayed exponents.

## 4. Recovering S and then I

Let V be U intersected with the three axis ideals just constructed.
Clearly I is contained in V. At each minimal prime, the three axis ideals
localize to the unit ideal and U agrees with I. At each diagonal-axis
prime, (1) and its symmetric copies show that V agrees with I. R/S has exactly the nine nonorigin associated primes. If V/S were
nonzero, its associated primes would lie among those nine. But its
localization at each of them is zero by the comparisons just made.
Therefore

    V = S.                                             (2)

More explicitly, all ten primary descriptions exist at this point: V
is an intersection of the six isolated and three axis-primary ideals, so
it is saturated at m. Hence S, the smallest m-saturated ideal containing
I, is contained in V; this justifies writing V/S above. Equivalently one
can compare the localizations of V and S at their possible associated
primes and use that any nonzero Noetherian module has an associated
prime.

Now apply the graded separation lemma to E=T=S/I and B=30. For

    L0=(a^31,b^31,c^31,d^31,e^31,f^31)

it gives S intersect (I+L0)=I. The ideal I+L0 is m-primary, since it is
proper and contains a positive power of every variable. Combining this
identity with (2) proves the full ten-component intersection claimed
above. It is irredundant: each of the ten distinct radicals is a known
associated prime of R/I, and an intersection with a redundant component
could not have that associated-prime set.
