# Ambient coefficient jets and infinity restriction certificates

15 November 2026. Selected latest-source audit and internal deductions;
independent review outstanding. The [registry](../../MATH_STATUS.json) remains
authoritative. This note does **not** close unrestricted Gaussian field
uniformity. It identifies the ambient module that must be restricted and
proves a usable polynomial-degree certificate under regularity and
non-characteristic hypotheses.

## 1. The October 2026 restriction paper: what it supplies

[Nakayama--Takayama,2510.05104v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.05104v2), revised
2 October 2026, was not in the current moment audit. Sections1,2 and7 were read,
including Algorithms1,2,9,10 and the explicit limitations in Remarks2,3.

The paper computes specialization-valid initial ideals and b-functions for
central parameters, but its general restriction algorithm is partial. The
remaining integer-root cases are addressed for specified GKZ/Horn systems
using contiguity isomorphisms. Its described subsets can include arithmetic
conditions on integer roots; they are not automatically a finite Zariski
chart cover with a constant degree bound. Derived restriction complexes retain
an additional unresolved comprehensive adapted-resolution step. None of these
producer algorithms was executed here.

The distinction matters twice for our problem. Gaussian coefficient variables
are differentiated in a joint module; the paper's central parameters are not.
One can translate a coefficient fiber to zero, making the translation values
central parameters, but must retain the original differential directions and
the marked generator. Also, finite isomorphism classes do not bound a marked
generator's polynomial degree: all cyclic modules generated by t^N are the
same module C[t], while the generator degree is N. The derivative
partial_t^N(t^N)=N! proves cyclicity. This is a control indexed by N, not a
counterexample in one fixed Gaussian coefficient family.

## 2. A regular-at-infinity polynomial-degree certificate

The relevant classical source is
[Dimca--Maisonobe--Saito--Torrelli,math/0601664v1](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0601664v1),
Theorem1.1, Sections2.6--2.11 and the proof in Section3. It concerns a
**regular holonomic** module and requires non-characteristic restriction for
both M and its localization M(*H). It gives compatibility of the canonical
V-filtration with restriction. This is a zero-weight coefficient-direction
statement, distinct from the earlier positive-weight restriction source.

Let U be a smooth coefficient chart and M an algebraic holonomic module on
P1_t times U extending the affine period presentation. Work near
H={t=infinity}, with coordinate u=1/t. Suppose:

1. M is regular on this neighborhood;
2. every coefficient fiber is non-characteristic for M and M(*H);
3. a specified algebraic marked section m of M(*H) belongs to
   V^(1-h) M(*H), for a single integer h>=0 throughout the chart;
4. the selected formal scalar series f_a(t) on each fiber is the image of
   m_a under its actual D_t-linear period map.

Then
\[
 \boxed{ f_a(t)\in\mathbb C[t]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
              \deg_t f_a\le h. }                            \tag{1}
\]
Only regularity near infinity is required; finite-time irregular singularities
are allowed. The full fiber image need not consist of polynomials.

Here is the generator-sensitive proof. The restriction theorem gives
m_a in V^(1-h)M_a(*H). Let N_a=D_t m_a be the cyclic submodule on the affine
t-line. If its selected scalar image f_a is polynomial, the map
N_a to C[t] is well-defined: polynomial coefficient differential relations
vanishing formally on a polynomial vanish identically. Localize this map at
infinity and use the cyclic module generated by the germ of m_a there.
Canonical V-filtrations are strict on regular holonomic submodules and
functorial for their morphisms. Equivalently, the germ b-function of m_a is
intrinsic to its cyclic module, since
the defining relation b(u partial_u+1)m_a in V^1D m_a uses only that cyclic
span. Mapping to a scalar preserves this relation. Thus the image belongs to
V^(1-h)O(*H). In the source's decreasing left-module convention,
\[
 V^{1-h}O(*H)=u^{-h}O,
\]
so a polynomial image has degree at most h. This proves (1) even if other
fiber generators have nonpolynomial periods.

On a quasi-compact algebraic chart with an algebraic V-filtration, finitely
many algebraic marked sections admit some common h: use exhaustiveness at
each point of H and take a finite open cover and the largest required h.
A concrete effective certificate still needs the module, non-characteristic
checks and a bound or membership computation; no general producer is run here.

For the retained [uniform-chart theorem](RESONANT_MULTIPLIERS_AND_UNIFORM_CHARTS.md),
this supplies its second hypothesis on any chart where the finitely many
resonant seed periods have such joint modules and certificates. Their h_j
replace the unproved specialization-wise polynomial-degree bounds. The first
hypothesis, a valid source b-function lift, is still required. A finite cover
by these certificates has **not** been proved for the universal Gaussian family.
Pointwise regularity of a selected connection does not establish joint
regularity, non-characteristic restriction, or the marked V-level bound.

## 3. Why the generator and both restrictions matter

A nonvacuous abstract chart is
\[
 f_a(t)=t^N+a\exp(t/(1-t)),\qquad N\ge0.                       \tag{2}
\]
Use the two period coordinates (exp(t/(1-t)),1) and marked section
(a,t^N). Near u=0 their connection matrix is
diag(-1/(u-1)^2,0), ordinary there and independent of a. The coefficient
fibers are non-characteristic; multiplying the marked section by u^N
makes it regular. Thus h=N is valid. Exactly a=0 gives a polynomial fiber,
of degree N. Indeed exp(t/(1-t)) has logarithmic derivative
(1-t)^(-2); a nonzero polynomial has only simple poles in its logarithmic
derivative, so this function is not polynomial. At a=0 the coefficient
derivative is still nonpolynomial. The argument in Section2 deliberately
uses the cyclic submodule of the marked section, not polynomiality of the
whole fiber. This is an abstract holonomic control, not a Gaussian example.

For why both non-characteristic checks are needed, consider the rational
family 1/(1-at), whose pole divisor at infinity is u-a=0. Its conormal at
(u,a)=(0,0) is spanned by du-da and is transverse to a coefficient fiber.
After localizing also along H:u=0, the intersection stratum has conormal
spanned by du and du-da; it contains da. The localization check therefore
fails there. This illustrates the theorem's hypothesis, not failure of its
conclusion for that rational family.

Finally, finite length of a joint holonomic module does not justify evaluating
only its cyclic tail generator. For
\[
 F(t,a)=ae^t+t^N,
\]
the seed partial_t^(N+1)F vanishes at a=0, but
partial_a partial_t^(N+1)F=e^t does not. A D_(a,t)-submodule generated by the
tail need not have zero period image after evaluation. This invalidates a
naive descending-submodule proof of uniform polynomial fiber degrees.

## 4. The correct fixed module for Gaussian safety

Fix Gaussian dimension n and phase degree D>=1. In the full ambient
coefficient space write
\[
 P_c(X)=\sum_{|\beta|\le D}c_\beta X^\beta,\qquad
 F(t,c)=\mathbb E\exp(tP_c(X)).
\]
The polynomial exponential integration construction gives a joint cyclic
holonomic presentation M=D_(t,c)/I with a marked generator m mapping to F.
I need not be the exact annihilator; an annihilating holonomic presentation
suffices. Every coefficient of F is polynomial in c, so all coefficient
derivatives can be evaluated coefficientwise.

For every multi-index alpha, the linear coefficient directions satisfy
\[
 \partial_{c_{e_1}}^{\alpha_1}\cdots
 \partial_{c_{e_n}}^{\alpha_n}F
       =t^{|\alpha|}\mathbb E(X^\alpha e^{tP_c}).              \tag{3}
\]
Every other coefficient derivative also inserts a polynomial multiplier
times a power of t. Now take the ordinary zeroth restriction
\[
 M_a=M/\sum_\beta(c_\beta-a_\beta)M.                          \tag{4}
\]
It is a holonomic D_t-module, and the coefficientwise period map descends
to it. Normal coefficient derivatives are **retained** by (4); it is not
the operation of discarding derivative symbols and substituting coefficients.

The image of this whole restriction lies in C[t] iff P_a is GMC-safe.
One direction follows because every coefficient derivative and time derivative
is a polynomial multiplier period with polynomial t factors. For the other,
(3) makes t^|alpha|F_(X^alpha) polynomial. Each F_(X^alpha) is formal at t=0,
so division by that power removes no infinite tail and it too is polynomial.
At a pure point F(t,a)=1, this image is therefore exactly C[t] iff safe.
Its Weyl closure contains C[t] because it contains1.

This realizes Gaussian safety as a generator-sensitive **whole period-image**
condition on restrictions of one fixed ambient holonomic module. Pointwise
restriction gives finite D_t generators and hence finitely many selected
periods to test, without requiring an exact ambient annihilator. It does not
prove a finite coefficient stratification of those tests or a uniform bound.
The case D=0 is elementary and needs no linear coefficient directions.

## 5. A genuine pure family loses its obstruction under intrinsic derivatives

The retained [quartic family](RANK_ONE_IMAGES_AND_NILPOTENT_CUTOFFS.md) is
\[
 P_e=W(1+eZ)-e(1+3eZ/2+e^2Z^2/2)T^2,
\]
with exact identities
\[
 \mathbb E e^{tP_e}=1,\qquad
 \mathbb E[Ze^{tP_e}]=t/(1-et).                              \tag{5}
\]
All intrinsic e-derivatives of the first series vanish. They see only a
constant seed, even when e!=0 and the phase fails GMC. Introduce an ambient
linear coefficient v before restricting to this pure line. Then
\[
 \left.\partial_v\mathbb E e^{t(P_e+vZ)}\right|_{v=0}
       =t\mathbb E[Ze^{tP_e}]=t^2/(1-et),                    \tag{6}
\]
which is nonpolynomial for every e!=0. Thus differentiating the pure identity
only along the pure locus is not a replacement for the ambient restriction
(4). The exact all-order identities in (5) remain imported from their retained
proof; the new checker only verifies a small transcription prefix.

## 6. The next genuine uniformity gate

The fixed ambient module formulation suggests computing polynomial quotients
of its coefficient restrictions while retaining the original period map and
marked generator jets. A uniform generator-sensitive polynomial Hom/base-change
theorem, with exceptional and irregular fibers controlled, would address the
remaining field-uniformity question. That theorem is **not proved here**.
Central-parameter hypergeometric contiguity alone does not provide it, and
the regular-near-infinity certificate does not cover arbitrary irregular
restrictions. The finite-length shortcut fails for the reason in Section3.

The [checker](verify_coefficient_restriction_certificates.py),
[receipt](coefficient_restriction_verification.json) and

source versions, finite Gaussian jet controls, V-index normalization and

general V-filtration/Hom producer was executed. Global complex-field uniformity,
ordinary nilpotent cutoff geometry and the protected numerical frontiers remain
