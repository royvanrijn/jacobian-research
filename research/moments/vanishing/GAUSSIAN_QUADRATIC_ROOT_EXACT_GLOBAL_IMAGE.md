# An exact global marked image for a quadratic source root

Let K be any field of characteristic zero. Use one Witt pair Z,W with
E[Z^aW^b]=delta_(a,b)a!, and put

    P=W(1+Z^2), Phi[Q]=E[Q exp(tP)],
    O=K[t], F=K(t), T=K[t,t^-1],
    Delta=1-4t^2, R=T[Delta^-1], f=Delta^(-1/2).       (Q1)

The branch of f has value one at zero. Write V=Phi[K[Z,W]] for the
literal image of fixed original markers and M=O V for their full
polynomial-time image. All intersections below use the actual selected
embedding in K((t)).

The exact global result is

    M=(R f+T) intersect K[[t]],
    F M=F f direct_sum F,
    M intersect F=O, V intersect F={0}.              (Q2)

In particular 1 belongs to M but not to V. The full generic rank is two,
whereas the unit-cyclic differential rank is one. The fixed original
kernel and degree filtration also admit exact formulas in Section 4.

This model refutes the rule that the full actual image is obtained by
multiplying f by the ring generated by the selected root and inverse
Jacobian. That ring contains Delta^-1, but M does not (Section 5).
The bounded internal checker supports explicit identities; it does not
prove the unrestricted assertions by a moment prefix. Independent replay,
formal verification and external review are not claimed.

## 1. The full original source and monomial coordinates

The selected root of z=t(1+z^2)+B and its Jacobian are

    zeta_B=(1-sqrt(Delta-4tB))/(2t),
    J_B=sqrt(Delta-4tB).

The exact original auxiliary source is

    E exp(tP+AZ+BW)=exp(A zeta_B)/J_B.               (Q3)

This is the specialization of
[the original c=0 source](GAUSSIAN_W_AFFINE_C_ZERO_GENERIC_IMAGE.md#2-full-original-auxiliary-source-identity),
equation (G5). It follows directly by circular Wick contraction and
formal residue substitution; every fixed source coefficient is finite.
The general selected-field setting is in
[the multibase generic-image theorem](GAUSSIAN_MULTIBASE_QUADRATIC_FIBRE_GENERIC_IMAGE.md).

Let (x)_fall b=x(x-1)...(x-b+1), with value one for b=0. Differentiating
(Q3) in A and B gives, for all a,b>=0,

    Phi[Z^a W^b]
      =(2t)^(-a)(-4t)^b
          sum_(j=0)^a (-1)^j binom(a,j)
              ((j-1)/2)_fall b Delta^((j-1)/2-b)
      =A_(a,b) f+B_(a,b).                            (Q4)

The even and odd j terms define the UNIQUE rational coordinates A,B,
because f is not rational. Indeed f^2=Delta^-1 has simple poles at the
two distinct points t=1/2,-1/2, whereas a rational square has even orders.

All even-j terms lie in Rf. If j=2k+1, its falling factorial vanishes
unless k>=b. A surviving odd-j term is a constant times

    t^(b-a) Delta^(k-b).

This is Laurent polynomial in t with highest exponent at most

    b-a+2(k-b)=2k-a-b<=-b-1.                         (Q5)

Thus B_(a,b) is a STRICTLY NEGATIVE Laurent polynomial, or zero.
This proves the forward inclusion in (Q2): every fixed marker lies
in Rf+T and is regular at zero, and multiplying by polynomials in t
preserves these two properties.

For reference, direct original Wick contraction gives the independent
coefficient formula

    [t^n]Phi[Z^aW^b]
      =(n+b)!/n! binom(n,(n+b-a)/2),                 (Q6)

where the value is zero unless the lower binomial index is an integer
in [0,n]. Equation (Q4) is a finite exact coordinate algorithm, and
Phi[Q]=0 precisely when both resulting rational coordinates vanish.

## 2. The origin regularizations are original fixed-marker periods

Define q_0=f and, for j>=1,

    q_j=t^(-j)(f-Taylor_(<j) f) in K[[t]].           (Q7)

Every q_j belongs even to V. To see this put

    H_a(t)=2^(-a) sum_(k=0)^floor(a/2)
                       binom(a,2k) Delta^k
          =sum_k c_(a,k)t^(2k).

For a>=1 the constant coefficient c_(a,0)=1/2, because the sum of
the even binomial coefficients is 2^(a-1). Also deg H_a<=a.
Equation (Q4) with b=0 says that the f-coordinate of Phi[Z^a] is
t^-a H_a. Replace each term t^(-(a-2k))f by q_(a-2k) and its strictly
negative Laurent principal part. By (Q5), the leftover rational
coordinate is strictly negative Laurent. The whole expression is
regular at zero, so that leftover is zero. Consequently

    Phi[Z^a]=sum_k c_(a,k) q_(a-2k).                 (Q8)

For a=0 this means Phi[1]=q_0. The coefficient 1/2 at q_a proves
q_a in V inductively, as every other index a-2k is smaller.

Two further original-source identities supply the remaining generators:

    Phi[W^b]=(2t)^b(2b-1)!! Delta^-b f,
    1=f-2t Phi[Z].                                  (Q9)

Here (-1)!!=1. Since gcd(t^b,Delta^b)=1, choose polynomials U_b,V_b with
U_b t^b+V_b Delta^b=1. Multiplying this identity by Delta^-b f
and using (Q9) proves

    O[Delta^-1] f subset M, O subset M.             (Q10)

No inverse of t has been applied to an unknown element of M.

Now let g=Af+B lie in the right-hand side of (Q2), with A in R and B
in T. Partial fractions at the coprime factors t and Delta give

    A=A_0+sum_(j=1)^N lambda_j t^-j,
    A_0 in O[Delta^-1], lambda_j in K.

Thus

    g=A_0 f+sum_j lambda_j q_j
          +B+sum_j lambda_j t^-j Taylor_(<j) f.

The first two terms belong to M and are regular at zero. The remaining
term is in T and is also regular at zero, so it lies in O subset M.
This proves the reverse inclusion in (Q2) with no origin-saturation
assumption.

## 3. Literal rational obstruction and its exact projection

On the faithful generic frame f,1 define the F-linear projection
pi(Af+B)=B. Equation (Q5) gives pi(V) subset t^-1 K[t^-1].
For a>=1, pi(Phi[Z^a]) has leading term -t^-a/2 and only smaller pole
orders at zero. These triangular base markers therefore span every
strictly negative Laurent polynomial. Hence

    pi(V)=t^-1 K[t^-1], pi(M)=T.                    (Q11)

If v in V is rational, uniqueness of its coordinates forces its
f-coordinate to be zero. Then v is strictly negative Laurent and also
regular at zero, hence v=0. Likewise, a rational element of M has
A=0 in (Q2) and belongs to T intersect K[[t]]=O. This proves both
rational-intersection assertions, and thus the polynomial assertions,
in (Q2). In particular the polynomial-time identity in (Q9) is not a
fixed spatial-marker identity.

The unit satisfies f'=4t Delta^-1 f, so its cyclic differential rank
is one. Since f and 1 both belong to M and are F-independent, the
full generic rank is two. These are different ranks of the same
actual original source.

## 4. Exact fixed kernel and literal degree filtration

There is a t-free tangent Stein operator

    L=W(1-Z^2)-(1+Z^2)partial_Z+2ZW partial_W.        (Q12)

Its image L K[Z,W] is contained in ker Phi. Indeed the polynomial vector
field v=(1+Z^2)partial_Z-2ZW partial_W has divergence zero and v(P)=0.
Gaussian integration by parts with Gaussian quadratic ZW gives exactly
Phi[LQ]=0 for every Q.

The leading term of LQ is -Z^2W times the highest homogeneous part of Q.
All remaining terms have smaller total degree. Eliminating monomials
divisible by Z^2W therefore reduces every marker, without increasing
degree, to a linear combination of

    Z^a (a>=0), W^b (b>=1), ZW^b (b>=1).           (Q13)

These normal forms are linearly independent under Phi. At degree D>=1,
their base-axis markers Z^a, 1<=a<=D, have triangular independent
pi-coordinates as in Section 3, so any relation has zero coefficient
on those markers. The remaining markers have pure f-coordinates,
because (Q3) gives, for b>=1,

    Phi[ZW^b]=Phi[W^b]/(2t).

After harmless nonzero scalar factors the remaining coefficient
functions are

    1, t^b Delta^-b (1<=b<=D),
       t^(b-1) Delta^-b (1<=b<=D-1).

At the highest pole order D of Delta only t^D occurs, so its coefficient
vanishes. Descending through lower pole orders, the numerator has form
t^(b-1)(c t+d). To lose both poles at t=1/2 and -1/2, the linear
polynomial ct+d must vanish at both points, forcing c=d=0. Finally the
constant coefficient is zero. This proves independence.

The reduction and independence give the exact conclusions

    ker(Phi:K[Z,W]->K[[t]])=L K[Z,W],
    dim_K V_D=3D for D>=1, dim_K V_0=1,              (Q14)

where V_D is the image of markers of total degree at most D.
The kernel is the LINEAR operator image in (Q14), not a spatial ideal.
The normal forms (Q13) of degree at most D give an explicit faithful
basis of V_D. In particular the reduction is unique.

If independent ordinary Gaussian variables Y are adjoined and remain
absent from P, first apply their Gaussian expectation E_Y coefficientwise
to an original marker Q(Z,W,Y). Its period equals Phi[E_Y Q], and
E_Y does not increase spatial degree. Thus the same filtered images
V_D occur, and the enlarged kernel is exactly the inverse image of
L K[Z,W] under E_Y. It is not just L K[Z,W,Y]; in particular every
centered inert marker must be retained in the enlarged kernel.

## 5. Why the selected localization ring is too large

Here zeta=(1-sqrt(Delta))/(2t), J=sqrt(Delta), and f=J^-1.
The candidate ring span O[zeta,J^-1]f contains f^2=Delta^-1:
J^-1 belongs to the ring multiplying f. This rational function is
regular at zero but is not polynomial, so (Q2) excludes it from M.
Taking an origin-regular intersection cannot repair this example.
In fact M is not an algebra, since it contains f but not f^2.

The exact module is not finite over O. It contains Delta^-b f for every
b, whose pole orders in the unique f-coordinate are unbounded at
t=1/2 and -1/2. A finite set of O-generators has bounded coordinate
pole orders at those points. Generic rank, a selected algebraic
field, and a convenient spatial localization ring consequently do
not determine the actual global polynomial-time lattice.
