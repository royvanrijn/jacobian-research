# Constant nonzero W-square coefficient: finite original images

All moments and markers are ORIGINAL unweighted Gaussian Wick moments. This extends
the retained constant-W-square purity proof by keeping both the scalar
exponential gauge and a possible polynomial vacuum factor.

Let K have characteristic zero, let E[ZW]=E[Y^2]=1 with all other basic
pairings zero, and consider

    P=lambda W^2+W(h(Z)+c(Z)Y)+aY^2+u(Z)Y+k(Z),
    lambda!=0, a,lambda in K, h,c,u,k in K[Z].                 (1)

For a phase P put Phi_P[Q]=E[Q exp(tP)], V=Phi_P[K[Z,W,Y]],
and M=K[t]V. The complete criterion is

    M finite over K[t]
      iff c is constant, h,u are affine, deg k<=2,
          and Gamma A is nilpotent,                         (2)

where the quadratic matrix A and covariance Gamma are specified below.
Every finite image has primitive original vacuum exp(R), and

    V=M=exp(R)K[t].                                         (3)

The phase is nonconstant because lambda!=0, so there is no constant-phase
exception in (3). Safety is equivalent to R=0 and implies purity. Conversely a polynomial
unit is a special case of (4) with psi=0; the scalar theorem forces p=1
and R=0. Thus polynomial unit, purity and safety are equivalent within
this constant-lambda, constant-a architecture.

There is also a scalar criterion: existence of

    Phi_P[1]=p(t)exp(psi(t)),
    p in K[t], p(0)=1, psi in tK[t]                         (4)

already implies the right side of (2) and p=1. The proof below in fact
uses (4), while finite M supplies (4) by the retained actual-image theorem.
This strengthening is not inferred merely from generic image rank one.

## 1. Exact generic Gaussian reduction and original source

Initially work over C; the final paragraph gives characteristic-zero
descent. Write d=1-2at. In the full phase

    S=-ZW-Y^2/2+tP

the fibre precision and source vector are

    M_f=[[-2lambda t, -c(z)t],[-c(z)t,d]],
    b=(t h(z)-z,t u(z))^T,
    Delta=det M_f=-2lambda t+(4lambda a-c(z)^2)t^2.

Completing the square over C(t)[z,Delta^-1] gives

    phi=t k+(1/2)b^T M_f^-1 b=N/(2Delta),

    N=z^2+t(-2zh-2az^2)
       +t^2(h^2+4azh-2czu-4lambda k)
       +t^3(-2ah^2+2chu-2lambda u^2+2(4lambda a-c^2)k).       (5)

The normalized scalar volume is Delta^-1/2 dz. Fibre contractions
identify the full top spatial quotient with this one-variable
half-density module. The z and t connections include respectively

    -(1/2)tr(M_f^-1 (M_f)_z),
    -(1/2)tr(M_f^-1 (M_f)_t).

Both are -(1/2) times the corresponding logarithmic derivative of Delta.
Moving-centre terms are spatial exact terms. Thus this is an identification
of connection and seed, not merely equality of stationary equations.

For c nonconstant put

    r=1/t, F=c^2-4lambda a,
    q=F+2lambda r, Delta=-t^2q.

The crucial ORIGINAL-source extension is legitimate because

    q^-1=t/(2lambda) sum_(j>=0)(-tF/(2lambda))^j.             (6)

Every t coefficient is a polynomial in z. Any element of
C(t)[z,W,Y,q^-1] therefore expands as a Laurent t series with polynomial
spatial coefficients and a finite principal part. Multiplication by
exp(tP), Wick contraction, integration by parts and parameter
differentiation are coefficientwise well-defined. The resulting
functional agrees with the original Phi_P on every polynomial marker
and is horizontal on the full localized spatial module.

No source-cleanliness or nonzero nullsource is needed here. No isotropic
conditional Wick integral at t=0 is asserted. By contrast, for variable
ell(z), the analogous inverse starts with t/(2ell(z)); it need not have
polynomial coefficients, so argument (6) does not extend to that case.

These identifications are the retained algebraic source bridge in
`GAUSSIAN_AFFINE_ISOTROPIC_CHART.md`, section
"An arbitrary WY coefficient with constant nonzero W^2 coefficient".
For scalar fibre matrix coefficients the precise determinant-factor
import is `results/gaussian-boundary/repeated_line_exclusion.tex`,

## 2. A polynomial-exponential vacuum gives a unit cyclic quotient

Assume (4). Gauge the reduced connection by subtracting psi from phi,
and multiply its original period functional by exp(-psi). Divide its
seed differential by p. The resulting normalized seed has value one.

The full localized formal functional need not converge. Its restriction
to the differential submodule generated by the normalized seed is
K-rational: the seed maps to one and every positive parameter derivative
maps to zero. At an ordinary parameter, extend this dual functional from
the cyclic submodule to the full fibre and parallel transport. Uniqueness
preserves its restriction. The retained perfect exponential period pairing
then represents it by an analytic period of the full reduced system.

Thus a contradiction may be obtained by proving that every zero-exponential
period of the normalized seed tends to zero at t=infinity. This step
does not replace the original source by a convenient stationary cycle.

## 3. Nonconstant c: positivity after ANY scalar gauge

Let n=deg c>=1. The smooth projective cover is

    V^2=q=F(z)+2lambda r,
    eta=r dz/V.                                             (7)

The actual half-density is a fixed nonzero constant multiple of eta.
The polynomial q has degree 2n and is squarefree over C(r): q'=F',
and at every root beta of F' its value F(beta)+2lambda r is not
identically zero. Hence the cover is geometrically connected of genus
n-1. On V!=0 its odd de Rham sector is exactly the Gaussian half-density
module; the actual source functional extends by zero on the even sector.
This preserves the original seed value.

Remove the phase polar divisor as well as the indicated localization and
parameter exceptional divisors. The total space is smooth, since its
equation solves r=(V^2-F(z))/(2lambda). Moreover phi-psi is spatially
nonconstant: at t=0 its Laurent expansion contains

    -z^2/(4lambda t)+O(1),

and subtracting psi cannot change that spatial coefficient. Thus the base
projection and phase are algebraically independent as required by the
retained complete exponential comparison.

Here are ALL generic component valuations. Normalize v(r)=1. On any
generic component of any finitely ramified marked model, relative
differentiation satisfies v(df)>=v(f).

* If z has nonconstant finite residue, or specializes to alpha with
  F(alpha)!=0, then v(V)=0 and v(eta)>=1.
* Suppose F(alpha)=0 has multiplicity m and N0=v(z-alpha)>0. If
  mN0<1, then v(V)=mN0/2 and
      v(eta)>=1+N0-mN0/2>0.
  If mN0>1, then v(V)=1/2 and v(eta)>=1/2+N0>0.
  The same bound holds at mN0=1 without further cancellation.
* At mN0=1 with v(V)>1/2, the relation
      2V dV=F'(z)dz, v(F')=(m-1)N0=1-N0
  gives
      v(eta)=1+v(dV)-v(F')>=v(V)+N0>0.
* At infinity, v(z)=-N0<0 gives v(V)=-nN0 and
      v(eta)>=1+(n-1)N0>0.

These estimates depend only on the cover, not the phase or its scalar
gauge. Therefore they also apply to EVERY extra component created when
one marks the fibres (phi-psi)=0,1,infinity and separates collisions.
No list of old stationary faces is being reused without checking the new
centres. All finite centres, collisions and infinity have been included.

At the two horizontal points at infinity, x=1/z gives eta equal to a
unit times r x^(n-2)dx: it is logarithmic for n=1 and holomorphic for
n>=2. It is holomorphic at every finite generic branch point. Thus the
only horizontal seed poles are logarithmic. Dividing by p affects no
spatial pole order and adds deg p to EVERY displayed infinity valuation:

    v(eta/p)=v(eta)+deg p>0.                              (8)

Use the retained proper marked semistable curve model, keeping all
positive-genus component cohomology. Positive generic order on adjacent
finite components gives a positive power times logarithmic neck length;
at a finite/pole node the positive finite-component order survives
descending exponential tail integration. The remaining transition scales
and inverse phase branches are algebraic and have only algebraic growth,
which the exponential cutoff dominates. Regular punctures have at most
logarithmic amplitude. The full gluing complex, including its annulus
and monodromy maps, is retained.

Consequently every zero-exponential eta/p period tends to zero. Section 2
would produce one with value one, a contradiction. Thus (4), and hence
finite M, is impossible for nonconstant c.

## 4. Constant c: the full leading-coefficient obstruction

Now c is constant, so Delta is a nonzero polynomial in t of degree
delta in {1,2}. Its lowest nonzero Taylor term is -2lambda t. The reduced phase
is polynomial in z. Under (4), its shifted phase and volume are

    phi-psi, H=Delta^-1/2/p.

Let D>=3 be its actual spatial degree. Its top coefficient equals
N_D/(2Delta), where N_D is a nonzero polynomial divisible by t:
the constant t coefficient of N is only z^2. Therefore

    v_infinity(H)=delta/2+deg p,
    v_infinity([z^D]phi)=delta-deg N_D,

    D v_infinity(H)-v_infinity([z^D]phi)
      =(D/2-1)delta+deg N_D+D deg p>0.                    (9)

The full ordinary formal stationary-ball theorem rules out the unit
cyclic quotient of Section 2. The exact import is
`results/gaussian-boundary/polynomial_stationary_balls.tex`,
Theorem `thm:stationaryballs`, applied after a finite cover for the
algebraic square root. Its use is also explicit in the determinant-factor
lemma. A corollary restricted to rational normalization would not suffice.
Subtracting psi leaves the spatial top coefficient unchanged, and that
theorem permits all the lower coefficients, including the scalar one.

Hence the reduced phase has spatial degree at most two. In (5), the
coefficient of t forces h affine. The coefficient of t^2 then gives
deg k<=max(2,deg u+1). If deg u=m>=2, the term -2lambda u^2 in the
t^3 coefficient has degree 2m, whereas all other terms have degree at
most m+1; cancellation is impossible. Thus u is affine and deg k<=2.
The ORIGINAL P is quadratic.

## 5. Exact quadratic criterion, original image and primitive vacuum

Write h=h0+h1 z, u=u0+u1 z, k=k0+k1 z+k2 z^2 and X=(Z,W,Y)^T.
Then P=X^T A X+l^T X+k0, where

    Gamma=[[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,1]],
    A=[[k2,h1/2,u1/2],[h1/2,lambda,c/2],[u1/2,c/2,a]],
    l=(k1,h0,u0)^T, B=Gamma A.

The exact original quadratic source is

    Phi_P[1]=D0^(-1/2)exp(R),
    D0=det(I-2tB),
    C_t=(I-2tB)^-1 Gamma,
    R=tk0+(t^2/2)l^T C_t l.                              (10)

The determinant kernel is classical quadratic-form calculus:
[Lancaster, *Traces and Cumulants of Quadratic Forms in Normal Variables*
(1954), Theorems 1-2, pp.247-248](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1954.tb00167.x)
gives the trace cumulants and the joint determinant generating function.
Its real standardized Gaussian statement extends to this split formal
covariance coefficientwise: each fixed moment is a polynomial identity
in the symmetric form and covariance entries. Expanding the logarithm
of D0 gives cumulants 2^(j-1)(j-1)! tr(B^j) for the homogeneous quadratic
part; Newton identities identify D0=1 with nilpotence. The linear source
completion and all-marker formula (13) are retained explicitly here.
This classical input does not prove the nonlinear reduction in Sections
3-4, or supply the rational-exponential argument below.

Assumption (4), after squaring, implies

    exp(2(R-psi))=D0 p^2.                                (11)

If the exponential of a rational function is rational, its logarithmic
derivative has only simple finite poles, whereas the derivative of a
rational function has no simple poles. Thus the exponent has no finite
poles and is polynomial; comparison at infinity then makes it constant.
Here its value at t=0 is zero, so (11) gives R=psi and D0 p^2=1.
The latter is a polynomial identity with D0(0)=p(0)=1. It forces

    D0=1, p=1, B nilpotent.                               (12)

Conversely B nilpotent makes C_t and the mean m_t=tC_t l polynomial
in t. The entire original source with an auxiliary marker variable J is

    E[exp(tP+J^T X)]
      =exp(R)exp(J^T m_t+(1/2)J^T C_t J).                 (13)

Every fixed J coefficient is exp(R) times a polynomial, so M is finite.
The retained original-image theorem yields the literal equality (3).
Since B^3=0, R is a polynomial of degree at most four. The unit is
primitive, and safety is exactly R=0.

The exponent bound four is attained: for

    P=W^2+2WY+4ZY-4Z^2+Z

one has B^3=0, B^2!=0, and (10) gives the exact vacuum
exp(t^3+2t^4). This is a finite primitive image and is unsafe. It
also checks that the argument has not silently forced psi to be linear.

This proves (2), together with the stronger scalar implication (4),
assuming the retained complete curve-comparison and full ordinary
stationary-ball inputs explicitly identified above. The proof is an
internal written argument; finite controls alone do not certify those
imports. No independent replay, formal verification, external review,
publication or priority claim is made.

Finally all algebraic conclusions descend to characteristic-zero K.
Embed the finitely generated field of phase coefficients into C. The
nonvanishing determinant, source relations and nilpotence identities are
algebraic; a finite image also supplies p and psi over K by the retained
image theorem. Including their finitely many coefficients in the embedding

## 6. Exact examples and verification scope

For P=(W+Y-Z/2)^2+Y+3 the precision is nilpotent of index two and
the unit is exp(3t+t^2/2+t^3). The quartic exponent bound is attained:

    P=W^2+2WY+4ZY-4Z^2+Z,
    Phi_P[1]=exp(t^3+2t^4),

with (Gamma A)^3=0 and (Gamma A)^2!=0 in convention (10). These phases
have finite primitive actual image and are unsafe. For comparison,
P=W^2+ZW has unit (1-t)^(-1) and a nonnilpotent precision, hence has
no finite actual image.

The [safe recomposition classification](GAUSSIAN_SAFE_RECOMPOSITION_CLASSIFICATION.md)
also shows that the quartic-exponent example has S_P=(0): no constant
shift can make exp(t^3+2t^4-lambda t) polynomial. Hence EVERY nonconstant
polynomial recomposition of this phase is unsafe, despite its primitive
finite actual image. This asserts unsafety for each recomposition;
no single common bad marker is specified.

The [exact checker](verify_gaussian_extended_fibre_finite_images.py)
verifies the generic determinant and Schur numerator, the polynomial
