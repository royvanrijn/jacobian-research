# Finite seeds give ordinary purity and uniformity for formal operators

15 November 2026. Internal written theorem, independent review pending.
This extends the [direct coefficient-power argument](POSITIVE_FIELD_THEOREMS_AND_ORDINARY_CUTOFF_STABILIZATION.md)
to unbounded operator order and formal constant-coefficient symbols. The
infinite coefficient ring is not assumed Noetherian. All uses of radical
computation and symbolic containment take place in specified finite rings.
The [small checker](verify_formal_operator_finite_seed.py) supplies exact
controls and one ordinary-ideal counterexample, not a universal proof.
No novelty or formal verification is claimed.

## 1. A criterion with finite field inputs

Fix n>=1, phase degree D>=1, and integers k0,C>=1. Suppose, for every
characteristic-zero field and every polynomial constant-coefficient operator
Lambda of arbitrary finite order, the following hold:

* for deg P<=D, the first k0 identities Lambda^j(P^j)=0 imply all positive
  pure identities;
* every such pure pair satisfies Lambda^m(QP^m)=0 for m>C deg Q.

Both constants must be independent of operator order. This is a conditional
criterion; it does not assert these hypotheses for arbitrary n,D.

They automatically extend to a formal symbol lambda in K[[xi]]: every fixed
expression Lambda^m(QP^m) uses only symbol coefficients of order at most
deg Q+Dm. Truncating the symbol beyond this and Dk0 preserves the field
tests and the expression. Phases and multipliers remain polynomials.

The conclusion below is stronger than the field hypotheses. There are
computable integers K,N depending only on these fixed data such that,
over every commutative Q-algebra and every formal constant-coefficient
symbol, the first K pure identities imply all pure identities and

    Lambda^m(QP^m)=0 whenever m>N deg Q.                       (1)

The full ordinary pure ideal is generated by those first K expressions,
and hence depends on only the symbol jet of order DK. All later formal
symbol coefficients are free even on the nonreduced pure coefficient
scheme. Neither K=k0 nor N=C is asserted.

## 2. The finite seed, radical and nilpotent exponent

Let R_r be the rational polynomial ring in all phase coefficients of
degree<=D and all symbol coefficients of order<=r. Write R_infty for the
polynomial ring in the phase coefficients and countably many symbol
coefficients. Each element of R_infty is still a finite polynomial.

Set r0=Dk0. In the finite ring R_r0, let A0 be the ideal of all spatial
output coefficients of

    Lambda^j(P^j), 1<=j<=k0,

and compute J0=sqrt(A0). Choose nu>=1 such that

    J0^nu subset A0.                                         (2)

This is a finite ordinary ideal membership statement in R_r0. Such nu
exists because R_r0 is Noetherian and J0/A0 is its quotient's nilradical.
An exact radical computation followed by ideal membership finds a sufficient
nu. It does not inspect an infinite ideal or use Noetherianity of R_infty.

Choose a positive integer h bounding the codimension of every complex
irreducible component of V(J0). Always one may take

    h=number of variables of R_r0
     =binomial(n+D,D)+binomial(n+Dk0,Dk0).                     (3)

A known smaller cylinder gives a better h. The seed is proper (the zero
phase satisfies it) and nonzero (the pair Lambda=1,P=1 does not), so a
positive bound suffices. Perfectness of Q ensures that J0 stays radical
after extension to C.

For r>=r0, the seed equations in R_r are identical to those in R_r0.
Its radical is J_r=J0 R_r. Polynomial extension preserves both radicality
and the component codimensions. The field hypotheses ensure that every
point of V(J_r) is pure and has the common mixed cutoff C. Thus the
codimension bound h stays fixed while the number of free higher symbol
coefficients grows without bound.

## 3. A fixed codimension replaces a growing parameter count

For a monomial multiplier Q=x^alpha of degree delta, let mu be any output
coefficient of Lambda^m(QP^m). It belongs to some R_r with

    r>=max(r0,delta+Dm).

The [power-alignment lemma](POSITIVE_FIELD_THEOREMS_AND_ORDINARY_CUTOFF_STABILIZATION.md)
shows that every coefficient partial derivative of mu of order l vanishes
on V(J_r) when

    m>C delta+(CD+1)l.                                      (4)

This includes derivatives in higher symbol coefficients. For u symbol
derivatives and v phase derivatives, put k=max(u,v). The remaining powers
are aligned as an outer constant-coefficient operator applied to

    Lambda^(m-k)[Q M_beta P^(k-v) P^(m-k)],

whose marker has degree at most delta+Dk. No symbol-order term enters this
degree. Operator commutativity is essential.

Apply radical Zariski--Nagata and Ein--Lazarsfeld--Smith in the finite
smooth coefficient space Spec(R_r tensor C). Since each component has
codimension<=h, their containments give

    m>C delta+(CD+1)(h nu-1)
       implies mu in J_r^nu subset A0 R_r.                    (5)

The ordinary containment is contracted faithfully back to Q. This uses
the codimension version of ELS, not the ambient variable count. A separate
finite R_r is used for each expression; there is no symbolic-power theorem
being applied to R_infty. Relation(2) extends to every one of these rings.

Put

    E=(CD+1)(h nu-1),   K=max(k0,E),   N=C+E.                 (6)

For constant marker Q=1, every pure output with m>E belongs to A0. All
remaining pure outputs occur among the first K. Consequently, in R_infty,

    I_pure,infty=A_K R_infty,                                (7)

where A_K is the ideal of the first K pure outputs in the finite ring
R_DK. This proves finite generation of this specific infinite-ring ideal;
it does not infer it from an ascending-chain condition in R_infty.

For delta>=1, m>N delta implies m>C delta+E. Formula(5) puts every such
marked coefficient in A0, hence in I_pure,infty. Degree-zero markers are
covered by pure nullity itself. Therefore the full ordinary cutoff ideal
in R_infty satisfies

    I_N,infty=I_pure,infty.                                  (8)

Equations(7)--(8) specialize under every coefficient map R_infty->A,
including non-Noetherian targets and arbitrary formal symbols. They also
specialize to every finite operator order by setting later symbol
coefficients to zero. For r>=DK the finite-order pure ideal is literally
the polynomial extension of A_K; this is an ordinary cylinder assertion.

There is an additional finite-test consequence. The first k0 pure tests
alone already give the affine eventual bound

    Lambda^m(QP^m)=0 for m>C deg Q+E,                         (9)

over all Q-algebras and formal symbols. To assert that every positive pure
expression vanishes, impose the first K tests as in(7). A field gate need
not be an ordinary gate of the same length.

## 4. Constructivity and the converse boundary

The theoretical producer computes A0,J0,nu in R_r0, chooses the explicit
bound(3) if no better h is known, and outputs(6). It can then compute A_K
in R_DK and a finite ordinary certificate for purity. The least ordinary
pure prefix is also theoretically computable: compare the ideals of each
prefix k<=K with A_K in R_DK. No infinite ideal computation is required.

No general producer is implemented or executed here. Nor is the least
order-independent mixed slope proved computable by this argument: below
the sufficient N, its infinitely many marked constraints require a further
stopping argument. Do not silently import the bounded-order cutoff algorithm
into an infinite symbol coefficient ring.

Conversely, any finite ordinary gate and common all-ring cutoff of the
kind(1) supply the two field hypotheses by restriction to fields and
polynomial symbols. Thus existence of order-independent finite purity
tests plus a common positive field cutoff is equivalent to existence of
some finite ordinary purity gate and common all-ring/formal-symbol cutoff.
This is a criterion for positive families, not a proof of the false general
vanishing conjecture in higher phase degree and dimension.

## 5. Quadratic phases in every dimension

The retained [quadratic theorem](QUADRATIC_PHASES_FOR_ALL_OPERATOR_ORDERS.md)
provides, independently of symbol order,

    D=2, k0=n+1, C=3(n+1), r0=2(n+1).

The field pure locus is already a cylinder over the phase and symbol
two-jets, including the zero-phase branch. Therefore one may take

    h=2 binomial(n+2,2),                                    (10)

even though the ordinary seed A0 is computed with symbol jets through
2(n+1). Let nu_n satisfy(2) in that finite seed ring, and put

    E_n=(6(n+1)+1)(2 binomial(n+2,2) nu_n-1),
    K_n=max(n+1,E_n),  N_n=3(n+1)+E_n.                       (11)

Every quadratic polynomial phase over every commutative Q-algebra and
every formal constant-coefficient symbol is pure exactly when its first
K_n pure identities vanish. Its full ordinary pure ideal is a cylinder
over the symbol jet of order2K_n. Pure inputs satisfy the common cutoff
m>N_n deg Q, with N_n independent of every finite or infinite symbol order.
Even first n+1 tests alone imply the affine eventual bound(9).

The previous finite-order restriction is thereby removed internally.
The field two-jet classifier remains valid; it has not been promoted to
an ordinary two-jet classifier. Neither nu_n,K_n,N_n nor the universal
ordinary ideal is numerically computed here. The field slope3(n+1) is not
asserted unchanged over rings. This proof uses the known quadratic field
theorem and the retained direct symbolic argument; it does not use general
Gaussian holonomic field stratification.

## 6. Univariate phases in every bounded degree

For n=1 and any polynomial phase of degree<=D, one can take k0=1,C=1.
Here is a complete elementary field proof, also valid for formal symbols.
If P!=0 has actual degree d and lambda!=0 has smallest nonzero derivative
order s, the leading coefficient of Lambda P is

    a_s lc(P) d!/(d-s)! at degree d-s, if s<=d.

Higher symbol orders give smaller polynomial degrees and cannot cancel it.
Thus Lambda P=0 iff s>d. The zero operator and zero phase are automatic
branches. For s>d,

    ord(Lambda^m)>=sm>dm+deg Q if m>deg Q,

so every marked expression is zero. Common field slope1 is sharp, using
Lambda=partial^(D+1),P=x^D,Q=x^delta,m=delta. This argument assumes fields;
zero divisors would invalidate its leading-coefficient conclusion.

This agrees with the polynomial-phase portion of
[van den Essen--Willems--Zhao0903.1478v2,Theorem2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/0903.1478v2#S2).
That source fixes a finite polynomial operator and also proves its input
formal phase must be polynomial. We do not attribute the interchange of
formal symbol and polynomial phase to its formal-phase theorem. Its
latest listed arXiv revision remains16January2011, not the2026 HTML body date.

Compute A0 from Lambda P in the ring of phase coefficients and symbol
coefficients through orderD; choose nu_D with sqrt(A0)^nu_D subset A0.
Its radical has a complete all-degree description. Write a_i for the
symbol coefficients and b_j for the phase coefficients. Then

    J0=(a_i b_j:0<=i<=j<=D)
      =intersection_(d=-1)^D (a_0,...,a_d,b_(d+1),...,b_D).   (12a)

The d=-1 component means all b_j=0. At every other field point take d
to be the actual phase degree; purity says exactly a_0=...=a_d=0.
This gives the zero set above. The displayed monomial ideal equals that
intersection: a monomial lies in every component exactly when it contains
some a_i b_j with i<=j. Indeed, otherwise every occurring b-index is below
every occurring a-index, and a split between them gives a component it
misses. Thus J0 is radical, and all its minimal components have
codimension D+1. Consequently one may choose

    h=D+1, E_D=(D+1)((D+1)nu_D-1),
    K_D=max(1,E_D), N_D=1+E_D.                               (12)

Equations(7)--(9) now give all-ring safety, a common order-independent
cutoff, and full ordinary finite-jet/prefix purity for univariate phases
of every fixed degree, with arbitrary formal symbols. This is an all-degree
structural extension, not a computed universal ideal in every degree.

## 7. Exact controls for the ordinary boundary

The field gate Lambda P=0 cannot generally be used literally over rings.
Let

    A=Q[a0,a1,a2,b0,b1,b2]/
      (a0 b2, a0 b1+2a1 b2, a0 b0+a1 b1+2a2 b2),
    P=b0+b1 x+b2 x^2, Lambda=a0+a1 partial+a2 partial^2.

Then Lambda P=0 by construction. In lexicographic variable order
a0,a1,a2,b0,b1,b2, the constant coefficient of Lambda^2(P^2) has nonzero
normal form

    a1^2 b1^2-4a2^2 b2^2.                                   (13)

All its positive spatial-degree coefficients reduce to zero. The square
of(13) reduces to zero. Thus the second pure expression is nonzero over
this explicit coefficient algebra, despite the field one-test criterion;
the failure is an ordinary nilpotent direction, not a GVC counterexample.
The checker retains the defining ideal, full Groebner basis and reductions.

A separate complete seed control is the affine univariate phase
P=b0+b1x. Here

    A0=(a0b1,a0b0+a1b1),
    J0=(a0b0,a0b1,a1b1), J0^2 subset A0, J0 not subset A0.

The radical identity follows from the squarefree monomial decomposition
J0=(b0,b1) intersect(a0,a1) intersect(a0,b1). Its components have
codimension2 and its seed nilradical exponent is exactly2. This is a small
finite certificate for the mechanism, not an assertion that the sufficient
K,N obtained from it are least or that this seed is a universal all-degree
computation. Higher symbol coefficients are retained in the formal theorem.

## 8. Remaining scope

These results close the finite-order restriction for quadratic GVC ring
uniformity, extend univariate positivity in every bounded phase degree to
arbitrary Q-algebras/formal symbols, and prove ordinary finite-jet purity.
They do not supply a positive field seed for higher-dimensional general
degree, decide dense SIC33, improve any protected operator/Laplacian or
ternary-prefix frontier, or cover nonpolynomial phases or multipliers.
The ideal can retain nonreduced higher-jet information below its proved
finite bound. Numerical general exponents,least mixed cutoffs,general
producer implementation and independent specialist review remain open.

Cohen--Macaulay pure quotient; affine-cone/irrelevant torsion must also be
retained. The next source gate is to compare that statement and its proof
with classical effective Nullstellensatz bounds before asserting a numerical
general cutoff. The results above use only the independently computable nu.
