# Projective modules with a formally free limit and no generic free frame

14 November 2026. **QPF1.** Finite generation, projectivity, stable freeness
and a nonzero algebraic dual still do not make a formal left frame
algebraic. There are explicit rank-one projective modules over the finite
parameter Weyl algebra whose inclusion into that algebra is an
isomorphism modulo every parameter power, but whose generic fibers are
not free. In Weyl rank one their generic Berest--Wilson invariant is
exactly one.

The construction works in every Weyl rank and with a divisor
x_1=t^(-m) for any integer m>=1. It has two generators and one relation
of spatial degrees at most two and one. Thus even a bounded presentation
and bounded generic charge do not repair this free-frame gap.

A compatible filtered frame does suffice: if an exhaustive filtration
bounded below has finite coefficient modules in each degree, its
associated graded is flat over the parameter ring, and its special
associated graded is the free polynomial module, then the module is
free. The compatibility of this associated graded with the special
fiber is a substantive hypothesis, absent from the examples below.

## The projective ideal

Let K have characteristic zero, let R be K[t] or K[[t]], and put
\[
 A_R=R\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n,p_1,\ldots,p_n\rangle/
 ([p_i,x_j]-t\delta_{ij},[x_i,x_j],[p_i,p_j]).
\]
Only finite sums of spatial PBW monomials are allowed in A_R. Fix m>=1
and abbreviate
\[
 x=x_1,\quad p=p_1,\quad z=t^{m-1},\quad
 q=1-tzx=1-t^m x,\qquad c=t^2z=t^{m+1}.
\]
Define the left ideal
\[
             I_m=A_R q^2+A_R(qp-2c).                    \tag{1}
\]
The commutator [p,q]=-c gives the relation
\[
                  -p q^2+q(qp-2c)=0.                   \tag{2}
\]
Here and below juxtaposition means the noncommutative Weyl product.

Consider the left-linear row map and the generator map
\[
 j:A_R\longrightarrow A_R^2,\quad a\longmapsto(-ap,aq),
 \qquad
 \pi(a,b)=a q^2+b(qp-2c).
\]
The sequence
\[
       0\longrightarrow A_R\xrightarrow{j} A_R^2
                     \xrightarrow{\pi} I_m
                     \longrightarrow0                 \tag{3}
\]
is exact and split on the left. In particular
\[
                      I_m\oplus A_R\simeq A_R^2.        \tag{4}
\]

Here is an explicit splitting, valid before inverting t. Put
\[
 a_0=zqx^2,\qquad b_0=1+tzx+zpx^2.
\]
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
             (-p)a_0+qb_0
 &=z(qp-pq)x^2+q(1+tzx)\\
 &=zcx^2+1-t^2z^2x^2=1.
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{5}
\]
Thus r(a,b)=aa_0+bb_0 satisfies rj=1 and j is a split
injection. Its cokernel P is a projective direct summand of A_R^2.

To identify P with I_m, it remains to prove that pi has no further
kernel. This can be done over the fraction field L of R and then
descended using R-torsion-freeness of P. Over L use
\[
                            u=q,\qquad v=-p/c.
\]
These are canonical linear coordinates, with [v,u]=1. Rescale the
spectator momenta by t^(-1). Up to a nonzero scalar in the second
generator, the generic ideal is therefore
\[
                         J=A_n(L)u^2+A_n(L)(uv+2).      \tag{6}
\]

For completeness, its only left syzygies are generated by (-v,u).
Filter by total Bernstein degree. A leading cancellation in
a u^2+b(uv+2)=0 gives
\[
                  \sigma(a)u^2+\sigma(b)uv=0.
\]
In the polynomial symbol ring this forces
sigma(a)=h v and sigma(b)=-h u. Subtract the corresponding lift
of h(v,-u), whose image is exactly zero since
v u^2=u(uv+2). The maximum coefficient degree decreases.
Induction proves that all syzygies are multiples of (-v,u).
This argument works with any number of spectator variables.

Consequently P_L maps isomorphically to (I_m)_L. The kernel of
P->I_m is R-torsion, whereas P is a direct summand of the R-free
module A_R^2 and hence R-torsion-free. The kernel is zero. This
proves (3)--(4), including projectivity over R rather than merely
over its fraction field.

## Every parameter jet is the regular left module

At t=0 the two generators in (1) become 1 and p, and (3) becomes
\[
 0\longrightarrow K[x_1,\ldots,p_n]
   \xrightarrow{a\mapsto(-ap,a)}
    K[x_1,\ldots,p_n]^2
   \xrightarrow{(a,b)\mapsto a+bp}
    K[x_1,\ldots,p_n]\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the inclusion I_m->A_R induces an isomorphism
\[
                            I_m/tI_m\simeq A_R/tA_R.   \tag{7}
\]
In particular I_m intersect tA_R equals tI_m. Since A_R is
R-torsion-free, repeated division by t gives
\[
                         I_m\cap t^N A_R=t^N I_m
                                  \quad(N>=1).        \tag{8}
\]
This proves injectivity of the induced inclusion modulo t^N.

The element q is a unit modulo t^N, with its finite geometric inverse.
Since q^2 belongs to I_m, that inclusion is also surjective. Explicitly
\[
 q^{-2}\equiv
 \sum_{0\le j\le \lfloor(N-1)/m\rfloor}
                  (j+1)t^{mj}x^j \pmod {t^N}.
\]
Therefore, compatibly for every N,
\[
 I_m/t^NI_m\simeq A_R/t^NA_R,\qquad
                   \widehat I_m\simeq\widehat A_R.     \tag{9}
\]
These are the actual maps induced by inclusion, not unrelated choices
of bases for different jets. The quotient A_R/I_m is nonzero
generically but has zero quotient modulo every t^N.

The inclusion is itself a nonzero algebraic left-dual section.
Moreover I_m is finite projective, so its algebraic left dual is a
direct summand of a finite free right module; its completion agrees
with the dual of the completed module. The failure here occurs after
the dual-section and finite-generation gates in
[QEF1](ALGEBRAIC_DUAL_AND_POLARIZATION.md).

## The generic module is not free

In the coordinates of (6),
\[
                    \operatorname{gr}J=(u^2,uv).       \tag{10}
\]
One can verify (10) without a bounded search: the only leading-symbol
syzygy of the two generators is generated by (-v,u), and its
lift cancels exactly. Removing any leading cancellation as above
shows that every leading symbol of J lies in (u^2,uv). The reverse
inclusion is immediate.

The ideal (u^2,uv)=u(u,v) in the polynomial symbol ring is not
principal, also after adjoining spectator variables. If J were a
free rank-one left A_n(L)-module, it would be principal as a left
ideal, say J=A_n(L)d. Additivity of Bernstein degree would then give
gr J=S sigma(d), a principal symbol ideal. This contradicts (10).
Thus
\[
                         (I_m)_L\text{ is not free}.   \tag{11}
\]

For n=1, dividing gr J by its homogeneous greatest common divisor u
gives the normalized ideal (u,v). Hence
\[
             \nu((I_m)_L)=\dim_L L[u,v]/(u,v)=1.        \tag{12}
\]
This uses the Berest--Wilson normalization described in
[Kanel-Belov--Fedorov, Section 7.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33919v1).
Its value is computed directly here; the example does not require
that preprint's arithmetic nonlifting theorem.

The special module (7) is free, so its corresponding defect is zero.
The generic charge stays one for every m. A bound on generic charge
therefore does not imply constancy of charge across the parameter
degeneration or effectivity of a free frame.

The equation q=0 places the finite affine defect near x=t^(-m).
In the projective x-line its closure is h-t^m x=0, where h is the
homogenizing coordinate; its special point lies at infinity. This
explains how formal affine agreement can coexist with a generic
defect. It does not identify the Bernstein normalized charge with
an ordinary point of affine phase space.

## A compatible filtered frame does descend

The following elementary criterion states sufficient compatibility
precisely. Let R=K[[t]], let A_R be as above with the Bernstein
filtration, and let M have an increasing, exhaustive filtration F_dM
bounded below, compatible with the A_R filtration. Assume:

1. Each graded component G_d of G=gr M is a finite R-module.
2. G is R-flat.
3. G/tG is a free graded S_K=K[x_1,\ldots,p_n]-module on one generator,
   with a specified degree shift.

Then M is a free rank-one left A_R-module.

To prove this, lift the special graded generator to e in G at its
specified degree. It defines a graded map S_R(-a)->G. In each degree,
the cokernel is a finite R-module with zero reduction, hence is zero
by Nakayama's lemma. The map is surjective degree by degree.
R-flatness of G makes reduction of its kernel exact on the left.
Each kernel component is finite over R and has zero reduction, so a
second application of Nakayama gives zero kernel. Thus G is freely
generated by e. Lift e to b in F_aM. Induction on filtration degree
shows that b generates M, and leading symbols rule out any relation
ab=0 for a nonzero a in A_R.

To infer hypothesis 3 from a known free special module M/tM, one
also needs the comparison
\[
       (\operatorname{gr}M)/t(\operatorname{gr}M)
                   \simeq\operatorname{gr}(M/tM)
\]
for the chosen filtration. Formal freeness alone does not give this
comparison with a free graded frame. The examples I_m cannot possess
all three hypotheses with such a frame: the conclusion would
contradict their generic nonfreeness.

This criterion supplies a free left module, not a right Weyl action
or a prescribed classical map. Those must still be constructed.
For the Dixmier route, the four right generator actions must preserve
the chosen algebraic model and satisfy all six relations.

## Consequences for the boundary approach

In [Ben-Zvi--Nevins, Definitions 3.1--3.5 and Proposition 3.6](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/31805),
D-bundles are already coherent and locally projective. The framed
theory adds filtration data at infinity and a finite-colength charge.
The proof of Proposition 3.6 explicitly uses finite generation.
Thus it cannot be used to establish that hypothesis for an arbitrary
formal graph model.

QPF1 shows that even supplying finite projectivity and a uniform
generic charge does not give the remaining free frame. A useful
application must control extension of the framing through the
parameter degeneration, or establish an equivalent symbol-level
compatibility such as the criterion above. The right actions also
require their own control.

No right Weyl action on I_m is constructed here. The regular right
action on its completion does not preserve this chosen algebraic
ideal: in (6),
\[
            (uv+2)u-vu^2=u,\qquad u\notin J.
\]
Thus J is not stable under regular right multiplication by u.
This refutes that particular descent, not every possible right
action. The regular module A_R is a good alternate algebraization
of the same formal left-module class. There is no obstruction to
existence of a good model for this formal class.

The next positive target is a compatible filtered lattice carrying
all right Weyl actions for the full Long graph, or an obstruction
that excludes every such choice. Neither a missing frame in this
example nor growing corrections in one representative excludes
all bounded lifts.

[Exact controls](check_projective_frame.py) verify the universal PBW
syzygy, splitting, associated idempotent, canonical coordinate change,
critical-pair cancellation and finite jet inverse controls. The
syzygy exhaustion, all-jet comparison, nonfreeness in every rank and
filtered-frame criterion are written proofs. No independent theorem
replay, formal verification, external review, novelty priority,
bounded Long lift or Dixmier solution is claimed.
