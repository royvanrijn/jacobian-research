# Ramification, position avoidance and the quantum polarization gap

> [QHR1](ELLIPTIC_RAMIFICATION_AND_STABLE_POLARIZATION.md) now excludes
> a full positional or cotangent form after arbitrary polynomial canonical
> source and target changes, even after identity stabilization. Other
> mixed target forms and genuinely high-order quantum images remain open.

14 November 2026. **QPA1.** Long's localized fiber map has a cubic
ramification boundary with two branches that force
\[
                 K(r)(S,T)\cap K(r)(v)=K(r).                    \tag{1}
\]
The intersection is inside K(r)(a,v), with the actual retained
S,T of QSO2 and r=R. This is a field statement, in every rational
function degree, not a finite elimination test.

It persists in formal quantum function fields: the completed quantum
fiber-image field intersects the completed multiplication field only
in the scalar field K(r)((h)). Thus every canonical pair in that image
which consists of finite differential operators over K(r)((h))(v)
has both orders at least three, by QSO2. This holds after arbitrary
polynomial Weyl automorphisms of the source fiber, of any degree,
with scalar parameter poles allowed. A rational source change is also covered
whenever its resulting two images are finite differential operators.

There is a second consequence requiring no scalar localization.
For any full formal Long lift whose first image U_h is positional,
even if U_h differs from R, the full image meets the target position
algebra exactly in the completed algebra generated by U_h. For a
spatially bounded lift, the corresponding finite-PBW intersection is
K((h))[U_h]. No polynomial source Weyl automorphism can then make
both position-generator images positional in the original target
polarization.

These are necessary restrictions. They do not exclude high-order
scalar pairs, nonpositional deformations of the first image, or
target canonical changes moving the position algebra.

## A general two-branch boundary lemma

Let pi:X -> Y be a finite dominant map of normal integral varieties
over a characteristic-zero field k. Let t in k(X) be nonconstant.
Suppose prime divisors E_0,E_1 of X dominate the same prime divisor
D of Y, t is regular at their generic points, and:
its residue on E_0 is c in k, while its residue t_1 on E_1 is
transcendental over k. Then
\[
                         k(Y)\cap k(t)=k.                      \tag{2}
\]
The fields are embedded in k(X) by pullback.

For a rational G(t) lying in k(Y), first suppose G has a pole at c.
Its order at E_0 is negative, while its order at E_1 is zero:
the nonzero rational function G(t_1) is defined at the generic point
because t_1 is transcendental. But the two orders are positive
ramification indices times the same order along D, a contradiction.
Hence G is regular at c.

Now the residue of G(t) on E_0 is G(c), and the residue-field
embedding k(D) -> k(E_0) implies its residue along D is that scalar.
Its residue on E_1 must consequently also be G(c). Thus
G(t_1)=G(c), forcing G to be constant. This proves (2). The argument
uses both branches; a single ramified branch would not suffice.

The lemma applies to any finite cover with these trace properties.
It is the same general use of contrasting boundary traces that
proved useful in the independent
[two-divisor graph argument](../../hessian/hc4-triangular-graphs/TWO_DIVISOR_EXTENSION.md).
No theorem from that other problem is imported.

## The actual cubic cover and its two boundary branches

Put k=K(r), with r transcendental, and use
[QSO2's retained Long formulas](QUADRATIC_OPERATOR_MATE_CLASSIFICATION.md):
\[
            s=2v+4a-3ra^2,\qquad
            t=(ra^3-a^2-va)/2.                                 \tag{3}
\]
Here a=eta+c(r,v), v=1/x and r=R, not QGR1's R/2.
The coefficient shift does not change the rational field or momentum
order. The variables v,a are independent over k, and the relative
Poisson bracket is {a,v}=1/v, so {s,t}=1.

Eliminating v gives
\[
 f(X)=rX^3-2X^2+sX+4t,\qquad
                 f(a)=0,\quad f'(a)=2v.                        \tag{4}
\]
The map pi:A^2_{a,v} -> A^2_{s,t} is finite of degree three.
Indeed v=(s-4a+3ra^2)/2, so its coordinate ring is
k[s,t,a]/(f(a)), free of rank three with basis 1,a,a^2.
For independence, over k(s), t=-(ra^3-2a^2+sa)/4 is a polynomial
of degree three in the transcendental a. The extension of rational
function fields therefore has degree three. The determinant of the
Jacobian of (s,t) with respect to (v,a) is -v.

On E_0={v=0}, put a=z. Its image is the irreducible divisor D
parametrized by
\[
             s_0=4z-3rz^2,\qquad
             t_0=(rz^3-z^2)/2.                                \tag{5}
\]
The same cubic at these values factors as
\[
             f(X)=r(X-z)^2(X-b),\qquad b=2/r-2z.
\]
The other point over this generic divisor has
\[
                a=b,\qquad
                v=v_1=(2-3rz)^2/(2r),                         \tag{6}
\]
and gives exactly the same (s_0,t_0). Its v coordinate is
nonconstant. This is the divisor
E_1={8rv-(3ra-2)^2=0}, which also dominates D.
Both source divisors are prime and distinct. The target and source
planes are normal, so the preceding lemma applies.

For an additional exact check, the discriminant is
\[
 \begin{split}
 \Delta(s,t)&=4s^2-4rs^3+128t-144rst-432r^2t^2,\\
 \Delta(s(a,v),t(a,v))
            &=4v^2((3ra-2)^2-8rv).                            \tag{7}
 \end{split}
\]
Thus E_0 is the double branch and E_1 the simple branch over the
generic discriminant. Although v=0 lies outside the original
x=1/v chart, valuations at this boundary are valid for its rational
function field. They are exactly the information lost by looking
only inside the open chart. Equations (2)--(6) prove (1).

The finite extension is essential to this argument. Birational
identity coordinates, for example s=v, t=a, have a nonconstant
intersection with k(v), and do not meet the two-branch hypotheses.

## Deformation preserves this field-intersection obstruction

Write C=k((h)), F=C(v), and let
\[
                 {\cal D}=F[\eta;h v^{-1}\partial_v].
\]
Its h-adic Gauss valuation is the minimum valuation of its finitely
many operator coefficients. Products add valuations: reduction of
normalized factors is multiplication of nonzero polynomials in eta
over k(v), since commutators are divisible by h. It extends to
the Ore division ring Q(D), whose residue field is k(v,eta).
Take its h-adic completion, denoted Ehat. It is a complete division
ring with the same residue field. The completed coefficient field
is Fhat=k(v)((h)); C is its scalar subfield.

Any formal exact-R fiber lift S_h,T_h, even with unbounded spatial
degree, embeds in Ehat. The polynomial Ore change to b,d in
[QGR1](BOUNDED_GAUGE_RIGIDITY.md) extends continuously to the h
completion. The exact commutator with R differentiates the d powers,
so all positive d coefficients vanish. Each remaining h coefficient
has finitely many b powers and localizes to a finite differential
operator. Thus the series converges in Ehat. The images satisfy
[S_h,T_h]=h and reduce to s,t from (3).

The source Weyl algebra over C with generators sigma,tau and
[sigma,tau]=h embeds by sigma -> S_h, tau -> T_h.
It preserves h valuation: the leading coefficient of any normalized
nonzero source polynomial maps to a nonzero polynomial in s,t,
because these are algebraically independent. The embedding extends
to its Ore division ring, then isometrically to its completion.
Let Bhat be the resulting complete image subfield of Ehat.
Its residue field is k(s,t). Consequently
\[
                              Bhat\cap Fhat=C.                 \tag{8}
\]

Here is the full argument for the last step. The first nonzero h
coefficient of an element in this intersection lies in both residue
fields, so (1) makes it scalar in k. Subtract that scalar times its
h power. Repeat at each successive valuation. Completeness of C
gives a scalar Laurent series with all the same coefficients.
Separatedness of the ambient valuation forces equality. Arbitrary
formal corrections and arbitrary degrees in the source do not evade
this conclusion.

Equation (8) is a general deformation lemma for an intersection of
residue fields equal to scalars. Boundedness is not needed for it.
For the next consequence boundedness matters: finite spatial support
places actual images in D over F=C(v), rather than only in Ehat or
among differential operators with potentially nonalgebraic coefficients.

## No scalar source polarization of order zero, one or two

Suppose P,Q lie in Bhat intersect D and have commutator
a nonzero element of C. Neither can have order zero by (8).
An order-one P also leads to a contradiction in every partner order.
Write P=a(v)eta+b(v). Its leading commutator with an order-m mate
forces the leading coefficient divided by a^m to be a differential
constant in C. Subtract that constant times P^m, and repeat.
The result is
\[
                      Q=A(P)+g(v),\qquad A\in C[t].             \tag{9}
\]
Then g lies in Bhat intersect F=C, making the commutator zero.
Equivalently one can use P itself as the first-order differential
generator, with coefficient derivation a(v)h*v^{-1}partial_v.

If P has order two, the
[QSO2 classification](QUADRATIC_OPERATOR_MATE_CLASSIFICATION.md)
gives Q=A(P)+Q_1 with A in C[t] and Q_1 of order one.
Both Q_1 and its mate P still lie in Bhat intersect D, which
the order-one argument forbids. We have proved
\[
 \boxed{\text{Every finite scalar Weyl pair in }Bhat\cap{\cal D}
                       \text{ has both orders at least three.}} \tag{10}
\]
The statement uses the generic algebraic coefficient field F=C(v).
It does not assert the same for finite-order operators whose
coefficients are arbitrary elements of k(v)((h)).

For a bounded exact-R Long lift, all polynomial expressions in its
fiber images over C belong to D. Every source Weyl automorphism of
this pair has a polynomial inverse and produces another pair in the
same image algebra. No degree, word length, h-adic contact or
regularity restriction on that source automorphism is required.
Even a rational source substitution is covered if it gives a
canonical pair consisting of two finite differential operators.
This closes low-order normalizations of the whole fiber image,
rather than merely constraining the displayed S_h.

Scalar coefficient-field coordinate changes, nonzero rescalings
of the differential generator and shifts of that generator by
coefficient functions preserve differential order. The conclusion
also survives these changes of scalar presentation. Mixed target
canonical changes need not preserve the coefficient field and
remain outside this assertion.

## Full image versus the position ring, including positional deformations

First establish the classical global intersection. Let Phi_0 be
Long's full polynomial symplectic map, from source coordinates
(X,Q,P,Z) to target (x,q,p,z), with first image R.
If Phi_0(H) lies in K[x,q], it commutes with R. Injectivity of
Phi_0 and Poisson compatibility imply H_P=0.
Thus H belongs to K[X,Q,Z]. Localizing K[X] at its nonzero
elements, equation (1) makes Phi_0(H) a rational function of R alone.
But
K(R) intersect K[x,q]=K[R]: a reduced denominator in R cannot
cancel its coprime numerator, and a nonconstant polynomial in R
is not a unit, as seen by setting x=1. Hence
\[
                \Phi_0(K[X,Q,P,Z])\cap K[x,q]=K[R].             \tag{11}
\]
Injectivity then says that its preimage consists exactly of K[X].

Now let Phi_h be any full formal polynomial-coefficient quantum
lift of Phi_0, and assume only that its first image U_h is positional:
U_h lies in K[x,q][[h]] and U_0=R. It need not equal R.
Classical injectivity makes Phi_h injective and valuation preserving.
For H in the source with Phi_h(H) positional,
[Phi_h(H),U_h]=0 implies [H,X]=0. The exact source centralizer
is K[X,Q,Z][[h]], because [H,X]=h*partial_P(H).

Split H=A(X,h)+G, where G has only source fiber PBW monomials
Q^i Z^j with i+j>0. Since A(U_h,h) is positional, Phi_h(G)
is positional as well. If G were nonzero, its first nonzero h
coefficient g would contain fiber variables, while Phi_0(g)
would be positional. This contradicts (11) and injectivity.
Therefore G=0, and
\[
 \operatorname{im}(\Phi_h)\cap K[x,q][[h]]
       =\{A(U_h,h):A\in K[X][[h]]\}.                            \tag{12}
\]
The notation is evaluation of coefficientwise polynomial series;
it does not claim finite polynomial generation.

If Phi_h has a common finite spatial bound, pass to L=K((h))
and its finite-PBW endomorphism. For a finite source polynomial
the same split has A in L[X], and a nonzero G has a least
scalar coefficient valuation even when poles are allowed. The
same argument proves the stronger finite statement
\[
                   \operatorname{im}(\Phi_L)\cap L[x,q]
                                      =L[U_h].                 \tag{13}
\]
No order constraint, scalar centralizer theorem or bound on
a conjugate image is assumed in (12)--(13).

It follows that no polynomial source Weyl automorphism, even one
moving X and of arbitrary degree, can make both position images
positional in this target chart: two algebraically independent
elements cannot lie in L[U_h]. Target automorphisms preserving
the target position algebra preserve the same conclusion.
General target changes moving that algebra remain open.

This is an unconditional obstruction to a source cotangent
polarization when the first image stays positional. It is
different from the prescribed arithmetic-center condition of
[QFL3](ARITHMETIC_COTANGENT_EXCLUSION.md). It does not exclude
every canonical word or every nonpositional first image.

## Ambient local nilpotence in the exact-R case

For a bounded exact-R lift there is also a complete description
of the ambient ad-nilpotent elements in its fiber image.
Here ad-nilpotent means its inner derivation is locally nilpotent
on the whole finite target Weyl algebra, not merely on the image.

In a characteristic-zero domain, if a locally nilpotent derivation
kills a nonzero product ab, it kills a and b separately.
Indeed exp(t delta)(a) and exp(t delta)(b) are polynomials in t.
Their product is the nonzero constant ab; polynomial degrees
add in a domain, so each polynomial is constant.

An element H commuting with R=2x(1-3xq/2) whose inner derivation
is locally nilpotent must therefore commute with x and
1-3xq/2, hence also q. The joint centralizer of x,q in A_2
is its position algebra. Conversely every positional polynomial
has locally nilpotent inner derivation, because commutation
strictly lowers momentum order. Combining this with (13) gives
\[
 \{H\in\operatorname{im}(\Phi_L):[H,R]=0,\
             \operatorname{ad}H\text{ locally nilpotent on }A_2(L)\}
                                      =L[R].                  \tag{14}
\]
Thus every noncentral source fiber observable loses ambient
ad-nilpotence, although the intrinsic fiber Weyl algebra has its
usual ad-nilpotent coordinate subalgebras. No contradiction follows:
local nilpotence on a proper subalgebra need not extend to the
ambient algebra.

This distinction is relevant to the
[Berest--Wilson theory of mad subalgebras](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0501191v3).
Their terminology and classification concern actual ambient
ad-nilpotence under specified hypotheses. No such classification
is silently applied to this higher-rank centralizer; the elementary
factor argument proves exactly (14).

## What remains and what was checked

The ramification boundary now forbids every positional observable
beyond the first image for positional-first full lifts, and forbids
every low-order scalar canonical pair in the exact-R generic
fiber image. Higher orders cannot be removed by arbitrary polynomial
source changes to reach an order-two string pair.

The unresolved possibilities are high-order scalar image pairs,
a genuinely different target polarization, or a first image with
momentum terms. The full conjugate relation does not by itself
supply the ambient local nilpotence or low-order normalization
that these results rule out. A new geometric constraint or an
actual bounded full construction is still required.
