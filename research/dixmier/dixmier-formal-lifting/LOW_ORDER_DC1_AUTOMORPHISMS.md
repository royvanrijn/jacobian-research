# Low differential order forces a rank-one Weyl endomorphism to be an automorphism

Let \(k\) have characteristic zero and write
\[
 A_1(k)=k[x][D;\partial_x],\qquad [D,x]=1.
\]
Differential order means order in \(D\), with order zero for a nonzero
multiplication polynomial. This is not total polynomial degree.

**QLO1.** If a unital k-algebra endomorphism of \(A_1(k)\) has at least one generator
image of differential order at most three, it is an automorphism.
Consequently a nonsurjective endomorphism must have both generator
orders at least four. The conclusion also holds after any finite source
or target Weyl automorphism. It supplies no theorem putting an arbitrary
endomorphism into this low-order class.

The order-two and order-three inputs are the finite scalar mate
classifications [QSO2](QUADRATIC_OPERATOR_MATE_CLASSIFICATION.md) and
[QSO3A](ALL_CUBIC_ALGEBRAIC_STRING_OBSTRUCTION.md). The global conclusion
below uses their polynomial-in-the-first-operator statement over the
original constant field. It does not treat an algebraic normalization
as a polynomial Weyl coordinate change.

## An actual first-order polynomial image generates the whole algebra with its mate

Suppose \(U,V\in A_1(k)\), \(U=a(x)D+b(x)\), \(a\ne0\), and
\([V,U]=\kappa\in k^\times\). If \(V\) has positive order \(m\) and
leading coefficient \(v_m(x)\), the order-\(m\) commutator coefficient is
\[
                    m v_m a'-a v_m'=0 .
\]
It follows that \((v_m/a^m)'=0\), hence \(v_m/a^m=c_m\in k\).
Subtracting \(c_mU^m\) lowers the differential order, stays in
\(A_1(k)\), and preserves the commutator. Finite repetition gives
\[
                         V=B(U)+f(x),
                         \qquad B\in k[t],\quad f\in k[x].
\]
The same identity holds with \(B=0\) if \(V\) starts at order zero.
Now
\[
                       [f,U]=-a(x)f'(x)=\kappa .
\]
The product of these two polynomials is a nonzero constant. Therefore
\(a\) is a nonzero constant and \(f=\alpha x+\beta\), \(\alpha\ne0\).

The subalgebra generated by \(U,V\) contains \(f=V-B(U)\), hence \(x\),
and then contains
\[
                         D=a^{-1}\bigl(U-b(x)\bigr).
\]
It is all of \(A_1(k)\). This argument constructs the required
polynomial expressions in the two images; no division by a spatial
polynomial is used.

If one member of a Weyl pair instead has order zero, it is a nonconstant
\(f(x)\). If its mate had order \(m>1\), their commutator would have the
nonzero order-\(m-1\) leading coefficient proportional to \(m f'\).
Thus the mate has order one, and the preceding argument applies.

## Applying the scalar classifications preserves global polynomial admission

Let \(P,Q\) be the images of the two Weyl generators, so their commutator
is a nonzero scalar. If one has order two or three, regard that image
as \(P\) and apply the corresponding classification in \(k(x)[D]\):
\[
                    Q=A(P)+Q_1,\qquad A\in k[t],
                    \qquad \operatorname{ord}_D Q_1=1 .
\]
Although a proof of the classification may temporarily normalize over
an algebraic coefficient extension, this displayed conclusion descends
to the original field \(k(x)\), with constants in \(k\).

Crucially, \(P,Q\) already belong to the polynomial Weyl algebra.
Therefore \(Q_1=Q-A(P)\) belongs to that same algebra. The subalgebra
generated by \(P,Q\) equals the one generated by \(P,Q_1\), and
\([P,Q_1]=[P,Q]\). The actual first-order argument proves that this is
all of \(A_1(k)\). This is actual global landing of \(Q_1\), not an
inference from a formal or algebraic coordinate normalization.

For completeness, unital Weyl endomorphisms in characteristic zero are
injective. A nonzero two-sided ideal contains a nonzero polynomial in
\(x\): repeatedly commute a nonzero operator with \(x\) to remove its
differential order. Repeated commutation of that polynomial with \(D\)
then gives a nonzero constant. Thus \(A_1(k)\) is simple. Surjectivity
proved above consequently makes the endomorphism an automorphism.

Finite precomposition or postcomposition by a Weyl automorphism
preserves being an automorphism. If any such presentation has an image
of order at most three, the original endomorphism is therefore an
automorphism as well.

## Scope

This is a global corollary of the stated scalar classifications and an
elementary first-order reduction. It does not prove that arbitrary
rank-one endomorphisms admit a low-order presentation, does not cover
every higher-order pair, and does not resolve DC1 or DC2. No bounded
rank-two lift is supplied. No finite search or new computational
certificate is needed for the global corollary; its mathematical inputs
remain the two scoped classifications. No formal verification,
independent whole-proof replay, external review or priority claim is made.
