# A nonradical cubic Weyl pair and the classical quintic cover square

**QCS1.** An explicit finite rational differential pair has a non-Galois
cubic division extension. Its classical covering is abstractly the same
cubic field extension as Long's generic relative fibre. Matching the
Poisson structures introduces a compatible pair of quintic covers.

This gives a concrete positive input beyond the radical-cover orbit
excluded by QRG1. The quintic square is classical; it does not supply
an actual finite Weyl realization of its upper field, a bounded Long
lift, or global admission in the polynomial centralizer of \(R\).

The displayed quantum compatibility model is now excluded by
[QQL1's exact scalar spectrum](QUINTIC_ORDERING_LAURENT_OBSTRUCTION.md).
In the entire actual source Weyl division ring, no required Euler
pair exists and no equation \(P^5=U G(J)\), with \(G\) rational
and nonzero, has a solution.
The classical square, its individual quantum models, and the conditional
fourth-image formulas below remain valid. Broader nonhomogeneous
quantum models remain open.

## An actual finite rational cubic pair

Let \(k\) have characteristic zero, \(h\in k^\times\), and use
\(k(y)[b;h\partial_y]\), with \([b,y]=h\). Put
\[
 f(y)=-\frac{y^3}{y+1},\qquad
 f'(y)=-\frac{y^2(2y+3)}{(y+1)^2},\qquad
 \mathsf T=(f'(y))^{-1}b
          =-\frac{(y+1)^2}{y^2(2y+3)}\,b .
\]
Then
\[
                              [\mathsf T,f]=h.              \tag{1}
\]
All operators are finite. Their coefficient poles are confined to
\(y=0,-1,-3/2\) and infinity. In particular they belong to a finite
rational differential algebra, with no formal series in the momentum.

Write \(E_0\) for the image Weyl division ring generated by \(f,\mathsf T\),
and \(Q_0=k(y)(b)\) for the ambient differential-operator division ring.
Then \(Q_0=\langle E_0,y\rangle_{\rm div}\), since
\(b=f'(y)\mathsf T\), and
\[
                              y^3+f y+f=0.                  \tag{2}
\]
The coefficient function-field extension \(k(y)/k(f)\) has degree three.
Its discriminant is \(-f^2(4f+27)\), which is nonsquare over algebraically
closed constants, so the cubic is non-Galois with normal closure \(S_3\).
The left and right division indices of \(Q_0/E_0\) are also three:
apply QDI1's differential-field basis argument to \(k(y)/k(f)\), with
the derivation \((f')^{-1}\partial_y\).
This example is outside QRG1's internal-radical-tower construction orbit.

For compatibility with the Poisson convention \(\{s,t\}=1\) below,
use the exact pair
\[
                  \mathsf U=f(y),\qquad
                  \mathsf Z=-\mathsf T,\qquad
                         [\mathsf U,\mathsf Z]=h .
\]
Its source conjugate is \(\boldsymbol\eta=-b\), so
\([y,\boldsymbol\eta]=h\) and
\(\boldsymbol\eta=f'(y)\mathsf Z\) exactly.
These signs only choose the order of the canonical pair.

## The Long cubic and the rational change of its base parameters

Work on the generic relative fibre \(r\ne0\) of Long's retained chart;
\(r\) is a differential constant for this two-dimensional calculation.
The [classical chart in QDI1](QUANTUM_DIVISION_AND_COMPLETION.md) gives
\[
 \{s,t\}=1,\qquad
      r a^3-2a^2+s a+4t=0,\qquad
      v=(s-4a+3ra^2)/2,\qquad \{a,v\}=1/v.                 \tag{3}
\]
Set
\[
 \begin{split}
 z&=a-\frac2{3r},\\
 p&=\frac sr-\frac4{3r^2},&
 q&=\frac{4t}{r}+\frac{2s}{3r^2}-\frac{16}{27r^3}.
 \end{split}
\]
Then
\[
                     z^3+pz+q=0,\qquad \{p,q\}=4/r^2.      \tag{4}
\]
On the function-field open \(pq\ne0\), put
\[
           w=q/p,\qquad u=p^3/q^2,\qquad z=wy.
\]
The inverse base change is \(p=uw^2,\ q=uw^3\). Equation (4)
becomes
\[
                           y^3+uy+u=0,\qquad u=f(y).       \tag{5}
\]
Thus, as abstract relative function-field extensions,
Long is precisely \(k(u,w)\subset k(y,w)\), the rational cubic
cover (2) with the independent parameter \(w\).

This rational identification of fields does not preserve the
standard cotangent Poisson structure.

## The canonical partner of \(u\) requires a quintic cover

Direct differentiation gives
\[
 dp\wedge dq=uw^4\,du\wedge dw,\qquad
                         \{u,w\}=\frac4{r^2uw^4}.
\]
Therefore
\[
                    \zeta=\frac{r^2}{20}uw^5,\qquad
                             \{u,\zeta\}=1.                \tag{6}
\]
But
\[
              [\,k(u,w):k(u,\zeta)\,]=5,\qquad
                      w^5=\frac{20\zeta}{r^2u}.            \tag{7}
\]
The degree is exactly five, for example by Eisenstein at \(\zeta\)
in the polynomial ring \(k(u)[\zeta]\).

Nor can another rational conjugate retaining \(u\) repair this.
The equation \(\{u,\widetilde\zeta\}=1\) forces
\(\partial_w\widetilde\zeta=r^2uw^4/4\), so
\(\widetilde\zeta=\zeta+g(u)\), \(g(u)\in k(u)\).
Every such choice generates the same subfield as \(\zeta\).
Thus none is a birational canonical normalization with position \(u\).

On the upper cubic field set
\[
                  \eta=f'(y)\zeta,\qquad \{y,\eta\}=1.
\]
Its pulled-back Poisson bracket is
\[
                    \{y,w\}=\frac4{r^2f(y)f'(y)w^4},
\]
and
\[
                  w^5=\frac{20\eta}{r^2 f(y)f'(y)}.        \tag{8}
\]
The four fields and their exact degrees form the square
\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 k(y,\eta)&\subset&k(y,w)\\
 \cup&&\cup\\
 k(u,\zeta)&\subset&k(u,w).
 \end{array}
\]
Both horizontal extensions have degree five. Both vertical extensions
have degree three. The upper field is the compositum of the upper-left
and lower-right fields, whose intersection is the lower-left field:
the degrees three and five are coprime. The left column is the
classical cotangent covering quantized by (1), with the sign convention
\(\zeta=-\mathsf T\). The right column is Long's generic relative map.

## The upper field really is Long's original source field

The source coordinates can be recovered without extracting a root:
\[
 \xi=a-\frac2{3r}=wy,\qquad
 v=\frac r2 w^2\frac{y^2(2y+3)}{y+1}.
\]
Conversely, put
\[
                \kappa=\frac{2v}{r\xi^2},\qquad
                y=\frac{3-\kappa}{\kappa-2},\qquad
                w=\xi/y .
\]
These are rational inverse formulas on function fields. With the
pulled-back bracket above they satisfy \(\{a,v\}=1/v\), exactly
as in (3). Thus the upper-right field has the required classical
Long source Poisson structure.

All these statements concern rational function fields on \(r\ne0\).
They assert no polynomial change of the original four variables,
no control at \(r=0\), and no preservation of the integral lattice.

## Actual quantum quintic models and an excluded compatibility equation

Each horizontal classical quintic cover separately has an actual Weyl
division model. Set \(L=K_0((h))\); the actual generic coefficient field
for the conditional model tested below is \(L(r)\), and its source division ring
is \(\operatorname{Frac}(L(r)(v)[b;h\partial_v])\).
For the classical-symbol assertions and the Long-matching
equation below, the ordering parameters \(\alpha,\gamma,\beta\) are
\(h\)-regular scalars, for example fixed elements of \(K(r)\) or elements
of \(K(r)[[h]]\).
The displayed generic canonical identities also hold for arbitrary
generic scalars, but parameter poles can change the classical cover.
For the target use an actual pair
\[
 [\mathsf p,\mathsf q]=c h,\qquad c=4/r^2,\qquad
 J=\mathsf q\mathsf p.
\]
For any scalar ordering parameter \(\beta\), put
\[
 \mathsf U_\beta=\mathsf p^5[J(J-\beta c h)]^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mathsf Z_\beta=\frac1{5c}\mathsf U_\beta^{-1}J.             \tag{9}
\]
Since \([J,\mathsf U_\beta]=-5c h\mathsf U_\beta\),
\[
 [\mathsf U_\beta,\mathsf Z_\beta]=h,\qquad
 J=5c\mathsf U_\beta\mathsf Z_\beta,\qquad
 \mathsf p^5=\mathsf U_\beta J(J-\beta c h).
\]
The ambient division ring is generated over the pair's image by
\(\mathsf p\), since \(\mathsf q=J\mathsf p^{-1}\).
All \(\beta\) have classical symbols \(u=p^3q^{-2}\) and
\(\zeta=p^{-2}q^3/(5c)=r^2uw^5/20\).
The value \(\beta=1\) gives the familiar ordering
\(\mathsf U_1=\mathsf p^3\mathsf q^{-2}\).
These formulas involve actual finite division expressions; they do
not assert finite polynomial differential order of every expression.

For the source let \([b,v]=h\), \(H_s=vb\), and choose scalars
\(\alpha,\gamma\). Define
\[
 \kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}=
       \frac2r v^5[(H_s+\alpha h)(H_s+\gamma h)]^{-1},
 \qquad
 K=-\frac15\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}^{-1}H_s .
\]
Then \([H_s,\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}]=5h\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}\)
and \([\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma},K]=h\).
The case \((\alpha,\gamma)=(2,1)\) is exactly
\(\kappa=(2/r)v^3b^{-2}\); all choices have that same classical
symbol. The source division ring is generated over the
\((\kappa,K)\)-image by the radical \(v\), with
\[
 v^5=(r/2)\kappa(H_s+\alpha h)(H_s+\gamma h),\qquad b=v^{-1}H_s.
\]

Both actual quintic division indices are exactly five, on the left
and right. For the source, the image division ring generated by
\(\kappa,K\) contains \(H_s\) and \(v^5\).
Conjugation by \(v\) sends \(H_s\) to \(H_s-h\) and sends \(\kappa\)
to \(\kappa Q(H_s)Q(H_s-h)^{-1}\), where
\(Q(H_s)=(H_s+\alpha h)(H_s+\gamma h)\).
It therefore preserves that image division ring in both directions.
The span of \(1,v,\ldots,v^4\) over it is a finite subring of the
ambient division ring; multiplication by any nonzero element is
injective and hence surjective on this finite-dimensional span.
The span is consequently a division ring and is the whole source.
The fifth-root deck action separates these five basis terms, proving
their independence after adjoining roots of unity, and thus before
that extension as well. The left-handed argument is identical.
For the target use \(\mathsf p,J\) in place of \(v,H_s\).
This proves the exact indices, rather than inferring them from a
noncommutative root relation alone.


Inside this actual source division ring abbreviate
\(\kappa=\kappa_{\alpha,\gamma}\) and set, with coefficients on the left,
\[
 y=\frac{3-\kappa}{\kappa-2},\qquad
 \boldsymbol\eta=-(\kappa-2)^2K,\qquad
 \mathsf U=f(y),\qquad \mathsf Z=(f'(y))^{-1}\boldsymbol\eta .
\]
These are actual canonical pairs:
\([y,\boldsymbol\eta]=[\mathsf U,\mathsf Z]=h\).
The cubic pair is the same finite rational cotangent construction
as (1), now embedded in the source Weyl division ring through its
quintic model.

The transported target Euler element and target root equation are explicit:
\[
 \begin{split}
 U(\kappa)&=\frac{(\kappa-3)^3}{(\kappa-2)^2},&
 R(\kappa)&=-\frac{(\kappa-3)(\kappa-2)}{\kappa^2},\\
 \mathsf J&=5c\mathsf U\mathsf Z=cR(\kappa)H_s,&
 \mathcal A_\beta&=
       \mathsf U\mathsf J(\mathsf J-\beta c h).              \tag{10}
 \end{split}
\]
Compatibility in this elementary model would require an actual element
\(\mathsf P\) of the source Weyl division ring satisfying
\[
 \boxed{\quad
      \mathsf P^5=\mathcal A_\beta,\qquad
      \bar{\mathsf P}=2v/r-3(b/v)^2 ,
 \quad}                                                    \tag{11}
\]
for some \(\alpha,\gamma,\beta\). Its classical reduction is already
the required fifth power by the preceding field square.

There is useful ordering freedom in this equation. In \(H_s\)-normal
order, using \([H_s,g(\kappa)]=5h\kappa g'(\kappa)\), one has
\[
 \mathcal A_\beta=c^2U
       \bigl(R^2H_s^2+h(5\kappa RR'-\beta R)H_s\bigr).
\]
For \(\beta=1\) the coefficient of \(H_s\) has a simple
\(\kappa=2\) pole. It cancels at the allowed ordering \(\beta=5/2\):
\[
 \mathcal A_{5/2}
 =c^2\left[
       \frac{(\kappa-3)^5}{\kappa^4}H_s^2+
       \frac{5h}{2}
        \frac{(\kappa-3)^4(\kappa+12)}{\kappa^4}H_s
       \right].                                           \tag{12}
\]
Thus that particular pole is not an invariant obstruction to this
construction. Equation (12) is an actual rational differential
operator in the canonical \(\kappa\)-chart. By QQL1 it is not
a fifth power in the actual source Weyl division ring.

A root in (11) would automatically extend the target canonical pair.
Indeed \([\mathsf J,\mathcal A_\beta]=-5c h\mathcal A_\beta\).
Put \(X=[\mathsf J,\mathsf P]+c h\mathsf P\).
Then \(\sum_{i=0}^4\mathsf P^iX\mathsf P^{4-i}=0\).
At the first nonzero \(h\)-valuation of \(X\), the commutative residue
is \(5\bar{\mathsf P}^{\,4}\bar X\), which is nonzero.
Hence \(X=0\). Therefore
\(\mathsf Q=\mathsf J\mathsf P^{-1}\) satisfies
\([\mathsf P,\mathsf Q]=c h\). Undoing (4) would give an actual
generic rational relative Long pair.
This proves sufficiency of the exact root identity, not its existence.
The resulting relative division index would be exactly three:
the source division ring has index \(5\cdot3=15\) over the lower
cubic image, while the embedded target division ring has index five
over that same image. Multiplication of the finite left and right
vector-space indices gives three in both directions.

## The conditional fourth-image formula

The following implication is algebraically valid, while QQL1 has
ruled out its displayed root premise in the elementary family.
Use \(L=K_0((h))\) and assume an actual root
\[
       \mathsf P\in
          \operatorname{Frac}\bigl(L(r)(v)[b;h\partial_v]\bigr)
\]
in (11), with its stated \(h\)-regular residue.
Require \(\alpha,\gamma,\beta\in K_0[[h]]\), independent of \(r\).
The larger coefficient field \(K_0(r)((h))\) cannot silently replace
\(L(r)\): an element of that larger field need not have one finite
rational presentation in the spatial variable \(r\).

In QPH1's exact chart \([d,r]=h\), \([a,v]=h/v\), with the other
chart brackets zero. Put
\[
 b=v\left(a-\frac2{3r}\right),\qquad
 \widetilde d=d+\frac{v^2}{3r^2},\qquad
 D_s=\widetilde d+\frac{H_s}{5r}
       =d+\frac{v^2a}{5r}+\frac{v^2}{5r^2}.
\]
Then \([\widetilde d,v]=[\widetilde d,b]=0\),
\([D_s,r]=h\), and \(D_s\) commutes with \(\kappa,K,\mathsf U,\mathsf Z\).
Since \(c=4/r^2\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 [D_s,\mathsf J]&=-2h\mathsf J/r,&
 [D_s,\mathsf P]&=-4h\mathsf P/(5r),&
 [D_s,\mathsf Q]&=-6h\mathsf Q/(5r).
 \end{split}
\]
The middle identity follows from (11) by the same first-residue
injectivity used above: \([D_s,\mathcal A_\beta]=-4h\mathcal A_\beta/r\).

Undo the depressed-cubic affine change by defining
\[
 S=r\mathsf P+\frac4{3r},\qquad
 T=\frac r4\mathsf Q-\frac S{6r}+\frac4{27r^2}.
\]
They satisfy \([S,T]=h\), and
\[
 h^{-1}[D_s,S]=\frac S{5r}-\frac8{5r^2},\qquad
 h^{-1}[D_s,T]=-\frac T{5r}+\frac S{10r^2}.
\]
The ordered quadratic polynomial
\[
 F=\frac{ST}{5r}-\frac{8T}{5r^2}-\frac{S^2}{20r^2},
 \qquad                         D=D_s+F
\]
therefore gives the entire actual rational tuple:
\[
                  [D,r]=[S,T]=h,\qquad
                  [D,S]=[D,T]=[r,S]=[r,T]=0.              \tag{12a}
\]
Only finite rational expressions were used, so this is an actual
rank-two Weyl division-ring realization over \(L\), conditional
on the root in the stated coefficient field. It is \(h\)-regular
for the rational Gauss valuation.

Its classical fourth image is exactly Long's prescribed image,
without an additional function of \(r\). Direct substitution of
(3) gives
\[
 \overline D
     =d+\frac{a^4}{4}-\frac{3ra^5}{10}+\frac{va^3}{2}.      \tag{12b}
\]
A root existing only after enlarging the scalar coefficient field
does not, by this argument, give a finite rational realization in
the original rank-two Weyl division ring.


Equation (11) is a closed negative construction gate.
A new construction must alter the elementary quantum model, rather than
seek another root or another scalar ordering in that equation.
A formal fifth root does not contradict this exclusion: its unbounded
spatial tails can lie outside the actual source division ring.

The [division-root and finite-completion obstructions QQL1](QUINTIC_ORDERING_LAURENT_OBSTRUCTION.md)
also rule out actual division-ring Euler pairs for every rational
homogeneous source Euler function and every target ordering retaining
the fixed cubic Euler relation. A bounded polynomial construction
therefore needs a genuinely nonhomogeneous source or cubic quantum
model, or another route with its own global admission proof.
The individual source and target Weyl division models do not make
their cubic transport compatible.

With Long's regular classical root, QQL1's deck-character obstruction
also excludes every rational homogeneous target Euler ordering
\(U=P^5F_t(J)\), not only the quadratic denominator in (9).
This does not exclude other quantum cubic embeddings or infer that
every operator commuting with \(J\) is rational in \(J\).

This square is not excluded by QRG1: the quintic operation changes
both the image and ambient division rings, and the cubic step is
nonradical. It is not an internal radical tower from the full Long
image division ring to its source division ring.

The coefficient poles in (1), and the additional denominators
\(r,p,q\) in the field changes, remain real admission questions.
In particular the exact rational cubic pair is not a globally admitted
pair in \(K[[h]][X,Q][B;h\Delta]\).

## An explicit binary-dihedral ambient fixed-division model

The nonradical cubic in (2) also has an actual finite-group Weyl
division model. This supplies an explicit ambient S3 fixed-division
model whose classical field is the cubic normal closure; it does not
change the Poisson matching requirement in (6)--(8).

Work over characteristic-zero constants containing i and a primitive
sixth root epsilon, and write omega=epsilon^2. In the Weyl division
ring D=k(x)(p), [p,x]=h!=0, take
\[
 g(x)=\epsilon x,\quad g(p)=\epsilon^{-1}p,\qquad
 j(x)=p,\quad j(p)=-x .
\]
Then G=<g,j> is binary dihedral of order twelve:
g^6=1, j^2=g^3=-I, and jgj^{-1}=g^{-1}.
Its subgroup H=<j> has order four and index three.

The following generator formulas include the quantum correction:
\[
 t=px^{-1},\quad Z=x^2,\quad z=-it,\quad
             b=-\frac{i}{2}Z+\frac{h}{4z},\qquad [b,z]=h.  \tag{13}
\]
Put D_0=k(z)(b). The central involution fixes D_0 and
\[
                              D^{\{1,-I\}}=D_0.           \tag{14}
\]
Here is a finite division-algebra proof. The inverse formulas recover
t and Z from z,b. Also x^2=Z and
x t x^{-1}=t-h/Z, so x normalizes D_0 and
D_0+xD_0 is a subring of D. Its two summands are independent:
the central involution fixes the first and negates the second.
Left multiplication by a nonzero member is an injective endomorphism
of a two-dimensional right D_0-vector space, hence is surjective.
Thus the subring is a division ring. It contains x and p=t*x,
so equals D, proving (14).

On D_0 the quotient group G/{1,-I} is exactly S3, acting by
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 g(z)=\omega^{-1}z,&g(b)=\omega b,\\
 j(z)=z^{-1},&j(b)=-z^2b .
 \end{array}                                             \tag{15}
\]
To check the correction in (13), the original identities are
[t,Z]=2h and j(Z)=t^2 Z-ht. They give (15) with the displayed
left coefficient ordering. Omitting h/(4z) fails the pure
cotangent formula. This is an exact rational quantum calculation,
rather than an inference from a classical invariant field.

Set
\[
 y=-\frac{3z}{z^2+z+1},\quad
 u=\frac{27z^3}{(z^3-1)^2}=f(y),\qquad
 \eta=-(y'(z))^{-1}b,\quad \zeta=-(u'(z))^{-1}b.           \tag{16}
\]
Then [y,eta]=[u,zeta]=h, eta=f'(y)*zeta exactly, and
\[
                      D^H=k(y)(\eta),\qquad
                      D^G=k(u)(\zeta).                   \tag{17}
\]
These fixed-field equalities can be proved without importing a
general noncommutative Noether theorem. The rational maps y and u
have degrees two and six, and are invariant under the reflection
and S3 actions respectively. Their coefficient fixed fields are
therefore k(y) and k(u).

For either coefficient action Gamma, let q be its invariant coordinate,
P_q=-(q')^{-1}b, and E=k(q)(P_q). A coefficient-field basis c_1,...,c_d
of k(z)/k(q) is a right E-basis of D_0. Independence follows by
clearing common right differential-operator denominators and comparing
the finite momentum coefficients. For spanning, the space sum c_i E
is preserved by left multiplication by k(z) and b: differentiation
preserves k(z). It is therefore preserved by every differential
polynomial, and by its inverse because injective right E-linear
endomorphisms of this finite-dimensional space are surjective.
It consequently equals the differential division ring D_0.

Gamma fixes P_q and acts only on the coefficient basis in this
right-vector-space description. Its averaging projector thus has
image k(q)E=E. This proves (17); no averaging of noncommutative
fractions as though they commuted is involved. The left basis argument
is the same. Consequently
\[
 [D_0:D^G]_\ell=[D_0:D^G]_r=6,\quad
 [D_0:D^H]_\ell=[D_0:D^H]_r=2,\quad
 [D^H:D^G]_\ell=[D^H:D^G]_r=3.                            \tag{18}
\]
The three conjugates y(omega^i*z) generate the coefficient normal
closure k(z), whose group is S3. Thus (14)--(18) give an explicit
ambient S3 fixed-division presentation above the same non-Galois cubic,
with actual Weyl division models for all displayed subrings.
No canonical noncommutative normal-closure construction is asserted.

The general fixed-Weyl-field phenomenon is due to
[Alev--Dumas, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1080/00927879708825943).
The author's [2011 exposition, Section 5.3.2](https://lmbp.uca.fr/~fdumas/fichiers/B-Mat60.pdf)
prints the theorem, proof and binary-dihedral generators on page 64
(PDF page 69). Its general theorem concerns finite automorphism groups
of the Weyl algebra, extended to its division ring. The special
formulas and fixed-field proof above are direct, so no uninspected
general theorem is used as a proof premise.

For the classical comparison, take k=k_0(h) with its h-adic valuation,
where i and epsilon belong to k_0. All formulas in (13)--(16) have
nonzero rational classical denominators. Their classical reductions
give the ordinary cotangent
inclusion
\[
                   k(u,\zeta)\subset k(y,\eta),
                         \quad \eta=f'(y)\zeta .
\]
This is exactly the left column of the existing square.
For Long's right column, retaining the same position u still forces
zeta=r^2*u*w^5/20, with degree five, as already proved in (6)--(8).
The binary-dihedral construction does not identify those two
Poisson coverings. A new equivalence that mixes the positions
with their partners, or a different quantum compatibility outside
the excluded homogeneous Euler model, would be needed. Neither all possible mixed equivalences nor
all alternative quantum invariant generators are excluded here.
