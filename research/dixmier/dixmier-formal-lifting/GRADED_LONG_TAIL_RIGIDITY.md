# Rigidity of the graded Long coefficient tail at cap 23

For the canonical Long symbols, the complete homogeneous third
coefficient has one free direction, generated by the Hamiltonian
\(R^6\). Every subsequent homogeneous coefficient map is injective.
The grading leaves no admitted coefficient beyond index 9. Therefore
later compatibility can impose additional equations but cannot
introduce further free coefficient parameters.

This statement concerns homogeneous coefficient maps. It proves
neither existence nor compatibility of the inhomogeneous tail.

## Complete coefficient spaces

Use source variables \((X,Q,P,Z)\), Poisson brackets
\(\{P,X\}=\{Z,Q\}=1\), weights \((-1,1,4,2)\), and \(h\)-weight 3.
Let \(f=(R,T,D,S)\) be the full canonical Long polynomial symbols from
[the exact input transcription](../dixmier-audit/verify_long_pbw.py),
with target weights \(w=(-1,2,4,1)\). Consider Weyl symbols
\[
                         F_i=\sum_{m\ge0}h^mF_{i,m},
 \qquad F_{i,0}=f_i,
\]
whose every coefficient has source degree at most 23 and weight
\(w_i-3m\).

The entire coefficient space \(V_m\) has basis \(X^aQ^bP^cZ^d\) in
component \(i\), with
\[
 a=b+4c+2d+3m-w_i\ge0,\qquad a+b+c+d\le23.             \tag{1}
\]
For \(G=(G_R,G_T,G_D,G_S)\in V_m\), define the six-component map
\[
 (\mathcal A_mG)_{ij}
                   =\{G_i,f_j\}+\{f_i,G_j\},\qquad i<j.       \tag{2}
\]
At commutator order \(h^{m+1}\), this is precisely the coefficient
map on the unknown \(F_m\). All other terms depend only on earlier
coefficients. No momentum-order or fixed-first-image restriction is
made in (1).

**Tail rigidity theorem.** Over every characteristic-zero field, the
complete ranks and kernels are as follows.

| \(m\) | Component counts \(R,T,D,S\) | Total | Rank | Kernel dimension |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | \(34,54,71,47\) | 206 | 205 | 1 |
| 4 | \(20,34,47,29\) | 130 | 130 | 0 |
| 5 | \(10,20,29,16\) | 75 | 75 | 0 |
| 6 | \(4,10,16,8\) | 38 | 38 | 0 |
| 7 | \(1,4,8,3\) | 16 | 16 | 0 |
| 8 | \(0,1,3,1\) | 5 | 5 | 0 |
| 9 | \(0,0,1,0\) | 1 | 1 | 0 |

The sole third-coefficient kernel is
\[
 \ker\mathcal A_3
 =K\,K_{R^6},\qquad
 K_{R^6}=\{R^6,f\}=(0,0,-6R^5,0).                            \tag{3}
\]
In particular, every two admitted solutions for the same
inhomogeneous \(F_3\) equation have identical \(R,T,S\) components.
For every \(m\ge10\), \(V_m=0\).

To prove the rank assertions, form all columns of (2) in the complete
basis (1). The [explicit witness](graded_long_tail_rigidity_witness.json)
specifies the following square minors at the prime \(1000003\):

| \(m\) | Matrix rows | Nonzero entries | Minor size | Determinant modulo \(1000003\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 1563 | 29539 | 205 | 351788 |
| 4 | 1207 | 17520 | 130 | 406976 |
| 5 | 888 | 9255 | 75 | 854936 |
| 6 | 646 | 4104 | 38 | 720220 |
| 7 | 427 | 1398 | 16 | 137481 |
| 8 | 200 | 338 | 5 | 375828 |
| 9 | 32 | 32 | 1 | 499967 |

Every determinant is nonzero and all matrix denominators are
invertible at this prime. They give the stated rational rank lower
bounds. For \(m\ge4\), each minor uses every coefficient column, which
proves injectivity. For \(m=3\), exact polynomial substitution verifies
the nonzero kernel (3), giving the matching upper bound. Thus no
symbolic nullspace solver is required to replay the proof.

Finally, (1) gives \(a\ge3m-\max_iw_i\). For \(m\ge10\), this is at
least 26, exceeding the entire degree cap. More precisely, the
maximal possible coefficient indices for \(R,T,D,S\) are
\[
                              (7,8,9,8).                       \tag{4}
\]
This is termination of the stated graded coefficient spaces, not
termination of arbitrary bounded lifts after a normalization.

## Consequence for parameter counts and uniqueness

The [separate odd-jet classification](ODD_GRADED_LONG_FIRST_JETS_AND_NORMALIZATION_GAP.md)
gives, after the two uniformly cap-preserving first gauges, the
surviving first Hamiltonian
\[
                         aY+bR^2T+cH_4
\]
and the complete second coefficient
\[
                    \Psi(a,b,c)+uK_{R^3}+vK_{R^4S}.
\]
Once their inhomogeneous third equation is compatible, (3) permits
only one further homogeneous parameter \(w\). Thus every compatible
normalized full tuple is determined by the six initial coordinates
\[
                              a,b,c,u,v,w.                     \tag{5}
\]
No later coefficient supplies another free direction.

If one additionally uses a separately established full \(h^4\)
family imposing \(a=544c/27\), the candidate coordinates reduce to
\[
                               b,c,u,v,w.                      \tag{6}
\]
This five-coordinate consequence is conditional on that nonlinear
compatibility calculation; its relation and sufficiency are not
proved by the homogeneous minors here. It is a bound on free
coordinates, not an assertion that all their values extend.

Indeed, choose a fixed rational left inverse of each injective
\(\mathcal A_m\), \(m=4,\ldots,9\). On its image, this recovers the
unique \(F_m\) from its earlier-coefficient right side. The maps are
constant rational matrices, so polynomial earlier families produce
polynomial candidate later coefficients. Their remaining row equations
are compatibility conditions. The finite coefficient indices (4)
make the full bounded graded lifting problem a finite polynomial
coefficient scheme in the initial coordinates. The scheme may impose
further relations or be empty; uniqueness gives no existence theorem.

## Correct commutator orders

Write \(\mathcal P_k(U,V)_{ij}=\Pi^k(U_i,V_j)\). The Moyal
commutator coefficient at order \(h^n\) is
\[
 \sum_{\substack{a+b+k=n\\ k\ge1\ {\rm odd}}}
       \frac{\mathcal P_k(F_a,F_b)}{2^{k-1}k!}.                \tag{7}
\]
In particular, the equation on \(F_3\) occurs at commutator order
\(h^4\), with right side
\[
 -\mathcal P_1(F_1,F_2)-\mathcal P_1(F_2,F_1)
 -\frac{\mathcal P_3(f,F_1)+\mathcal P_3(F_1,f)}{24}.           \tag{8}
\]
The equation on \(F_4\) occurs at commutator order \(h^5\), with
right side
\[
\begin{split}
 &-\mathcal P_1(F_1,F_3)-\mathcal P_1(F_3,F_1)
  -\mathcal P_1(F_2,F_2)\\
 &-\frac{\mathcal P_3(f,F_2)+\mathcal P_3(F_2,f)
                         +\mathcal P_3(F_1,F_1)}{24}
  -\frac{\mathcal P_5(f,f)}{1920}.                             \tag{9}
\end{split}
\]
These follow by enumerating (7). In particular the
\(\mathcal P_3(F_1,F_1)\) term is present, while the
\(\mathcal P_1(F_2,F_2)\) term is counted once.

## A target normalization valid only through \(F_4\)

There is a useful limited statement about the second homogeneous
parameter \(u\). In the abstract target Weyl algebra, the generator
\(hR^3\) gives the exact finite shear
\[
 R,T,S\ {\rm fixed},\qquad
                         D\longmapsto D-3u h^2R^2.            \tag{10}
\]
Suppose \(R_1=R_2=0\) and \(R_0\) is the positional canonical first
image. The coefficients of \(h\) and \(h^2\) in the Weyl symbol of
\(R(h)^2\) are then zero: the possible ordering terms
\(\Pi(R_0,R_0)/2\) and \(\Pi^2(R_0,R_0)/8\) vanish because both
arguments are positional. Therefore (10), through \(F_4\), changes
only
\[
                         D_2\longmapsto D_2-3uR_0^2.          \tag{11}
\]
This correction has degree 6. Applying the inverse shear with parameter
\(-u\) removes an existing \(+uK_{R^3}\) second coefficient through
that finite stage while preserving cap 23.
The argument does not assume \(R_3=0\).

Equation (11) proves no all-orders common-cap preservation for (10):
later products can involve higher corrections of \(R\). In particular,
it does not justify discarding \(u\) from an unrestricted later
compatibility system without checking the required finite stage.
Likewise no \(vK_{R^4S}\) normalization is asserted here; its separate
degree-24 warning in the odd-jet note remains relevant.
