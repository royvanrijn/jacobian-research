# Higher-root exclusion for polynomial Weyl pairs of differential-order gcd two

**Theorem PolynomialGcdTwoLeadingFactor.** Let \(k\) have characteristic zero
and let \(P,Q\in k[x][D;\partial_x]\) have differential orders \(2a,2b\), where
\(a,b\ge2\) are coprime. If \([P,Q]=\kappa\in k^\times\), their leading
coefficients have the form \(\alpha h^a,\beta h^b\) for \(\alpha,\beta\in k^\times\)
and \(h\in k[x]\) of degree at most one.

The proof uses the monic residue identity to produce a multiple root of
\(h\) if its degree is at least two. Near such a root the common leading
symbol is quadratic. The conductor of the semigroup generated by \(a,b\)
allows all higher commuting symbols to be subtracted using the two actual
operators. The resulting scalar commutator equation gives a bound on the
original coefficients at the root, which conflicts with the integer
weights of the original pair.

## Common factor and exact differential

Write \(\ell_P,\ell_Q\) for the leading coefficients. The order-\((2a+2b-1)\)
coefficient of the commutator gives
\(a\ell_P\ell_Q'-b\ell_Q\ell_P'=0\).
Thus \(\ell_Q^a/\ell_P^b\in k^\times\), and unique factorization gives
\(\ell_P=\alpha h^a,\ell_Q=\beta h^b\).
Extend constants to an algebraic closure. With \(\lambda^2=h\) and
\(E=\lambda D\), scaling by constants makes the operators monic over
\(k(x,\lambda)\). The
[general leading-factor residue constraint](POLYNOMIAL_WEYL_LEADING_FACTOR_RESIDUE.md)
excludes an exact double root when \(g=2\). When there are at least two
distinct roots, it also forces one of multiplicity greater than two.
If \(\deg h\ge2\) and there is only one root, its multiplicity is at least
three since exact double roots are excluded. Thus whenever \(\deg h\ge2\)
there is a root \(x_0\) of multiplicity \(m\ge3\).

## The quadratic leading symbol at a higher root

Set \(\tau=x-x_0=t^{-e}\), with \(e=1\) for even \(m\) and \(e=2\) for odd
\(m\), and work in \(k((t^{-1}))\). Scale \(E\) so that its derivation of
\(t\) is \(t^{1-s}(1+O(t^{-1}))\), where \(s=e(m-2)/2>0\).
Retain monicity by constant scaling and use \(\kappa_*\ne0\) for the
scaled commutator. The constants in differential order zero before a
shear are bounded at \(t=\infty\), since each positive power of the
original \(D\) has a right factor \(E\). Depress the monic first operator
by a derivative shear, denoting the resulting operators by \(U,V\).

The monic root \(L=U^{1/(2a)}\) in formal pseudodifferential operators exists
by coefficient recursion. Each \((L^j)_+\) has commutator with \(U\) of
order at most \(2a-2\), since the negative part has order at most minus
one. For an order-\(j\) residual with that same bound, the leading
coefficient is constant by the order-\((2a+j-1)\) coefficient.
Successively subtracting multiples of \((L^j)_+\) gives a finite
constant decomposition of \(V\). If \(U\) had all coefficients bounded,
the root recursion and the decomposition would make \(V\) bounded too.
The derivation induces zero on the residue of the bounded ring, so
\([U,V]=\kappa_*\) precludes this.

Let \(r>0\) be the largest pole degree of a lower coefficient of \(U\)
divided by its order deficit. Give \(t\) weight one and \(E\) weight \(r\).
Each contraction loses at least \(r+s\), and the leading Poisson bracket
has \(\{\xi,t\}=t^{1-s}\). The same root recursion and subtraction show
that \(U,V\) have weights \(2ar,2br\) with monic leading symbols.
Write these as \(t^{2ar}f(\xi/t^r)\) and \(t^{2br}g(\xi/t^r)\), where
\(f,g\) are monic. A fractional Laurent variable may be used to express
these two homogeneous symbols.

The reverse shear is \(E\mapsto E+A(t)\). If \(\deg_t A>r\), its effect on
the order-zero coefficient of \(U\) has leading term \(A^{2a}\), since the
lower terms have smaller weight at derivative weight \(\deg_t A\), and
all contractions reduce weight. This contradicts boundedness before
depression. So \(\deg_t A\le r\), and the leading shift of \(\xi/t^r\) is
a constant \(c\), zero when the strict inequality holds. The bounded
order-zero coefficients give \(f(c)=g(c)=0\).

The scalar bracket requires \((2a+2b-1)r\ge s\). In the equality case the
leading bracket is \(2r(b f'g-a f g')=\kappa_*\), which is impossible at
\(c\). In the strict case the same expression vanishes, giving \(f^b=g^a\)
by monicity. Since \(a,b\) are coprime and \(U\) is depressed, the leading
symbols are \(H^a,H^b\) where
\[
                            H=\xi^2+\sigma t^n,\qquad n=2r,\quad \sigma\ne0 .
\]
Here \(\sigma\ne0\) because a lower coefficient realizes \(r\). The common
root \(c\) is nonzero, since \(c^2+\sigma=0\). Thus \(r=\deg_t A\) is a
positive integer. The Laurent polynomial ring \(k[t,t^{-1},\xi]\)
contains \(H\), and \(H\) is squarefree there.

## Descent to a single equation on a generic level

Begin with \(R=V^a-U^b\), of weight less than \(2abr\). The commutator
\([U,R]=a\kappa_* V^{a-1}\) has weight \(2b(a-1)r\). To retain its leading
term, \(R\) must have weight at least
\[
                         L_0=(2ab-2a-2b+1)r+s .
\]
At a greater weight the leading symbol \(F\) commutes with \(H\).
The homogeneous Euler identity in \(t,\xi\) then gives
\(2rH\,dF=(\operatorname{wt}F)F\,dH\) over the rational function field.
Thus the rational function \(F^{2r}/H^{\operatorname{wt}F}\) is constant.
Taking a valuation at a simple prime divisor of \(H\) gives the integer
\(j=\operatorname{wt}F/(2r)\). Hence \(F\) is a constant multiple of \(H^j\). The weight bounds imply
\[
                         ab-a-b+1\le j<ab .
\]
Each such integer is \(Aa+Bb\) with \(A,B\ge0\): choose \(0\le B<a\)
with \(Bb\equiv j\pmod a\); then \(j-Bb\ge1-a\) and is an integer
multiple of \(a\), hence is nonnegative. Subtract a constant multiple of
\(U^AV^B\) from \(R\). If \(B>0\), the commutator of the subtracted term
has weight \(2(j-b)r<2b(a-1)r\), so the leading commutator is unchanged.
Only finitely many \(j\) occur, and the descent ends at weight \(L_0\).
At that weight its leading symbol \(F\) satisfies
\[
                         \{H,F\}=\kappa_*H^{(a-1)(b-1)}.
\]

On the indeterminate level \(T=H\), write \(F=A_0(t,T)+\xi B_0(t,T)\),
where \(A_0,B_0\in k(T)[t,t^{-1}]\). The part even in \(\xi\) gives
\[
 2T\partial_t B_0-\sigma t^{n-1}(2t\partial_t B_0+nB_0)
             =\kappa_*T^{(a-1)(b-1)}t^{s-1}.                    \tag{1}
\]
If \(B_0\) had a negative least exponent, its derivative would give an
uncancellable term below the right side. Hence all exponents are
nonnegative. In each nonempty residue class modulo \(n\), the largest
exponent \(j\) contributes a nonzero term of degree \(j+n-1\) with no
other term of that degree in the class. Thus the only possible class is
\(s\pmod n\), with largest exponent \(s-n\). The least exponent must be
zero: if it were positive, its derivative would be nonzero at a degree
strictly less than \(s-1\) and than any term from the second summand.
Consequently \(s=nJ=2rJ\) for an integer \(J\ge1\). This excludes odd \(m\),
for which \(s=m-2\), and gives \(m/2=2rJ+1\) for even \(m\).

## Return to the original polynomial pair

Now \(e=1\). In \(E^j\) the coefficient of \(D^i\) has
\(\tau\)-valuation at least \(i+j(m/2-1)\), by induction under left
multiplication by \(E\), a unit times \(\tau^{m/2}D\). Reverse shear
preserves the weight bound \(2ar\). Thus if \(p_i\) is the coefficient
of \(D^i\) in \(P\), its valuation is bounded below by
\[
 \min_{j\ge i}\bigl(i+j(m/2-1)-(2a-j)r\bigr)
       =i+\bigl((2J+1)i-2a\bigr)r .
\]
In particular, this is strictly greater than \(i\) for every \(i\ge a\).

For \(0<u<1\) assign weight \(i-u j\) to \(\tau^j D^i\) in the
original polynomial pair and let \(M_P(u),M_Q(u)\) be the two maximum
weights. A contraction loses \(1-u\), so
\(M_P(u)+M_Q(u)\ge1-u\). At small positive \(u\), the leading monomials
are proportional to \(\tau^{am}D^{2a}\) and \(\tau^{bm}D^{2b}\).
Their weights are positive in ratio \(a:b\). Away from finitely many
ties, if both maximum weights are positive, the leading monomials have
proportional exponent vectors. Otherwise their nonzero Poisson
bracket would be a scalar, requiring the exponent vectors in
\((D,\tau)\) to sum to \((1,1)\). This cannot give positive weights
for both vectors. Continuity therefore preserves the ratio \(a:b\)
on the initial interval of positivity. It cannot end before \(u=1\),
since both weights would tend to zero there against the lower bound
on their sum. This is the
[original-coefficient weight comparison](POLYNOMIAL_FOUR_SIX_STRING_EXCLUSION.md#the-weighted-orders-near-the-root)
with general \(a,b\).

At \(u=1\), the weights are nonnegative integers with
\(bM_P(1)=aM_Q(1)\). The previous valuation bound and polynomiality
give \(M_P(1)\le a-1\): high differential orders have negative weight,
and orders below \(a\) have weight at most \(a-1\).
Coprimality forces both maximum weights to be zero. It follows that
both \(P\) and \(Q\) preserve the ideal \((\tau)\) in \(k[[\tau]]\), since
each coefficient of positive differential order \(i\) is divisible by
\(\tau^i\). On the one-dimensional quotient their commutator vanishes.
This contradicts \(\kappa\ne0\) and rules out every higher root.
