# Constant and linear common leading factors for gcd-two polynomial Weyl pairs

**Theorem PolynomialGcdTwoLowDegreeLeadingFactor.** Let \(k\) be a
characteristic-zero field and \(D x-xD=1\). Suppose \(P,Q\in k[x][D]\)
have differential orders \(2a,2b\) with \(a,b\ge2\) coprime, and
\([P,Q]=\kappa\in k^\times\). Write their leading coefficients as
\(\alpha h^a,\beta h^b\) with \(h\in k[x]\) and
\(\alpha,\beta\in k^\times\). Then \(\deg h\ge2\).

The argument applies the same weighted comparison and Laurent
obstruction as the
[linear-factor order-four and order-six case](LOW_DEGREE_LEADING_FACTOR_POLYNOMIAL_FOUR_SIX_EXCLUSION.md).
It uses the numerical semigroup generated by \(a,b\) to perform
all the higher weight subtractions. In the last constant-leading
case, a polynomial change of Weyl generators makes the orders
exactly \(a,b\), so the
[coprime scalar exclusion](ALL_COPRIME_ALGEBRAIC_STRING_OBSTRUCTION.md)
applies.

## Constant-leading Laurent normalizations

Write \(p_{2a},q_{2b}\) for the original leading coefficients.
The order-\((2a+2b-1)\) commutator coefficient makes
\(q_{2b}^a/p_{2a}^b\) constant. Factorization in \(k[x]\)
gives the asserted leading coefficients because \(\gcd(a,b)=1\).

Extend constants to an algebraic closure, still denoted by \(k\).
For constant \(h\), set \(t=x\) and \(E=D\); the coefficients
are in \(k[t]\). For linear \(h\) with root \(x_0\), put
\[
                 x=x_0+t^2,\qquad D=(2t)^{-1}E,\qquad [E,t]=1.
\]
The coefficients are in \(k[t,t^{-1}]\), and the involution
\((t,E)\mapsto(-t,-E)\) fixes the original operators. In both
cases the leading coefficients for \(E\) are nonzero constants.
Scale to make the operators monic, retaining \(\kappa\) for the
resulting nonzero constant commutator. Depress \(P\) by replacing
\(E\) with \(E-A\), where \(A\) is \(1/(2a)\) times the coefficient
of \(E^{2a-1}\). This Ore automorphism preserves the respective
coefficient rings. In the linear case \(A\) is odd in \(t\), so
the automorphism commutes with the involution. In particular the
coefficient of \(E^{2a-2}\) in the depressed \(P\) is even in \(t\).

Write \(L=P^{1/(2a)}\) for the monic formal root and
\(B_j=(L^j)_+\) for its finite differential parts. Comparing coefficients
in \(L^{2a}=P\) determines each new coefficient by division by
\(2a\). If the coefficients of \(P\) were bounded at \(t=\infty\),
the coefficients of \(L\), and hence those of each \(B_j\), would
be bounded. The negative part of \(L^j\) has order at most minus
one, so \([P,B_j]\) has order at most \(2a-2\). For a finite
operator \(U\) of order \(j\) with such a commutator bound,
the order-\((2a+j-1)\) coefficient of \([P,U]\) is \(2a\)
times the derivative of the leading coefficient of \(U\).
This derivative vanishes by the commutator bound, so the
leading coefficient of \(U\) is constant. Repeatedly subtracting
constant multiples of \(B_j\) expresses \(Q\) as a finite
constant combination of the \(B_j\). This shows that \(Q\)
would also have bounded coefficients. Reducing both operators
at infinity would give a pair in the commutative \(k[E]\),
since the induced derivation on the residue field is zero.
The commutator would still be \(\kappa\), which is impossible.

## The binomial leading symbols and the descent

Some lower coefficient of \(P\) has positive degree at infinity.
Use the weight
\[
     \operatorname{wt}(t)=1,\qquad
     \operatorname{wt}(E)=r=\max_{i<2a,\ p_i\ne0}
                       \frac{\deg_t p_i}{2a-i}>0 .
\]
Here \(p_i\) denotes the coefficient of \(E^i\) after depression.
Each Ore contraction loses \(r+1\). The root recursion just
used shows that every term in \(L\) has weight at most \(r\):
each product of known terms has weight at most \(2ar\), and
comparison with \(P\) gives the same bound on the unknown
term in the root. Consequently \(B_j\) has weight \(jr\),
attained by \(E^j\), and \(Q\) has weight \(2br\).

Since \(r\ge 1/(2a)\), the first possible commutator weight
\((2a+2b-1)r-1\) is positive. Thus the leading symbols \(p,q\)
Poisson-commute for \(\{\xi,t\}=1\). The weighted Euler
identities and this commutation give
\(d(q^a/p^b)=0\). Both symbols are monic in \(\xi\), so
\(q^a=p^b\). Unique factorization and monicity give
\[
                  p=H^a,\qquad q=H^b,\qquad
                  H=\xi^2+\sigma t^n,\qquad n=2r.
\]
Indeed the common factor \(H\) is quadratic in \(\xi\),
and depression removes its linear term. The lower coefficient
attaining the maximal ratio defining \(r\) makes
\(\sigma\ne0\); the coefficient of \(\xi^{2a-2}\) in \(p\)
then makes \(n\) a positive integer. In the linear leading-factor
case its parity makes \(n\) even.

Set \(R=Q^a-P^b\). Its weight is less than \(2abr\), while
\([P,R]=a\kappa Q^{a-1}\) has weight \(2b(a-1)r\).
Preserving this leading commutator keeps the weight of \(R\)
at least
\[
                       \theta=(2ab-2a-2b+1)r+1.
\]
Above \(\theta\) its leading symbol Poisson-commutes with \(H\).
The weighted Euler identities force it to have a constant
ratio of powers with \(H\). The latter is squarefree in
\(k[t,t^{-1},\xi]\), since its \(\xi\)-derivative is \(2\xi\)
and its constant term is a Laurent unit. Unique factorization
then makes the leading symbol a constant multiple of \(H^j\).
The Laurent units introduce no other term because the ratio
of powers is constant.

The strict weight bounds give
\((a-1)(b-1)\le j<ab\). Every integer \(j\ge(a-1)(b-1)\)
is a nonnegative combination \(au+bv\): choose
\(0\le v<a\) with \(j-bv\) divisible by \(a\), and
\(j-bv\ge-(a-1)\) shows that this multiple of \(a\) is
nonnegative. Subtract the corresponding constant multiple
of \(P^uQ^v\) from \(R\). If \(v>0\), its commutator with
\(P\) has weight \((2j-2b)r<2b(a-1)r\), and if \(v=0\)
it commutes with \(P\). Thus the leading commutator is
preserved. The weights are discrete, so the descent ends at
\(\theta\). Its leading symbol \(F\) there satisfies
\[
                         \{H,F\}=\kappa H^{(a-1)(b-1)}.
\]

## The generic level and the remaining Weyl coordinates

On the indeterminate level \(T=H\), write
\(F=C(t,T)+\xi B(t,T)\). Reduction by
\(\xi^2=T-\sigma t^n\) makes this representation unique. The bracket with \(H\)
acts with \(T\) fixed. For the zero-\(\xi\) component of the bracket equation
one obtains
\[
     2T B_t-\sigma t^{n-1}(2tB_t+nB)
                  =\kappa T^{(a-1)(b-1)}
                  \qquad\text{in }k(T)[t,t^{-1}].
\]
If the least exponent of \(B\) were negative, its derivative
would give a nonzero term below both the right side and all
terms from the \(\sigma\) part. So \(B\) is a polynomial
in \(t\), and it is nonzero. If its greatest exponent is
\(j\ge0\), the \(\sigma\) part contributes a nonzero term
of exponent \(j+n-1\), since \(2j+n>0\). For \(n\ge2\)
this is strictly above every contribution of the derivative
part and above the right side. Thus \(n\ge2\) is impossible.
In particular this proves the linear leading-factor exclusion.

In the constant-leading case with \(n=1\), \(r=1/2\).
Write \(H_0=E^2+\sigma t\), a polynomial operator with
symbol \(H\). Then \(P-H_0^a\) and \(Q-H_0^b\) have weights
strictly below \(a,b\), respectively. Take new Weyl generators
\[
       Y=E,\qquad Z=-H_0/\sigma,\qquad [Z,Y]=1,\qquad
       t=-Z-Y^2/\sigma.
\]
They generate the same polynomial Weyl algebra. A monomial
\(t^jE^i\) of weight \(j+i/2<a\) has \(j<a\) and has
differential order in \(Z\) at most \(j\). The strict weight
bound therefore puts \(P-H_0^a\) below order \(a\) in \(Z\).
The same reasoning puts \(Q-H_0^b\) below order \(b\).
As \(H_0\) is a nonzero constant multiple of \(Z\), the new
exact orders are \(a,b\). The coprime scalar exclusion over
\(k(Y)\) rules out their nonzero constant commutator.

## Primary comparison

Previato, Rueda and Zurro,
[*Commuting Ordinary Differential Operators and the Dixmier Test*,
SIGMA 15 (2019), 101](https://doi.org/10.3842/SIGMA.2019.101),
Lemma 5.3 records the highest-weight Poisson comparison.
Their Theorem 6.2 treats commuting operators of even order
under a degree condition on the quartic, and their Example 3.2
constructs commuting order-four and order-ten operators in a
specified coefficient slice. The nonzero scalar on the right
of the bracket equation above is essential here.
