# Branched-cover homology of the Eliahou–Kauffman–Thistlethwaite links

The two-component links in the named families \(LL_1\) and \(LL_2\)
of Eliahou, Kauffman and Thistlethwaite have double branched covers
with first homology \(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/d\), with \(d>1\) for
every nonzero iteration of their tangle move from the split starting
links. This excludes every such member from an oriented joining band
to the unknot. The same homology calculation applies to the move
starting with any determinant-one numerator knot and their auxiliary
tangle \(\infty+2\). More generally, a nonsplit link in the EKT Hopf
clasp pattern with the unlink Jones polynomial can pass the homology
test only if one clasp tangle is nonrational with determinant-zero
denominator closure.

The construction and the relevant Jones-polynomial statements are in
Theorem 3.3 and Theorems 4.1 and 4.3 of [Eliahou, Kauffman and Thistlethwaite, *Infinite families of links with trivial Jones polynomial*](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~kauffman/ekt.pdf).
The authors' published \(LL_2(j)\) has the unlink polynomial at even
\(j\). The published \(LL_1(m,n)\) has it for \(m\ge1\) and for \(m=0\)
with even \(n\). The formulas below concern the underlying unoriented
links and use the indices defined in Sections 4.1 and 4.2.

## From an oriented band to a Jones counterexample

Let \(L\) be an oriented nonsplit two-component link with
\(V_L(t)=-t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2}\). An orientation-compatible joining band whose
result is the unknot would give a nontrivial knot of unknotting number one
with Jones polynomial 1. In fact, the full-twist family of the band would
contain infinitely many distinct knots with that polynomial.

Take a ball around the band, with the attaching sides on one pair of
opposite edges. The link and its banding are the two complementary
crossingless fillings of the outside tangle. Write \(K_j\) for the banding
with \(j\) signed full twists. Adjacent bandings are related by a single
crossing change, since changing one crossing in a two-crossing twist
allows the pair to cancel. The oriented smoothing reconnects each
attaching side to itself and hence recovers \(L\). In the rational-tangle
convention of §5 and Figure 11 of [Kotelskiy, Lidman, Moore, Watson and Zibrowius](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-023-02697-5),
the smoothing has slope \(0\) and the bandings have slopes \(1/(2j)\), with
\(1/0=\infty\). In particular, the given banding itself can be a member
of a crossing-change pair even though its band ball is crossingless in
these coordinates.

The skein right-hand side is
\((t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2})V_L=t^{-1}-t\), so across each adjacent pair
\(V_{K_+}-1=t^2(V_{K_-}-1)\). If any one banding is the unknot, every
\(K_j\) has polynomial 1. At a nugatory crossing the oriented smoothing
is split, since the nugatory sphere separates the two smoothed strands.
The established cosmetic crossing theorem for the unknot, recalled in
§1.2 of the same paper, therefore makes either neighbor of an unknot
banding nontrivial. The outside tangle is also not horizontally split,
since a horizontal splitting disk would give a splitting sphere for
the slope-zero closure \(L\). Their Theorem 5.3 gives pairwise distinct
unoriented knots for all sufficiently large \(|j|\).

In the double branched cover, the band ball gives a solid torus. If a
banding is the unknot then the lifted outside tangle is the exterior of
the solid torus core \(J\) in \(S^3\). The two complementary
crossingless fillings lift to slopes intersecting once on the boundary
torus, so the \(L\)-filling is integer surgery on \(J\) in the meridian
coordinates of the unknot filling. The determinant identity
\(\det(L)=|V_L(-1)|=0\) gives infinite first homology. For integer surgery
on a knot in \(S^3\), first homology is generated by the meridian with
the surgery coefficient as its sole relation. The coefficient must
therefore be zero, and \(H_1(\Sigma_2(L);\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\).

Conversely, a knot of unknotting number one with Jones polynomial 1 gives
a two-component smoothing with the unlink polynomial. The smoothing
admits a band restoring the unknot. If the smoothing were split, the
Scharlemann–Thompson split-band theorem, stated as Theorem 1.1 in
[Ishihara and Motegi](https://www.smm.org.mx/boletin_anterior/v15/n1.pdf),
would force a splitting sphere meeting this band in one transverse arc.
That sphere passes through the band as a separating disk in the band
ball, so both twist choices are connected sums of the two component
knots and both are unknots. Hence the smoothing is nonsplit. The band
target over all nonsplit two-component links is thus equivalent to
Jones counterexamples of unknotting number one.

## Integral coloring at the Hopf clasp

Use the universal Fox group of a diagram, with one integer generator per
arc and relation \(u+v=2o\) at a crossing with under-arcs \(u,v\) and
over-arc \(o\). On a closed link \(L\) the group is
\(\mathbb Z\oplus H_1(\Sigma_2(L);\mathbb Z)\), where \(\Sigma_2(L)\)
is the cover of \(S^3\) branched twice over \(L\). The first summand
is accounted for by the split map sending every arc generator to 1.
[Przytycki, *3-coloring and other elementary invariants of knots*, Theorem 3.3](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/bcp/bcp42/bcp42120.pdf)
The determinant is \(|V_L(-1)|\), and equals the order of that homology
when it is finite, or zero when it is infinite.
[Stoimenow, *Graphs, determinants of knots and hyperbolic volume*, p. 423](https://msp.org/pjm/2007/232-2/pjm-v232-n2-p11-p.pdf)

For a tangle write its endpoint elements in the order \(NW,NE,SW,SE\).
The alternating boundary equality is \(NW+SE=NE+SW\).
[Dymara, Januszkiewicz and Przytycki, *Symplectic structure on colorings, Lagrangian tangles and Tits buildings*, Proposition 2.2](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/114131)
Their equality over \(\mathbb Z/m\) gives the universal equality by
applying it to every homomorphism from the finitely generated Fox
group to a finite cyclic group. Write \(x=NE-NW\) and \(y=SW-NW\).
The four endpoints are then \(w,w+x,w+y,w+x+y\) with \(w=NW\).
Write \(G_T\) for the kernel of the map sending the generators of
the Fox group of \(T\) to 1. The independent base \(w\) splits off.

Consider the exterior of the two tangle disks in EKT Figure 1. Follow
each pair of parallel arcs from the east side of its disk to the west
side. At the left clasp, the \(T\)-pair passes under the inner and then
outer arc of the \(U\)-pair. At the right clasp, the \(U\)-pair passes
under the outer and then inner arc of the \(T\)-pair. At \(T\) the outer
arc is the north one. At \(U\) the inner arc is the north one. Each
underpass with over-color \(v\) sends color \(c\) to \(2v-c\), so a
double underpass adds twice the difference of the second and first
over-colors. The north and south arcs undergo the same addition within
each pair, so their difference is constant along the exterior. The
addition is \(2y_U\) to each \(T\)-arc and \(2y_T\) to each \(U\)-arc.
Thus the clasp imposes precisely
\[
x_T+2y_U=0,\qquad x_U+2y_T=0.
\]
This elimination keeps the two base colors free. Removing one overall
base gives the formula
\[
H_1(\Sigma_2(H(T,U));\mathbb Z)
\cong \mathbb Z\oplus
\frac{G_T\oplus G_U}{\langle x_T+2y_U,\ x_U+2y_T\rangle}.
\]
In particular, if \(G_T=\mathbb Zs\), \(G_U=\mathbb Zt\), and the
two endpoint difference vectors are \((a,b)s\) and \((c,d)t\), the
remaining presentation matrix is
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&2d\\2b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
Its determinant is \(ac-4bd\).
If a tangle consists of two interval strands, cutting
at each undercrossing in a diagram with \(k\) crossings gives \(k+2\)
arc generators and \(k\) crossing relations. Thus its reduced group
\(G_T\) has free rank at least 1.

## An obstruction for arbitrary tangles in the Hopf clasp

Let \(T,U\) each consist of two properly embedded arcs. If \(H(T,U)\)
has the two-component unlink Jones polynomial and its double branched
cover has first integral homology \(\mathbb Z\), then both numerator
closures have determinant 1 and at least one denominator closure has
determinant zero. When both tangles are rational, the link is split.

Write \(f_T,g_T\) for the coefficients of the horizontal and vertical
crossingless diagrams in the bracket of \(T\), and put \(\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}\).
The numerator and denominator closures have brackets \(\delta f_T+g_T\)
and \(f_T+\delta g_T\), respectively. At \(A_0=e^{\pi i/4}\), the moduli
\(|g_T(A_0)|,|f_T(A_0)|\) are their determinants because the writhe
correction has modulus one. In EKT Proposition 2.1′, the clasp bracket is
\(h_{00}f_T f_U+h_{01}(f_Tg_U+g_Tf_U)+h_{11}g_Tg_U\). Dividing by
\(\delta\) and evaluating at \(A_0\) gives
\[
\left.\frac{\langle H(T,U)\rangle}{\delta}\right|_{A_0}
 =g_T(A_0)g_U(A_0)-8 f_T(A_0)f_U(A_0),
\]
since \(h_{00}=\delta^7-7\delta^5+15\delta^3-8\delta\),
\(h_{01}=\delta^2\), and \(h_{11}=\delta\). For the unlink Jones
polynomial the displayed expression has modulus 1. Thus each tangle
has at least one closure of nonzero determinant.

The numerator and denominator closures give reduced Fox groups
\(G_T/\langle x\rangle\) and \(G_T/\langle y\rangle\), since the
alternating boundary relation makes the two cap equations equivalent.
At least one quotient is finite, so \(G_T\) has free rank exactly 1.
Write \(d_T\) for the order of its torsion subgroup and \(x_T,y_T\) for
the integral coordinates of the endpoint differences in the free
quotient after choosing a generator. If an element has free coordinate
\(s\ne0\), quotienting
by it gives order \(d_T |s|\), because the original torsion subgroup
injects and the quotient by that subgroup has order \(|s|\). If \(s=0\),
the filled homology is infinite. This proves
\(|g_T(A_0)|=d_T|x_T|\), \(|f_T(A_0)|=d_T|y_T|\), including zero values.

The horizontal positive crossing changes the first endpoint difference
to \(x_T-y_T\). Its bracket matrix in EKT §3 gives
\(g_{T+1}(A_0)=A_0(g_T(A_0)+A_0^{-2}f_T(A_0))\). Consequently
\[
|g_T(A_0)+A_0^{-2}f_T(A_0)|=d_T|x_T-y_T|.
\]
With the two individual moduli, this equality forces the complex
summands to be collinear, with the sign prescribed by \(x_T,-y_T\).
If either summand vanishes, choose the phase from the other. Thus
\(g_T(A_0)=\lambda_T x_T\) and \(f_T(A_0)=-A_0^2\lambda_T y_T\), where
\(|\lambda_T|=d_T\). The clasp equation and \(A_0^4=-1\) now imply
\[
 d_Td_U|x_Tx_U+8y_Ty_U|=1.
\]
In particular \(d_T=d_U=1\). Set \(\epsilon=x_Tx_U+8y_Ty_U\in\{1,-1\}\).
The integral clasp presentation above has determinant
\(\epsilon-12y_Ty_U\), which is nonzero. The gcd of its four entries
\(x_T,x_U,2y_T,2y_U\) is 1 because it divides \(\epsilon\). Hence
\[
H_1(\Sigma_2(H(T,U));\mathbb Z)
\cong \mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/|\epsilon-12y_Ty_U|.
\]
This group is \(\mathbb Z\) precisely when \(y_Ty_U=0\). In that case
\(x_T,x_U\in\{1,-1\}\). For a rational tangle, \(y=0\) is precisely the
vertical crossingless slope \(\infty\) by the coloring form of Conway's
classification. It has a numerator-splitting disk and the corresponding
Hopf clasp is split by EKT §2. Thus a nonsplit
candidate within this pattern needs a nonrational tangle with
determinant-zero denominator closure.
[Kauffman and Lambropoulou, *On the classification of rational tangles*, Theorem 1 and §5](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~kauffman/rattl.pdf)

## Clasping two unknot numerators

Suppose the two numerator closures are unknots and \(H(T,U)\) is a
nonsplit oriented two-component link with the unlink Jones polynomial.
Then at least one denominator closure is itself a nonsplit oriented
two-component link with the unlink Jones polynomial and an oriented
band to the unknot. Thus finding such a clasp already requires a
solution to the band target among its two inputs.

Let \(p_T=\delta f_T+g_T\), the bracket of the numerator, and define
\(p_U\) likewise. Expanding EKT Proposition 2.1′ in terms of these
quantities gives
\[
\frac{\langle H(T,U)\rangle}{\delta}
=p_Tp_U +(\delta^2-1)(\delta^2-2)(\delta^2-4) f_T f_U.
\]
Here the coefficient of \(f_T f_U\) is \(h_{00}/\delta-\delta^2\).
The two numerator bracket polynomials are signed monomials because
the knots are unknots. The left side is also a signed monomial by
the hypothesis on the Jones polynomial. Their difference has a zero
of order at least two at \(A=1\), since
\(\delta^2-4=(A^2-A^{-2})^2\). The value and first derivative at 1
determine the sign and exponent of a signed monomial. Therefore
the monomials agree, and the Laurent polynomial domain gives
\(f_Tf_U=0\).

Assume \(f_T=0\), exchanging the tangles if necessary. The denominator
has bracket \(f_T+\delta g_T=\delta p_T\), of magnitude two at \(A=1\),
so it has two components. Indeed the magnitude of the bracket of an
\(r\)-component link at 1 is \(2^{r-1}\), by crossing changes to the
unlink and the writhe normalization. Each denominator cap closes
one interval strand. The orientation on the strands from the
numerator therefore extends over the denominator caps, with the same
crossing signs. Its normalized Jones polynomial is exactly the unlink
polynomial, and the replacement of denominator caps by numerator caps
is an oriented band to the unknot.

If this denominator closure were split, the split-band theorem cited
above gives a splitting sphere meeting the band in one transverse arc
and says both denominator components are unknots. Intersecting that
sphere with the outside tangle gives a disk separating its two
strands. Each strand is boundary-parallel in its ball, since closing
it by a boundary arc gives its unknotted denominator component.
This makes \(T\) a rational tangle of vertical slope. By EKT §2 its
Hopf clasp is split, contrary to the assumed clasp link. Hence the
denominator gives the stated nonsplit band target.

## The determinant-one starting tangle

Suppose \(T^N\), the numerator closure of \(T\), is a knot of
determinant one. The numerator closing arcs impose \(x_T=0\), because
the two horizontal endpoint differences coincide by the alternating
boundary equality. The quotient \(G_T/\langle x_T\rangle\) is
\(H_1(\Sigma_2(T^N);\mathbb Z)=0\). Because the numerator is a knot,
the tangle consists of two interval strands, so \(G_T\) has free rank
at least 1. It follows that \(G_T\) is infinite cyclic, with
generator \(x_T\). Write \(y_T=h x_T\) for its uniquely specified
integer \(h\).

For the auxiliary tangle \(\infty+2\) the reduced endpoint vector
is \((1,0)\) times an integral generator. In the vertical trivial
tangle \(\infty\) the west endpoints agree, as do the east endpoints,
and no equation identifies their two arc colors. Appending integer
twists to a pair of equal colors preserves both colors.

At a positive horizontal twist the ordered pair of west colors
\((u,v)\) becomes \((2u-v,u)\) on the east. Appending a horizontal
integer \(k\) sends \((x,y)\) to \((x-ky,y)\). The diagonal reflection
in the EKT product \(T\cdot U\) swaps \(x,y\). Hence the move
\(T^\omega=(T+2)\cdot1\cdot2\) sends the retained endpoint column to
\[
M\binom{x}{y},\qquad M=\begin{pmatrix}3&-8\\-1&3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every crossing introduces and eliminates an outgoing under-color with
coefficient 1. These changes preserve the integral group. The
opposite move \(\bar\omega\) uses \(M^{-1}\).

For \(j\in\mathbb Z\), write
\[
M^j=\begin{pmatrix}a_j&-8c_j\\-c_j&a_j\end{pmatrix},
\qquad a_j+c_j\sqrt8=(3+\sqrt8)^j.
\]
Thus \(a_j^2-8c_j^2=1\), \(a_j\) is positive and odd, and
\(\gcd(a_j,2c_j)=1\). In
\(H(T^{\omega^j},(\infty+2)^{\bar\omega^j})\) the two vectors are
\((a_j-8c_jh,-c_j+a_jh)\) and \((a_j,c_j)\). The clasp determinant is
\(1+12c_j(c_j-a_jh)\), and the gcd of the presentation entries is
1. Consequently the cover homology is
\[
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/\bigl|1+12c_j(c_j-a_jh)\bigr|.
\]
For \(j\ne0\), one has \(0<|c_j|<a_j\). Then \(c_j-a_jh\ne0\),
so the determinant is an integer congruent to 1 modulo 12 and differs
from 1. Its absolute value is greater than 1. This argument applies
to both directions of iteration, with the inverse move used for
negative exponents.

## Exact formulas for the published examples

For \(LL_2(j)\) the starting tangle is \(\infty-2\) and has \(h=0\).
The torsion order is therefore \(1+12c_j^2=a_j^2+4c_j^2\). For example,
\(LL_2(2)\) has \(a_2=17\), \(c_2=6\), and homology
\(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/433\).

For \(LL_1(m,n)\), Proposition 4.2 of EKT says that the numerator
of \(T_{m,n}=n\cdot1\cdot(1\cdot1/2)^m\cdot(-1)\) is the unknot.
Calculate its \(h\) before applying \(\omega\) as follows. Start at
\((x_0,y_0)=(n+1,-n)\) from \(n\cdot1\). A block \(\cdot1\cdot1/2\)
first changes a vector \((x,y)\) to \((x,y-x)\) before the horizontal
sum with \(1/2=2^\rho\). The appended piece has endpoint differences
\((1,-2)\) times a generator. If \(s\) is the previous generator and
\(t\) is the new generator before gluing, the shared side imposes
\((x-y)s=2t\). Because \(x-y\) is odd, the integral quotient is
infinite cyclic with \(s=2s'\) and \(t=(x-y)s'\). The resulting
vector is \((3x-y,-2x+2y)\). Its first entry is odd and its second
entry even, so the argument iterates from the opposite parities of
\(n+1\) and \(-n\). In these coordinates
\[
x_m+y_m=1,\qquad x_m=\frac{(3n+2)4^m+1}{3}.
\]
The last operation \(\cdot(-1)\) gives \((x_m+y_m,x_m)=(1,x_m)\),
so the seed has \(h=x_m\). In the orbit formula above, \(j=1\) is the
published \(LL_1(m,n)\). Its torsion order is
\[
\bigl|12(3n+2)4^m-1\bigr|.
\]
For example, \(LL_1(0,0)\) gives
\(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/23\) and \(LL_1(1,-1)\) gives
\(\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/49\). Further iterations of the clasp
move are covered by the same determinant-one calculation.

With the definitions in EKT §4, the example \(LL_1(0,-1)\) has torsion
order 13. Its rational seed has the vertical slope, so after one move
it is \(LL_2(1)\). The duplicate listed as \(LL_2(0)\) in EKT §5
uses an inconsistent index: the zeroth member of §4.1 has torsion order 1.

The [Smith-form checker](verify_ekt_band_homology.py) constructs full
integral Fox presentations from EKT's tangle words, then computes
their Smith forms using SymPy. It tests \(LL_2(j)\) for \(0\le j\le5\)
and the \(LL_1\) seed orbits for \(0\le m\le3\), \(-3\le n\le3\)
and \(-2\le j\le3\). The three clasp controls are \(H(0,0)\),
\(H(0,\infty)\), and \(H(\infty,\infty)\). It reproduced the stated
orders, free ranks and cyclic torsion in the finite sample. This
internal run used Python 3.12.3 and SymPy 1.14.0. Replay from the
repository root with `python3 research/jones-unknot/verify_ekt_band_homology.py`.
