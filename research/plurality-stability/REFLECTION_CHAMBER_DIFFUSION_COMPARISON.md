# Sharp diffusion comparisons in reflection chambers

The ordered truncated Gaussian determinant is the permutation-group
case of a comparison for any finite real reflection group. The
weight is the Jacobian of the diffusion flow. Multiplying by a
product of reflecting linear forms then gives a bound whose
exponent counts both the ambient dimension and the selected
reflecting hyperplanes.

## The reflection comparison

Let \(W\subset O(n)\) be a finite group generated by reflections,
and \(C\) an open chamber of its reflecting hyperplanes. Write
\(\chi:W\to\{-1,1\}\) for a homomorphism and \(\mathcal H_\chi\)
for the hyperplanes whose reflections \(s_H\) have \(\chi(s_H)=-1\).
For each \(H\) choose a unit normal \(\alpha_H\) positive on \(C\).
Put
\[
 D_\chi(x)=\prod_{H\in\mathcal H_\chi}(\alpha_H\cdot x),
 \qquad N=|\mathcal H_\chi|.
\]
The empty product is one. Let \(X^x\) solve \(dX_t=dB_t+b_t(X_t)\,dt\)
from \(x\), with \(b_t\) equivariant under \(W\), continuous in time,
and continuously differentiable in space with bounded derivative
on each fixed compact time interval under consideration. Write
\[
 J_t^x=\exp\left(\int_0^t\operatorname{div}b_s(X_s^x)\,ds\right),\qquad
 Q_t(x,A)=\mathbb E_x[J_t^x\mathbf1_{\{X_t\in A\}}],
 \qquad
 Q_t^\chi(x,A)=\sum_{g\in W}\chi(g)Q_t(x,gA),\quad A\subset C.
\]
The formula for \(J_t^x\) is the determinant of the derivative in
the initial point, by differentiating the integral equation with
common additive noise and applying the matrix determinant identity.

**Theorem (Reflection-chamber comparison).** Suppose
\(\tfrac12(Db_t+Db_t^\top)\preceq-K_t\) for continuous symmetric
matrices \(K_t\). For \(x\in C\), \(Q_t^\chi(x,\cdot)\) is a
nonnegative measure. It satisfies
\[
 \int_C D_\chi(y)Q_t^\chi(x,dy)
 \le D_\chi(x)
    \exp\left[-\int_0^t
       \left(\operatorname{tr}K_s+
        \sum_{H\in\mathcal H_\chi}\alpha_H^\top K_s\alpha_H\right)ds\right].
 \tag{RC1}
\]
In particular \(K_t=\kappa_t I\) gives the factor
\(\exp[-(n+N)\int_0^t\kappa_s\,ds]\).

To see the positivity, let \(\tau\) be the first hit of a selected
hyperplane. For a point off the reflecting hyperplanes, let
\(\pi(y)\) be its unique orbit representative in \(C\), and let
\(g_y\) be the unique element with \(y=g_y\pi(y)\).
The path contribution to \(Q_t^\chi\) is
\(J_t^x\chi(g_{X_t})\mathbf1_{\{\pi(X_t)\in A\}}\).
If a path hits a selected hyperplane by time \(t\), reflect its
future in the first such hyperplane, choosing from simultaneous
hits by a fixed order. The conditional path law is preserved
by the strong Markov property and equivariance. The first hit,
chosen hyperplane, orbit representative and Jacobian weight
are unchanged. The character changes sign. The fixed-time
endpoint lies off the walls almost surely, since the diffusion
has a density. One way to see absolute continuity is to localize
the drift on bounded balls and use the Brownian change of
measure there. Thus the hitting contributions cancel.

The polynomial \(D_\chi\) satisfies
\(D_\chi(gx)=\chi(g)D_\chi(x)\). In fact \(W\) permutes the
selected hyperplanes, so \(D_\chi\) transforms by signs. Near
a generic point of any reflecting hyperplane the sign is
negative exactly when that hyperplane supplies a factor.
This identifies the signs on the generating reflections.
A surviving path therefore has \(D_\chi(X_t)>0\) and
\(\chi(g_{X_t})=1\). The measure has the explicit representation
\[
 Q_t^\chi(x,A)=
  \mathbb E_x[J_t^x\mathbf1_{\{\tau>t\}}
                   \mathbf1_{\{\pi(X_t)\in A\}}].
 \tag{RC2}
\]
For unweighted heat kernels, the character-weighted reflection
formula is given by Stempak, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2.
The cancellation above includes the Jacobian weight and
time-dependent equivariant drift. [Stempak, *Finite reflection
groups and symmetric extensions of Laplacian*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03045)

Every polynomial transforming by \(\chi\) is divisible by all
the selected linear factors, because it vanishes on the
corresponding hyperplanes. The Laplacian of \(D_\chi\) transforms
by \(\chi\) and has smaller degree, so it vanishes. Also, applying
the drift inequality to \(x\) and \(s_Hx\), and using equivariance,
gives
\[
 \frac{\alpha_H\cdot b_t(x)}{\alpha_H\cdot x}
 \le -\alpha_H^\top K_t\alpha_H\qquad(x\notin H).
\]
Indeed the two differences \(x-s_Hx\) and \(b_t(x)-b_t(s_Hx)\)
are twice their respective projections onto \(\alpha_H\).
Integrating the symmetric derivative along the segment gives the
displayed bound. The divergence is at most \(-\operatorname{tr}K_t\).
The generator acting on the stopped Jacobian-weighted polynomial
is \(\tfrac12\Delta+b_t\cdot\nabla+\operatorname{div}b_t\).
Its zero-order upper bound on \(D_\chi\) is the negative rate
in (RC1). Itô's formula, stopped at \(\tau\), gives (RC1)
using nonnegativity and localization. At the killing boundary
\(D_\chi\) vanishes. In the affine examples below, the
local martingale after removing that rate is a true martingale
on finite intervals, by Gaussian polynomial moments, so the
bound is attained.

### Gaussian endpoint tilts and the exact anisotropic factor

Let a constant symmetric matrix \(K\) commute with \(W\), with
\(I+K\) positive definite. Tilt Brownian paths of length one
by \(g(X_1)=e^{-V(X_1)}\), where \(V\) is \(W\)-invariant and
\(V(y)-\tfrac12y^\top K y\) is proper lower-semicontinuous convex
with full-dimensional domain. Infinite values allow hard
restrictions to convex sets. Define \(h_t=P_{1-t}g\), the heat
convolution. For \(t<1\) the tilted diffusion has
\(b_t=\nabla\log h_t\), as follows by differentiating the Doob
transition densities
\(p_{t-s}(x,y)h_t(y)/h_s(x)\) of the heat kernel \(p\).

For \(s=1-t>0\), complete the square in the convolution. Factoring
\(\exp[-\tfrac12x^\top K(I+sK)^{-1}x]\) leaves, up to a positive
constant, the convolution of the log-concave function
\(\exp[-V(y)+\tfrac12y^\top Ky]\) with a Gaussian of covariance
\(s(I+sK)^{-1}\) at \((I+sK)^{-1}x\). The slices are integrable
by the affine lower bound on the convex part and \(I+sK\succ0\).
The remaining factor is log-concave by Prékopa's marginal
theorem, applied to the jointly log-concave Gaussian product.
Therefore
\[
 D^2\log h_t(x)\preceq-K(I+(1-t)K)^{-1}.
\]
Differentiating the heat convolution also gives
\(D^2\log h_t=-s^{-1}I+s^{-2}\operatorname{Cov}(Y)\), where
\(Y\) has density proportional to \(p_s(x,y)g(y)\).
This supplies the lower bound \(-s^{-1}I\) and the required
space Lipschitz bound on every \([0,T]\), \(T<1\).
[Prékopa, *On logarithmic concave measures and functions*,
Theorem 6](sources/prekopa-logarithmic-concave-1973.pdf)

The path exists continuously through time one by its definition
as a Brownian path tilted by its endpoint. The upper divergence
bound shows that \(J_t\exp(\int_0^t\operatorname{tr}K_s\,ds)\)
is nonincreasing in \(t<1\), with
\(K_t=K(I+(1-t)K)^{-1}\). Hence \(J_1=\lim_{t\uparrow1}J_t\)
exists and is bounded. The tilted endpoint has Gaussian tails,
and given the endpoint the path is a Brownian bridge. Thus
the path supremum has every finite polynomial moment. The
endpoint is off the walls almost surely, so (RC2) extends to
time one and the polynomial estimates pass by dominated
convergence.

Commutation makes each reflecting normal an eigenvector of
\(K\). If its eigenvalue is \(\lambda_H\), integration gives
\[
 \int_C D_\chi(y)Q_1^\chi(x,dy)
 \le
 \frac{D_\chi(x)}
 {\det(I+K)\displaystyle\prod_{H\in\mathcal H_\chi}(1+\lambda_H)} .
 \tag{RC3}
\]
Equality holds when \(V(y)=\tfrac12y^\top Ky+\eta\cdot y+c\),
\(\eta\in\operatorname{Fix}(W)\). Then the drift is affine,
its symmetric derivative exactly \(-K_t\), and the displacement
\(\eta\) contributes zero to every reflecting normal.

These are also the only equality cases, up to changing the tilt
on a null set. If the trace of
\(D^2\log h_t+K_t\preceq0\) vanished throughout \(C\) for any
\(0<t<1\), real analyticity of the heat convolution in the
starting point would give that trace identity everywhere.
Negative semidefiniteness would then force the entire residual
Hessian to vanish. The conditional endpoint log moment
generating function is
\[
 \log\mathbb E[e^{x\cdot Y/s}]
   =\log h_t(x)-\log h_t(0)+|x|^2/(2s),\qquad s=1-t,
\]
where \(Y\) has density proportional to \(p_s(0,y)g(y)\).
The vanishing residual Hessian makes this log moment generating
function quadratic. Uniqueness of the Gaussian moment generating
function gives that \(Y\) is Gaussian and hence
\(g(y)=e^{-y^\top Ky/2-\eta\cdot y-c}\) almost everywhere for
some \(\eta\in\operatorname{Fix}(W)\). Otherwise the trace is
strictly negative on an open spacetime neighborhood in the
chamber. For any starting \(x\in C\), paths staying in the
chamber reach this neighborhood with positive probability.
This follows from a Brownian tube around a path in the convex
chamber and the positive finite-time Doob weight. The stopped
expectation in (RC1) then loses mass strictly before time one.

### Radial tails and moments under a scalar curvature bound

Set \(K_t=\kappa_t I\), \(U=\operatorname{Fix}(W)^\perp\),
\(m=\dim U\ge1\), and \(R(y)=|P_U y|^2\). Define
\[
 a_t=\exp(-\textstyle\int_0^t\kappa_s\,ds),\qquad
 v_t=\int_0^t(a_t/a_s)^2\,ds,\qquad d_*=m+2N .
\]
For every nonnegative nondecreasing test \(\psi\) of the squared
radius, and \(Z\sim N(0,I_{d_*})\), we have
\[
 \int_C D_\chi(y)\psi(R(y))Q_t^\chi(x,dy)
 \le D_\chi(x)a_t^{n+N}
      \mathbb E\psi\!\left(\left|a_t P_Ux+\sqrt{v_t}\,Z\right|^2\right),
 \tag{RC4}
\]
embedding \(U\) isometrically into \(\mathbb R^{d_*}\).
Indeed \(b_t(P_{\operatorname{Fix}(W)}x)\) lies in the fixed
space, so the drift inequality gives
\((P_Ux)\cdot b_t(x)\le-\kappa_t R(x)\).
The reflecting normals lie in \(U\), and homogeneity gives
\(\nabla D_\chi(x)\cdot P_Ux=ND_\chi(x)\). For a smooth
nonnegative increasing function \(u(r)\) the Jacobian-weighted
generator applied to \(D_\chi u(R)\) is therefore at most
\[
 D_\chi\{2R u''(R)+(d_*-2\kappa_tR)u'(R)
                         -(n+N)\kappa_t u(R)\}.
\]
Start with bounded continuous increasing \(\psi\). At time
\(s<t\), let \(u_s(r)\) be the expected terminal test of the
squared radius under the linear drift \(-\kappa_v z\) on
\(s\le v\le t\) in dimension \(d_*\), starting at radius
\(\sqrt r\). It solves the backward radial equation with
generator \(2r\partial_r^2+(d_*-2\kappa_s r)\partial_r\).
It is increasing in \(r\): condition on transverse Gaussian
coordinates, and use that the probability of a fixed symmetric
interval for a one-dimensional Gaussian decreases with the
absolute shift of its mean. This gives monotonicity of the
radial upper tails and then of the increasing test.
Apply stopped Itô to
\(J_s D_\chi(X_s)(a_t/a_s)^{n+N}u_s(R(X_s))\).
The backward equation and the generator estimate give
nonpositive drift. The polynomial moments of the path supremum
and boundedness of the test justify localization and passage
to \(s=t\). This proves (RC4) on bounded continuous tests.
At positive time the squared radius has no atoms, by endpoint
absolute continuity. Approximation thus gives radial tail
indicators and general increasing tests, allowing an infinite
right side. In particular (RC4) covers \(\psi(r)=r^j\).
Affine drift of slope \(-\kappa_t\) with an equivariant
translation attains equality.

For the terminal scalar bound \(K=\kappa I\), put
\(q=(1+\kappa)^{-1}\), so \(a_1=v_1=q\). For example the
weighted second moment of the radius in (RC4) is bounded by
\(D_\chi(x)q^{n+N}(q^2R(x)+q(m+2N))\). In the ordinary
ordered permutation chamber, \(m=n-1\), \(N=n(n-1)/2\),
and \(d_*=n^2-1\). Thus the comparison also bounds higher
moments of the spread after the common trace is removed.

## Moment determinants for a common Gaussian tilt

In one dimension let \(Y_x\) have density
\(p_x(y)\propto \exp[-(y-x)^2/2-V(y)]\), where
\(V(y)-\kappa y^2/2\) is convex as above and \(\kappa>-1\).
Write \(F_x\) for its distribution function,
\(M_j(x)=\mathbb E[Y_x^j]\), and \(w(x,y)=-\partial_xF_x(y)\).
The time-one Jacobian-weighted transition has density \(w(x,y)\).
Indeed at \(T<1\), differentiating the flow against a smooth
test \(\varphi\) gives
\(\partial_x\mathbb E\varphi(X_T^x)=\mathbb E[J_T^x\varphi'(X_T^x)]\).
Integration of the differentiated transition density in the
endpoint shows that its derivative weight has density
\(-\partial_x\Pr[X_T^x\le y]\). The transition density is
\(p_T(x,y)h_T(y)/h_0(x)\). Its \(x\)-derivative is dominated
for \(T\) near one by a Gaussian times a polynomial for each
fixed \(x\). Indeed the convex part of \(V\) has an affine
lower bound, and the quadratic decay coefficient for the
product is at least
\(1/T+\kappa/[1+(1-T)\kappa]=(1+\kappa)/(T[1+(1-T)\kappa])>0\),
uniformly near one. The density converges in
integrals on compact ranges, or directly by taking the
differentiated expectation identity to time one. In particular
\[
 M_j'(x)=j\int_{\mathbb R} y^{j-1}w(x,y)\,dy .
 \tag{RC5}
\]

For \(W=S_n\) on \(\mathbb R^n\), independent coordinates have
Jacobian weight equal to the product of the scalar Jacobians.
The sign kernel in (RC2) is exactly \(\det[w(x_i,y_j)]\) on
\(x_1>\cdots>x_n,\ y_1>\cdots>y_n\), and is nonnegative.
Applying the same argument for every size makes the scalar
kernel totally nonnegative in decreasing coordinates.
With \(\Delta(x)=\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)\) and \(N=n(n-1)/2\),
(RC3) and antisymmetrization give the sharp inequality
\[
 0\le(-1)^N\det\left[\frac{M_j'(x_i)}{j}\right]_{i,j=1}^n
 \le q^{n(n+1)/2}\Delta(x).
 \tag{RC6}
\]
The determinant of \(y_i^{j-1}\) is \((-1)^N\Delta(y)\).
Expanding its product with the kernel determinant and
folding the integral to ordered \(y\) gives (RC6) with no
additional factorial. This is the common-tilt extension of
the [ordered-facet comparison](ALL_EXTERIOR_HOOK_GAPS.md).

### Signed permutations and even or odd moments

Assume \(V\) is even. On \(C=\{x_1>\cdots>x_n>0\}\), let
\(w_-(x,y)=w(x,y)-w(x,-y)\) and \(w_+(x,y)=w(x,y)+w(x,-y)\).
For signed permutations the determinant character reads
\(\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\prod_i\varepsilon_i\), with
\(\varepsilon_i\) the coordinate signs. Expanding the
character sum gives \(\det[w_-(x_i,y_j)]\). Its selected
hyperplanes are \(x_i=0\) and \(x_i=\pm x_j\), giving
\(D_\chi\) proportional to \((\prod_i x_i)\Delta(x^2)\)
and \(N=n^2\). The permutation sign alone gives
\(\det[w_+(x_i,y_j)]\), selects only \(x_i=\pm x_j\),
and has \(D_\chi\) proportional to \(\Delta(x^2)\) and
\(N=n(n-1)\). The corresponding Brownian signed formulas
are in Grabiner, Section 4. [Grabiner, *Brownian motion in
a Weyl chamber*](sources/grabiner-weyl-chamber-1999.pdf)

Integrating \(w_-\) over \(y>0\) against \(y^{2j-1}\)
gives \(M_{2j}'(x)/(2j)\). Integrating \(w_+\) against
\(y^{2j-2}\) gives \(M_{2j-1}'(x)/(2j-1)\). Consequently
\[
 \begin{split}
 0\le(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}
   \det\left[\frac{M_{2j}'(x_i)}{2j}\right]_{i,j=1}^n
 &\le q^{n(n+1)}(\textstyle\prod_i x_i)\Delta(x^2),\\
 0\le(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}
   \det\left[\frac{M_{2j-1}'(x_i)}{2j-1}\right]_{i,j=1}^n
 &\le q^{n^2}\Delta(x^2).
 \end{split}
 \tag{RC7}
\]
Both constants are attained for \(V(y)=\kappa y^2/2+c\),
by (RC3).

### Finite windows and two boundary columns

If the effective support of \(g=e^{-V}\) is \((a,b)\)
up to endpoints with \(a,b\) finite, write
\(Z_x=\int \phi(y-x)g(y)\,dy\) and \(\mu_x=M_1(x)\).
The two endpoint kernels are
\[
 B_b(x)=\frac{(b-\mu_x)\phi(b-x)}{Z_x},\qquad
 B_a(x)=\frac{(\mu_x-a)\phi(a-x)}{Z_x}.
\]
In fact \(w(x,y)=\int_y^b (z-\mu_x)p_x(z)\,dz\) and
also \(w(x,y)=\int_a^y(\mu_x-z)p_x(z)\,dz\), by direct
differentiation in \(x\). Dividing by \(\int_y^b g\)
or by \(\int_a^y g\) respectively gives the two endpoint
limits, including cases where \(g\) vanishes at an endpoint.

Let \(x_1>\cdots>x_p\), \(p\ge2\), and put \(r=p-2\).
The upper bound with both endpoint columns is
\[
 \begin{split}
 0\le(-1)^{r(r-1)/2}
   \det[B_b(x_i),M'_1(x_i),\ldots,M'_r(x_i),B_a(x_i)]_{i=1}^p
 \le r!\,B_b(x_1)B_a(x_p)q^{r(r+1)/2}\Delta(x_2,\ldots,x_{p-1}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{RC8}
\]
Use the leading and trailing corner-minor bounds (S63) of
the [simplex interface reduction](SIMPLEX_INTERFACE_SYMMETRY.md)
on a \(p\)-by-\(p\) ordered kernel matrix, before taking
the outer endpoint limits. At \(T<1\) strict positivity of
each determinant follows from the killed density and
separated tubes for the conditioned Brownian bridges, as
in the ordered-facet proof. Thus the corner-minor upper
bounds pass to \(T=1\) with weak inequalities. Divide by
the two endpoint integrals of \(g\), take the endpoint
limits, and integrate the \(r\) middle columns by (RC5)
and antisymmetrization. Equation (RC6) bounds the middle
determinant. The case \(r=0\) uses the empty determinant
and empty Vandermonde. A one-sided version follows by
using only the leading or trailing corner.

The constant in (RC8) is sharp over finite windows. For
\(g(y)=e^{-\kappa y^2/2}\mathbf1_{\{|y|\le L\}}\), fix the middle
starts and choose \(x_1=(1+\kappa)L\), \(x_p=-(1+\kappa)L\).
Multiply both boundary columns by \(e^{-\kappa L^2/2}\).
The first entry of the upper-end column and the last entry of
the lower-end column converge to \(2/\pi\), by the half-Gaussian
endpoint density and mean displacement. Every other entry
of either boundary column is exponentially small in \(L^2\).
The moment derivative entries have at most polynomial growth
in \(L\), by \(\partial_x p_x(y)=(y-\mu_x)p_x(y)\) on the
window. Expanding on the two boundary columns and then
letting \(L\to\infty\) reduces the ratio of the two sides to
the sharp quadratic case of (RC6) on the middle starts.

The determinant estimates use a common endpoint tilt
on exchangeable coordinates. An interface application
also requires identifying its transport and balance
relations with the chamber comparison. In particular the
simplex hook proof uses the conditional wedge identity
(S92a) in addition to the scalar determinant estimate.
