# Rigidity of simultaneous angular saturation

Fix $0<\rho<1$. If a balanced planar Gaussian noise-stability maximizer attains the exact circular pairing maximum for almost every pair of radii, it is the regular simplex. On a compact interval of positive correlations, nonsimplex maximizers have a uniform positive gap from their own integrated exact circular envelope. Thus a nonsimplex tied maximizer would have to lose stability relative to its envelope. The global inequality remains open.

The main input is a large-angular-parameter comparison. Two different interior circular mass vectors cannot realize their exact paired maximum by consecutive arcs once the angular parameter is sufficiently large. The comparison is uniform over compact sets of separated interior mass pairs, including pairs agreeing in one coordinate.

## Exact pair values and their boundary behavior

Write $d\sigma=d\theta/(2\pi)$, $d\mu(r)=r e^{-r^2/2}dr$, and let $p(r)\in\Delta_3$ be the circular mass vector of the planar partition. Use the probability convolution kernel
\[
 k_\kappa(\theta)=e^{\kappa\cos\theta}/I_0(\kappa),\qquad
 \kappa(r,s)=\frac{\rho rs}{1-\rho^2}.
\]
For circular partitions $F,G$, write $B_\kappa(F,G)=\sum_i\langle 1_{F_i},k_\kappa*1_{G_i}\rangle_\sigma$. Write $E_\kappa(p,q)$ for its maximum at prescribed masses $p,q$. For arguments including $\kappa=0$, use $\widehat k_\kappa=(k_\kappa-1)/\kappa$ and $\widehat k_0=\cos$. This changes the pairing by a mass-dependent constant and a positive factor when $\kappa>0$. The normalized kernels are continuous in $C^1$ up to zero. The paired maximum for either kernel is jointly continuous in the mass vectors and kernel in the uniform norm. Indeed one can transfer the surplus masses within a slice to the deficient labels at total indicator $L^1$ cost exactly the mass-vector $\ell^1$ distance. Test a maximizing pair after these transfers and then reverse the roles of the mass vectors. Existence of a maximizing pair and the corresponding continuity argument also follow from [JA2 and the finite arc reduction](THREE_CELL_JOINT_ANGULAR_ENVELOPE.md#exact-pair-envelope-near-balanced-masses).

For equal prescribed masses, the exact pair maximum is attained by identical consecutive arcs, with empty or full arcs allowed. At positive parameter these are the only ordinary maximizing pairs, up to null sets. Use the positive semidefinite circular convolution and bound the pairing by the mean of the two self-pairings. In each self-pairing, separate a positive cosine term and apply the circular rearrangement inequality to the nonnegative monotone remainder as in JA2. Equality forces every nonempty proper label to be an arc by its strict cosine moment cap. At positive parameter, every Fourier eigenvalue is positive, so equality in the mean-of-self-pairings comparison gives equality of the two partitions.

A pair consisting of a two-positive consecutive arc slice and a three-positive consecutive arc slice never attains the exact paired maximum, including at zero for the normalized kernel. The two scalar matched-label bounds can both be attained by allowing the three-positive slice to have its two matched arcs centered at the two antipodes of the two-positive slice. The unmatched third cell is their complement and can be disconnected. Any attaining pair must share the centers of these two matched labels. At positive parameter, this follows by separating the positive cosine term, whose equality requires matched centers to coincide. For the cosine itself, use its moment formula directly. The two centers in the two-positive slice are antipodal, whereas the distance between the corresponding centers in a consecutive three-positive slice is strictly less than $\pi$.

There is also a neighborhood of each pure mass vector in which a three-positive consecutive arc slice cannot be a best response to a fixed three-positive consecutive arc source. This holds uniformly under convergence of the source arcs and convergence of the parameter in a compact set, including zero for the normalized kernels. To prove it, suppose that the target masses tend to pure label 1 while the source tends to $G$ with three positive arcs. The two small target arcs for labels 2 and 3 must be adjacent, so their centers converge along a subsequence to the same angle. Put $W_i=\widehat k_\kappa*(1_{G_i}-1_{G_1})$ for $i=2,3$, using the limiting parameter. These two functions have disjoint maximizing sets. At a common maximizing angle all three convolved label scores would have zero derivatives, since the two contrast derivatives vanish and the three derivatives sum to zero. The convolution score of a single proper source arc has critical points only at its center and antipode. Indeed its derivative is the difference of the kernel values at the two shifted endpoints, which has strict sign on the open semicircles because the kernel is strictly increasing as a function of the cosine. The three distinct centers of consecutive positive arcs cannot lie on one diameter.

At least one of the two small target arcs therefore approaches an angle at positive distance from the maximizing set of its corresponding contrast. Move just that small arc to a neighborhood of one of its maximizing angles, leaving the other small target arc fixed. This exchanges the moved mass with label 1 and preserves all masses. Uniform convergence of the contrasts gives a strict gain per unit of moved mass for all sufficiently large indices. The argument does not require comparable widths for the two small arcs.

## Large angular parameter at separated widths

**Pair comparison.** For any compact set of pairs of different interior mass vectors bounded away from the diagonal, there is a finite parameter above which no pair of consecutive arc partitions attains $E_\kappa(p,q)$.

First, two maximizing consecutive arc partitions with three positive masses have the same cyclic order. Otherwise their nondegenerate closing triples of first moments $u_i,v_i$ obey $v_i=Lu_i$ for a linear map $L$ of negative determinant. A real left eigenvector $n$ for a negative eigenvalue gives $(u_i\cdot n)(v_i\cdot n)\le0$ for every $i$, strictly for at least one. Reflect the second partition across $n^\perp$. The matched center dot products all increase or stay the same, with at least one strict increase. For two fixed arc widths, their pairing is strictly decreasing with circular center distance. This follows by integrating the strictly distance-decreasing convolved score of the source arc over the sliding target arc and taking the difference of its endpoint values. Thus the reflection gives a strict improvement.

Suppose for contradiction that consecutive maximizing pairs $F,G$ exist along a sequence of parameters tending to infinity and mass pairs in the prescribed compact set. Pass to a subsequence and simultaneously rename labels so that $p_1<q_1$ and $p_2>q_2$ with both gaps bounded below. Set $\varepsilon=\kappa^{-1/2}$, an angular scale in radians. For any competitor define its loss from the maximal possible overlap by
\[
 L_\kappa(F,G)=\sum_i\left[\min(p_i,q_i)-\langle 1_{F_i},k_\kappa*1_{G_i}\rangle_\sigma\right].
\]
Each term is nonnegative. It is the leakage probability from the narrower matched set to the complement of the wider one, by symmetry of the convolution.

We use three elementary estimates on the angular displacement $Y_\kappa$ of density $k_\kappa\,d\sigma$, represented on $[-\pi,\pi]$. The law of $Y_\kappa/\varepsilon$ tends to the standard normal law, its density is uniformly bounded by $C e^{-c z^2}$, and $k_\kappa(\theta)\ge c/\varepsilon$ for $|\theta|\le 2\varepsilon$. Indeed after rescaling, the density is proportional to $exp(\kappa(\cos(\varepsilon z)-1))$ on the rescaled interval. Apply $1-\cos\theta\ge c\theta^2$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$, the Taylor expansion at zero, and dominated convergence for the normalizing integral. In particular the displacement has first absolute moment $O(\varepsilon)$. The convolution pairing of any two arcs differs from their overlap by $O(\varepsilon)$, uniformly in the arcs, since shifting an arc by angle $y$ changes it in measure by at most $|y|/\pi$.

Construct a comparison pair as follows. Choose concentric arcs for $F_3,G_3$, with their prescribed widths. Inside the complement of their union, place $F_1,G_2$ as disjoint arcs separated from each other and the two endpoints of the complement by fixed positive margins. This is possible uniformly, because $p_1+q_2$ is less than both $1-p_3$ and $1-q_3$ by the two fixed positive mass gaps. Use the remaining sets for $F_2,G_1$. Their two narrower matched arcs $F_1,G_2$ are nested in the corresponding wider cells with fixed positive margins, so their leakage is $O(e^{-c\kappa})$. The remaining term is the leakage of the concentric label-3 pair. If the difference of its two angular widths divided by $\varepsilon$ tends to infinity in absolute value, that leakage is $o(\varepsilon)$. To verify this last assertion, let $a$ be half the absolute width difference. A point in the narrower arc at distance $t$ from its nearer endpoint has distance $a+t$ from the wider arc's complement. Integrating the Gaussian tail bound in $t$ from either endpoint gives a bound $C\varepsilon\int_{a/\varepsilon}^\infty e^{-c u^2}du$. Without that divergence, the reference pair still has total leakage $O(\varepsilon)$.

Now take the contrary consecutive maximizing sequence. By the reference bound and the overlap error, all narrower arcs are contained in the corresponding wider arcs in any limiting overlap configuration. If the width difference for label 3 diverges in units of $\varepsilon$, pass to a subsequence on which the same side has the narrower label 3. On that side it shares an endpoint with another narrower matched label, which is label 1 on the $F$ side or label 2 on the $G$ side. Their common endpoint converges to the corresponding common endpoint in the other partition. Indeed in the overlap limit it belongs to the closures of the disjoint adjacent wider arcs. Along the two nearby endpoints, one of the two narrower arcs extends at least to its matched wider endpoint. Take adjacent subintervals of length $\varepsilon$ on either side of that narrower endpoint, one inside the narrower arc and one outside its matched wider arc. They exist because the adjacent arc lengths are bounded below and the corresponding endpoints are nearby. The angular displacement density on their product is at least $c/\varepsilon$. The leakage is at least $c\varepsilon$, contradicting the reference loss $o(\varepsilon)$.

It remains to handle label-3 widths differing by $O(\varepsilon)$, including equal widths. By a common rotation and, if necessary, reflection, use cyclic order $1,3,2$ and put the left endpoint of $G_3$ at zero. Let $t\varepsilon$ be the nearby left endpoint of $F_3$ and let $d\varepsilon$ be the angular width of $F_3$ minus that of $G_3$. Their right endpoint difference is $(t+d)\varepsilon$. The endpoints are nearby because the label-3 arcs coincide in the limiting overlap configuration. Both $t$ and $d$ are bounded. For $t$, the overlap deficit of the two label-3 arcs, expressed in radians, is at least the absolute left endpoint difference minus the absolute width difference. The overlap error and the reference leakage bound make this deficit $O(\varepsilon)$. Take further subsequences so that both scaled parameters converge, and use $t,d$ for their limits.

Define
\[
 \ell(u)=\int_{-\infty}^u \Phi(v)dv=\phi(u)+u\Phi(u).
\]
If the right endpoints of a narrower and wider arc differ, in that order, by $u\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon)$, the right-end leakage divided by $\varepsilon/(2\pi)$ tends to $\ell(u)$. For a left endpoint the argument changes sign. One obtains this by putting the wider endpoint at zero, integrating the rescaled source coordinate up to $u+o(1)$, and using the limiting Gaussian probability $\Phi(v)$ of displacement outside that endpoint. The sub-Gaussian density estimate supplies domination on the negative half-line. This is a lower bound when only a selected endpoint is counted. For the concentric label-3 reference both ends give the full limit, since the rest of the narrower arc stays a fixed distance from either endpoint. All other endpoints remain a fixed distance from the local intervals under consideration.

At the left boundary of label 3, the narrower label $F_1$ contributes $\ell(t)$. At the right boundary of label 3, the narrower label $G_2$ contributes $\ell(t+d)$. If $d\ge0$ and $G_3$ is narrower, its two endpoint contributions are $\ell(t)$ and $\ell(-t-d)$. If $d\le0$ and $F_3$ is narrower, they are $\ell(-t)$ and $\ell(t+d)$. Thus the lower bound for the total scaled consecutive-pair loss is
\[
 \begin{cases}
  2\ell(t)+\ell(t+d)+\ell(-t-d),&d\ge0,\\
  \ell(-t)+\ell(t)+2\ell(t+d),&d\le0.
 \end{cases}
\]
At $d=0$ the formulas agree. The function $\ell$ is strictly positive and convex, so both bounds are strictly larger than $2\ell(0)$. The scaled loss of the reference tends to $2\ell(-|d|/2)\le2\ell(0)$. This is the required contradiction, including the common-coordinate case.

## From simultaneous saturation to the simplex

Suppose an actual balanced planar global maximizer attains its exact pair envelope almost everywhere. Choose the full-measure set of radii where its slices are Lebesgue points as maps into $L^1(S^1)^3$, where the slice agrees almost everywhere with the strict adjusted-score winners, and where the pair equality holds for almost every partner. The strict winner property follows by polar Fubini from the analytic scoring rule. No adjusted pair contrast is identically zero, since injectivity of the positive-correlation Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator would then make the two indicator functions differ by a constant. Approaching a good radius by partners whose slices converge in $L^1$ gives self-equality, by continuity of the pair value and maximum. Thus almost every slice consists of consecutive arcs, possibly with empty or full labels.

Two-positive and three-positive slices cannot coexist on positive-measure sets by the boundary comparison. If there are only pure and two-positive slices, every label occurring on two-positive slices has a common fixed center, because pairs sharing that label must attain its scalar aligned-arc bound. For two slices with the same two labels both scalar bounds can be attained simultaneously, because both center pairs are antipodal. For distinct two-label types only the shared label contributes. Thus each occurring two-label type requires its two fixed centers to be antipodal. All three types are impossible, and at most two types put all nonconstant centers on a single diameter. Pure slices have zero angular moments. All three Gaussian first moments would therefore be collinear, contrary to [full moment rank at an actual maximizer](THREE_CELL_GLOBAL_OBSTRUCTIONS.md#one-active-dimension-is-impossible). There are consequently three-positive slices on a positive-measure set, and no two-positive slices except on a null set.

Fix one good three-positive partner and discard the null set of radii that do not pair optimally with it. The boundary comparisons show that the remaining three-positive profiles stay uniformly interior on each compact radial interval, and also as $r\downarrow0$. For a contrary sequence, pass to a limit of the consecutive arcs and parameters. A two-positive limit contradicts the two-versus-three comparison by continuity, using the normalized kernel at parameter zero if needed. A pure limit contradicts the separated-maximizer argument for the two small labels. At every compact interval and at the origin the fixed partner keeps the parameters in a compact range.

Pure and three-positive radii cannot both occur among the good radii. Otherwise their closed radial supports meet at some $r_0>0$, since their union contains the full-measure good set and the positive radial axis is connected. Take pure radii tending to $r_0$ with fixed winning label 1, and three-positive radii tending to $r_0$ whose arcs converge. The limiting arc lengths are all positive by the interior bound. At radius $r_0$, continuity of the analytic adjusted scores $V_i$ implies that $V_1$ is largest everywhere from the pure sequence, while each $V_i$ is largest on its limiting open arc. Thus $V_1=V_i$ on the open limiting arc of $i$, and analyticity in angle gives equality of all three adjusted scores on the entire circle.

For each pair contrast take its first radial Taylor coefficient at $r_0$ that is not identically zero in angle. This coefficient exists because the scores are analytic and the contrast cannot vanish identically on a planar open set. Its angular zeros are finite. Away from the union of those zero sets, the signs of all pair contrasts stabilize on each radial side of the circle, locally uniformly in angle. Hence the two sequences approach from opposite sides. Orient the radial differential toward the side with three-positive slices. Along the circle the differential of $V_1$ is minimal among the three by purity on the opposite side. It is also maximal on the limiting open label-1 arc. All three radial differentials therefore agree on that arc, hence identically on the circle by analytic continuation in angle. Their tangential differentials agree because the scores agree there. On a subarc of limiting label 2 avoiding the finite angular zero sets, the stabilized winners are 1 and 2 on the two sides. This is an actual regular jump arc with zero pair-score gradient, which contradicts [the reciprocal-gradient bound (G20)](THREE_CELL_GLOBAL_OBSTRUCTIONS.md#regular-interface-arcs-and-isolated-triple-junctions).

It follows that almost every radius has three positive labels, uniformly bounded away from zero near the origin. The origin is in the closure of all three strict winner sets, so it is a genuine triple junction. The [distinct-gradient conclusion following (G20)](THREE_CELL_GLOBAL_OBSTRUCTIONS.md#regular-interface-arcs-and-isolated-triple-junctions) applies there. It combines the local analytic jump-arc analysis with the prohibition on such an arc accumulating where its pair gradient vanishes. The three gradients are noncollinear as well. For three collinear distinct gradients the middle linear score wins no positive opening angle, and the corresponding circular mass tends to zero by linearizing the scores at the origin. The same linearization with noncollinear gradients gives $L^1$ convergence of the actual slices at the origin to the three positive arc sectors of the linear winner rule. Write $p_0$ for their interior mass vector.

The good tail profiles also stay uniformly interior. Otherwise take good radii $r_n\to\infty$ with masses tending to the boundary. For each $r_n$ choose a good partner $s_n$ from its full-measure set of equality partners such that $r_n s_n\to c_0$ for a fixed $c_0>0$. The partner slices converge to the origin arcs, and the parameters tend to a strictly positive finite number. The same two boundary comparisons exclude both two-positive and pure limits for $p(r_n)$.

Choose any interior accumulation point $p_\infty$ of profiles on the good tail radii. For a fixed good radius $r>0$, choose a sequence of its good equality partners tending to infinity whose profiles tend to $p_\infty$. This can be done for almost every $r$ with the same accumulation point: choose $p_\infty$ in the essential tail limit set, namely the intersection over tail cutoffs of the closed supports of the tail profile pushforward measures. These supports are nonempty nested compact sets, uniformly interior for large cutoffs. Each tail neighborhood of this point has a positive-measure preimage, which intersects the full-measure partner set. The angular parameters tend to infinity. If $p(r)\ne p_\infty$, the compact separated large-parameter comparison contradicts equality for these pairs of consecutive arcs. Therefore $p(r)=p_\infty$ almost everywhere. Balance gives $p_\infty=(1/3,1/3,1/3)$, and equal-mass pair equality makes the same three arcs occur at almost every radius. These are the regular simplex sectors.

## Uniform slack on compact correlation intervals

Let $J\subset(0,1)$ be compact. Integrate the exact paired maximum against the Gaussian joint radial measure at correlation $\rho$, and denote the result by $\mathcal U_\rho(p)$. If $J$ contains any correlations with nonsimplex global maximizers, then
\[
 \inf\{\mathcal U_\rho(p_A)-S_\rho(A):\rho\in J,
        \ A\text{ a nonsimplex balanced planar global maximizer}\}>0.
\]
Indeed the joint radial measure has marginals $\mu$, independently of correlation, and $0\le E_\kappa\le1$. Radial tails can therefore be discarded uniformly; on compact positive radial boxes the kernel and pair maximum are continuous in correlation and uniformly Lipschitz in prescribed masses. Strong convergence of planar partitions implies $L^1(\mu)$ convergence of their profiles by Fubini. Thus the expression in braces is continuous along strongly convergent maximizing sequences. The [strong compactness of actual maximizers and fixed-correlation simplex isolation](THREE_CELL_GLOBAL_OBSTRUCTIONS.md#one-simplex-cell-determines-the-whole-partition) give a nonsimplex maximizing limit for any sequence in this family. A sequence with loss tending to zero would give exact pair saturation almost everywhere at the limit, since the joint radial density is strictly positive. That contradicts the rigidity just proved.

As a consequence, a weak upper bound $\mathcal U_\rho(p)\le S_\rho(T)$
for every balanced profile at one correlation already proves uniqueness
of the simplex there, even if other profiles attain equality in the
envelope inequality. Any actual maximizer would have to saturate its
paired envelope. Strong maximizer compactness and the uniform local
simplex comparison then imply exact simplex optimality with equality
rigidity at every correlation in some open neighborhood of this point.
Indeed, a sequence of nonsimplex maximizers at neighboring correlations
would converge, up to a subsequence and rotations, to a simplex at the
original correlation. The uniform local comparison excludes that sequence.
This also applies to all dimensions via planar reduction of actual
maximizers.

The compact-band slack has no explicit value in this proof. A comparison that rules out nonsimplex ties with positive envelope slack remains open. This is a written internal result. Independent replay, formal verification and external review have not been performed.
