# Complementary bounds and a radial trial for the six-cell gap

There are two directed comparisons for the ordinarily balanced
standard sector of the six-cell simplex. At \(\rho=3/4\), a
degree-13 signed finite envelope has a certified leading-mode
bracket from a radial Krylov trial and an independent spectral
count. A second comparison acts on the true operator. It
uses an exact identity for its radial action and a separate
Hermite bound on the complement of the radial moment. The
second comparison gives a cover of \([7/10,77/100]\) and
certifies the sufficient stationary-gap ratio throughout
that cover. With the previous initial interval and the
settled nonstandard sectors, the full balanced six-cell
interface gap modulo rotations holds for all
\(0<\rho\le77/100\) in every ambient dimension at least five.

Use the facet Gaussian probability \(\mu\), facet mass \(p\),
and forward gap coordinates of the
[degree-14 standard-sector proof](SIX_CELL_WEIGHTED_STANDARD_GAP.md).
Write \(D=D_\rho\) for its row normalization and \(L_\rho=\sigma A_\rho\)
for its interaction, where \(\sigma=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\). Thus the
normalized standard form is \(D-L_\rho\) on
\(\mu\)-mean-zero functions invariant under the untied permutations,
and
\[
       1\le D\le(3p)^{-1},\qquad p>747/5000 .
\]
On a slab \([a,c]\subset(0,1)\) write \(E=E_a\) for the existing
fourfold product reciprocal envelope, so
\(D_\rho^{-1}\le E\) throughout the slab.
The directed ambient Hermite cutoff of degree \(n\) gives
\[
               L_\rho\preceq K+tI_\mu,\qquad t\ge c^n/p,
                                                               \tag{SG1}
\]
where \(K\) retains the signed finite tail corrections of
(FV5). This analytic estimate controls the entire infinite tail.
The 35 tilted Gaussian terms for \(E\), the one orthant measure
for \(\mu\), and the exact Hermite coefficients are the same
inputs as in the previous checker.

## A complementary enclosure at a fixed cutoff

For any positive weight \(w\), let \(G_w\) be the Gram of the
finitely many monomial orbit features after eliminating the
ordinary balance constraint in \(L^2(w^{-1}d\mu)\). For a
linear combination \(v\) of the features its quadratic form is
\[
        G_w[v]=\inf_{h\in\mathbb R}\int w(v-h)^2\,d\mu .
\]
We need \(K\prec D-t\). Put \(z=D^{-1}\) and \(z_0=2241/5000\).
The convex function \(z/(1-tz)\) lies below its secant on
\([z_0,1]\). Substituting \(z\le E\) into that increasing
affine secant gives
\[
 (D-t)^{-1}\le \alpha E-\beta,\qquad
    \alpha=\frac1{(1-t)(1-tz_0)},\quad \beta=tz_0\alpha .
                                                               \tag{SG2}
\]
The weight on the right is positive since \(E\ge z_0\).
Evaluating the infimum in \(G_{\alpha E-\beta}\) at the
\(E\)-weighted mean of \(v\) gives the directed domination
\[
             G_{(D-t)^{-1}}\preceq G_*:=\alpha G_E-\beta G_1 .
                                                               \tag{SG3}
\]
The negative term is valid because evaluation at the same
constant gives at least \(G_1[v]\) in the subtracted integral.
Also \(G_*\succ0\) since \(G_E\succeq z_0G_1\) and the polynomial
features are independent modulo constants. If \(Q\) is the
symmetric coefficient matrix of \(K\), the abstract finite
operator \(T=QG_*\) is self-adjoint in the \(G_*\) metric.
An upper spectral bound \(u>0\) for \(T\) transfers to \(K\)
in the \(D-t\) metric. Indeed
\(Q\preceq uG_*^{-1}\preceq u G_{(D-t)^{-1}}^{-1}\).
This argument permits the signed cutoff corrections in \(Q\).

The complementary estimate is the right-of-shift bound in
[Zimmermann–Mertins, Remark 1.7 and Theorem 4.2](https://doi.org/10.4171/ZAA/677)
and [Barrenechea–Boulton–Boussaïd, Corollary 11](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00211-016-0822-1).
The latter orders eigenvalues on the right from the nearest to
the farthest from the shift. To use its first positive bound for
the top eigenvalue, it is necessary to count the right-side modes.
Here an even trace satisfies
\[
          \operatorname{tr}(T^{2j})<2s^{2j}
              \ \Longrightarrow\ \#\{\lambda(T)>s\}\le1
                                                               \tag{SG4}
\]
for a positive shift \(s\). The count uses no trial information.
A unit trial \(w\) with Rayleigh value \(q>s\) proves existence
of one such mode. Its directed squared residual is \(R=\|(T-q)w\|^2\)
in the \(G_*\) metric. The first complementary estimate gives
\[
       q\le\lambda_{\max}(T)\le
                               q+\frac{R}{q-s}.             \tag{SG5}
\]
In this one-mode case the upper bound follows directly from
\((x-s)^2\ge(\lambda_{\max}-s)(x-s)\) for each spectral value
\(x\), since the other ones are at most \(s\).
The trial is generated by exact powers of \(T\) on the radial and
linear features. The radial seed has coefficients \(5\) on the
average of \(g_i^2\) and \(-3\) on the average of \(g_i g_j\),
giving \(3|x|^2/2\). The directed Gram, trace and residual tests
do not use an approximate eigensolver.
With \(u<1\) from (SG5), (SG1) and \(D\ge1\) give
\[
                L_\rho\preceq[t+(1-t)u]D .                 \tag{SG6}
\]
The upper bound on the finite surrogate eigenvalue supplies a
bound for the true operator through (SG1)–(SG3); the lower
bound in (SG5) refers to the surrogate.

## The independently controlled true complement

Put \(v=|x|^2-4\). The cone leaves the Gaussian radial law
unchanged, so \(\int v\,d\mu=0\) and \(\int v^2\,d\mu=8\).
Use the codimension-one subspace
\[
          U=\{f:\ \int f\,d\mu=\int fv\,d\mu=0\}.
\]
In \(L^2(E^{-1}d\mu)\) within the ordinary-balanced space,
the radial moment is represented by the radial feature vector
centered in the \(E\) measure. Its removal replaces the feature
Gram by
\[
 G_\perp=G_E-\frac{(G_E r)(G_E r)^{\mathsf T}}{r^{\mathsf T}G_E r},
                                                               \tag{SG7}
\]
where \(r\) is the radial seed above. This is the Gram of
the compressed kernel's representers. The nonzero eigenvalues
of \(QG_\perp\) are real by self-adjointness. The 16th trace bounds
its norm and, by (SG1) and \(D\ge E^{-1},1\), gives
\[
       \langle f,L_\rho f\rangle_\mu\le c_* \|f\|_D^2
       \quad(f\in U),\qquad
       c_*=t+\bigl(\operatorname{tr}[(QG_\perp)^{16}]\bigr)^{1/16}.
                                                               \tag{SG8}
\]
This bound excludes a second eigenmode above \(c_*\) for the
true compressed interaction. It is independent of the
accuracy of any leading-mode approximation.

## A residual bound for the true radial trial

Use the reversible chain \(P=\rho D^{-1}L_\rho\) from
[the stationary normalization](SIX_CELL_REVERSIBLE_CHAIN.md).
The row identity on the cone is
\[
               Pv=\rho^2v+\sigma^2\,\frac{q_\rho}{D},
                        \qquad q_\rho=x\cdot\nabla D .        \tag{SG9}
\]
For each self or adjacent term \(k(x,y)=M_\rho(x,Ry)\),
Gaussian divergence in \(y\) gives
\(\mathbb E|Y|^2=4\sigma^2+\rho\,\mathbb E[x\cdot RY]\) in
that conditional integral. The boundary flux vanishes because
\(y\) is tangent to all cone faces. The \(x\)-radial logarithmic
derivative of \(k\) is
\((-\rho^2|x|^2+\rho x\cdot Ry)/\sigma^2\).
Summing the resulting identity with the row kernel weights
and dividing by \(D\) proves (SG9).

We bound \(q_\rho\) uniformly using the same conditional
probability as the row proof. Put \(s_i=\rho g_i/\sigma\),
let \(\Phi,\phi\) be the standard Gaussian distribution and
density, and let \(W=(S+N)/\sqrt2\) with independent standard
normal \(S\) and Rayleigh \(N\). Then
\[
    3pD=\mathbb E\prod_i\Phi(W+s_i),\qquad
    3p q_\rho=\mathbb E\sum_i s_i\phi(W+s_i)
                                      \prod_{j\ne i}\Phi(W+s_j).
                                                               \tag{SG10}
\]
In particular \(q_\rho\ge0\). For fixed \(W=w\), write
\(a_i=w+s_i\), \(t_i=\log\Phi(a_i)\), and \(m(a)=\phi(a)/\Phi(a)\).
The conditional summand is \(e^{\sum_i t_i}\) times the sum of
the four terms \(H(t_i)\), where \(H(\log\Phi(a))=(a-w)m(a)\).
Its derivatives are
\[
 H'(t)=1-(a-w)(a+m(a)),\qquad
 H''(t)=-\frac{a+m(a)+(a-w)(1+m'(a))}{m(a)}<0 .
\]
Here \(a+m(a)>0\) and \(1+m'(a)>0\) are the strict mean and
variance statements for a standard normal truncated above at \(a\).
Consequently Jensen's inequality at fixed \(\sum_i t_i\)
bounds the full conditional summand by
\(\max_{a\ge w}4(a-w)\phi(a)\Phi(a)^3\).
This scalar maximum decreases as \(w\) increases. Its strictly
concave logarithm in \(a>w\) has derivative
\(1/(a-w)-a+3m(a)\), so directed root brackets suffice.

Integration by parts in the Rayleigh variable gives the exact
distribution function
\[
                     \Pr(W\le w)=
           \Phi(\sqrt2 w)-2^{-1/2}e^{-w^2/2}\Phi(w).
\]
The checker integrates an upper step function on \([-6,6]\)
at 768 bins of width \(1/64\), with each bin evaluated at
its left endpoint. On \(w\ge6\) the conditional maximum is at
most \(4\phi(0)\). On \(w\le-6\) it is at most \(1+2|w|\),
using \(4|a|\phi(a)\le1\) and \(4\phi(a)\le2\).
The latter tail is bounded by the same expectation for
\(S/\sqrt2\le-6\), since \(W\ge S/\sqrt2\).
Dividing the resulting upper bound by \(2241/5000<3p\)
gives a strict directed ceiling \(q_*\) for \(q_\rho\)
at all positive correlations.

Let \(\mathcal T\) be the compression of \(P/\rho\) in
\(L^2(D\,d\mu)\) to the ordinarily balanced space. Projection
onto that space removes the direction \(D^{-1}\). Hence we
may subtract \(q_*/2\) from \(q_\rho\) in (SG9) when bounding
the compressed residual. Put
\[
 s_E=\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb R}\int E(v-\xi)^2d\mu,\qquad m_E=\int E\,d\mu .
\]
These are directed Gram and mass integrals at the left endpoint.
For the minimizing \(\xi\), weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\(8=\int v(v-\xi)d\mu\le\|v\|_D\sqrt{s_E}\).
With \(v_D=v/\|v\|_D\), over the whole slab
\[
      \|(\mathcal T-\rho)v_D\|_D^2\le
      \delta^2=\left(\frac{1-a^2}{a}\right)^2
                         \frac{q_*^2\,m_E s_E}{256}.         \tag{SG11}
\]

## The nonorthogonal block comparison

The space \(U\) is radial-orthogonal in \(\mu\), so its unit
vectors need not be orthogonal to \(v_D\) in the \(D\) norm.
The appropriate angle bound is
\[
 |\langle v_D,u\rangle_D|\le
 \kappa=\frac{1-z_0}{1+z_0}\qquad(u\in U,\ \|u\|_D=1).
\]
The \(\mu\) norms of \(v_D+u\) and \(v_D-u\) agree. Their
squared \(D\) norms have ratio at most \(1/z_0\), in either
order, because \(1\le D\le1/z_0\). This proves the bound.

For \(b>\max(c,c_*)\), set \(x=\langle(\mathcal T-\rho)v_D,v_D\rangle_D\)
and \(A=b-\rho\). The diagonal of \(b-\mathcal T\) on \(v_D\)
is \(A-x\). The part of the residual orthogonal to \(v_D\)
has squared norm at most \(\delta^2-x^2\). Thus for unit
\(u\in U\) the cross term, divided by \(\sqrt{A-x}\), is
bounded by
\[
      \kappa\sqrt{A-x}+\sqrt{\frac{\delta^2-x^2}{A-x}} .
\]
If \(b-c>\delta\), differentiation on \(|x|\le\delta\) gives
\(\max_x(\delta^2-x^2)/(A-x)=
2\delta^2/(A+\sqrt{A^2-\delta^2})\). Therefore the sufficient
directed test for \(b-\mathcal T\succeq0\) is
\[
 \left[
 \kappa\sqrt{b-a+\delta}
   +\sqrt{\frac{2\delta^2}{b-c+\sqrt{(b-c)^2-\delta^2}}}
 \right]^2 < b-c_* .                                        \tag{SG12}
\]
Indeed the diagonal on \(U\) is at least \(b-c_*\) by
(SG8), and every speed is a linear combination of \(v_D\)
and a vector in \(U\). This is a Schur test on that
decomposition. It accounts for the change of metric
between the independent count and the radial residual.

The resulting \(L_\rho\preceq bD\) gives
\(\gamma\ge1-\rho b\) for the reversible gap \(\gamma\)
by subtracting the ordinary mean in the stationary
Dirichlet form. Using \(Z<117/100\) from (MC2), the
stationary sufficient ratio on the slab is at least
\((1-ab)/[(1-a)(117/100)]\).
