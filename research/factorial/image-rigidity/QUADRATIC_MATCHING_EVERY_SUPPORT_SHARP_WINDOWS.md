# Exact sharpness of quadratic matching windows on every compatible support

The all-dimension upper bound used below is the complete written theorem
in QUADRATIC_STRONG_FACTORIAL_WINDOWS_FROM_MATCHINGS.md, portable
SHA256 b2bdec02fdd5d49e31fddd2c45c9bd157413aae145e3e9a13b58c29f2bdf5048.

Fix m>=1 and rational numbers c_ij of odd denominator for i<j, including0.
Fix subsets U,V of {1,...,m} and assume p=|U|+|V|>=1.
The intended diagonal support is U, the intended linear support is V, and
the intended cross support is

 E={ij:i,j in U, i<j, c_ij!=0}.

Let Lambda_m(X_1^a1...X_m^am)=a1!...am!.

**Theorem 1 (sharpness at every start on every compatible support).**
For every positive integer n there is a polynomial f over Qbar, with zero constant and satisfying
B_ij^2=4c_ij A_i A_j, whose nonzero diagonals are exactly U, nonzero
linears exactly V, and nonzero crosses exactly E, such that

 Lambda_m(f^j)=0 for n<=j<=n+p-2,
 and Lambda_m(f^(n+p-1))!=0.                              (1)

Its actual number of monomials is s=p+|E|. The universal p=k+ell window
is therefore optimal at EVERY positive starting index, even with this
exact support fixed. The witness f may depend on n. If E is empty,
this also proves sharpness of its actual-support s-window. If E is
nonempty, (1) proves sharpness of the STRONGER p-window only; it does
not assert sharpness of the longer actual-support s-window.

## 1. A homogeneous parameter family with no nonzero basepoint

For each pair i<j choose b_ij in Qbar with b_ij^2=4c_ij, setting b_ij=0
if c_ij=0. Fix any choices of the nonzero square roots. Introduce one
parameter u_i for each i in U and one separate parameter v_i for each
i in V. Even when i is in both sets these are different parameters.
There are exactly p parameters.

Put u_i=0 for i outside U and v_i=0 for i outside V, and define

 F(u,v;X)=sum_i u_i^2 X_i^2
          +sum_(i<j) b_ij u_i u_j X_i X_j
          +sum_i v_i^2 X_i.                              (2)

The relations B_ij^2=4c_ij A_i A_j hold identically.
Each coefficient of F is homogeneous of degree2 in the p parameters.
Hence H_j(u,v)=Lambda_m(F^j) is an ordinary homogeneous polynomial
of degree2j, or is identically zero, over Qbar.

The parameter map has no nonzero basepoint: if F is the zero polynomial,
its diagonal coefficients give every u_i^2=0, hence every u_i=0; its
linear coefficients then give every v_i^2=0, hence every v_i=0.
Thus every nonzero parameter vector gives a nonzero phase. This argument
uses monomial independence, not a generic-rank or cancellation claim.

## 2. Classical homogeneous existence

Fix any n>=1. For p>=2 apply the following standard algebraic-geometric
fact to the at most p-1 nonzero forms H_n,...,H_(n+p-2) in p variables:

Over an algebraically closed field, fewer than p homogeneous polynomials
of positive degree in p variables have a common nontrivial zero.

An exact primary reference is The Stacks Project, Lemma59.67.7, tag03RB:
https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03RB.
Its proof is by projective dimension theory. Equivalently, if the common
affine zero set were only the origin, the Nullstellensatz would make
the radical of their ideal the height-p maximal ideal of the origin;
the generalized principal ideal theorem bounds the height of an ideal
generated by at most p-1 elements by p-1, a contradiction.
Identically zero forms may simply be omitted.

We therefore obtain a nonzero parameter vector over Qbar with
H_n=...=H_(n+p-2)=0. If p=1 choose any nonzero parameter vector; there
are no imposed equations. In every case Section1 ensures the resulting
f is nonzero. The finitely many algebraic coordinates and b_ij lie in
one number field, so the witness can also be taken over a number field.
No claim of rational coordinates is made.

## 3. The upper bound forces the entire intended support

Let k' and ell' be the numbers of nonzero diagonal and linear
coefficients of this resulting f. They count exactly the nonzero u
and v parameters. Thus 1<=k'+ell'<=p.

The matching upper-bound theorem says that any nonzero admissible phase
has no k'+ell' consecutive zero factorial moments at a positive start.
If one of our p parameters vanished, then k'+ell'<=p-1. For p>=2,
the first k'+ell' moments of the specified block starting at n would
all vanish by Section2, a contradiction.
For p=1 the selected parameter is already nonzero.
Consequently every intended u_i and v_i is nonzero, giving exactly
the claimed diagonal and linear supports.

A cross coefficient b_ij u_i u_j is nonzero precisely when i,j are in U
and c_ij!=0. Its support is therefore exactly E, without any additional
cancellation or genericity condition. We have k'+ell'=p and actual
support s=p+|E|.

Applying the matching theorem once more at start n to this same f,
its p moments beginning at n cannot all vanish. The first p-1 of that
block already do, so Lambda_m(f^(n+p-1))!=0. This proves Theorem1.

## 4. Concrete uniform consequences and limits

Taking U=V={1,...,m} gives p=2m for EVERY fixed weight pattern.
At every positive start n there are examples with the2m-1 factorial
moments n,...,n+2m-2 zero and the moment n+2m-1 nonzero, with every
permitted coefficient nonzero.

For c_ij=1 choose b_ij=2. Then

 f=(sum_i u_i X_i)^2+sum_i v_i^2 X_i,

all u_i,v_i nonzero, has actual support m(m+3)/2, exactly2m-1 zeros in
the specified block, and its next moment nonzero. Thus the uniform2m bound for rank-one
quadratic parts plus arbitrary linear terms is sharp in every dimension.
In dimension3, taking n=1, this gives actual support9, five initial
zero moments, and a nonzero sixth moment. It is not a Strong Factorial counterexample.

For c_ij=0 the polynomial is diagonal, with actual support2m, so the
actual-support Strong Factorial bound itself is sharp. More generally
every fixed compatible diagonal/linear support has its sharp p-window.

For m=1, f=X^2-2X has Lambda_1(f)=0 and Lambda_1(f^2)=8.
For m=2 the rank-one dense support has five monomials and the sharp
uniform-four behavior already exhibited by an explicit algebraic witness;
the present existence proof does not require that explicit formula.

The all-dimension upper-bound theorem and the homogeneous existence
theorem are genuine mathematical inputs. The construction does not
imply either of them in reverse. No explicit coefficient formula,
irreducibility, numerical extrapolation, formal verification, external
review or priority claim is supplied. Full arbitrary quadratic SFC,
unrestricted SFC(2), and SFC(3) remain open here.
