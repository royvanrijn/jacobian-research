# Field discriminant certificates for ordinary consecutive factorial moments

For distinct ordinary exponent columns, take the two consecutive
polynomials \(P_n,P_{n+1}\), with \(n\ge2\). Replace each by its
primitive integral squarefree part \(F_m\), using the same variable.
This preserves the root sets. Put
\[
      G=\gcd\bigl(|\operatorname{disc} F_n|,
                   |\operatorname{disc} F_{n+1}|\bigr).
\]
A common irreducible factor can be screened by the field
discriminant of any one of its roots. The screen uses the
intersection of the adjacent Newton faces, including their
stepped residual factors. It permits arbitrary root moduli,
private rows and mixed orientations.

## The common field

Suppose \(H\) is a primitive irreducible common factor, \(\alpha\)
one of its roots, and \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) of degree \(D\).
The even factorial moment is strictly positive on the real line.
Indeed it is the expectation of an even power of a nonzero
real binomial on independent unit exponential inputs.
Thus \(K\) is totally imaginary and \(D\) is even.

The signed field discriminant \(\Delta_K\) satisfies
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 |\Delta_K|\mid G,\qquad
 \operatorname{sgn}\Delta_K=(-1)^{D/2},\qquad
 \Delta_K\equiv0,1\pmod4,\\
 |\Delta_K|\ \ge\
              \left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)^D
                      \frac{D^{2D}}{(D!)^2}.
 \tag{1}
 \end{gathered}
\]
The lower bound is Minkowski's discriminant bound with no
real embeddings. The sign follows from the signature of the trace
form. For the congruence, write the determinant of an embedding
matrix for an integral basis as \(S_{\mathrm e}-S_{\mathrm o}\),
the difference of its two permutation-parity sums. Its square is
\((S_{\mathrm e}+S_{\mathrm o})^2-4S_{\mathrm e}S_{\mathrm o}\).
Both \(S_{\mathrm e}+S_{\mathrm o}\) and
\(S_{\mathrm e}S_{\mathrm o}\) are rational algebraic integers,
since Galois automorphisms permute the rows of the matrix and either
preserve or interchange the two sums.

Divisibility holds even for nonmonic \(H=a_0 z^D+\cdots+a_D\).
For \(1\le k<D\) use the elements
\[
        \xi_k=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_i\alpha^{k-i}.
\]
They are algebraic integers: at each finite place use this
expression if \(\alpha\) is integral there. Otherwise use
\(H(\alpha)=0\) to write the same element as
\(-\sum_{i=k}^D a_i\alpha^{k-i}\), whose powers of
\(\alpha\) are all nonpositive. The lattice generated by
\(1,\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{D-1}\) has full rank in the ring of
integers. Its discriminant is \(\operatorname{disc}H\)
by the triangular basis change of determinant \(a_0^{D-1}\).
Consequently \(\Delta_K\mid\operatorname{disc}H\).
Gauss's lemma and
\(\operatorname{disc}(H B)=\operatorname{disc}(H)
\operatorname{disc}(B)\operatorname{Res}(H,B)^2\) give the
first assertion of (1).
[The binary-form order and its discriminant](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.5508)

The bound \(v_p(G)\) can use independently chosen rational
changes of the variable in the two arrays. A nonzero rational
rescaling of a root generates the same field. For example, if
\(F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^m a_kz^k\) is primitive and \(r\in\mathbb Z\),
scaling \(z\) by \(p^r\) and restoring primitive content gives
discriminant valuation
\[
 v_p(\operatorname{disc} F)
 +(m-1)\left(mr-2\min_{a_k\ne0}(v_p(a_k)+rk)\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
Formula (2) follows from scaling the roots and the leading
coefficient in the discriminant. It can be minimized over
integer \(r\) by the convex piecewise-linear formula. This
improves the cap on the field discriminant. The shared-branch
computation below still compares both polynomials in the
original common variable.

## The local branch and discriminant ledger

Fix \(p\mid G\) and write the common root valuation on a Newton
face as \(s=a/b\) in lowest terms, with \(b>0\). Read the leading
coefficient units on that face with index spacing \(b\), stripping
the left-index monomial. This gives the stepped residual polynomial
over \(\mathbb F_p\). Let \(g\) be a monic irreducible factor common
to the two residual polynomials at the face, of degree \(f\).
Let \(M\) be the smaller of its two multiplicities. Treat this
choice of \(p,s,g\) as one branch.

For a local irreducible factor of \(H\) in this branch, let \(t\)
be its residual multiplicity. Its degree is \(bft\). The
ramification index and residue degree of its root field
over \(\mathbb Q_p\) have the form
\[
                 e=bu,\qquad F=fv,\qquad uv=t .
 \tag{3}
\]
Furthermore the sum of \(t\) over the local irreducible factors
at a branch is at most \(M\).

To verify these claims, in a common algebraic closure choose a
uniformizer scale \(\pi^b=p\) and substitute \(z=\pi^a x\).
The valuation-zero roots of the rescaled polynomial reduce to the
nonzero roots of its residue on that face. After removing a
monomial the residue is in \(x^b\), because equality of the
weighted coefficient valuations forces the index spacing \(b\).
Gauss normalization commutes with multiplication, so a common
factor's stepped residual polynomial divides both parents' stepped
residual polynomials.
[Stepped residual comparison](MULTIVARIATE_PADIC_DIGIT_AUTOMATON.md#exact-recognition-of-the-newton-and-residue-data)

All conjugates of a local irreducible factor have the same
valuation and their values of \(\alpha^b/p^a\) reduce to
conjugates of a single residue root of \(g\). Thus the stepped
residue for that local factor is a power \(g^t\). Its width,
and hence its degree, is \(bft\).
The root value group forces \(b\mid e\), and the residue of
\(\alpha^b/p^a\) forces \(f\mid F\).
As the local degree is \(eF\), these facts give (3).
They do not require separable stepped roots in the unstepped
coordinate. They are a first-order instance of the
[Newton polygon and residual polynomial factorization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0807.2620).

The local contribution to \(v_p(\Delta_K)\) of these \(e,F\) is
\[
 \begin{cases}
 F(e-1),&p\nmid e,\\
 \text{an integer in }[Fe,\ F(e-1+e\,v_p(e))],&p\mid e.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4}
\]
Here the field extension is totally ramified of degree \(e\)
over its maximal unramified subextension. Over that subextension,
a uniformizer has an Eisenstein minimal polynomial of degree \(e\)
and generates the ring of integers. The valuation of its
derivative in the uniformizer gives the different exponent.
The derivative's \(i\)-th term, for \(1\le i\le e\), has valuation
congruent to \(i-1\) modulo \(e\) when nonzero, so the minimum
cannot cancel. At the top term the valuation is
\(e-1+e\,v_p(e)\). Every other term has valuation at least \(e\)
by the Eisenstein condition. Thus the different exponent is
\(e-1\) when \(p\nmid e\) and lies between \(e\) and the displayed
top valuation otherwise. The norm of the local different
multiplies the exponent by \(F\), giving (4).

For each branch, take any total \(0\le T\le M\), partition
it into positive integers \(t\), and use all factorizations
\(t=uv\) in (3). Sum the local degrees and the exponents allowed
by (4). Then sum over the branches at that prime. This is a
finite necessary list of pairs
\((D,v_p(\Delta_K))\), discarding exponents above \(v_p(G)\).
For each even \(D\) allowed by any separate degree bounds,
combine the lists over \(p\mid G\), using exponent zero at all
other primes. Every common factor's field discriminant must be
one of the resulting signed products and must satisfy (1).
If each candidate degree has an empty list or fails (1), the
two moments are coprime.

In particular an admissible local branch with integral slope
and residual multiplicity used just once can contribute no
ramification: \(b=t=1\) forces \(e=1\) in (3), even if the
two residual face polynomials themselves have repeated roots.
If every feasible local allocation at every discriminant prime
for a given degree is unramified, the sole candidate absolute
discriminant is \(1\), which (1) excludes.
The bound in (1) can be weakened to the exact rational bound
\((3/4)^D D^{2D}/(D!)^2\), which is greater than one for \(D\ge2\).

This is a physical coprimality certificate. If the certificate or
other proofs cover each terminal below the
[effective absolute ordinary cutoff](MULTIVARIATE_EFFECTIVE_ABSOLUTE_EVENTUAL_ANGLES.md),
all higher pairs are covered by that theorem. Terminal two is
covered by positivity of the quadratic at real arguments.
The local tests do not assert that every allocation or every
permitted discriminant lifts to a common root.

## An exact fully shared example

Use the two unpaired shared rows \((3,4),(9,6)\), at orders
\(2,3\). Their primitive arrays in increasing coefficient order
are
\[
 (477360,\ 1365,\ 2),\qquad
 (1288450800,\ 2202480,\ 1995,\ 1).
\]
Their discriminants are \(-1955655\) and
\(-7269307276032648000\), with \(G=45\).
At \(p=3\) the two common branches have \(s=1,2\), each of
capacity one and residual degree one. A degree-two candidate
must therefore use them both, and is unramified at \(3\).
At \(p=5\) the only common branch has \(s=1/2\), capacity one
and residual degree one. A degree-two candidate there has
\(e=2,F=1\), so its discriminant has valuation one at \(5\).

Both primes leave degree two in the residual-degree screen.
Combining their field information with \(G=45\) and the
totally imaginary signature leaves only the signed value
\(-5\), which is \(3\bmod4\). This illustrates the extra
discriminant exclusion at an adjacent pair already covered by
the general low-order theorem.

The following exact fixture checks the factorial arithmetic,
the discriminants and the two branch inventories. Polynomial
coefficient lists increase in degree.

```python
from fractions import Fraction
from functools import reduce
from math import comb, factorial, gcd

def primitive(m):
    coefficients = [comb(m, k) for k in range(m + 1)]
    for a, b in ((3, 4), (9, 6)):
        scalar = factorial(m * min(a, b))
        for k in range(m + 1):
            coefficients[k] *= factorial((m-k)*a+k*b) // scalar
    content = reduce(gcd, coefficients)
    return [v // content for v in coefficients]

def units(values, p):
    result = []
    for value in values:
        exponent = 0
        while value % p == 0:
            value //= p
            exponent += 1
        result.append((exponent, value % p))
    return result

def faces(coefficients, p):
    data = units(coefficients, p)
    slopes = set()
    for i, (vi, _) in enumerate(data):
        for j in range(i+1, len(data)):
            vj = data[j][0]
            s = Fraction(vi-vj, j-i)
            if all(v+s*k >= vi+s*i for k,(v,_) in enumerate(data)):
                slopes.add(s)
    out = {}
    for s in slopes:
        h = min(v+s*k for k,(v,_) in enumerate(data))
        on = [k for k,(v,_) in enumerate(data) if v+s*k == h]
        l, r = min(on), max(on)
        out[s] = [data[k][1] if k in on else 0
                  for k in range(l, r+1, s.denominator)]
    return out

quadratic, cubic = primitive(2), primitive(3)
assert quadratic == [477360, 1365, 2]
assert cubic == [1288450800, 2202480, 1995, 1]
c, b, a = quadratic
left = b*b-4*a*c
d, c, b, a = cubic
right = b*b*c*c-4*a*c**3-4*b**3*d-27*a*a*d*d+18*a*b*c*d
assert (left, right, gcd(left, right)) == (
    -1955655, -7269307276032648000, 45)
assert faces(quadratic, 3) == {1: [2,2], 2: [1,2]}
assert faces(cubic, 3) == {1: [1,2,1], 2: [2,1]}
assert faces(quadratic, 5) == {Fraction(1,2): [2,2]}
assert faces(cubic, 5) == {1: [2,1], Fraction(1,2): [1,1]}
```
