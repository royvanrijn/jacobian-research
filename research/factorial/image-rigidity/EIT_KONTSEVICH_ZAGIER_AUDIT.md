## Ordinary periods and the exponential variants

The original Kontsevich–Zagier paper, *Periods* (2001), defines ordinary periods using absolutely convergent rational-function integrals over domains given by rational polynomial inequalities. Section 1.1 (printed p.3, PDF p.4) gives the definition. Section 1.2, Conjecture 1 (printed p.7, PDF p.8), concerns deriving equality of two such presentations using additivity, algebraic changes of variables, and Stokes. It does not assert that an arbitrary displayed integral is nonzero.

Exponential periods replace the algebraic differential by an expression e^q times that differential, with twisted differential d+dq wedge. The ordinary conjecture and the restricted exponential statements in II–III have different domains. In particular, the title of III does not establish the ordinary conjecture in all dimensions, completeness for all curves, or numerical completeness for arbitrary rapid-decay exponential periods.

Section 4.3 (printed pp.34–35, PDF pp.35–36) introduces exponential periods and proposes an appropriate extension of Conjecture 1. It does not prove unrestricted exponential formal-period injectivity.

The primary ordinary source is [Kontsevich–Zagier, Periods](https://www.ihes.fr/~maxim/TEXTS/Periods.pdf). Its conjectural status is preserved in the graph. No claim about a resolution of the full ordinary conjecture is imported from the EIT repository.

Section 4.1 of the original paper (printed p.31, PDF p.32) formulates the abstract period algebra and conjectures numerical evaluation is an isomorphism. The formal torsor description in the following discussion is not itself a proof of numerical injectivity.

A relevant established ordinary result has a separate boundary: [Huber–Wüstholz, Transcendence and linear relations of 1-periods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.10104v8), Theorem 13.3 (printed p.110, PDF p.126), treats linear relations between ordinary degree-at-most-one periods. Remark 13.2(3) explicitly distinguishes this vector space from a ring closed under multiplication. This is not an automatic polynomial/tensor relation theorem, exponential theorem, or arbitrary higher-dimensional result. Only the exact theorem and scope remarks were inspected here; no new validation of its long proof or application is asserted.

## The exact claims in Papers II and III

Write k for the algebraic closure of Q. Paper II takes q,r in k[x], a finite algebraic degree-zero endpoint cycle Delta, and a compact piecewise smooth chain Gamma with boundary Delta. Its generators have numerical value

    I(q,r,Delta) = integral_Gamma r(x) exp(q(x)) dx,

together with exp(beta) for beta in k. The formal ambient relations allow smooth affine curves, but the theorem concerns the subspace generated by the affine-line integrals and the zero-dimensional exponentials.

Paper II, thm:main, claims injectivity of this linear period map. The proof reduces amplitudes by twisted exactness, separates endpoint exponentials, transfers numerical relations to functional relations using E-functions, and realizes the latter on a simultaneous finite algebraic cover. The essential exact interfaces are lem:twisted-reduction, thm:moment-package, thm:direct-sum-rigidity, thm:transfer, thm:combined-moment-rigidity, lem:simultaneous-cover, and thm:functional-relations-formal. The mixed-phase direct-sum argument and common-cover realization are additional work beyond the previously audited statement in I.

Paper III, def:formal-algebra and thm:main, forms the symmetric algebra of the linear formal space and imposes

    E(0)=1,
    E(lambda) E(mu)=E(lambda+mu),
    E(lambda) P(q,r,Delta)=P(q+lambda,r,Delta).

Its claimed injectivity concerns this tensor-generated algebra. An arbitrary coupled multivariate phase or arbitrary multidimensional simplex period has not thereby acquired a presentation in that algebra.

The nonlinear input is substantive: thm:functional-linearity separates an affine constrained block from a full vector-translation block after adjoining a finitely generated group of exponentials. This uses the horizontal-endomorphism normal form, spectral gauge, reductivity after base change, the affine Galois exact sequence, and meromorphic exclusion. Polynomial value transfer then uses Beukers lifting and Paper II. Merely knowing linear injectivity does not supply this nonlinear theorem.

The graph records internal reproductions of the exact II–III main claims through the explicit moment-package, lifting, formal-realization and Picard–Vessiot premises below. This does not establish the representation or nonzero-class premises needed for a new FC/SFC application.

## Theorem: complete Gamma-packet functional kernel over actual constants

Fix an integer N>=2, put gamma_m=Gamma(m/N) for 1<=m<N, and let

    C_N = Qbar(gamma_1,...,gamma_(N-1)).

On the universal cover of C^* define G_m(z)=gamma_m z^(-m/N). The map from C_N(z)[X_1,...,X_(N-1)] to meromorphic functions, sending X_m to G_m, has kernel generated by

    gamma_1^m X_m - gamma_m X_1^m       (2<=m<N),
    X_1^N - gamma_1^N z^(-1).

Proof. All gamma_m are positive and nonzero by their defining convergent real Gamma integrals. Write t=z^(-1/N). The function t is transcendental over the constant field C_N and z=t^(-N). The extension C_N(t)/C_N(t^N) has degree N: its degree is at most N from the displayed power equation, while t->zeta t for each of the N distinct Nth roots of unity supplies N distinct automorphisms fixing t^N. Thus 1,t,...,t^(N-1) are linearly independent over C_N(z).

The displayed relations vanish upon evaluation. In their quotient the linear relations eliminate each X_m with m>=2, since gamma_1 is invertible in C_N. The quotient is therefore

    C_N(z)[X_1] / (X_1^N-gamma_1^N z^(-1)).

Its evaluation X_1->gamma_1 t is injective by the preceding basis independence. Hence these relations generate the full kernel. For N=2 the first list is empty and the same argument applies.

For Re(z)>0, substituting y=zx in the convergent Gamma integral on the positive real axis first gives integral_0^infinity x^(m/N-1)e^(-zx)dx=gamma_m z^(-m/N) for positive real z; holomorphy gives the identity throughout that half-plane, followed by analytic continuation to the cover. Thus this is the complete fixed-denominator packet calculation in IV, cor:complete-gamma-packet (lines 2860 onward), independently of its general relative theorem.

The same basis argument gives thm:functional-gamma-presentation for any nonempty subpacket with exponents m_1,...,m_s between 1 and N−1. Over C_Gamma(z), write M(a)=sum_j m_j a_j. Group the monomials by M(a) modulo N. Within each residue the relations are generated by the differences gamma^b X^a − z^(−q) gamma^a X^b with M(a)−M(b)=qN. Different residues are independent by the cyclic basis. The actual constants C_Gamma already contain the gamma_j. Over Qbar(z), the same residue grouping instead leaves coefficient equations among the constant Gamma monomials, separately for each residue.

There is a Laurent model of this generic quotient over C_Gamma[z,z^−1]. Let e=gcd(N,m_1,...,m_s), n=N/e and u=t^e for t=z^(−1/N). The image algebra is exactly C_Gamma[u,u^−1] with u^n=z^−1. Each t^(m_j) is invertible since its N-th power is an invertible power of z. Bézout then generates t^e and its inverse. Thus the generic degree is n, and the model is free of rank n over the Laurent base.

At z=1 the Laurent model is C_Gamma[u]/(u^n−1). All n roots of unity are in C_Gamma and distinct. The positive-real branch evaluates u at 1, selecting a single factor of the product of n copies of C_Gamma. The evaluation kernel is (u−1) in that fiber and is nonzero since n≥2. Equivalently it is generated there by the X_j−gamma_j. This gives an explicit specialization where generic functional injectivity over actual constants coexists with additional numerical relations. The argument concerns a specified coefficient field containing the Gamma values. Their relations over Qbar and numerical comparison for other Gamma-weighted families remain open.

## Proposition: injectivity needs a nonzero formal class

Let A be precisely the formal algebra defined in III, let per:A->C be its period map, and let J be a proposed angular period. If there is an explicitly justified xi in A with per(xi)=J, xi is nonzero in A, and per is injective, then J is nonzero.

Proof. If J were zero, injectivity would force xi=0, contrary to the second hypothesis.

This conditional implication has three separate inputs. Defining xi in an ambient, larger period category does not establish membership in A. Giving an integral presentation does not establish xi is nonzero. A functional theorem over a constant-period field does not automatically establish numerical injectivity at a chosen algebraic parameter.

## Proposition: polynomial amplitudes can give zero compact periods

The nonzero polynomials q(x)=x^2 and r(x)=2x, on the compact chain from -1 to 1 with distinct algebraic endpoints, satisfy

    integral_(-1)^1 2x exp(x^2) dx
      = [exp(x^2)]_(-1)^1 = 0.

Proof. The integrand is the derivative of exp(x^2), and the endpoint values coincide. Equivalently r=q' and the twisted class is an endpoint term which cancels.

This example refutes blanket nonvanishing for nonzero polynomial amplitudes. It is fully compatible with linear or polynomial formal-period injectivity, since its formal class is already zero by twisted Stokes. It does not contradict the dx theorem of I.

## Proposition: even a strictly positive amplitude can cancel

The simplex candidate gives a stronger obstruction. Set

    g(t)=t^2(1-t)^2,
    p(t)=g''(t)+25g(t)
        =25((t-1/2)^2-1/100)^2+14/25 > 0.

Nevertheless integral_0^1 p(t) exp(5it) dt=0. Indeed,

    d/dt [(g'(t)-5i g(t)) exp(5it)] = p(t) exp(5it),

and g and g' vanish at both endpoints. This is an exact twisted-Stokes identity with a rational, strictly positive polynomial amplitude and algebraic complex phase.

The [pinned simplex candidate](https://github.com/long-mathematics/exponential-integral-theorem/blob/92db79eedeb064694ff20e9201d73fc2bc0857f4/notes/candidates/2026-10-10-simplex-program/simplex-exponential-periods.tex), prop:counterexample, records this example. It rules out an induction that replaces the actual sliced simplex weight by an arbitrary positive polynomial weight. It does not refute constant-amplitude simplex nonvanishing, nor the particular Gamma/binomial weight families.

## Proposition: direct ordinary factorial moments reduce to boundary values

Let h be a polynomial with algebraic coefficients, and define

    R_h(x) = sum_(j=0)^deg(h) h^(j)(x).

Then R_h-R_h'=h and

    integral_0^infinity h(x) exp(-x) dx
      = R_h(0) = sum_k k! [x^k] h(x).

Proof. Differentiating -exp(-x) R_h gives exp(-x) h. The exponential dominates every polynomial at positive real infinity, so the endpoint at infinity is zero and the endpoint at zero gives R_h(0). The finite derivative sum at zero equals the factorial coefficient sum.

These direct factorial integrals have a noncompact domain and are not among the compact generators in II–III as presented. Even in a larger rapid-decay formalism their twisted-exact calculation gives algebraic boundary values. Their formal presentation alone supplies no proof that two consecutive such values cannot both vanish. For complex polynomial coefficients, any passage to algebraic coefficients is a further specialization argument and must be justified in the intended reduction.

## Theorem: exact polynomial moment package used by II–III

For each polynomial q in k[x] of degree n>=2 and algebraic endpoint cycle, its reduced moment vector U=(integral x^j exp(zq)dx)_(0<=j<n-1) satisfies

    U'=(C+D/z)U + z^(-1) sum_lambda b_lambda exp(lambda z).

The coordinates are E-functions. The homogeneous differential module is semisimple, C is semisimple, Spec(D)={-1/n,...,-(n-1)/n}, and the compression of D to each C-eigenspace has every eigenvalue in (-1,0). These properties are preserved by finite direct sums, including coincident critical values belonging to different phases.

Proof and retained dependency. The existing LONG_EIT_AND_FC2_AUDIT.tex, Section 1, proves this exact package, rather than only the dx nonvanishing conclusion. Divide qx^j=h_jq'+r_j and use integration by parts; the residue is the transpose of x^j -> -h_j'. The E-function height and common-denominator bounds are for every reduced coordinate, not just U_0. The semisimplicity argument uses the finite polynomial direct image, its small-map intermediate extension, Riemann–Hilbert, Fourier equivalence and generic localization, with signs checked there. On k[x]/(q'), multiplication by q is semisimple. At a critical point of multiplicity m, the local flag q'/(x-tau)^j has compressed residue diagonal -m/(m+1),...,-1/(m+1). Critical points with a common value remain separate local factors. Thus all assertions here are precisely among the previously retained internally audited inputs. No new geometric comparison theorem is assumed.

## Theorem: internally reproduced compact linear KZ statement

For exactly the compact affine-line formal space defined in II, the numerical period map is injective, under the retained moment package and the matched linear E-function lifting and Lindemann–Weierstrass inputs.

Proof. First use r=R'+q'R+s, deg(s)<=deg(q)-2, at z=1. Leading-degree elimination proves existence and uniqueness. Twisted Stokes replaces the exact term by sum_alpha d_alpha R(alpha)E(q(alpha)). Constant and linear phases reduce completely to endpoint exponentials; for a nonconstant linear phase the map R -> R'+q'R is invertible on each polynomial degree filtration. The reduction is at z=1 and is not asserted to remain the same as z varies.

For a finite family of reduced phases, take the direct-sum matrices C,D of the preceding theorem. Every rational horizontal endomorphism P satisfying P'=[C+D/z,P] is constant. There is no finite pole away from zero by order comparison. A pole of order a at zero would give -a in Spec(ad D), a subset of (-1,1). Thus P is polynomial. If its degree d is positive, its leading term commutes with C. Compressing the next equation to a nonzero C-eigenblock gives dP_d=[D_bar,P_d], again impossible since the commutator spectrum lies in (-1,1). This works with repeated critical values and does not require diagonalizability of D_bar.

Semisimplicity now makes every differential submodule the scalar extension of a constant C,D-invariant subspace. Suppose a numerical linear relation between the reduced moments and finitely many endpoint exponentials holds at z=1. Adjoining these exponentials gives an E-function system with only possible finite pole zero, so Beukers lifting applies at 1. Let M be the row module of rational combinations of U lying in the rational span of these exponentials. Differentiation shows M is a differential submodule; hence M=W tensor C(z) for constant W. The lifted polynomial row has prescribed value v at 1. Evaluating constant annihilators of W shows v belongs to W. Consequently the original constant linear combination F=vU is a rational combination of the finitely many exponentials.

Choose a constant basis for W. In its exponential expansion, each rational coefficient vector r_lambda solves

    r_lambda'=(C_W-lambda+D_W/z)r_lambda+b_lambda/z.

Pole order excludes all nonzero finite poles, and a negative integer leading exponent at zero contradicts Spec(D_W) contained in (-1,0). Thus all coefficients are polynomials. Their coefficients are algebraic: sufficiently many algebraic Taylor coefficients determine them through the invertible confluent Vandermonde matrix for the distinct algebraic exponential indices.

Choose one embedded algebraic polygonal tree through all endpoint values and all critical values of every phase. For each polynomial, its inverse graph is connected because polynomial monodromy on a regular fiber is transitive and is generated by loops around the finite critical values. Thus all endpoint cycles admit graph-chain presentations. Over each open edge the combined pushforward density Psi_e is an analytic sum of inverse-branch derivatives times the reduced amplitudes. Its endpoint singularities have exponent at least 1/r-1>-1 and are integrable.

An exponential polynomial F is annihilated by a nonzero constant-coefficient differential operator H(partial_z). Differentiating the compact integrals therefore makes all moments of H(t)Psi_e(t) vanish. Their Cauchy transform vanishes near infinity and then on the connected complement of the tree. At an interior edge point, split off a slightly larger subarc than the working disk intersects. The other edges and the remainder are holomorphic across that disk. The Cauchy jump is +/-2pi i H(t)Psi_e(t), so it is zero. Analyticity gives Psi_e=0 on every edge. It follows F is identically zero. The remaining numerical relation between distinct exponentials has zero coefficients by Lindemann–Weierstrass.

It remains to realize this functional identity formally. Normalize the affine target line in a common finite Galois extension containing all roots of all q_j(X)-t. The resulting connected smooth affine curve Y maps finitely to the target. Each inverse root is integral over k[t], hence defines a global regular map rho:Y->A1 with q_j rho=pi. Above an edge interior choose one lift eta. Finiteness gives compact closure; the local model t-t0=u^r gives unique endpoint limits and a piecewise C1 reparametrization. The endpoints are algebraic. All inverse branches of all phases are realized by the different global rho maps on this one eta.

The combined pulled-back regular differential Omega on Y restricts to Psi_e(t)dt=0 on an analytic arc. Therefore Omega is identically zero. Functoriality and bilinearity give the formal relation for that edge. The edge lifts may be chosen independently; no global glued lift is required. Summing edge relations proves that the original functional identity is zero in II's formal space. Together with the exact amplitude reduction this proves numerical injectivity. This reproduces the stated main theorem, not every optional extension or supplementary assertion.

## Lemma: the exponential-base functional relation ideal is affine-linear

Let Gamma be a finitely generated group of algebraic exponential indices containing the forcing indices for a finite direct-sum moment vector. Put A_Gamma=C[Gamma], R_Gamma=C[z][Gamma], and K_Gamma=Frac(R_Gamma), with epsilon_mu represented by exp(mu z). The kernel of R_Gamma[X]->analytic functions, X->U, is generated by affine-linear polynomials in X.

Proof. Distinct exponentials are linearly independent over C(z), so the group algebra embeds as a Laurent polynomial ring; K_Gamma has constants C. The torus rescales the basis exponentials. A finite-dimensional torus-orbit span in K_Gamma splits into finitely many characters, whose coefficients lie in C(z).

Horizontal endomorphisms have finite-dimensional solution space, so every such P is a sum exp(mu z)R_mu(z). Pole comparison makes R_mu polynomial. A positive-degree leading block maps a C-eigenspace of eigenvalue alpha into one of eigenvalue beta=alpha+mu. Compressing its next equation gives

    d R_(beta,alpha)
      = D_bar_beta R_(beta,alpha)-R_(beta,alpha)D_bar_alpha.

Only these diagonal compressions occur: the leading block's weight condition eliminates all other intermediate eigenspaces. The operator on the right has spectrum in (-1,1), excluding a positive integer d. Hence P is Laurent-exponential with constant coefficient blocks.
