# Divided-power dominance at prime-power progressions

Let \(F\) have finite nonempty support in \(\mathbb N^d\), let
\(\Lambda(X^\alpha)=\alpha!=\prod_i\alpha_i!\), and let \(s\) be the number
of its nonzero coefficients. The first two criteria give nonzero ordinary
factorial moments at every positive order divisible by a chosen prime power
\(q\). They prove the support-size window whenever \(q\le s\). They impose
support and valuation hypotheses, with no cutoff on degree, dimension,
support size or starting order.

Work in characteristic zero with a valuation \(v\) extending the \(p\)-adic
valuation and normalized by \(v(p)=1\). Only the subfield generated by the
coefficient ratios needs such a valuation. None of the arguments uses
completeness.

## 1. A least monomial and the sharp uniform radius

Put \(q=p^e\), \(e\ge1\), and \(R_q=v(q!)/q=(1-q^{-1})/(p-1)\).
Suppose the support has a coordinatewise least column \(\beta\) with
coefficient \(c\ne0\). Write \(F=cX^\beta G\), where
\[
 G=1+\sum_{\nu\ne0}u_\nu\,X^\nu/\nu!,\qquad
 u_\nu=\nu!\,c_{\beta+\nu}/c.
\]
If \(v(u_\nu)>-R_q\) for every nonzero \(u_\nu\), then for every positive
multiple \(m\) of \(q\),
\[
 \frac{\Lambda(F^m)}{c^m(m\beta)!}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\mathfrak m\) denotes the positive-valuation ideal, so (1) includes
integrality and valuation zero of the normalized moment.

Write \(E_\nu=X^\nu/\nu!\), let \(\mathcal D=\bigoplus_\nu\mathcal O E_\nu\)
be the divided-power polynomial lattice, and let \(v_{\mathcal D}\) take
the minimum coefficient valuation in this basis, with
\(v_{\mathcal D}(0)=+\infty\). The integer binomial structure constants
give \(v_{\mathcal D}(AB)\ge v_{\mathcal D}(A)+v_{\mathcal D}(B)\).
For constant-free \(T=\sum_{\nu\ne0}t_\nu E_\nu\), one also has
\[
 v_{\mathcal D}(T^j/j!)\ge j\min_\nu v(t_\nu)\qquad (j\ge1).
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed \(E_\nu^k/k!\) is an integer multiple of \(E_{k\nu}\). The multiplier
counts partitions of labeled colored objects into \(k\) unordered blocks
of profile \(\nu\). Permutations act freely on the ordered blocks because
\(\nu\ne0\). Expand the divided power of the sum and use multiplicative
integrality. This is the standard divided-power structure on the
augmentation ideal. See the
[Stacks Project, Section 23.5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07H4).

For \(1\le j\le q\), Legendre's identity gives
\[
 v(q!/(q-j)!)\ge jR_q,
 \tag{3}
\]
strictly if \(j<q\). In fact for \(0<\ell<q\) the base-\(p\) digit sum
satisfies \(s_p(\ell)\ge1>\ell/q\), and
\(v(\ell!)=(\ell-s_p(\ell))/(p-1)\).
See [Granville, Section 2](https://www.cecm.sfu.ca/organics/papers/granville/paper/binomial/html/node3-an.shtml).
Expand
\[
 G^q-1=\sum_{j=1}^q\frac{q!}{(q-j)!}\frac{(G-1)^j}{j!}.
\]
By (2) and (3) the coefficients have positive valuation. Thus \(G^m-1\)
also has coefficients of positive valuation for every positive multiple
of \(q\). The shifted factorial functional
\[
 L_\gamma(E_\nu)=\frac{\Lambda(X^\gamma E_\nu)}{\gamma!}
               =\prod_i\binom{\gamma_i+\nu_i}{\nu_i}
 \qquad(\gamma\in\mathbb N^d)
 \tag{4}
\]
is integral on \(\mathcal D\) and sends \(1\) to \(1\). Apply (4)
with \(\gamma=m\beta\) to prove (1).

For a particular polynomial, the single test \(v_{\mathcal D}(G^q-1)>0\)
suffices. This allows unequal valuations and cancellation between
contributions to the same exponent.

The radius is sharp for the progression statement. At order \(q\),
\[
 \Lambda((1+uX)^q)=\sum_{j=0}^q\frac{q!}{(q-j)!}u^j .
\]
Every root over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\) has valuation \(-R_q\).
For \(v(u)>-R_q\) the constant term has strictly lowest valuation.
For \(v(u)<-R_q\) the leading term has strictly lowest valuation, again
by (3). The polynomial has degree \(q\) and nonzero constant term.
This is sharpness for one tested order on a progression. It does not
give a full zero window.

## 2. A pivot that need not be a least monomial

Suppose the support has a pivot that is least in all but one coordinate,
where its exponent is a power of the same prime. Write, in coordinates
\(x,Y\),
\[
 F=cY^\beta\bigl(x^a G(x,Y)+H(x,Y)\bigr),\qquad
 a=p^b,\quad b\ge0,\quad c\ne0 .
 \tag{5}
\]
Use the pivot coefficient for \(c\). The columns with \(x\)-exponent below \(a\) go into \(H\). The other
columns go into \(x^aG\).
The remaining exponents can be arbitrary. Assume

- \(G(0)=1\) and \(v_{\mathcal D}(G^q-1)>0\). Set
  \(L=\min_{0\le r<q}v_{\mathcal D}(G^r)\le0\).
- \(\deg_x H<a\). Set \(A=(a-1)/(p-1)\). Each nonzero nonconstant
  coefficient of \(\widetilde H=p^{-A}H\) in the divided-power basis
  has valuation \(>-L\). Its constant coefficient, if nonzero, has
  valuation \(\ge-L+1/(p-1)\).

Then (1) holds with the denominator \(c^m(am)!(m\beta)!\).
If \(G\) is divided-power integral, then \(L=0\) and the first valuation
condition is automatic by Section 1. The radius bound in Section 1
also supplies a test when \(G\) is not integral.

For \(k>0\) we have \(v_{\mathcal D}(\widetilde H^k/k!)>-kL\ge-L\).
For a nonconstant contribution, apply (2) to the constant-free
part. For a nonzero constant \(h_0\), the valuation of \(h_0^j/j!\),
\(j>0\), exceeds \(-jL\) by at least \(s_p(j)/(p-1)>0\).
The divided-power expansion of the sum proves the bound. Also
\(v_{\mathcal D}(G^j)\ge L\) for all \(j\ge0\), by dividing \(j\)
by \(q\) and using the integrality of \(G^q\).

At order \(m\), the part with \(k\) copies of \(H\) in the normalized
moment is exactly
\[
 U_{m,k}\, L_{(a(m-k),m\beta)}
                 \left(G^{m-k}\widetilde H^k/k!\right),
 \qquad
 U_{m,k}=p^{Ak}\frac{m!}{(m-k)!}\frac{(a(m-k))!}{(am)!}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Every \(U_{m,k}\) is a unit, since scaling by \(a=p^b\) preserves digit
sums and \(v((aj)!)=Aj+v(j!)\) for \(j\ge0\).
For \(k>0\), (4) and the above bounds give positive valuation of (6).
For \(k=0\), (6) is congruent to one if \(q\mid m\). This proves the result.

The extraction in (6) cannot be transferred to an arbitrary positive
pivot just by changing \(A\). Write \(a=p^b u\), where \(u>1\) is prime
to \(p\), and fix \(k=q\). Choose positive multiples \(m\ge q\) of
\(q\) tending to \(q/u\) \(p\)-adically. The integers \(um-q\) belong
to \((u(m-q),um]\) and have unbounded valuation.
Meanwhile \(v(m!/(m-q)!)\) stays bounded, since \(q/u\) is not an
integer. Apply the factorial scaling formula for \(p^b\) to
\((a(m-q))!\) and \((am)!\). It follows that \(v(U_{m,q})\)
tends to \(-\infty\) for any fixed \(A\), even on the chosen progression.

## 3. Integral tails with arbitrary prime-power axis coefficients

### One axis coefficient and two pivots

Let \(P\) be any polynomial integral in the **ordinary** monomial basis
over \(\mathcal O\), with zero constant coefficient and zero coefficient
of \(x^a\). For \(a=p^b\) and \(\beta\in\mathbb N^{d-1}\), set
\[
 F=cY^\beta\bigl(1+(u/a!)x^a+P(x,Y)\bigr).
 \tag{7}
\]
If
\[
 v(u)>-R_q\qquad\text{or}\qquad v(u)\le -1/(p-1),
 \tag{8}
\]
then \(\Lambda(F^m)\ne0\) for every positive multiple \(m\) of \(q\).
If \(v(u)\) is an integer, (8) covers every possibility, including
\(u=0\) with \(v(0)=+\infty\). In particular for \(p=2,\ q=2\),
the even moments of (7) are nonzero whenever \(u\) is rational
and the other ordinary coefficients inside the parentheses are
2-integral. There is no bound on the exponents in \(P\).

For the first region of (8) use the least monomial \(Y^\beta\).
The divided-power coefficient of \(x^a\) after factoring it out is
\(u\), and all the coefficients contributed by \(P\) are integral.
For the second region use the pivot \(x^aY^\beta\) with coefficient
\(cu/a!\). The ordinary shifted coefficients in \(G\) are integral
because \(v(a!/u)=A-v(u)>0\). Thus \(L=0\). The nonconstant coefficients
of \(p^{-A}H\) are of positive valuation, and its constant coefficient
has valuation \(-v(u)\ge1/(p-1)\), since \(v(a!)=A\).
The two assertions follow from Sections 1 and 2. Over ramified
fields this argument leaves the explicit interval
\(-1/(p-1)<v(u)\le -R_q\). Its width is \(1/(q(p-1))\).
If \(v\) has value group \(e_v^{-1}\mathbb Z\) with \(e_v<q\),
the interval contains no value of \(v\). Indeed the distance of
\(k/e_v\) from \(1/(p-1)\), when nonzero, is at least
\(1/(e_v(p-1))\).

### Several axis coefficients and the residue denominator

Let \(X,Y\) be disjoint coordinate groups and
\[
 F=cY^\beta\left(1+\sum_{i,b}u_{i,b}\frac{X_i^{p^b}}{(p^b)!}
                     +P(X,Y)\right),
 \tag{9}
\]
with \(P\) ordinary integral, constant-free, and disjoint from the
displayed axis monomials. Put \(\lambda=\min_{i,b}v(u_{i,b})\), with
\(\lambda=+\infty\) if the axis sum is empty. If \(\lambda>-R_q\),
Section 1 gives a unit moment on the progression. If
\(\lambda\le-1/(p-1)\), choose \(u_*\) of valuation \(\lambda\).
In each coordinate with a term of valuation \(\lambda\), let
\(\ell_i,h_i\) be the smallest and largest indices \(b\) attaining it.
In the second case there is a nonzero moment in every window of
length \(\sum_i p^{h_i-\ell_i}\). In particular, one exceptional
prime-power coefficient per coordinate gives the support-size window
in both cases when \(q\le s\). This covers every such phase with
\(e_v<q\), irrespective of the number and degree of the exceptional
terms. Several terms in one coordinate are allowed when the displayed
sum is at most \(s\).

To prove the second case, divide the parentheses in (9) by \(u_*\)
and write the result as \(t+K\) with \(t=1/u_*\) and \(K\) constant-free.
Each \(t^k/k!\), \(k>0\), has positive valuation. By (2), all divided
powers of \(K\) are integral and their residues are determined by the
divided-power coefficient residues of \(K\). Consequently
\[
 \frac{\Lambda(F^m)}{(cu_*)^m m!(m\beta)!}
 \equiv L_{(0,m\beta)}(K^m/m!)\pmod{\mathfrak m}.
\]
Only the retained \(X_i\) columns occur in the residue, so
\(L_{(0,m\beta)}\) acts on them as the ordinary factorial functional.
The singleton part of
[the factorial series formula (4)](FACTORIAL_MOMENT_SERIES_PRIME_POWER_RATIONALITY.md#degree-bounds-in-characteristic-p)
has one factor for each retained coordinate. With
\(A_{i,t}=1-\sum_{b\le t}(\overline{u_{i,b}/u_*}\,T)^{p^{t-b}}\)
over the retained terms, that factor is
\[
       \frac{\prod_{t=\ell_i}^{h_i-1} A_{i,t}^{p-1}}{A_{i,h_i}} .
\]
In formula (4), \(A_{i,h_i+1}=A_{i,h_i}^p\) cancels the last power
in the numerator. The denominator degree is \(p^{h_i-\ell_i}\)
and the numerator degree is one less. This computation uses only
base-\(p\) digits over the residue field, and our estimate for the
discarded constant also holds when \(v(t)=1/(p-1)\). The core
case of a sum of independent prime powers is treated by
[Rocks, Theorem 3.10](https://people.math.sc.edu/rocks/BradyRocksSFC2015.pdf).

The product is strictly proper with constant coefficient one.
If the denominator has degree \(D\), then \(D\) consecutive
zeros at a positive start force the preceding coefficient to
vanish by the denominator recurrence and its nonzero leading
coefficient. Iterating reaches the nonzero constant coefficient.
One may use the degree of the reduced denominator if cancellation
lowers it. In particular, if (9) has one axis coefficient per coordinate,
the integral tail is available at a place over \(p\), and \(q\le s\),
a hypothetical full zero window forces ramification index at least \(q\)
at that place. It also forces \(-1/(p-1)<\lambda\le-R_q\) there.

## 4. A necessary denominator bound for counterexamples

For a support with a least monomial over a number field, a full zero
window forces the following condition at **each** \(q=p^e\le s\)
and **each** place \(\mathfrak p\) above \(p\). Some \(u_\nu\ne0\)
has \(v_{\mathfrak p}(u_\nu)\le-R_q\). Otherwise the window contains
a positive multiple of \(q\) and (1) excludes it at that place.

For rational coefficients the common reduced denominator of the
\(u_\nu\) is divisible by every prime \(p\le s\).
For number-field coefficients the common denominator ideal
\[
 \mathfrak d=\prod_{\mathfrak p}
  \mathfrak p^{\max\{0,\max_\nu(-\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}u_\nu)\}}
\]
has exponent at least \(\lceil e_{\mathfrak p}R_q\rceil\) above \(p\),
where \(e_{\mathfrak p}\) is the ramification index. The largest power
of \(p\) at most \(s\) gives the strongest bound for that prime.
In particular the result covers every ordinary integer phase with a
least monomial of coefficient \(1\) or \(-1\).

The fixed-support, fixed-window equations have integer coefficients.
If a solution with the specified nonzero support coordinates exists
over any characteristic-zero field, the normalization at the pivot
and the equations adjoining inverses for the other support coordinates
give a nonzero finitely generated \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra. A maximal ideal
gives a solution over a number field. Thus this denominator bound applies
to the search for an algebraic counterexample even if the starting
field was arbitrary.

These criteria do not supply a good place for every coefficient vector.
Some supports have neither a least monomial nor the pivot in (5).
Both issues remain for full SFC(2). The assertion of infinitely many
nonzero moments in
[Long, Theorem 1.1](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00813v1) under
the nonflat hypothesis gives no support-size bound. The present argument
is a direct local finite-window certificate. No formal verification,
independent replay, external review or priority is claimed.
