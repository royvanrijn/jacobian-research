# Freezing algebraic arcs in factorial collision loci

Fix a finite ordinary two-monomial core and \(k\) additional
row types with smaller exponents \(a_i+s_iT\), nonnegative
integer \(a_i,s_i\), fixed positive gaps \(d_i\), and fixed
orientations. The multiplicities \(r_i\) are arbitrary
nonnegative integers. Require at least one row with
\(s_i>0\) to occur. Equal rows can be omitted. Call \(T\)
exceptional if some admissible multiplicities give a common
root of ordinary consecutive moment polynomials at some order.

**Theorem.** There are constants \(C,\kappa\) such that at
most \(C(\log(2+H))^\kappa\) integers \(0\le T\le H\) are
exceptional. No balance, spacing, positivity of limiting
curvature, or divisor-rank condition on the moving types
is required. The bound holds uniformly over all
multiplicities and all orders.

More precisely, fix the number of types \(k\) and an upper
bound \(D\) on their gaps. With
\[
       M_H=1+\max_i(a_i+s_i H+d_i),
\]
there is an effective estimate \(C_{k,D}(\log(2+H+M_H))^{\kappa_{k,D}}\),
uniform over the core, the affine forms and the orientations.
Here the degree cutoff used in the estimate is absolute and
computable. Neither the cutoff nor the point-counting
constants are numerically evaluated.

The argument first shows that a semialgebraic arc along
which the height varies would force an identity at fixed
integer row multiplicities. Ax's differential theorem gives
the requisite algebraic relations among the exponential
coefficients, even when some of their logarithms are
dependent. The fixed-multiplicity affine-ray theorem rules
out that identity. A point-counting theorem then bounds the
number of heights supporting integer collisions.

## Freezing the multiplicities on an algebraic arc

Let \(T\) vary on a real open interval and let
\(r=(r_1,\ldots,r_k)\) there be real analytic and algebraic
over \(\mathbb C(T)\). For positive real rational functions
\(R_{ji}(T)\), \(1\le j\le m,\ 1\le i\le k\), set
\[
       G_j(T)=\sum_i r_i(T)\log R_{ji}(T),\qquad
       Z_j(T)=\exp G_j(T).
\]
Suppose \(Z\) satisfies Laurent polynomial equations over
\(\overline{\mathbb C(T)}\), with the local branches of their
coefficients fixed. Suppose the arc contains or limits to
a point with \(T=T_0\), \(r=r^0\), all \(R_{ji}\) positive
and finite there. Then the frozen functions
\[
                   Z_j^0(T)=\exp\!\left(
                            \sum_i r_i^0\log R_{ji}(T)\right)
\]
satisfy the same equations as germs. In applications below
the Laurent polynomial equation has constant coefficients.

To prove this, choose from the real logarithms a basis
\(L_1,\ldots,L_q\) of their rational span modulo complex
constants. Each \(e^{L_\nu}\) is algebraic over
\(\mathbb C(T)\). Consequently Ax's power-series theorem
(SP) makes the \(L_\nu\) algebraically independent over
\(\overline{\mathbb C(T)}\). In fact applied to this independent
list modulo constants, it gives transcendence degree at least
\(q+1\) for \((L,e^L)\) over \(\mathbb C\), whereas \(T\)
and \(q\) logarithms give the matching upper bound. Adjoining
any algebraic extensions of \(\mathbb C(T)\) leaves their
independence unchanged.
[Ax, On Schanuel's conjectures, (SP)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1971/93-2/p03)

In terms of this basis write
\[
                   G_j=\sum_{\nu=1}^q A_{j\nu}(r)L_\nu+D_j(r),
\]
where each \(A_{j\nu}\) is a rational linear form and each
\(D_j\) is a complex linear form. The \(D_j\) retain the
constant terms in the original logarithms. A relation
\(b\cdot G=\sum_\nu c_\nu L_\nu+d\), with \(b\in\mathbb Q^m\),
\(c\in\mathbb Q^q\), \(d\in\mathbb C\), holds precisely when
\[
              b^T A(r)=c,\qquad b\cdot D(r)=d.
\]
This is an equality of algebraic functions by the independence
of the \(L_\nu\). In particular it continues to hold with
\(r^0\) substituted for \(r\).

Let \(\Gamma\subset\mathbb Z^m\) consist of the integer \(b\)
in such relations. This is a saturated subgroup: a nonzero
integer multiple of \(b\) has the stated property only if \(b\)
does. Extend a lattice basis of \(\Gamma\) to one of
\(\mathbb Z^m\), and let \(t\) be its rank. In the new monomial
coordinates the first \(t\) entries of \(Z\) are algebraic over
\(\mathbb C(T)\), since they have the form \(e^d\prod_\nu
(e^{L_\nu})^{c_\nu}\). Their values as functions of \(T\)
are identical for \(Z^0\). The remaining transformed
logarithms of \(Z\), together with the \(L_\nu\), are
independent over \(\mathbb Q\) modulo constants by the
definition of \(\Gamma\). Ax gives a transcendence lower
bound of \(q+m-t+1\) if this list is nonempty. The
logarithms of \(Z\) and the exponentials of \(L\) lie
in the field generated by \(\overline{\mathbb C(T)}\)
and the \(L_\nu\). It follows that the \(m-t\) remaining
transformed coordinates of \(Z\) are algebraically independent
over \(\overline{\mathbb C(T)}(L)\).

Thus over \(\overline{\mathbb C(T)}\) the Zariski closure of
\(Z\) is exactly the torus coset specified by its first \(t\)
transformed coordinates. If \(t=m\) the assertion holds
directly. The equality of the fixed coordinates puts \(Z^0\)
in that coset, proving the claim. The algebraicity of the
functions \(r_i(T)\) was essential only on the arc. They may
have arbitrary real values at its limiting point.

## The real ordinary collision set

Fix orders \(n,N=n+1\). At order \(j=n,N\) strip each
moving row's scalar factorial \((j(a_i+s_iT))!\). Its remaining
factor at index \(h\) is the positive polynomial
\[
       P_{j,h,i}(T)=
       \prod_{v=1}^{d_i h_i}(j(a_i+s_i T)+v),
       \qquad
       h_i=\begin{cases}h&\text{increasing row},\
                        j-h&\text{decreasing row}.
                        \end{cases}
\]
The normalized order-\(j\) coefficients are consequently
\[
             C_{j,h}\prod_i P_{j,h,i}(T)^{r_i},
\]
with \(C_{j,h}\) the positive ordinary core coefficient,
including the binomial factor. This equality needs no
rescaling of the argument and hence preserves physical
common roots. For all real \(T,r_i\ge0\) the same expression
gives positive coefficients by using real logarithms.
Let \(\mathcal X\) in these real coordinates be the zero
set of the resultant of the displayed pair. The resultant
is a polynomial with constant coefficients in the
coefficient multipliers.

An ordinary point \((T_0,r^0)\in\mathcal X\) with a moving
row present cannot be in the germ of a regular semialgebraic
set contained in \(\mathcal X\) on which \(T\) is locally
nonconstant. Indeed semialgebraic curve selection and
monotonicity give a semialgebraic arc in that germ limiting
to the point, with \(T\) the parameter on a one-sided open
interval. After shrinking, its \(r_i(T)\) are real analytic
algebraic functions. The freezing result gives resultant zero
identically for the fixed integers \(r_i^0\). The factorial-
stripped coefficients for these counts are polynomials in
\(T\), so this identity holds on the entire affine ray.
That contradicts the
[all-directions affine-ray theorem](MULTIVARIATE_AFFINE_BASE_RAY_ALL_DIRECTIONS.md).
The moving-row hypothesis is needed for this application,
as a fixed colliding core with no moving row would give a
vertical family of coefficients and every height would count.

## Uniform point counting and all degrees

Let \(J\) be the
[effective absolute eventual angular cutoff](MULTIVARIATE_EFFECTIVE_ABSOLUTE_EVENTUAL_ANGLES.md).
Only \(N<J\) need be counted. The
[absolute curvature twelve theorem](MULTIVARIATE_ABSOLUTE_CURVATURE_12.md)
forces each row type at a collision to satisfy
\[
                  r_i\,\frac{d_i^2}{a_i+s_i T+d_i}<12 .
\]
For \(0\le T\le H\) all ordinary collisions therefore lie in
the box \(0\le r_i\le 12M_H\).

For each such box the collision set is restricted
sub-Pfaffian of format and degree bounded solely in
\(k,D,J\). To see this without normalizing by a height-
dependent analytic expression, take auxiliary bounded
coordinates \(x_{j,h,i}\) with
\[
              \exp x_{j,h,i}=P_{j,h,i}(T).
\]
For \(0\le T\le H\) the right side lies between one and an
explicit positive upper bound, so all the \(x_{j,h,i}\)
range in a finite box. The coefficient multipliers in the
resultant equation are \(\exp(\sum_i r_i x_{j,h,i})\).
On these boxes the exponential of each fixed-degree
polynomial \(f\) is a Pfaffian chain entry, since its
derivatives are \(\exp(f)\partial f\). Affine box rescaling
to a unit cube changes the coefficients of the chain
equations but preserves the number and degrees of those
equations. Projecting out the \(x\) and returning to the
original rational coordinates use a bounded number of
projections and linear equations. Hence neither the
height bound nor the sizes of the affine forms and
core coefficients enter the format or degree.

Theorem 2 of
[Binyamini, Novikov and Zak, Wilkie's conjecture for Pfaffian structures](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2024.199.2.5)
covers the rational points of height at most
\(\max(2,H,12M_H)\) in this set by at most
\(C_{k,D}(\log(2+H+M_H))^{\kappa_{k,D}}\)
connected regular blocks. Each block is contained in a
regular semialgebraic manifold of the same dimension. The
block is locally open there by equality of the dimensions.
Let \(S\) denote the semialgebraic closure of a block. At
each ordinary point in the block, \(T\) must be locally
constant on \(S\), since the block is locally open in \(S\)
and the preceding arc argument applies. Consider the
semialgebraic set
\[
  S_0=\{p\in S:\ \exists\epsilon>0\ \forall q\in S,\
                 \|q-p\|<\epsilon\ \Longrightarrow\ T(q)=T(p)\}.
\]
Every ordinary point of the block lies in \(S_0\), and
\(T\) is constant on each of its connected components.
The format and degree bounds for the closures in Theorem 2,
and the fixed-depth formula and component bounds in equation
(13) and Proposition 6 of the same paper, give a polynomial
in \(\log(2+H+M_H)\) bound on the number of these components.
Multiplying by the block count preserves a polylogarithmic
estimate. This step allows closures with regions on which
\(T\) is locally constant and others on which it varies.

Taking a finite union over \(N<J\) proves the claimed
all-degree count. The localization applies to ordinary
integer witnesses. It leaves open whether there can be
infinitely many exceptional heights in a given template.

## Independent height coordinates

Let \(T=(T_1,\ldots,T_b)\). Allow the smaller height of type \(i\)
to be
\[
             a_i+\sum_{\ell=1}^b s_{i\ell}T_\ell ,
          \qquad a_i,s_{i\ell}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},
\]
with fixed gap \(d_i\) and fixed orientation. Take nonempty finite
sets \(E_\ell\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) with maximum at most \(H\).
For each allowed multiplicity vector, require a present row with
\(s_{i\ell}>0\) in every direction \(\ell\) used for slicing below.
This holds in particular when all multiplicities are positive and
each slope column is nonzero. Put
\[
       L=1+\max_i\left(a_i+d_i+H\sum_{\ell=1}^b s_{i\ell}\right).
\]
Say a grid point is exceptional if some admissible integer
multiplicities support a physical consecutive collision.

**Corollary.** For any such slicing direction \(\ell\), the number
of exceptional grid points in \(\prod_{j=1}^b E_j\) is at most
\[
            C_{k,D} \bigl(\log(2+H+L)\bigr)^{\kappa_{k,D}}
                             \prod_{j\ne\ell}|E_j| .           \tag{1}
\]
The constants do not depend on the fixed core, slopes, intercepts,
or the number of parameter coordinates. The count is simultaneous
over all adjacent orders and all multiplicities.

Fix the other coordinates in the grid. The remaining ray has
intercepts \(a_i+\sum_{j\ne\ell}s_{ij}T_j\), slopes \(s_{i\ell}\),
and at least one moving row for each admissible witness.
Every lower and upper endpoint on \(0\le T_\ell\le H\)
is bounded by \(L\). The format-uniform one-parameter bound
therefore applies with the same constants on every slice.
There are exactly \(\prod_{j\ne\ell}|E_j|\) slices in the
chosen direction, proving (1).

For instance, take \(b=k\) and the smaller heights \(T_i\)
independently in \(\{0,\ldots,H\}\), with every type present.
For any fixed choices of gaps and orientations, the number of
exceptional height tuples is \(O_{k,D}(H^{k-1}(\log(2+H))^\kappa)\),
uniformly over the core. A fraction \(1-O_{k,D}((\log(2+H))^\kappa/H)\)
of the tuples gives all-order coprimality for *every* choice of
positive integer multiplicities. Gaps in \(\{1,\ldots,D\}\) and
both orientations can even be allowed to vary with the tuple:
take the union of the at most \((2D)^k\) exceptional sets.
Unlike a one-scale conclusion, this permits every height to
vary independently. The estimate allows sparse exceptional
tuples and its format bound depends on the number of types and
the gap bound.
