# A common monomial power times a linear form satisfies Strong Factorial

Internal research consequence, 21 November 2026. Inspected retained base:
`19eb9912fb60142f8b620dac24896931d4ff3e46`.

## Result, dependencies, and assurance

For every integer `q>=1`, every number of variables, and every characteristic-zero
coefficient field, the family

\[
 f(Y_1,\ldots,Y_r)=(Y_1\cdots Y_r)^q\sum_{j=1}^{r}c_jY_j
\tag{1}
\]

satisfies Strong Factorial at **every starting index**, with window length equal
to the number of its nonzero terms. Thus this is an all-order family with
arbitrarily many monomials, not only an ordinary binomial result. The support
restriction in (1) is essential to the proof. No unrestricted SFC theorem is
claimed.

The indispensable input is the retained, unconditional written proof of full
polynomial inverse rigidity, specifically `cor:rigidity` in
[RIGIDITY_CRITICAL_VALUES.tex](RIGIDITY_CRITICAL_VALUES.tex), lines 110–122.
It is proved there, rather than assumed as the Rigidity Conjecture. Its dependency
chain is `lem:basins` (29–56), `lem:contact` (58–91), and `thm:composition`
(93–108). The argument is credited to **liqsweep**, *Polynomial composition
rigidity via critical values*, September 2026, Sections 2–5 of the
[pinned primary source](https://github.com/blueberryvertigo/polynomial-composition-rigidity/blob/051c74aad9ef9bf8804c371964803aee099c5b63/rigidity.typ).
The repository retains those source bytes as
[sources/rigidity.typ](sources/rigidity.typ).

Both the retained proof and its application below received internal written
review. Exact checks independently reconstruct the moment/inverse coefficient
identity and its index conversion. They do not replace the analytic rigidity
proof, establish literature priority, or constitute external peer review or
formal verification. The global registry and generated status pages are unchanged.

## 1. The precise inverse-rigidity input

We use the following statement over characteristic zero:

**Polynomial inverse rigidity.** If `a(z)=z+O(z^2)` is a polynomial of degree at
most `d+1`, and `a^{-1}` has `d` consecutive zero coefficients starting at any
degree at least two, then `a(z)=z`.

For completeness, the retained proof has the following structure. Every full
basin of an attracting fixed point of a polynomial contains a critical point:
otherwise inverse branches on one fixed disk stay in a bounded escape disk while
their derivatives grow without bound, contradicting Cauchy's estimate. Distinct
basins therefore contain distinct critical values. If
`F(z)=z+c z^(k+1)+O(z^(k+2))`, multiplication by `1+delta^k` splits the fixed point
into `k` attracting fixed points for small positive `delta`. Their multipliers
are `1-k delta^k+O(delta^(k+1))`. Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_0(F-z)-1\leq\#\operatorname{CV}(F).
\]

The chain rule gives
`CV(a composed with b) subset CV(a) union a(CV(b))`, hence

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_0(a\circ b-z)\leq\deg a+\deg b-1
 \quad\text{unless }a\circ b=z.
\tag{2}
\]

Truncating `a^{-1}` immediately before a putative zero block contradicts (2);
if the composition is the identity, multiplicativity of degree forces both
polynomials to be linear. These are ordinary polynomial and complex-analytic
arguments. No factorial conjecture, positivity of the factorial functional,
eventual binomial theorem, or Riemann-hypothesis claim enters that proof.

The characteristic-zero extension needs only an embedding into `C` of the
finitely generated field generated by the finitely many coefficients of a
putative counterexample. It does not require an embedding of an arbitrary
entire coefficient field into `C`.

## 2. Exact factorial/inverse coefficient identity

Let `r>=1`, `q>=1`, and let the coefficients `c_j` belong to a characteristic-zero
field `K`. Write

\[
 a(z)=z\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-c_jz^q),\qquad b(z)=a^{-1}(z),\qquad
 \mathcal L(Y^\alpha)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\alpha_j!.
\tag{3}
\]

The inverse exists formally because `a(0)=0` and `a'(0)=1`. For every integer
`m>=0`, direct expansion of (1) gives

\[
 \mathcal L(f^m)
 =m!\sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_r=m}
      \prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(qm+k_j)!}{k_j!}\,c_j^{k_j}.
\tag{4}
\]

Lagrange inversion gives an independent expression:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 [z^{qm+1}]b(z)
 &=\frac1{qm+1}[u^{qm}]\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-c_ju^q)^{-qm-1}\\
 &=\frac1{qm+1}
   \sum_{k_1+\cdots+k_r=m}
      \prod_{j=1}^{r}\binom{qm+k_j}{k_j}c_j^{k_j}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal L(f^m)
 =m!\bigl((qm)!\bigr)^r(qm+1)[z^{qm+1}]b(z).}
\tag{6}
\]

Every scalar factor in (6) is nonzero. When `m=0`, both sides equal one,
including when all coefficients vanish.

More generally, Lagrange inversion at degree `ell` gives
`[z^ell]b=(1/ell)[u^(ell-1)] product_j(1-c_j u^q)^(-ell)`.
The product on the right involves only powers of `u^q`. Therefore

\[
 [z^\ell]b=0\qquad\text{whenever }\ell\not\equiv1\pmod q.
\tag{7}
\]

This sparsity is an exact formal identity. It does not assert that every
coefficient with degree congruent to one is nonzero.

## 3. The all-order support-size theorem

**Theorem.** Let `K` be any characteristic-zero field, let `r,q>=1` be integers,
and let `f` be as in (1). Suppose `f!=0`, and put
`s=#{j:c_j!=0}`. For every integer `n>=1`,

\[
 \bigl(\mathcal L(f^n),\mathcal L(f^{n+1}),\ldots,
          \mathcal L(f^{n+s-1})\bigr)\ne(0,\ldots,0).
\tag{8}
\]

**Proof.** The `s` active terms in (1) have distinct exponent vectors
`q(1,...,1)+e_j`, so `s` is the actual number of nonzero monomials. The active
factors in (3) give `deg a=qs+1`; their leading coefficient is a product of
nonzero field elements and cannot cancel.

Suppose all moments in (8) vanish. Formula (6) kills the inverse coefficients
of degrees

\[
 qn+1,\ q(n+1)+1,\ \ldots,\ q(n+s-1)+1.
\]

Together with (7), these are **every** inverse coefficient in the consecutive
interval

\[
 q(n-1)+2\ \leq\ \ell\ \leq\ q(n+s).
\tag{9}
\]

Its length is

\[
 q(n+s)-\bigl(q(n-1)+2\bigr)+1
   =q(s+1)-1\ \geq\ qs=\deg a-1.
\tag{10}
\]

The first degree in (9) is at least two, even when `n=1`. Apply polynomial
inverse rigidity to any `qs` consecutive coefficients in this interval.
It forces `a=z`, contradicting `deg a=qs+1>=2`. This proves (8) over `C`.
For arbitrary `K`, the field generated over `Q` by the finitely many `c_j`
embeds into `C`; that embedding preserves the polynomial degrees, all moment
equalities, and the nonzero coefficients. The same contradiction applies. QED.

Inactive coefficients require no extra assumption. Equivalently one can remove
the inactive variables and use

\[
 \mathcal L(f^m)=\bigl((qm)!\bigr)^{r-s}
                 \mathcal L(f_{\mathrm{active}}^m).
\tag{11}
\]

The all-zero coefficient vector gives `f=0` and is excluded only from the
nonzero conclusion, not from identities (4)–(7). A single active coefficient
gives a monomial, as the theorem correctly predicts. The separate case `q=0`
is an ordinary linear form and is already covered by the classical complete
homogeneous recurrence; formulas (5)–(10) are asserted only for `q>=1`.

### Sharpness of the support-size window

For `s>=2`, take `r=s` and `c_j=zeta^(j-1)` for a primitive `s`th root of unity.
Then

\[
 a(z)=z(1-z^{qs}),\qquad
 [z^{qm+1}]b(z)=0\quad(1\leq m<s),\qquad
 [z^{qs+1}]b(z)=1.
\]

Hence the first `s-1` positive factorial moments vanish, whereas

\[
 \mathcal L(f^s)=s!\bigl((qs)!\bigr)^s(qs+1)\ne0.
\tag{12}
\]

Thus the universal length `s` in this family cannot be shortened. This example
is compatible with SFC and demonstrates the needed cancellation over complex
coefficients.

## 4. Genuine transfer consequences and existing overlap

The case `q=1` is exactly the family in Edo–van den Essen,
[arXiv:1304.3956v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.3956v2), Definition 2.24,
Theorem 2.25, and Lemmas 2.26–2.27; it is already transferred from full rigidity
in the retained note. The identity (6) and the sparse inverse-window count
extend that written bridge to every integer `q>=1`. We did not locate this
extension in the inspected sources; no claim of literature priority follows.
It is a consequence of the credited full rigidity argument, not a new proof
of that argument. In particular it is not obtained by applying a general
moment-preserving substitution `Y_j -> Y_j^q`, which would be invalid.

Two already valid constructions enlarge the displayed family. Let `U_1,...,U_t`
and `Z_1,...,Z_v` be variables independent of all `Y_j`, let the `lambda_i` be
nonzero, and let `M(Z)` be a monomial. Then

\[
 M(Z)\left((Y_1\cdots Y_r)^q\sum_jc_jY_j+
                 \sum_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_iU_i\right)
\tag{13}
\]

satisfies the window of length `s+t` at every positive starting index. To see
the addition step, put `f_m=L(f^m)/m!` and `g_m=L(g^m)/m!`. Independence gives

\[
 \sum_{m\geq0}g_mz^m
 =\left(\sum_{m\geq0}f_mz^m\right)
        \prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-\lambda_i z)^{-1}.
\]

Multiplying by the degree-`t` product shows that a zero block of `s+t` values
of `g_m`, starting at `n>=1`, forces the `s` values of `f_m` at
`n+t,...,n+t+s-1` to vanish, a contradiction. The independent monomial factor
multiplies the `m`th moment by a nonzero product of factorials. These mechanisms
are classical: Rocks, 2015 author preprint, Theorem 3.12 and Corollary 3.13
(printed pages 12–13), retained as
[sources/rocks-2015-author-preprint.pdf](sources/rocks-2015-author-preprint.pdf).
They are also reproduced in
[EVENTUAL_BINOMIAL_ALGEBRAIC_CONSEQUENCES.md](EVENTUAL_BINOMIAL_ALGEBRAIC_CONSEQUENCES.md),
Section 3. The new input here supplies their all-order family (1).

## 5. What this leaves open for unrestricted SFC

The new proof uses the special support vectors `q(1,...,1)+e_j`. An arbitrary
integer exponent matrix does not have this form. Unequal common exponents,
replacing the linear terms by arbitrary powers, dependent additions, and
identifying variables are not justified by (6). In particular no claim for
`product_j Y_j^(q_j) * sum c_j Y_j` with arbitrary unequal `q_j` follows here.

The universal marked Vandermonde and factorial Gram constructions reconstruct
coefficients after sufficiently many **marked** moments are known to vanish.
They do not turn a scalar support-size window into those marked zeros. For a
fixed support `A` of size `s`, put

\[
 I_{A,n}=\bigl(M_n(c),\ldots,M_{n+s-1}(c)\bigr)
       \subset\mathbb Q[c_1,\ldots,c_s],\qquad
 M_m(c)=\mathcal L\!\left(\left(\sum_jc_jX^{a_j}\right)^m\right).
\]

The missing full-SFC assertion is exactly `sqrt(I_(A,n))=(c_1,...,c_s)` for
every distinct integer support and every `n>=1`. An auxiliary proof must derive
that assertion from new structure rather than assume the required marked zeros.
Already in degree `n`, the moment ideal has the one-dimensional part `Q M_n`,
while the different coefficients of the formal power span a space indexed by
the sumset `nA`; their coefficient-variable monomial supports are disjoint.
For `s>=2` that space has dimension at least two. Thus all same-power marked
moments cannot belong directly to `I_(A,n)`, even for families where SFC is true.

The retained
[UNRESTRICTED_SFC_MECHANISMS.tex](UNRESTRICTED_SFC_MECHANISMS.tex), lines 1114–1197,
states a sufficient discrete route to all supports: prove preservation under
adjoining a whole unit row to a nonconstant column, and under merging two
integer rows while keeping columns distinct. Neither operation is supplied by
this transfer. General Gram positivity, formal shift identities, and finite
ordinary-binomial bad-set finiteness do not fill that gap.
