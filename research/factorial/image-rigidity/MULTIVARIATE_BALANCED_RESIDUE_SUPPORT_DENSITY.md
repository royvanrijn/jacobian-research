# Density-one heights for the balanced factorial residue test

Fix the core \(C\), the gap \(d\ge2\), and the four balanced
row types from the
[linked-residue theorem](MULTIVARIATE_BALANCED_WIDTH_TWO_LINKED_RESIDUES.md).
The four types occur with the same multiplicity \(r\) and have
smaller exponents \(M\) and \(M+d\). Write \(g=2d-1\). For
\(q=1,d+1\), let \(C_q,T_q\) be the fixed nonzero rationals
in equation (3) of that theorem, computed in the original
orientation at \(q=1\) and in the reversed orientation at
\(q=d+1\). Set
\[
             A_q=T_q^g,\qquad B_q=C_q^{-1},\qquad
             \mathcal E_q=\{t\in\mathbb Z:A_q^t=B_q\}.
\]

**Theorem.** If \(\mathcal E_1\cap\mathcal E_{d+1}\) is empty,
there is a set of heights \(M\) of natural density one on which
the original ordinary binomial satisfies SFC(2) in every degree
for every positive multiplicity \(r\), over every
characteristic-zero field. For every rational
\(\varepsilon>0\), there is a computable finite collection of
arithmetic progressions with total union density at least
\(1-\varepsilon\) on which this holds above a computable
height.

If the intersection is nonempty, there is instead an explicit
positive-density set of heights on which infinitely many
positive multiples \(r\) of \(g\) satisfy all the integer-mark
conditions (2), (5), and (6) from the linked-residue theorem,
with (5) and (6) considered at their prescribed depths.
These are conditions on the two linked residues. Coprimality
on this exceptional class requires additional information.

Both alternatives are decidable from the signed prime
factorizations of the four rationals. For example, a
non-torsion \(A_q\in\mathbb Q^\times\) determines at most
one candidate exponent from a nonzero prime valuation, and
the candidate can be checked by rational equality. For
\(A_q=1\) or \(-1\) the check is direct.

## A prime support lemma

For \(A,B\in\mathbb Q^\times\) with \(B\notin\langle A\rangle\),
the primes \(p\) at which \(A,B\) are units and
\(B\bmod p\notin\langle A\bmod p\rangle\) contain a positive
natural density set. To prove this, use
[Schinzel, Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-27-1-397-420)
with \(K=\mathbb Q\) and one multiplicative generator. Its
contrapositive supplies a prime \(p_0\) with the asserted
failure outside any fixed finite set of primes. Let
\(s=(p_0-1)/\operatorname{ord}_{p_0}(A)\). In
\(\mathbb F_{p_0}^\times\), the subgroup generated by \(A\)
is precisely the group of \(s\)-th powers. Thus \(X^s-A\)
has a root in \(\mathbb F_{p_0}\), and \(X^s-B\) has none.

As \(p_0\nmid s\) and both constants are \(p_0\)-units, the
two reduced polynomials are separable. Hensel lifting in an
unramified local extension splitting both reductions shows that
the joint splitting field is unramified at \(p_0\).
On the two root sets its Frobenius class fixes a root in the
first set and fixes none in the second. By the Frobenius
fixed-point criterion and the Chebotarev density theorem,
every prime with this class outside the finite bad set
preserves the two root-solvability statements, and these
primes have positive density. This is the same Frobenius
solvability argument used in the
[proof of Schinzel's Theorem 1, p. 413](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa27/aa27128.pdf).
At any such prime \(A\bmod p\) is an \(s\)-th power, so
all its integral powers are also \(s\)-th powers.
The lack of an \(s\)-th root of \(B\bmod p\) excludes
membership as claimed.

In particular, for any non-torsion rational \(A\) and any
prime \(\ell\), apply the lemma to \(A^\ell,A\).
The exact membership \(A\in\langle A^\ell\rangle\)
would force \(A^{\ell t-1}=1\), impossible.
The failure modulo \(p\) is equivalent to
\(\ell\mid\operatorname{ord}_p(A)\), by cyclicity of the
finite subgroup generated by \(A\). Hence the primes with
that divisibility also contain a positive-density set.
The elementary modular tests in both applications of the
lemma are enumerable.

## Applying the lemma to the two shifts

The [linked-residue theorem](MULTIVARIATE_BALANCED_WIDTH_TWO_LINKED_RESIDUES.md)
already excludes all consecutive common roots at sufficiently
large \(M\) if \(g\nmid r\). For \(r=gt\), \(t>0\), assume
there is a common root. Outside a fixed finite bad set
\(\mathcal S\), equations (5)–(6) there imply
\[
                    A_q^t\equiv B_q\pmod p
               \qquad\text{for each }p\mid M+q.              \tag{1}
\]
All height thresholds and bad primes here depend only on the
core and \(d\).

If either \(\mathcal E_q\) is empty, the prime support lemma
gives a positive-density prime set \(\mathcal P_q\), outside
\(\mathcal S\), where no exponent \(t\) can satisfy (1).
A single such prime dividing \(M+q\) suffices.

Suppose both \(\mathcal E_q\) are nonempty but disjoint.
For a non-torsion \(A_q\), the set is a singleton; for
\(A_q=-1\) it is one parity class; for \(A_q=1\) it is
all integers and cannot participate in this disjoint case.
There is a prime \(\ell\) for which the two exponent sets
have disjoint images modulo \(\ell\). For two different
singletons choose a prime not dividing their difference.
If a parity class occurs take \(\ell=2\).
For each non-torsion \(A_q\) choose the positive-density
set \(\mathcal P_q\) with \(\ell\mid\operatorname{ord}_p(A_q)\)
from the lemma. For \(A_q=-1\), all odd good primes have
order two. If \(k_q\in\mathcal E_q\), a prime
\(p\in\mathcal P_q\) dividing \(M+q\) forces
\(A_q^{t-k_q}\equiv1\pmod p\) by (1), and therefore
\(t\equiv k_q\pmod\ell\). At heights with a prime hit for
each \(q\) these two constraints are inconsistent.
The required exclusion is uniform over \(t\).

For either type of prime set used here the reciprocal prime
sum diverges, by partial summation of the positive-density
Chebotarev set contained in it. Given a finite subset
\(\mathcal P_q'\), the density of heights with no
\(p\in\mathcal P_q'\) dividing \(M+q\) is
\(\prod_{p\in\mathcal P_q'}(1-1/p)\), by the Chinese
remainder theorem. This product tends to zero as the
subsets exhaust the prime set. In the two-hit case a union
bound on the two missing-hit sets suffices. This proves
density one and gives the effective finite progression
collection by searching until the product, or the sum of
the two products, is at most \(\varepsilon\).
Removing the finitely many heights below the earlier
threshold does not affect the density.

For example, for empty core one has \(C_q=(2d+1)/2\)
by its binomial coefficients. For every prime \(\ell\mid g\),
\(B_q\) has \(2\)-adic valuation one, whereas every rational
power \(A_q^t\) with integer exponent \(t\) has \(2\)-adic
valuation divisible by \(\ell\).
Thus the criterion applies for every \(d\ge2\) in this case.

## Exact compatibility when there is a joint exponent

Suppose \(t_0\in\mathcal E_1\cap\mathcal E_{d+1}\).
Use the integers \(K_p\) from the linked-residue theorem;
its bad set consists of the \(p\) with \(K_p>0\).
Restrict \(M\) by requiring
\[
                 v_p(M+q)\le K_p
                         \qquad(p\in\mathcal S,\ q=1,d+1).
\]
For each bad prime this excludes at most two residues
modulo \(p^{K_p+1}\), a modulus of size greater than two.
The allowed heights form a nonempty finite union of residue
classes of positive density. For any such height, let \(L_M\)
be a common multiple of the orders of \(A_q\) modulo
\(p^{v_p(M+q)}\), over \(q=1,d+1\) and the good primes of
these shifts. The orders are defined because the constants
are units at good primes. Take any sufficiently large
positive \(t\equiv t_0\pmod{L_M}\), and set \(r=gt\).
Then
\[
                A_q^t\equiv B_q\pmod{p^{v_p(M+q)}}
\]
at every good prime of each shift. At these primes the
cleaned ordinate corrections in (5) vanish, and the
divisibility \(g\mid r v_p(M+q)\) holds. At bad primes the
bounds used to derive (5)–(6) require depth above \(K_p\),
so those implications impose no condition on these heights.
Finally, the valuation of each \(M+q\) at bad primes
is absorbed in the fixed \(K_0\) of (2).
This proves compatibility of the specified linked tests for
infinitely many \(r\) at each allowed height.

A concrete compatible core is \((0,1),(1,0)\).
At either orientation and in order \(j\) its moment
coefficients are all \(j!\), since
\(\binom jk k!(j-k)!=j!\).
It gives \(C_q=1\) at both marks, and \(t_0=0\) is a joint
exponent. This example is itself regular by the
[both-private theorem](MULTIVARIATE_BOTH_PRIVATE_ALL_DEGREES.md).
The compatibility conclusion concerns only the integer marks.
