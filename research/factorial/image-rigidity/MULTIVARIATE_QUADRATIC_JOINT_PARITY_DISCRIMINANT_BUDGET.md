# Joint prime budgets for a primitive quadratic common factor

Consider adjacent ordinary factorial moments \(F_n,F_N\) with
\(N=n+1\) and \(n\ge2\). Work in any common rational argument
variable. Suppose an irreducible quadratic factor, written
primitively in that variable, is
\[
                  H(z)=A z^2+Bz+C,\qquad A,C>0,\quad B\ne0.
\]
The even parent is strictly positive on the real line, so
\(\Delta=B^2-4AC<0\). Write the reduced angle invariant and
its complement as
\[
        \xi=\frac{B^2}{AC}=\frac{U}{D},\qquad
        V=4D-U>0,\qquad \gcd(U,D)=1.                           \tag{1}
\]
This is \(4\cos^2(\arg\alpha)\) for either root \(\alpha\)
of \(H\). The three integers \(D,U,V\) have comparable sizes
near any fixed \(\xi_*\in(0,4)\). They have pairwise disjoint
odd-prime supports. At each prime one root valuation
allocation must account for all their exponents.

## The primitive coefficient and slope constraints

For roots of valuations \(s\le t\), with \(v_p(p)=1\),
Gauss's lemma gives the exact endpoint valuations
\[
       a_p=v_p(A)=(-s)_++(-t)_+,\qquad
       c_p=v_p(C)=s_++t_+.                                    \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the minimum coefficient valuation of the monic
quadratic is the sum of the negative root valuations.
Primitivity subtracts that minimum, and the constant
coefficient of the monic quadratic has valuation \(s+t\).

When \(s<t\), the quadratic splits over \(\mathbb Q_p\) and
\(v_p(B)=a_p+s\), since the two terms of its monic trace
have unequal valuations. Thus
\[
             (v_p(D),v_p(U),v_p(V))=(t-s,0,0).                 \tag{3}
\]
Both slopes are integers in this case. When \(s=t\),
\(v_p(B)\ge a_p+s\) and \(p\nmid D\). The angle numerator
records the excess in this inequality.

For the native factorial variable, the
[oriented factorial block bounds](MULTIVARIATE_ORIENTED_BLOCK_DENOMINATOR_GCD.md)
give
\[
 A\mid\operatorname{lcm}(G_1^+,G_2^+),\qquad
 C\mid\operatorname{lcm}(G_1^-,G_2^-),\qquad
 D\mid G_1^+G_1^- .                                           \tag{4}
\]
For the first two bounds choose the number of strictly negative
or positive root valuations in (2), respectively, when applying
the oriented block bounds. The last bound uses the difference
of the two root valuations. In any rational normalization one
can use the reduced actual neighboring coefficient ratios in that
variable for the corresponding block bounds.

There is also an absolute bound for the middle coefficient in
the common rational variable. Homogenize the two even and two
odd coefficient polynomials of the primitive parents in an
auxiliary variable \(w=z^2\), using their natural degrees
\(\lfloor j/2\rfloor,\lfloor(j-1)/2\rfloor\).
Let \(I_m\) be the subgroup of binary integral forms of degree
\(m\) generated by their degree-\(m\) monomial multiples.
If the quotient by \(I_m\) is finite, its exponent is divisible
by \(|B|\). To prove this, reduce the factorization by \(H\)
modulo \(B\). Each of the four forms vanishes at the
same point \((-C,A)\) modulo \(B\), since \(H\) reduces to
\(Aw+C\) in each parity. Evaluation is surjective onto
\(\mathbb Z/B\mathbb Z\) at degree \(m\): at every prime
of \(B\) at least one of \(A,C\) is a unit by primitivity,
so the values of the degree-\(m\) monomials generate the
unit ideal there. Hence the quotient by \(I_m\) surjects
onto \(\mathbb Z/B\mathbb Z\). The exponent can be computed
as its largest Smith invariant. This mixes the parents
at the same projective point. In addition, for each proposed
\(A,C\), \(B\) divides the gcd of the four actual homogeneous
evaluations at \((-C,A)\).

## Scale-invariant bounds for the two numerator channels

Fix an equal root valuation \(s\). Let \(e\) be its reduced
denominator, let \(\pi^e=p\) and let \(\mathcal O\) be the
valuation ring of \(\mathbb Q_p(\pi)\), with valuation \(w_\pi=e v_p\).
Substitute \(z=\pi^{es}x\) in both parents. Divide each
parent by a power of \(\pi\) so its coefficients are integral
with at least one unit. Write the resulting polynomials
as \(F_{j,s}(x)=P_{j,s}(x^2)+xQ_{j,s}(x^2)\).
The monic quadratic in this variable has the form
\[
                  H_s(x)=x^2+b x+c,\qquad c\in\mathcal O^\times,
                               \quad b\in\mathcal O .
\]
It divides each \(F_{j,s}\) over \(\mathcal O\), by monic division.
Equations (1) and (2) give
\[
      w_\pi(b)=\frac e2v_p(U),\qquad
                 w_\pi(b^2-4c)=e\,v_p(V),\qquad v_p(D)=0.       \tag{5}
\]

For integral local polynomials \(G_i(w)\), a unit evaluation bound
is a number \(L\) such that at every \(w\in\mathcal O^\times\)
at least one \(G_i(w)\) has valuation at most \(L\).
These bounds are certified, for example, by checking the
simultaneous zero test among unit residues modulo \(\pi^{L+1}\).
One can instead exhibit a polynomial combination of the
\(G_i\) whose lowest-valuation terms consist of one monomial
of coefficient valuation at most \(L\). Its value has that
valuation at every unit. This combines the two parents at
the same possible coordinate, and works with local
truncations whenever their discarded terms of known depth
cannot change the claimed residue obstruction.

Use \(L_{\rm par}(p,s)\) for any such bound for the four
\(P_{j,s},Q_{j,s}\). Modulo \(b\) these four polynomials
vanish at the unit \(-c\). In fact, writing a quotient as
\(E(x^2)+xO(x^2)\), the two parity parts of its product with
\(H_s\) are \((w+c)E+bwO\) and \((w+c)O+bE\).
Consequently
\[
                         v_p(U)\le
                           \left\lfloor\frac{2L_{\rm par}(p,s)}
                                                      {e}\right\rfloor. \tag{6}
\]
This supplies a numerator bound independent of the physical
scale and of the endpoint block overlap.

Similarly use \(L_{\rm der}(p,s)\) for a unit evaluation
bound for the four polynomials \(F_{j,s},F'_{j,s}\) in \(x\).
Put \(d=b^2-4c\). If \(w_\pi(b)=w_\pi(2)\), the critical
point \(x=-b/2\) is a unit and
\[
                    H_s(-b/2)=-d/4,\qquad H'_s(-b/2)=0 .
\]
Both parents and their derivatives there have valuation
at least \(w_\pi(d)-2w_\pi(2)\). If
\(w_\pi(b)\ne w_\pi(2)\), then
\(w_\pi(d)=2\min(w_\pi(b),w_\pi(2))\) by the two distinct
valuations in \(b^2-4c\). In particular
\[
       v_p(V)\le
           2v_p(2)+\left\lfloor\frac{L_{\rm der}(p,s)}{e}\right\rfloor.
                                                                    \tag{7}
\]
At odd primes the bound uses the derivative test only when
\(b\) is a unit. This is the case \(p\mid V\).
One can use the primitive squarefree parents for the two
local bounds, retaining the original arrays for (4).

The other local factor conditions still apply, for example
shared Newton width and
[compatibility of the truncated faces](MULTIVARIATE_PHYSICAL_TRUNCATED_NEWTON_FACES.md).
If only one of two roots fits an equal slope there can be
no allocation of degree two at that slope. At \(p=2\) in
the equal-slope case, \(D\) is odd and the relation \(V=4D-U\)
also gives
\[
 v_2(V)=v_2(U)\ {\rm if}\ v_2(U)<2,\qquad
 v_2(V)=2\ {\rm if}\ v_2(U)>2,\qquad
 v_2(V)\ge3\ {\rm if}\ v_2(U)=2 .
\]

## The discriminant squareclass ties the places together

The quadratic field has squareclass
\[
                       \Delta\sim -UV .                       \tag{8}
\]
In fact \(\Delta/B^2=(U-4D)/U\).
If \(p\mid D\) is odd, then \(-UV\equiv U^2\pmod p\),
so the completion splits.
At \(2\mid D\), \(U\) is odd and \(-UV\equiv U^2\equiv1\bmod8\),
which also gives splitting. At an odd prime of \(UV\), an
odd valuation of \(UV\) means ramification. For an even
valuation, splitting is determined by
\(\bigl(\frac{-p^{-v_p(UV)}UV}{p}\bigr)\).
At \(2\), the stripped odd residue modulo \(8\) and parity
of the removed valuation determine the squareclass. These
are tests on the same integers \(U,D,V\) across primes.
They can be intersected with the
[stepped face field certificates](MULTIVARIATE_TAME_FIELD_DISCRIMINANT_SQUARECLASSES.md).
For example, at an odd prime \(p\mid U\), formula (5) forces
\(v_p(U)\) even at integral \(s\); a half-integral \(s\)
forces \(v_p(U)\) odd and hence ramification.

## Consequence near an isolated transverse contact

Suppose the ordinary profiles under consideration approach
a fixed positive diffuse reference with a simple transverse
nonreal contact and \(\xi_*\in(0,4)\) for the matched quadratic.
The [physical diffuse comparison](MULTIVARIATE_DIFFUSE_PHYSICAL_EXCEPTION_POINTS.md#a-local-bound-when-the-contacting-roots-are-simple)
gives \(|\xi-\xi_*|=O(\eta^\gamma)\), with \(\gamma=1\) at a
simple transverse contact. Here \(\eta\) is the largest added
relative gap. One can also just assume this error bound.

At each prime take the maximum \(M_p(\lambda)\) of
\(\lambda_D d_p+\lambda_U u_p+\lambda_V v_p\) over its
allowed degree-two allocations and exponent triples, where
\((d_p,u_p,v_p)\) are for \(D,U,V\) and the \(\lambda\)'s
are nonnegative with sum one. In the unequal-slope case use
(3). In the equal-slope case use (6), (7), the odd-prime
disjointness and the relation at \(2\) above. One may keep
further branch information, including (2) for the physical
coefficients, in calculating the maximum. Then
\[
             \log D\le \sum_p M_p(\lambda)\log p+O(1),         \tag{9}
\]
since \(\log U=\log D+O(1)\) and \(\log V=\log D+O(1)\)
near the fixed \(\xi_*\). A failed local allocation itself
excludes the quadratic. When the parity polynomials have no
common nonzero root over characteristic zero and squarefree
parents are used for the derivative conditions, the bounds
in (9) can be made finite with finite prime support.
Indeed away from the finite endpoint support all slopes
are zero. Bezout identities over \(\mathbb Q\) among the
respective polynomials, after stripping powers of the
variable and clearing denominators, supply a nonzero constant
combination. At any other prime with zero valuation of that
constant the unit evaluation bound is zero. At nonzero
slopes use the local fields and the same argument. A positive
real nonzero argument scaling neither creates nor removes
a shared opposite pair.

If \(\xi_*\) is irrational algebraic, (9) excludes
accumulating ordinary common quadratics as soon as its
right side is uniformly bounded above by
\((\gamma/2-\epsilon)\log(1/\eta)+O(1)\) for some
\(\epsilon>0\). This uses
[Roth's rational approximation theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300000644);
the neighborhood is qualitative. For an effective version
replace \(\gamma/2\) by
\(\gamma/[\mathbb Q(\xi_*):\mathbb Q]\), using the
nonzero integer obtained by evaluating the minimal polynomial
at \(U/D\) and clearing its denominator. For a rational
limit and \(\xi\ne\xi_*\) one can replace it by \(\gamma\),
by subtracting the two rationals. A rational exact angle
attainment still needs a separate exclusion.

## Exact strict instances

At orders \(6,7\), take rows \((21,18),(9,12)\) and \(p=53\).
The primitive parent coefficient lists in increasing order,
reduced modulo \(53\), are
\[
    (41,7,35,48,20,27,20),\qquad(47,33,12,36,24,37,6,12).
\]
Their unit parity gcds are linear with roots \(50\) and \(44\)
respectively. Thus each parent allows a primitive middle
coefficient divisible by \(53\) in its parity test,
but the joint test has \(L_{\rm par}(53,0)=0\).
All roots have valuation zero at this prime.

At orders \(2,3\), take the single row \((34,30)\) and
\(p=43\). The normalized reductions, again in increasing
order, are \((13,40,1)\) and \((13,22,9,1)\).
The gcds with their derivatives have roots \(23\) and \(36\)
respectively. Both individual discriminants are divisible by
\(43\). The joint test gives \(L_{\rm der}(43,0)=0\).
These compare the individual and joint bounds on ordinary
profiles. They do not require either pair to have a common
factor.

## What finite-prime interpolation still allows

For any three distinct prescribed odd primes, the
[local coefficient interpolation](MULTIVARIATE_UNIT_GAP_LOCAL_COEFFICIENT_INTERPOLATION.md)
can force arbitrarily large local budgets in the three
channels on the same ordinary profile. Use target pairs
whose factors at the respective primes are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{channel}&\text{common local quadratic}&\text{depth}\\ \hline
 D &(z+p^E)(1+p^E z)&v_p(D)=2E\\
 U &z^2+p^E z+1&v_p(U)=2E\\
 V &z^2+2z+1+p^E&v_p(V)=E .
 \end{array}
\]
In the second row the parity common point is \(-1\)
modulo \(p^E\); in the third the critical point is \(-1\)
with parent and derivative depth at least \(E\).
Choosing each precision sufficiently greater than \(E\)
preserves these permitted allocations. The gap parameter
\(\eta\) and the real positive diffuse limit can be selected
independently, and extra coprimality primes can certify the
same constructed profile in every degree. Thus the upper
budgets in (9) still require a global factorial bound, and
the mere size of the separate finite-prime allowances
cannot establish one.

A stronger limitation covers all primes of one candidate's
arithmetic support. Fix \(n\ge4\) and use the common rational
argument normalization by the lower tail first coefficient
factor. Choose any primitive irreducible quadratic with
\(B\ne0\) close to the real reference selection. The prime divisors of its \(ABC\Delta\) and any other prescribed finite
prime set can all admit the same candidate to prescribed local precision.
Choose rational target parents \(H Q_j\) of degrees
\(n,N\), with nonzero coefficients. Normalize \(H(0)=1\)
when choosing the cofactors. Set \(Q_n(0)=1\) and prescribe
its linear coefficient so the lower first ratio equals the
core first ratio. The remaining cofactor coefficients can be
chosen outside finitely many rational hyperplanes of vanishing
target coefficients. Here \(n\ge4\) and \(B\ne0\) provide
a free coefficient in each of the last two nonconstant
constraints. The upper cofactor can be chosen freely with
the same nonvanishing requirement.

Use these rational parents as the local targets at every
listed prime to arbitrary specified relative precision.
The actual lower tail first coefficient factor is then a
unit congruent to one at each of them, so taking the common
rational normalization preserves the prescribed precision.
In that variable the actual parent coefficients are locally
as close to the targets as desired. The same rational
candidate therefore satisfies the induced local factor
congruences at all those primes, with its own global
squareclass (8). A sequence of rational quadratics can
approach a prescribed nonreal reference selection at
any specified approximation rate by ordinary rational
approximation of its two monic coefficients. The diffuse
row construction makes the real profile approach the same
reference independently of the finite local prescriptions
at each term of the sequence.

This construction allows the candidate and its arithmetic
support to grow with the profile. It establishes no rational
common factor. It shows that checks of bounded precision
restricted to the primes supporting the candidate's primitive
coefficients and discriminant require a further ingredient,
such as a constraint at other primes or a global factorial
bound tying the supports together.
