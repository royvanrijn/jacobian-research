# Factorial tails with a unique highest-degree monomial

Let \(\Lambda(X^\alpha)=\prod_{\nu=1}^r\alpha_\nu!\). Suppose the
highest homogeneous part of \(F\) is the single monomial \(cX^b\),
where \(c\ne0\) and \(d=|b|\ge1\). Write \(G\) for the part of
degree \(d-1\). Then
\[
                \lim_{m\to\infty}
    \frac{\Lambda(F^m)}{c^m\prod_\nu(mb_\nu)!}
       =\exp\!\left(\frac{G(b)}{c\,b^b}\right).
 \tag{1}
\]
Here the evaluation \(b^b\) uses \(0^0=1\). In particular all
sufficiently large moment orders are nonzero. The moment cutoff can
depend on the coefficients. The zero-free tail holds over every
characteristic-zero field by embedding the field generated by the
coefficients into \(\mathbb C\). A nonzero constant has nonzero
moments at all orders.

For each \(r,d\), the
[uniform propagation theorem](FACTORIAL_INHOMOGENEOUS_UNIFORM_PROPAGATION.md)
gives a computable bound \(B(r,d)\) for a nonvanishing window,
independent of the coefficients and the starting order, in this class.
In particular this gives coefficient-uniform finite windows for
every support with pairwise distinct total degrees, since removing
zero coefficients preserves the unique top monomial. The window
length from propagation can exceed the support size.

## Concentration at maxima of the top phase

The following argument also gives a criterion for top parts with
several monomials. Let the homogeneous part of degree \(d\ge1\)
be \(H=\lambda H_0\), where \(\lambda\ne0\) and \(H_0\) is real.
Put \(S(y)=\sum_\nu y_\nu\), and let \(K\) be the set of maximizers
of \(|H_0(y)|e^{-S(y)}\) on the nonnegative orthant. It is compact
because the function tends to zero at infinity, and its maximum
\(L\) is positive. Suppose \(H_0\) has one sign on \(K\). Flip
\(H_0\) and \(\lambda\) together if necessary so that the sign is
positive. Then
\[
 \operatorname{dist}\!\left(
      \frac{\Lambda(F^m)}{\Lambda(H^m)},\
      \operatorname{conv}\{\exp(G(y)/H(y)):y\in K\}
                     \right)\longrightarrow0 .
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular \(0\) outside this convex hull implies eventual
nonvanishing. With either sign at the different maxima, the same
conclusion holds along the even orders. If \(K\) consists of a
single point, (2) is a limit to the indicated nonzero exponential.
It does not require a nondegenerate Hessian at the maximum.

To prove (2), set
\[
 p(y)=\frac{H(y)e^{-S(y)}}{\lambda L},\qquad
 p_m(y)=\frac{m^{-d}F(my)e^{-S(y)}}{\lambda L}.
\]
The factorial Gamma integral, after scaling \(X=my\), gives
\(\Lambda(F^m)=m^{r+dm}(\lambda L)^m\int p_m^m\,dy\), and
the corresponding integral for \(H\) has \(p^m\) in place of
\(p_m^m\). Integrate on the nonnegative orthant throughout.

Choose a bounded neighborhood \(U\) of \(K\), relative to the
orthant, with \(|p|\ge1/2\) on its closure, and with \(p>0\) there in the one-sign case. There
\[
       (p_m/p)^m\ \longrightarrow\ \exp(G/H)
\]
uniformly, by the homogeneous expansion
\(p_m/p=1+m^{-1}G/H+O(m^{-2})\). Outside \(U\), the function
\(|p|\) is bounded by \(1-\delta\) for some \(\delta>0\).
The difference \(p_m-p\) tends uniformly to zero on the entire
orthant because each lower-degree term, multiplied by \(e^{-S}\),
is bounded. There is also a common integrable bound for
\(|p_m|+|p|\) there. Thus the contribution of the complement of
\(U\) to either integral is \(O((1-\delta/2)^m)\).

For the allowed orders \(p^m=|p|^m\) on \(U\). The mass
\(I_m=\int_U |p|^m\) dominates the omitted bound. In fact for
every sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\), the relatively open set
\(\{y\in U:|p(y)|>1-\epsilon\}\) has positive orthant measure,
even for a maximum on a face. This bounds \(I_m\) below by a
positive constant times \((1-\epsilon)^m\). The probability
density \(|p|^m/I_m\) on \(U\) therefore concentrates on \(K\):
on the complement of any neighborhood of \(K\), compactness gives
a fixed loss from the maximum. The quotient of the factorial
moments is consequently the expectation of \(\exp(G/H)\) under
this density, up to a term tending to zero. By continuity,
the expectation's distance from the convex hull in (2) tends
to zero. For the unique top monomial, each factor
\(y_\nu^{b_\nu}e^{-y_\nu}\) has its unique maximum at \(b_\nu\),
so \(K=\{b\}\) and (1) follows.

The related flat binary calculation in
[Long and Le Blanc, *Factorial Moments in Two Variables*, §6](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00813v1)
finds the degree-\((d-1)\) exponential correction when
\(H\) is proportional to \((X_1+X_2)^d\). There the maximum
extends over a segment, and §6 also determines its limiting
measure. The argument here gives a point limit for a unique
maximum in any dimension and the convex-hull criterion
without an assumption on the geometry of a larger maximizing set.
