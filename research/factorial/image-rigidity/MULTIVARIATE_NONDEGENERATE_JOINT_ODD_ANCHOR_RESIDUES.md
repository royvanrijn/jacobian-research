# Simultaneous nondegenerate residues at linked odd-corner anchors

Fix an even order \(m=2q\ge6\) and the next order \(n=m+1\). There are ordinary simultaneous-corner profiles with arbitrary fixed core in which the two selected primitive anchor moduli pass both scalar remainder tests at every prime divisor. At every such prime the marked reduction is a prescribed squarefree quadratic and the unmarked reduction has full degree and a unit leading coefficient. The same construction controls the large-prime parts of all the other linked moduli simultaneously. Both selected moduli tend to infinity and their product is of order \(B^2\), where the calibration rows lie in intervals of length \(O(B)\).

The conclusion holds at prescribed diffuse limits \(\tau>0,\ |\lambda|\le\tau\), and any prescribed integer signed gap of the rows added to the core. The profiles may be taken to satisfy SFC(2) at every physical degree. The nondegenerate unit comparison strengthens the reduction-collapsing simultaneous divisibility construction at these primes. [Simultaneous divisor tests](MULTIVARIATE_ODD_ANCHOR_DIVISIBILITY_OBSTRUCTION.md)

## The selected moduli and suitable heights

The anchor fan has the indices \((s,h)\) where \(s=m,n\) and \(1\le h\le3q-s\). Its primitive moduli are \(V_i(B)=a_iB+b_i=(sB+h)/\gcd(s,h)\). Let \(d\) be the number of anchors. The cross determinants \(a_i b_j-a_j b_i\) are nonzero for distinct anchors. The two selected indices have \(s=m\), and \(h=p_1-2,p_1-1\) for an odd prime \(p_1\mid m\), or \(h=1,3\) if \(m\) is a power of two. At the prime 2, at least one of these two primitive forms is always odd: the first selected \(h\) is odd, so the reduced slope is even and the reduced constant is odd. At every other prime the two forms exclude at most two residue classes. [Linked quadratic specializations](MULTIVARIATE_ODD_CORNER_LINKED_QUADRATIC_ANCHORS.md)

Let \(P\) be a large fixed integer to be chosen. For every prime \(p\le P\), choose a residue class in which the two selected forms are units. Within this class prescribe a finite exact valuation of all the other forms by choosing a lift modulo \(p^E\) with \(p^{E-1}>d\) and avoiding the at most \(d\) roots of the primitive forms modulo \(p^E\). Also take an auxiliary prime \(p_0>P\) exceeding all degrees below the absolute angular cutoff and their squares, and the factorial arguments of the fixed core in those degrees. Choose a class at \(p_0\) avoiding every anchor modulus. These conditions give a fixed progression \(B\equiv B_0\bmod Q\). The small-prime parts \(c_i\) of \(V_i(B)\) at primes \(p\le P\) are fixed throughout, and \(c_i=1\) for the two selected anchors. The prime \(p_0\) divides none of them.

Put \(M(B)=\prod_i V_i(B)/c_i\). For the rest of the proof we may require, at arbitrarily large \(B\) in the progression, that \(M(B)\) is squarefree, every \(p\mid M(B)\) satisfies \(P<p\le B^{1-\delta}\) for some fixed \(\delta>0\), and
\[
                          \sum_{p\mid M(B)}\frac1p<\varepsilon .    \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\varepsilon>0\) can be chosen in advance, and \(\delta\) can depend on \(Q\). To prove these statements, count heights in \([X,2X]\). If \(P\) exceeds the absolute cross determinants and the slopes \(a_i\), no large prime can divide two forms, and for \(p>P,\ p\ne p_0\) each condition \(p^e\mid V_i(B)\) removes at most \(X/(Qp^e)+1\) integers of the progression. The \(p^2\) exceptions with \(p^2=O(X)\) have total count \(O_d(X/(QP)+\sqrt X)\). The average value of the sum in (1) is \(O_d(1/P)+o(1)\): use \(1/p\) times the count at \(e=1\) and \(\sum_{p\le C X}p^{-1}\le O(\log X)\). Markov's inequality leaves an arbitrarily large fraction for (1) if \(P\) is sufficiently large.

For a prime \(p>X^{1-\delta}\) dividing an anchor write \(V_i(B)=kp\), with \(k\le C X^\delta\). For each \(k\) there are at most \(\pi(CX/k)\) such primes even across all \(B\in[X,2X]\). The elementary Chebyshev upper bound \(\pi(Y)\ll Y/\log Y\), summed over \(k\), bounds the total number of exceptions by \(O_m(\delta X)+o(X)\) for \(0<\delta<1/4\). The constant here is independent of the progression. Choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small after \(Q\) is fixed leaves a positive fraction of the progression satisfying all three properties. The use of \(X^{1-\delta}\) also gives the asserted bound with \(B^{1-\delta}\).

## Joint unit interpolation over the product modulus

Use the fraction set \(\mathcal R=\{k/j:j=m,n,\ 1\le k\le j\}\) of the [single-prime construction](MULTIVARIATE_HEIGHT_CAPPED_NATIVE_UNIT_INTERPOLATION.md). Write \(u=|\mathcal R|\). Let \(I\) be an interval of integer bases of length comparable to \(B\), or such an interval restricted to a fixed residue class modulo \(p_0\), with the comparison constants fixed. Remove all bases where any \(A+r\), \(r\in\mathcal R\), is zero at any \(p\mid M(B)\). Call the resulting set \(D\). Finitely many additional bases, or \(O_m(\log B)\) bases, may also be removed. For every \(L\in G=((\mathbb Z/M(B)\mathbb Z)^\times)^{\mathcal R}\), there are \(K=O_m(\log B)\) labelled bases from \(D\) for which
\[
                            \prod_{\nu=1}^K(A_\nu+r)=L_r
                            \quad(r\in\mathcal R).              \tag{2}
\]
Constants may depend on the fixed interval proportions and the fixed auxiliary prime. The same \(K\) can be used at every target. The proof uses a bound on every nontrivial multiplicative character of \(G\), with a gap uniform in \(B\).

Parameterize the interval or progression by consecutive integers \(x\) in an interval of length \(H\asymp B\), so \(A=A_0+ax\) with \(a=1\) or \(p_0\). Each local character product is a phase \(g_p(x)\) of absolute value one after assigning the value one to factors at their zero residue. The phase has period \(p\). For a nontrivial character choose a prime \(p_\ast\) at which one local coordinate character is nontrivial. Since \(M(B)\) is squarefree, the remaining active primes can be grouped into pairwise coprime blocks \(Q_j\), all coprime to \(p_\ast\), with \(Q_j\le B^{1-\delta/2}\) and with number \(k\le 4d+1\) for sufficiently large \(B\). To see this, greedily pack consecutive prime factors up to the threshold. Adjacent complete blocks have product exceeding the threshold, and the product of all the blocks is \(O_m(B^d)\). The integer \(a\) is a unit at every such prime.

For a unit phase \(F\) on the integers, put \(S(F)=H^{-1}\sum_{x\in I_x} F(x)\), where \(I_x\) is the interval of consecutive parameter integers. Put \(\partial_v F(x)=F(x+v)\overline{F(x)}\) and \(T=\lfloor B^{\delta/4}\rfloor\). Averaging shifts by \(tQ_j\), \(1\le t\le T\), changes the mean by \(O(TQ_j/H)\). Cauchy–Schwarz in \(x\), followed by shifting each of the resulting intervals back to \(I_x\), gives
\[
 |S(F)|^2\ll T^{-1}+TQ_j/H+
              T^{-1}\sum_{h=1}^{T-1}|S(\partial_{hQ_j} F)| .
\]
Apply this successively for the \(k\) blocks. Jensen's inequality for the subsequent powers gives
\[
 |S(F)|^{2^k}\ll_d T^{-1}+T\max_j Q_j/H+
 T^{-k}\!\sum_{1\le h_1,\ldots,h_k<T}
         \left|S(\partial_{h_kQ_k}\cdots\partial_{h_1Q_1} F)\right|. \tag{3}
\]
For \(k=0\), use the original mean directly. Taking \(F=\prod_p g_p\), differencing by \(hQ_j\) cancels the complete \(Q_j\) block, because all its phases have period dividing \(Q_j\) and absolute value one. The final mean is at the single prime \(p_\ast\).

At \(p_\ast\), the possible zero locations of its factors are the zeros of
\[
               A_0+a\left(x+\sum_{j\in E}h_j Q_j\right)+r,
                 \qquad E\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\},\ r\in\mathcal R .
\]
At a fixed \(E\) they are distinct, since \(p_\ast>P>mn\). For two different subsets a collision is a nontrivial linear congruence in at least one \(h_j\) with coefficient \(\pm aQ_j\), a unit. Its fraction among the shift tuples is \(O(1/p_\ast+1/T)\). There are \(O_{m,d}(1)\) pairs of locations. For every tuple without collisions, at least one coordinate character in the resulting product is nontrivial at its distinct linear root. Expressing all its coordinate characters as powers of a primitive character and dividing multiplicities and character order by their gcd gives the distinct-root Weil bound \(O_{m,d}(\sqrt{p_\ast})\) for the complete sum. Restoring the phase convention at the finitely many zero locations changes this bound by \(O_{m,d}(1)\). Breaking \(I_x\) into periods plus a remainder bounds its mean by \(O_{m,d}(p_\ast^{-1/2}+p_\ast/H)\). [Mauduit and Sárközy, Lemmas 1 and 2](https://matwbn.icm.edu.pl/ksiazki/aa/aa82/aa8244.pdf)

By (3) the mean of the original product is bounded by a quantity tending to zero as \(P\to\infty\), uniformly for all sufficiently large \(B\) at this \(P\). Indeed the number of steps is bounded independently of \(\delta\) and \(P\); after fixing \(P,Q,\delta\), the boundary, averaging, and period-remainder errors tend to zero as \(B\to\infty\). The deletion from \(I\) to \(D\) costs at most \(uH\sum_{p\mid M(B)}1/p+u\,\omega(M(B))+O_m(\log B)\), where \(\omega\) counts distinct prime factors. Choose \(\varepsilon\) small and then \(P\) large. We get \(\left|\sum_{A\in D}\chi((A+r)_r)\right|\le |D|/2\) for every nontrivial character. The proof is unchanged for a prescribed class at the fixed prime \(p_0\), even when \(p_0\) was chosen after \(P\), since only the comparison constant for \(H\asymp B\) changes. Finally \(|G|\le M(B)^u=O_m(B^{du})\). Orthogonality counts representations (2) with main term \(|D|^K/|G|\) and relative error at most \((|G|-1)2^{-K}\). Taking \(K>\log_2|G|\) proves (2).

The left-right fraction graph for adjacent orders is a forest. As in the single-prime theorem, solve each requested unit ratio as a quotient of two vertex labels, now in \((\mathbb Z/M(B)\mathbb Z)^\times\). Apply (2) once for each orientation. The resulting \(K\) increasing and \(K\) decreasing unit rows multiply the required coefficient ratios by arbitrary prescribed units modulo every prime of \(M(B)\). Their net signed gap is zero. Since both orientations use \(K\) rows, the factors of the order \(j\) cancel in every ratio. All added ramps are units at each of these primes.

## Simultaneous scalar remainder consequences

Start with the fixed core and the two top rows \((B-1,B)\), \((B-1+q,B-1)\). All the remaining rows have bounded gap and height strictly below the top. For each \(p\mid M(B)\) there is a unique associated anchor. The reversed stripped parents at that prime have a marked quadratic with all three coefficients units and an unmarked polynomial with every coefficient a unit, before the unit calibration. For the top rows this follows by evaluating at the rational specialization \(X=-h/s\). Exactly the marked coefficients beyond degree two have an identically zero top-row factor there. Every other top factor has one of finitely many fixed nonzero rational values, so it is a unit at \(p>P\) once \(P\) exceeds their possible prime divisors. At the physical height \(B\) these values give the same residues. The fixed core is a unit contributor when \(P\) exceeds its factorial arguments at the two orders. Choose all further row bases to avoid zeros in their full ramps at all primes dividing \(M(B)\). This is possible in any interval of length comparable to \(B\) or to \(\sqrt B\), also after prescribing a fixed congruence class at \(p_0\). The union bound used for \(D\) applies with the bounded number of full factors for any of their fixed gaps. Decrease \(\varepsilon\) in (1) if necessary.

At each such prime prescribe the normalized marked polynomial \(H(z)=(1+z)(1+2z)\). Independently prescribe the full-degree unmarked polynomial to be \(H(z)(1+z)^{t-2}\) or \((1+3z)^t\), where \(t\) is the unmarked order. Every displayed nonzero target coefficient is a unit when \(P\) is large enough. All primes can be assigned their own choice by CRT. The joint unit calibration forces these reductions without altering any valuation of any stripped physical coefficient at any \(p\mid M(B)\). Since \(H\) is squarefree and has leading coefficient a unit, the linked scalar remainder is simultaneously zero at all these primes if the first target is chosen throughout. The two selected moduli are squarefree and have no small-prime part, so this is compatibility at their complete moduli. Alternatively one may select incompatible reductions at arbitrary prime subsets with the same valuation arrays and the same upper envelopes.

For the real diffuse limits, use the filler and signed-gap correction of the single-prime construction, taking the small filler base in an interval of length comparable to \(\sqrt B\) and the correction base in an interval of length comparable to \(B\), all below the two top rows. Choose those bases to be zero modulo \(p_0\) as well as units in every required ramp at the primes of \(M(B)\). The filler counts can be defined with their actual bases. Their signed gap is \(O(\sqrt B)\), and the correction at height comparable to \(B\) has \(o(1)\) curvature. The calibration rows have curvature \(O_m(\log B/B)\) and gap zero in total.

To ensure physical all-degree separation, take a fixed cutoff \(J\) as in the [opposed-row theorem](MULTIVARIATE_UNIVERSAL_OPPOSED_PADIC_ANGLE_MARKING.md) and choose \(p_0>J^2\) also above all core arguments through degree \(J\). Add \(O_m(\log B)\) mirrored pairs of gap \(J\) at an anchor-unit base of height comparable to \(B\) congruent to \(-1\bmod p_0\). At each \(2\le j<J\) the sole nonunit ramp factor in each row is \(j(A+1)\), present once in each joint endpoint and twice at each interior. The top rows contribute \(O_J(\log B)\) possible downward chord error and the fixed core, fillers, repairs, and calibration rows contribute none. Repeating the pairs sufficiently often gives a strict single chord and binomial reductions in all these degrees. Their root-quotient argument and the absolute cutoff then prove all-degree angular separation. The added pairs have \(O_m(\log B/B)\) curvature and signed gap zero.

The construction controls every large prime of the full fan and both complete selected moduli by nondegenerate scalar remainders. It does not assert a common factor of the rational parents. Small-prime residues at the other anchors and further arithmetic conditions may still distinguish a physical common factor.

## Exact checks

The [finite checker](verify_multivariate_nondegenerate_joint_odd_anchor_residues.py) uses \(m=6\), \(B=8656\), and selected moduli \(51937=167\cdot311\) and \(25969\). In an interval with 610 simultaneously allowed bases it calibrates these three primes and the further native primes \(199\) and \(10099\) of other anchors. The exact arithmetic uses simultaneous linear congruences for the base multiplicities in discrete logarithms. It recomputes all stripped factorial ramp valuations and each residue coefficient at all five primes, then checks both simple roots of the prescribed marked quadratic. The fixture was checked both with all remainders vanishing and with all five residual pairs coprime. Its multiplicities come from the congruence solver. The \(O_m(\log B)\) row count is the analytic conclusion of the character argument.
