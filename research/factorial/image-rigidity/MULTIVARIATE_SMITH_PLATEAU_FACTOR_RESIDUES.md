# Canonical local factor residues from stable Smith widths

A truncated Sylvester comparison can retain the coefficients of a candidate common factor as well as bound its degree. If the number of surviving Smith entries is constant across a precision interval, a colon ideal gives a canonical monic approximate common factor. This calibrates partial repeated residual branches without guessing their digit corrections. The same ordinary factorial arrays and Newton slope normalizations are used as in the [truncated face comparison](MULTIVARIATE_PHYSICAL_TRUNCATED_NEWTON_FACES.md).

## The finite precision statement

Let \(\mathcal O\) be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer \(\pi\), and let \(F,G\in\mathcal O[X]\), with at least one of the two monic. Write \(\lambda_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\cup\{\infty\}\) for the Sylvester Smith valuations and let
\[
                              c(t)=\#\{i:\lambda_i\ge t\}.
\]
Fix \(b\ge0,L\ge1\) and suppose
\[
                            c(b+1)=c(b+L)=d .                  \tag{1}
\]
This condition is read at precision \(\pi^{b+L}\). Put \(R=\mathcal O/(\pi^L)\) and form the ideal
\[
     \mathcal J=
     \{U\in \mathcal O[X]:\ \pi^b U\in (F,G)+\pi^{b+L}\mathcal O[X]\}.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
Its reduction in \(R[X]\) is generated by a unique monic polynomial \(\widehat H\) of degree \(d\). In particular \(\widehat H\) divides both parents modulo \(\pi^L\). Every monic factor common to both arrays at precision \(\pi^{b+L}\) reduces to a divisor of \(\widehat H\) at precision \(\pi^L\). Thus a possible exact common degree-\(d\) factor is determined modulo \(\pi^L\). If its degree is smaller, the same polynomial restricts the possible reductions at that smaller degree.

The loss \(b\) is the numerical cost of the Smith entries before the plateau. It need not be a sum of their valuations. The statement neither assumes an exact common factor of degree \(d\) nor asserts that \(\widehat H\) lifts to a common factor at the full input precision.

**Proof.** The cokernel of the ordinary Sylvester map identifies as an \(\mathcal O\)-module with \(\mathcal A=\mathcal O[X]/(F,G)\). In fact take \(F\) monic and reduce any multiplier of \(G\) modulo \(F\); the other multiplier then has the Sylvester degree bound whenever the output degree does. Reduction modulo \(F\) also makes the map onto the quotient surjective.

By (2), \(\mathcal O[X]/\mathcal J\) is isomorphic as an \(\mathcal O\)-module to \(\pi^b(\mathcal A/\pi^{b+L}\mathcal A)\). The summand with Smith valuation \(\lambda_i\le b\) contributes zero. The summand with \(\lambda_i\ge b+L\) contributes a copy of \(R\). Condition (1) excludes all intermediate valuations, so \(R[X]/\overline{\mathcal J}\) is free of rank \(d\) over \(R\).

Modulo the maximal ideal its quotient is a cyclic polynomial quotient of dimension \(d\), hence its basis is the classes of \(1,X,\ldots,X^{d-1}\). By Nakayama's lemma these same classes form an \(R\)-basis. Expressing \(X^d\) in the basis gives a monic polynomial in \(\overline{\mathcal J}\) of degree \(d\); monic division and independence of the basis show that it generates \(\overline{\mathcal J}\) and is unique. The case \(d=0\) gives the generator one.

If a monic \(H'\) divides both \(F,G\bmod\pi^{b+L}\), reduce the condition \(\pi^b U\in(F,G)+\pi^{b+L}\mathcal O[X]\) modulo \(H'\) at that precision. The remainder of \(U\) is zero modulo \(\pi^L\), because the remainder module is free over \(\mathcal O/(\pi^{b+L})\). Therefore \(\bar H'\) divides every element of \(\overline{\mathcal J}\), including \(\widehat H\).

For context, recovery of a \(p\)-adic gcd from a Sylvester lattice with precision loss measured by a reduced resultant is used by [Ford, Pauli and Roblot, Appendix A](https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2002__14_1_151_0/). The finite plateau above replaces the hypothesis of an exact gcd of specified degree by a flatness condition on the truncated quotient. The resulting monic generator can exist even if neither parent has a common monic divisor of that degree at the full precision.

## Use on the original Newton slope groups

First take the full monic unit-root group of each parent at the chosen slope after the usual \(\pi\)-argument scaling. Each full group lifts from its unit-constant residual part by the [coprime block construction](MULTIVARIATE_SHARED_RESIDUAL_BLOCK_LIFTING.md), since its residual complement only has zero or infinity roots. The two lifted group polynomials are monic and their roots are units. Apply (1)–(2) to them. In a full unit chart no separate slope-group extraction is needed.

In coordinates, one can decide \(U\bmod\pi^L\in\overline{\mathcal J}\) by testing whether the coefficient vector of \(\pi^b U\) belongs to the Sylvester image modulo \(\pi^{b+L}\). This is a finite Smith comparison with a target vector. It determines \(\widehat H\) by solving for its degree-\(d\) monic representative. If another constraint forces common local degree \(d\), this supplies all the coefficients at once for the [projective shape reconstruction](MULTIVARIATE_GROUPED_SHAPE_CONGRUENCES.md). If the degree may be smaller, one only has to test its reductions inside the canonical higher-precision polynomial of degree \(d\). Different plateaus yield simultaneous necessary divisibilities at their respective depths. The method does not require choosing partial multiplicities of a repeated first residual factor.

## A repeated ordinary unit block

Take four copies of \((2,3)\) and four copies of \((3,2)\) in orders \(6,7\). At \(p=5\) the endpoint coefficients are units after removing the minimum coefficient valuation. The monic scaled arrays modulo \(125\), in increasing coefficient order, are
\[
               (1,16,90,0,90,16,1),\qquad
               (1,0,101,85,85,101,0,1).
\]
Their residual gcd is \((X+1)^5\). The first three Smith widths are \(5,2,2\). For \(b=1,L=2\), the saturated ideal gives
\[
                              \widehat H=X^2+12X+1\pmod {25}.
\]
All 25 monic quadratic lifts of \((X+1)^2\) modulo 25 divide both parents at that precision. The stable Smith interval computed from precision 125 retains one of them. None of the monic degree-two candidates divides both at precision 125. In this example the precision-25 factor predicted by the plateau therefore excludes the whole branch upon exact comparison at the next digit. These checks do not assume any lack of other certificates at the same orders.

The [exact checker](verify_multivariate_smith_plateau_factor_residues.py) derives both input arrays directly from the original factorial rows, diagonalizes the modular Sylvester maps, and checks the saturated image and the monic divisibilities. A uniform proof that useful plateaus or other excluding primes occur in every unrestricted profile remains open.
