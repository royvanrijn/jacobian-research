# Factorial nonvanishing under blockwise radial perturbations

Partition the variables into nonempty blocks \(I_1,\ldots,I_b\).
Write \(d_g=|I_g|\), \(t_g=d_g-1\), \(L_g=\sum_{i\in I_g}X_i\), and
let \(A_g\ge0\). Put
\[
 P_0=\prod_g L_g^{A_g},\qquad
 F=c\left(P_0+\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal H} h_\nu X^\nu\right),
 \quad c\ne0 .
\]
The perturbation has arbitrary finite support subject to
\(d_g(\nu)=\sum_{i\in I_g}\nu_i\ge A_g\) for every \(g,\nu\).
Work over a characteristic-zero field with a real-valued extension \(v\)
of the \(p\)-adic valuation, normalized by \(v(p)=1\). Let \(s_p\) denote
base-\(p\) digit sum.

If for every nonzero perturbation coefficient
\[
 v(h_\nu)>
 \frac{\sum_i s_p(\nu_i)+
       \sum_{g:\ d_g(\nu)>0}\max(0,s_p(t_g)-1)}{p-1},
 \tag{1}
\]
then every moment \(\Lambda(F^n)\), \(n\ge1\), is nonzero. More precisely,
\[
 \frac{\Lambda(F^n)}
      {c^n\displaystyle\prod_g (A_g n+t_g)!/t_g!}
 \equiv 1\pmod{\{x:v(x)>0\}} .
 \tag{2}
\]
The exponents, dimension, number of blocks, support size and starting
order have no upper bounds. Over an arbitrary characteristic-zero
field it suffices that the subfield generated by the \(h_\nu\) admit
the indicated valuation.

For a prime power \(q=p^e\), \(e\ge1\), the same unit congruence
at every positive multiple of \(q\) needs only
\[
 v(h_\nu)>\frac{W_\nu-1+1/q}{p-1}\quad(\nu\ne0),\qquad
 W_\nu=\sum_i s_p(\nu_i)+
             \sum_{g:\ d_g(\nu)>0}\max(0,s_p(t_g)-1).
 \tag{2a}
\]
A perturbation constant, possible only when all \(A_g=0\), can
be included with \(v(h_0)>0\). With \(q\) at most the actual
support size this proves the support-size window.

For any strictly positive valuations of the \(h_\nu\), even when (1)
fails, there is an effective uniform approximation to the left side of
(2) at each fixed precision. Over a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\)
it is the restriction of a locally analytic function on \(\mathbb Z_p\).
This gives a decision at the chosen precision for all starts of a
prescribed window simultaneously.

## 1. Sum the tied monomials of the radial block

Let \(k=(k_\nu)\) be a vector of perturbation counts, \(K=\sum_\nu k_\nu\),
\(\gamma=\sum_\nu k_\nu\nu\), and \(D_g=\sum_{i\in I_g}\gamma_i\).
For \(K\le n\), the block indexed by \(k\) after expanding \(F^n/c^n\)
is
\[
 \binom nK\binom K{(k_\nu)}\prod_\nu h_\nu^{k_\nu}
                 X^\gamma\prod_g L_g^{A_g(n-K)} .
\]
For one group, a multinomial expansion and the coefficient of \(z^m\)
in \(\prod_{i\in I_g}(1-z)^{-\gamma_i-1}\) give
\[
 \Lambda_g\!\left(X^\gamma L_g^m\right)
  =\prod_{i\in I_g}\gamma_i!\,
        \frac{(m+D_g+t_g)!}{(D_g+t_g)!}.
\]
For \(P_0\) this gives the denominator in (2) with the scalar \(c^n\).
The point is to sum the powers of \(P_0\) before valuation comparison.
Even a unit-coefficient linear sum in one block has exact ties among
its pure and mixed terms.

Put \(C_g(k)=\binom{D_g+t_g}{\,t_g,(\gamma_i)_{i\in I_g}}\).
The normalized \(k\)-contribution is then
\[
 R_k(n)=\binom nK\binom K{(k_\nu)}\prod_\nu h_\nu^{k_\nu}
 \prod_g\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{D_g-A_gK}(A_g n+t_g+j)}
                 {C_g(k)} .
 \tag{3}
\]
The length \(D_g-A_gK\) is nonnegative by the degree hypothesis.
The same formula gives zero when \(n\ge0\) and \(K>n\), using the
polynomial binomial \(\binom nK\). The \(k=0\) term is one.
For the unperturbed comparison, [Rocks, Theorem 3.3](https://people.math.sc.edu/rocks/BradyRocksSFC2015.pdf)
proves the support-size window for a power of a general linear form.
Here the equal-weight radial seed allows the exact aggregation in (3).

## 2. Uniform dominance from bounded digit losses

Legendre's formula gives the entire possible denominator loss in (3):
\[
 v(C_g(k))=\frac{s_p(t_g)+\sum_{i\in I_g}s_p(\gamma_i)
                               -s_p(D_g+t_g)}{p-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
It is zero if \(D_g=0\). Otherwise the last digit sum is at least one,
and \(s_p(t_g)-s_p(D_g+t_g)\le\max(0,s_p(t_g)-1)\).
The latter bound can be charged to any count with \(d_g(\nu)>0\).
Also
\[
 s_p(\gamma_i)\le\sum_\nu s_p(k_\nu)s_p(\nu_i)
                \le\sum_\nu k_\nu s_p(\nu_i).
\]
The first inequality follows from digit-sum subadditivity under
addition and submultiplicativity under multiplication, by multiplying
digits before performing carries. The two multinomials and all
numerator factors in (3) are integers. Thus (1) makes \(v(R_k(n))>0\)
uniformly for every \(K>0\). This proves (2).

The dependence on degree in (1) is through digit sums. For example,
for \(P_0=(x+y)^A\) this gives nonvanishing at every order when the
added terms all have degree at least \(A\) and
\(v(h_{u,v})>(s_p(u)+s_p(v))/(p-1)\).
There need be no least monomial in the support and no unique pure term
of minimum valuation.

At positive multiples of \(q=p^e\), keep the valuation of the
multinomial in (3). For \(1\le j\le e\), the last \(K\) integers up
to \(n\) contain \(\lceil K/p^j\rceil\) multiples of \(p^j\).
Thus
\[
 v\!\left(\binom nK\binom K{(k_\nu)}\right)
 \ge\frac{\sum_\nu s_p(k_\nu)-K/q}{p-1}.
\]
For the denominator, the proof of (4) above bounds
\((p-1)\sum_g v(C_g(k))\) by \(\sum_\nu W_\nu s_p(k_\nu)\):
the charge for each occupied group can use \(s_p(k_\nu)\ge1\)
in place of \(k_\nu\). If all positive counts have \(\nu\ne0\),
then \(W_\nu\ge1\) and \(s_p(k_\nu)\le k_\nu\). The valuation of
(3) consequently exceeds zero by (2a). When a constant is present
and all \(A_g=0\), remove \(k_0\) from the counts in this argument.
The valuation of the full multinomial is at least that of the
multinomial with the same \(n\) and only the nonconstant counts,
by first choosing the nonconstant counts and then the constant
count. Neither the factorial ratios nor the denominators in (3)
change. The factor \(h_0^{k_0}\) gives strictness for the
constant-only contribution.

For an example beyond the displayed bounds, take \(p=2\) and
\(F=x+y+hxy\) with \(v(h)=1\). The \(k\)-term of (3) has
\(v(C_1(k))=s_2(k)\). Thus all contributions with \(k\ge2\)
have positive valuation, since \(k>s_2(k)\). The remaining
perturbation term is \(h\,n(n+2)/6\), also of positive valuation
at every even order. Thus all even moments are nonzero in this
case even when the conservative bound (2a) fails.

## 3. Uniform precision, analytic continuation and window tests

For nonempty \(\mathcal H\) with all \(v(h_\nu)>0\), set
\(\eta=\min_\nu v(h_\nu)\) and let \(M\ge1\) bound the exponent entries
in \(\mathcal H\). Now use
\[
 \sum_g v(C_g(k))\le
 d(1+\lfloor\log_p(MK)\rfloor)+\frac{\sum_g s_p(t_g)}{p-1}
       \qquad(K\ge1),\qquad d=\sum_g d_g .
 \tag{5}
\]
This follows from \(\gamma_i\le MK\) and (4). Thus
\[
 v(R_k(n))\ge K\eta - d(1+\lfloor\log_p(MK)\rfloor)
                  -\frac{\sum_g s_p(t_g)}{p-1}
 \tag{6}
\]
also for \(n\in\mathbb Z_p\). The polynomial binomial is integral on
\(\mathbb Z_p\) by continuity from nonnegative integers. For each
precision \(J\), the inequality in (6) exceeds \(J\) at every \(K\)
above an effective cutoff. One can choose it on blocks
\(p^r\le K<p^{r+1}\), where the linear-in-\(K\) lower bound grows
exponentially in \(r\) and the digit bound is linear in \(r\).
Thus \(\mathcal F(n)=\sum_k R_k(n)\) converges uniformly over a complete
valued extension. It equals the normalized original moment at every
nonnegative integer by (3).

To see local analyticity choose \(l\) with
\(1/(p^l(p-1))<\eta\) and write \(n=r+p^l z\), \(0\le r<p^l\).
The minimum coefficient valuation of
\(\prod_{j=0}^{K-1}(r+p^l z-j)\) is at least
\(\sum_{q=1}^{l}\lfloor K/p^q\rfloor\). For each \(q\) in that sum,
at least \(\lfloor K/p^q\rfloor\) factors have both coefficients
divisible by \(p^q\). The bound follows by multiplying these
individual factor bounds. After dividing by \(K!\) the loss is at
most \(K/(p^l(p-1))\). The other numerator factors in (3) have
integral coefficients after the same substitution. Together with
(5), this bounds the coefficient valuations of \(R_k(r+p^l z)\)
below by a quantity tending to infinity with \(K\). The sum thus
converges in the complete ring of power series in \(z\) with
coefficients tending to zero. This proves analyticity on each such
disk.

For a window of length \(w\), truncate (3) uniformly at the chosen
precision. The resulting finite sum is periodic at that precision,
with an effective power of \(p\) as period. For its binomial factors
use
\[
 \binom{n+p^m}{K}-\binom nK
       =\sum_{j=1}^{K}\binom{p^m}{j}\binom n{K-j},\qquad
 v\!\left(\binom{p^m}{j}\right)\ge m-v_p(j)\quad(1\le j\le p^m).
\]
For \(m\) large enough that \(K\le p^m\) for all retained terms, this
and the integral polynomial factors in (3) give the claimed period
after allowing for the finitely many denominators. Test \(w\) offsets
at every residue of \(n\) for this period. A passing result proves a
nonzero exact moment in every positive window. This tests the
specified phase and precision. It does not assert that the residues
separate for all coefficients.

There is also an exact compactness formulation of the precision
obstruction. After multiplying by one fixed power of \(p\), all
values of \(\mathcal F\) on \(\mathbb Z_p\) are integral by (6).
Every precision has a surviving simultaneous zero window if and
only if the \(w\) continuous functions \(\mathcal F(n),\ldots,
\mathcal F(n+w-1)\) have a common zero in \(\mathbb Z_p\).
For the forward implication the corresponding nonempty closed
sets of possible starts are nested in compact \(\mathbb Z_p\).
For the reverse implication use continuity and density of positive
integers. A common \(p\)-adic start need not be a positive integer.

## 4. Lower block degrees break this normalization

Take \(P_0=x+y\) and \(F=P_0+h\) with \(v(h)>1/(p-1)\).
An exact binomial expansion gives
\[
 \frac{\Lambda(F^n)}{(n+1)!}
       =\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^n(n+1-k)h^k/k! .
\]
At \(n=p^r-1\) with \(r>v(h)\), the summand \(k=1\) in the
numerator has valuation \(v(h)\). The \(k=0\) summand has valuation
\(r\). For \(k\ge2\), \(v(h^k/k!)>v(h)\) since
\(v(k!)\le(k-1)/(p-1)\). The valuation of the normalized moment is
therefore \(v(h)-r\), unbounded below even for an arbitrarily small
constant. This shows precisely where the radial normalization fails
outside the degree condition. It leaves the ordinary support-size
window a separate question for arbitrary perturbations.

## 5. One descending radial block after changing the reference

Let one marked block have \(a=A_0=p^b\), \(b\ge0\), and
\(t=t_0=d_0-1\). Keep the block-degree condition in all other groups.
In the marked block allow arbitrary perturbation degrees, including
terms below \(a\). Continue to define \(W_\nu\) by (2a). Put
\[
                  B_a=\frac{a-1}{p-1}.
\]
Suppose \(v(h_\nu)>B_a+W_\nu/(p-1)\) for every nonconstant term,
and \(v(h_0)\ge B_a+1/(p-1)\) if a constant occurs. Then for all
\(n\ge1\) the moment normalized by
\[
                  c^n(an)!\prod_{g\ne0}(A_g n+t_g)!/t_g!
 \tag{7}
\]
is integral and congruent to \(\binom{an+t}{t}\) modulo the
positive-valuation ideal. There is a nonzero moment in every
window of length \(\lfloor t/a\rfloor+1\), which is at most the
actual support size. If \(t<a\), every moment is nonzero.
In particular this allows nonhomogeneous tails with lower degrees
in a block containing arbitrarily many variables.

For each \(k\) with \(K>0\) and \(K\le n\), aggregate as in Section 1.
After division by (7), the marked group's part together with the
multinomial count contributes
\[
 \frac{n!}{(n-K)!}\frac{(a(n-K))!}{(an)!}
 \ \frac{\prod_\nu h_\nu^{k_\nu}}{\prod_\nu k_\nu!}
 \ \frac{\prod_{j=1}^{D_0+t}(a(n-K)+j)}{t!\ C_0(k)} .
 \tag{8}
\]
The remaining groups have the same factors as in (3).
Since scaling by a power of \(p\) preserves digit sums,
\(v((aj)!)=B_a j+v(j!)\) for every \(j\ge0\). The first two
factorial ratios in (8), taken together, have valuation
exactly \(-B_a K\). In the last ratio, the product divided
by \(t!\) is integral. Its first \(t\) factors divided by
\(t!\) form a binomial coefficient and the remaining factors
are integers. All other groups have integral numerator products.

By (4), their combined denominator loss, including \(C_0(k)\),
is at most \(\sum_\nu W_\nu s_p(k_\nu)/(p-1)\).
After accounting for \(v(k_\nu!)=(k_\nu-s_p(k_\nu))/(p-1)\),
the lower bound for the valuation of the entire term is
\[
 \sum_\nu\left[
 k_\nu\big(v(h_\nu)-B_a-1/(p-1)\big)
       -(W_\nu-1)s_p(k_\nu)/(p-1)\right].
\]
For \(\nu\ne0\), \(W_\nu\ge1\) and \(s_p(k_\nu)\le k_\nu\),
so every such nonzero count contributes strictly positively.
For the constant, \(W_0=0\) and its assumed valuation makes its
contribution at least \(s_p(k_0)/(p-1)\). Thus every nonzero count
has positive valuation. The contribution from \(k=0\) gives the
claimed binomial residue.

Write \(t=aq+r\) with \(0\le r<a\). By the base-\(p\) binomial
rule, \(\binom{an+t}{t}\equiv\binom{n+q}{q}\pmod p\).
The \(q\)-th forward difference of the latter sequence is one,
so \(q+1\) consecutive residues cannot vanish.
Finally \(q+1\le d_0\), and the original phase has at least
\(d_0\) monomials with nonzero coefficients. Choose the pure
\(a\)-th power of each marked variable in turn, and a fixed
pure power in every other block. The coefficients of those
monomials in \(P_0\) are one and cannot cancel against a
positive-valuation perturbation. This proves the support-size
bound.

The cancellation in (8) uses the same digit-sum identity as
the [one-coordinate prime-power pivot](SFC_DIVIDED_POWER_DOMINANCE_WINDOWS.md#2-a-pivot-that-need-not-be-a-least-monomial).
Here the group sum in (8) allows many marked variables, and the
remaining binomial residue is handled by finite differences.

This cancellation cannot be used twice with the same multinomial.
For example, take \(P_0=(x+y)^p(z+w)^p\), perturb by a nonzero
constant \(h\), and use \((pn)!^2\) as the factorial part of the
reference. The normalized term with one constant equals
\[
 nh\left(\frac{(p(n-1)+1)!}{(pn)!}\right)^2 .
\]
The factorial ratio inside the square has valuation
\(-1-v(n)\), by separating the factor \(pn\) from the remaining
\(p-2\) factors prime to \(p\). Hence the displayed term has
valuation \(v(h)-2-v(n)\), unbounded below even if \(h\) is
arbitrarily small. This concerns termwise suppression for the
two descending blocks at that reference.
