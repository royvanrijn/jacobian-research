# Prime-power Galois cycles separate consecutive root arguments

Let \(P_N\in\mathbb Q[z]\) have degree \(N\), with nonzero constant
and linear coefficients. Suppose its Galois group contains a cycle
on all \(N\) roots.

**Theorem.** If \(N\) is a prime power, no quotient of distinct
roots of \(P_N\) equals a quotient of distinct roots of a rational
polynomial of degree less than \(N\). In particular, ordinary
factorial moments of orders \(N-1,N\) have disjoint root arguments
whenever the upper moment satisfies the cycle hypothesis. The
angular conclusion holds under independent positive rescalings.

The cycle may be certified from a tame primitive full Newton edge,
or from irreducible reduction with the same degree. For a primitive
edge the theorem supplies angular separation in addition to
physical coprimality from irreducibility.

## The stabilizer of a quotient

Write \(\sigma\) for the cycle and \(\alpha\) for an upper root,
so the upper roots are \(\sigma^i\alpha\), \(0\le i<N\).
The following assertion holds for arbitrary \(N\). For
\(0<h<N\), the stabilizer in \(\langle\sigma\rangle\) of
\[
                         R=\frac{\sigma^h\alpha}{\alpha}
\]
has order coprime to \(N/\gcd(N,h)\).

Suppose a prime \(q\) divides both orders. The unique subgroup of
order \(q\) is contained in both the stabilizer and
\(\langle\sigma^h\rangle\). It is generated by
\(\tau=\sigma^{N/q}\). From \(\tau R=R\) and commutation we get
\[
          \sigma^h B=B,\qquad B=\frac{\tau\alpha}{\alpha}.
\]
Thus \(\tau B=B\), and \(\tau^q\alpha=\alpha\) gives \(B^q=1\).
Also \(B\ne1\), since \(\tau\) moves \(\alpha\). For each root
\(\sigma^i\alpha\), let \(B_i=\sigma^i B\). It is a primitive
\(q\)-th root of unity fixed by \(\tau\). The \(\tau\)-orbit of
\(\sigma^i\alpha\) consists of the products of that root with all
\(q\)-th roots of unity. Hence the upper root set is invariant
under multiplication by any \(q\)-th root of unity. Since
\(q\mid N\), the monic upper polynomial then belongs to
\(\mathbb Q[z^q]\), contradicting its nonzero linear coefficient.
This proves the assertion. The same proof works whenever
\(P_N\notin\mathbb Q[z^q]\) for every prime \(q\mid N\).

When \(N=q^a\), the assertion makes the stabilizer trivial for
every \(0<h<N\). Every ordered pair of distinct roots is a
\(\sigma\)-translate of one of these pairs, so its quotient has
orbit length \(N\) under \(\sigma\). Lift \(\sigma\) to an
automorphism of the compositum of the upper and lower splitting
fields. If a lower quotient were equal to the upper quotient,
its orbit length would divide the order of the induced permutation
of the lower roots. A permutation with order divisible by \(q^a\)
has at least one cycle of length divisible by \(q^a\), which
requires at least \(N\) roots. This proves quotient separation.

For rational polynomials two nonreal roots with the same argument
give equal quotients upon dividing each by its complex conjugate.
The even ordinary moment is strictly positive at every real
argument. It is the expectation of an even power of a nonzero
real binomial in independent exponential variables and the
integrand is positive on an open set. This excludes both real
rays and proves the stated angular consequence. A common complex
root is then impossible, and a rational Bézout identity gives
coprimality over every characteristic-zero field.

## Native prime certificates

Suppose the upper Newton polygon at \(p\) is one edge of width
\(N\) and integer rise \(T\), with \(\gcd(T,N)=1\) and \(p\nmid N\).
Use a finite unramified extension \(K/\mathbb Q_p\) containing
the \(N\)-th roots of unity and the roots of the edge binomial
in its residue field. Set \(\pi^N=p\). Substituting
\(z=\pi^{-T}x\) and dividing out the common endpoint valuation
gives a binomial reduction with \(N\) simple nonzero roots.
Indeed no interior coefficient can attain the chord, since its
abscissa would have to be divisible by \(N\). Hensel lifting
puts every upper root in \(K(\pi)\), and the valuation
\(-T/N\) of each root forces its degree over \(K\) to be \(N\).
Thus the generator of the cyclic extension \(K(\pi)/K\) acts
as an \(N\)-cycle. It restricts to an \(N\)-cycle in the rational
Galois group after embedding the rational splitting field into
the local splitting field. This argument allows \(p<N\).

Alternatively, at a prime preserving degree with irreducible
reduction, all upper roots lift over an unramified extension
and residue Frobenius permutes them in one cycle.

To see that the primitive-edge hypothesis occurs for ordinary
shared profiles at small primes, fix any ordinary core, a
prime-power terminal \(N\ge3\), and any \(p\nmid N\). Append
a shared unit-gap row \((\ell,\ell+1)\) chosen so that
\(v_p(N\ell+1)=A\). Arbitrarily large \(\ell\) satisfy this
condition for each \(A\), by a congruence modulo \(p^{A+1}\).
For \(p^A>N\), its stripped contribution at index \(k\ge1\)
has valuation \(A+v_p((k-1)!)\), while its contribution at index
zero has valuation zero. Hence each interior ordinate rises
above its new endpoint chord for sufficiently large \(A\), with
the core fixed. If the core's endpoint rise is \(T_0\), then
choosing \(A\equiv1-T_0-v_p((N-1)!)\pmod N\) makes the new rise
coprime to \(N\). This also allows the added curvature
\(1/(\ell+1)\) to be arbitrarily small.

The prime-power comparison uses the entire cyclic orbit of
each root quotient. The
[upper native-prime logarithm criterion](MULTIVARIATE_NATIVE_PRIME_CHORD_ANGULAR_CERTIFICATE.md)
instead applies to general degrees under an explicit interior
valuation condition. The
[global orbit criterion](MULTIVARIATE_GALOIS_ORBIT_ANGULAR_SEPARATION.md)
uses pair transitivity in general degrees.
