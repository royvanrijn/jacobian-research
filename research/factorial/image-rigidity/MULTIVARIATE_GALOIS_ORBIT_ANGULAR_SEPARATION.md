# Global Galois certificates for ordinary factorial root arguments

Let \(P_N\) and \(P_{N-1}\) be the ordinary moment polynomials of two
distinct exponent columns with arbitrary shared and private rows.
A Galois condition on \(P_N\) alone suffices for separation under
independent positive rescalings.

**Theorem.** For \(N\ge3\), the consecutive root arguments are disjoint
if either of the following holds.

- The Galois group of \(P_N\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is transitive
  on the unordered pairs of its \(N\) roots.
- \(N\) is an odd prime and \(P_N\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).

In the first case it suffices to prove irreducibility of \(P_N\) by
any method and find a prime giving a single delayed factorial mark
as specified below. Thus the proof applies to original profiles
for which no primitive endpoint chord can be certified. If one of
the two conditions holds at every \(3\le N<J\), the
[absolute angular cutoff](MULTIVARIATE_EFFECTIVE_ABSOLUTE_EVENTUAL_ANGLES.md)
gives separation in every degree. The first pair uses strict
positivity of the quadratic on the real line.

## Relations among root quotients

Let \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_N\) be the distinct roots of the
irreducible upper polynomial, \(L\) its splitting field, and
\(G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\). All roots are nonzero.
Let \(V=\{a\in\mathbb Q^N:\sum a_i=0\}\), and put
\[
 \mathcal R=\left\{a\in V:
     \prod_i\alpha_i^{d a_i}\text{ is a root of unity for an integer }
     d>0\text{ with all }d a_i\in\mathbb Z\right\}.
\]
The condition is independent of the positive common denominator:
a power of a nonzero number is a root of unity precisely when the
number itself is a root of unity. Thus \(\mathcal R\) is a
rational \(G\)-invariant linear subspace of \(V\).

Transitivity on unordered pairs implies transitivity on
points for \(N\ge3\). Otherwise a pair inside a nontrivial
point orbit and a pair joining two orbits cannot be in the
same pair orbit. If all point orbits are singletons, pair
transitivity also fails.
Under the unordered-pair hypothesis, \(V\) is irreducible
over \(\mathbb Q\). View the orthogonal projection onto any
invariant rational subspace of \(V\) as an operator on
\(\mathbb Q^N\) vanishing on the constant line. It commutes
with \(G\) and its matrix is symmetric, so the entries
are constant on the diagonal and constant off the diagonal,
by the two transitive actions just noted. It acts as a scalar
on \(V\), so its image is either zero or all of \(V\).
When \(N\) is prime and \(P_N\) is irreducible, transitivity
and Cauchy's theorem give an \(N\)-cycle in \(G\). The
restriction of that cycle to \(V\) has irreducible cyclotomic
characteristic polynomial \(1+x+\cdots+x^{N-1}\). Thus in this
case too \(\mathcal R=0\) or \(\mathcal R=V\).

The latter possibility would make every root quotient a
root of unity. Let \(L_0\) be the subfield generated by all
quotients. It is contained in a cyclotomic field and hence
abelian over \(\mathbb Q\).
Any automorphism of \(L\) fixing \(L_0\) must multiply all
roots by the same scalar. The root sum is a nonzero rational
number, because the upper polynomial has positive coefficients
in degrees \(N,N-1\). Thus the scalar is one and \(L=L_0\).
For \(N\ge4\), an abelian faithful transitive group has
trivial point stabilizers and order \(N\): each stabilizer
fixes every translate of its point. It cannot act transitively
on the \(\binom N2>N\) unordered pairs. For odd \(N\),
a real root exists and complex conjugation is central in the
abelian group \(G\). It fixes all roots by transitivity,
which is impossible for the ordinary odd moment.
This deals with \(N=3\) as well as the odd prime alternative.
In fact for real \(z\) its derivative is the
strictly positive integral of \(N V_0(U_0+zV_0)^{N-1}\), where
\(U_0,V_0\) are the two positive monomials under the product
exponential measure. It therefore has exactly one real root.

We have proved \(\mathcal R=0\) under either hypothesis.
In particular the \(N(N-1)\) ordered quotients of distinct roots
are distinct even as classes modulo roots of unity. If two
were equal as classes, their relation would give a nonzero
vector \(e_i-e_j-e_k+e_l\) in \(\mathcal R\). The vector can
vanish only when the ordered index pairs agree.

## The orbit size excludes all lower quotients

For the unordered-pair hypothesis use the trace
\(t_{ij}=\alpha_i/\alpha_j+\alpha_j/\alpha_i\). If two such
traces coincide, their quotient pairs are both the roots of
\(x^2-t_{ij}x+1\). Hence by the distinctness just proved
the unordered index pairs agree. Each upper trace therefore
has degree \(\binom N2\). A lower trace has at most
\(\binom{N-1}{2}\) conjugates. A shared quotient would give equal upper and lower traces, so the quotient sets are disjoint.

In the odd prime case, an \(N\)-cycle acts freely on the
ordered upper pairs, so every Galois orbit of upper quotients
has size divisible by \(N\). A lower quotient lies in the
lower splitting field of degree dividing \((N-1)!\), so its
degree cannot be divisible by \(N\). Again no quotient of
distinct roots can occur in both degrees.

Equal nonreal arguments would give an identical quotient after
dividing each root by its complex conjugate. Equal real arguments
are excluded by strict positivity of the even ordinary moment
on the real line. This proves the angular conclusions.

## A native delayed factorial mark

Suppose \(p>N\), and strip the scalars \((N\ell_i)!\) from the
upper coefficients, where \(\ell_i\) is the smaller exponent
of the \(i\)-th unequal row. One row has a factorial factor
\(N\ell+h\) of valuation one at an offset
\[
                              d<h\le2d ,
\]
with \(d\) the gap of that row. Require all other sampled factors
\(N\ell_i+h'\), \(1\le h'\le N d_i\), to be units at \(p\).
If the marked row is decreasing, reverse the columns first.
In the resulting upper moment the coefficient valuations at
indices \(0,1\) are zero, and those at \(2,\ldots,N\) are one.
Indeed the marked factor is sampled exactly at indices \(k\ge2\)
and the binomial coefficients are units.

For completeness this gives an \((N-1)\)-cycle in the Galois
group. Write the polynomial as \(\sum_k a_k z^k\).
There is a simple unit root, by lifting the linear reduction.
In the reciprocal polynomial \(x^N P_N(1/x)\) substitute
\(x=\pi y\) with \(\pi^{N-1}=p\), then divide by \(p\).
The reduction now has just the binomial terms at \(0,N-1\),
both units. Over an unramified field \(K\) containing the
residual roots of that binomial and \(\mu_{N-1}\), each of its
\(N-1\) distinct roots lifts in \(K(\pi)\) to a unit \(y\).
Each corresponding \(x\) has valuation \(1/(N-1)\), so it
has degree at least \(N-1\) over \(K\) and generates \(K(\pi)\).
The cyclic tame action on the roots therefore gives one
\((N-1)\)-cycle, fixing the remaining root which is already
in \(K\). A local permutation here is a global Galois
permutation, by restricting the local splitting automorphism
to the splitting field over \(\mathbb Q\).
If the upper polynomial is also irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\),
the global group is transitive. A transitive group containing
an \((N-1)\)-cycle is doubly transitive because the stabilizer
of its fixed point is transitive on all other points.

The delayed mark alone also gives physical coprimality under
every positive rational rescaling between the orders. More
generally, it suffices that the upper Newton polygon contain
an edge of width \(N-1\) whose rise is relatively prime to
\(N-1\), at any prime. Each root on the edge has valuation of
denominator \(N-1\) and hence local degree at least \(N-1\).
One global irreducible upper factor has degree at least that
large. If its degree is \(N\), the upper polynomial is
irreducible and is coprime to the lower one. If its degree
is \(N-1\), a common factor of this degree would make the
lower polynomial divide the upper one. The
[consecutive nondivisibility theorem](MULTIVARIATE_TWO_MONOMIAL_NONDIVISIBILITY.md#an-inequality-using-the-endpoint-coefficients)
rules this out. Its endpoint coefficient ratios are unchanged
by independent positive rescalings, so the argument includes
the stated rational twists. The remaining upper factor is
linear and cannot be common, since an even ordinary moment
has no real zeros. Rational rescaling preserves the local
factor degrees and the Newton edge widths and denominators.

As a simultaneous construction, fix one existing shared row's
gap and orientation and retain all other rows. At each \(N<J\),
take distinct large primes \(p_N,q_N>N\). In that row require
\[
       N\ell+1\equiv p_N\pmod{p_N^2},\qquad
       N\ell+d+1\equiv q_N\pmod{q_N^2}.
\]
The primes can exceed all sampled retained factorial arguments
and \(Nd\), so the first congruence gives a primitive upper chord
and irreducibility by the Newton polygon, and the second is
the delayed mark above. CRT gives an explicit progression of
bases making all these upper groups doubly transitive. This
also applies to a Cartesian product of progressions for all
varying bases by setting the other bases to zero modulo
each selected prime and taking the primes above their sampled
gap bounds. As in the
[single movable row theorem](MULTIVARIATE_SINGLE_MOVABLE_ROW_PADIC_PROGRESSION.md),
the angular assertion is all-degree on the progression.

## An odd-order criterion from a third-index mark

An irreducible upper moment of odd degree \(N\ge5\) is also
covered by a longer delay. Let \(p>N\) and suppose the upper
coefficient valuations are \(0\) at \(k=0,1,2\) and a
positive integer \(E\) for \(3\le k\le N\). Assume
\(\gcd(E,N-2)=1\). For an original profile this is supplied
by a single increasing-row factorial factor with offset
\(2d<h\le3d\), valuation \(E\), and all other sampled factors
units after removal of the row-minimum factorials.
Reverse the columns for a decreasing mark.

In the reciprocal polynomial the long edge has width
\(W=N-2\) and slope of valuation \(E/W\) for the roots.
Just as for the second-index mark, scaling the reciprocal
variable by \(\pi^E\), where \(\pi^W=p\), gives a simple
residual binomial for the \(W\)-root cluster. Over an
unramified base field these roots generate a cyclic tame
extension of degree \(W\), by the coprimality hypothesis.
Lift its generator to an automorphism of the full local
splitting field. It cycles the \(W\) roots and acts on
the remaining two roots by a permutation of order dividing
two. Since \(W\) is odd, its square is a single \(W\)-cycle
fixing the two remaining roots.

A transitive group on odd \(N\) containing a single
\((N-2)\)-cycle is transitive on unordered pairs.
To see this, take any orbit of unordered pairs as the edge
set of a graph. It is regular by point transitivity.
At a fixed point of the long cycle its neighborhood is a union
of the following cycle orbits:
the other fixed point and the \(N-2\) moving points.
Thus its degree is among \(0,1,N-2,N-1\). The degree must be
even for a regular graph on an odd number of vertices,
so a nonempty orbit has degree \(N-1\) and is complete.
The unordered-pair condition of the theorem now applies.

For even \(N\), if the residual unit quadratic is separable,
both unit roots already lift in the unramified field and
the long generator gives a single \((N-2)\)-cycle as well.
The same graph argument shows that either the group is
transitive on unordered pairs or it preserves a perfect matching,
from an orbit of edges of degree \(1\) or the complement of
one of degree \(N-2\). A single odd cycle of length at least
three, fixing the other points, rules out such a matching.
The partners of moving points cannot be fixed, and a
fixed-point-free matching on that one odd cycle is impossible.
Thus another native odd cycle can also certify angular
separation in this even case.

## Density of the Galois certificate on fixed-gap grids

Suppose at least one base varies over an integer interval or a
fixed progression, and the other varying bases are independent.
Keep all gaps, orientations and fixed rows fixed. The two
native marks are available at every \(3\le N<J\) for a
proportion \(1-O(e^{-\sqrt{\log H}/4})\) of the profiles
as the free progression indices range through \(1,\ldots,H\).

Apply the
[native-prime density argument](MULTIVARIATE_NATIVE_PRIME_CHORD_ANGULAR_CERTIFICATE.md#density-among-unmodified-profiles)
to the two affine functions \(N\ell+1\) and \(N\ell+d+1\).
With \(P=\exp(\sqrt{\log H}/4)\), the fraction having no
prime factor above \(P\) is \(O(e^{-c\sqrt{\log H}})\)
for some \(c>1/4\), by the Euler-product count there.
The fraction divisible by \(p^2\) for some \(p>P\) is
\(O(1/P+1/\sqrt H)\). Outside these events choose for each
function a prime \(>P\) dividing it once. For large \(H\)
the two primes are distinct because their common divisors
divide \(d\). They exceed all sampled gaps and fixed
factorial arguments. For each chosen prime the proportion
of independent other bases with a sampled factor divisible
by that prime is \(O(1/P+1/H)\), by counting the individual
affine congruences. The two marks and hence double transitivity
hold at every terminal after a finite union bound.

## An original profile outside the primitive-edge test

Take the ordinary one-row profile \((4,5)\) at upper order \(6\).
After stripping \(24!\) the polynomial is
\[
  1+150z+9750z^2+351000z^3+7371000z^4+
                      85503600z^5+427518000z^6 .
\]
At \(p=13\) it has the delayed mark \(26=6\cdot4+2\) in
the ramp \(25,\ldots,30\), meeting the condition above.
The monic reduction \(f\) modulo \(127\) is
\[
 z^6+51z^5+46z^4-22z^3-50z^2+9z+28.
\]
Irreducibility is certified by
\(z^{127^6}\equiv z\pmod f\) and
\(\gcd(f,z^{127^j}-z)=1\) for \(j=2,3\).
For an exact replay, compute successive \(127\)-th powers
in \(\mathbb F_{127}[z]/(f)\). At \(j=2,3\) the residues are
\[
 \begin{split}
 &-45z^5+54z^4+19z^3+26z^2-31z-22,\\
 &46z^5-25z^4-38z^3-32z^2+23z-51.
 \end{split}
\]
The two Euclidean gcds after subtracting \(z\) are one.
The degree-six group is thus doubly transitive, and the
fifth and sixth factorial moments have disjoint root arguments.
At primes \(p>7\) the only nonunit ramps occur at \(13,29\).
Both marks are delayed, so neither gives a strict primitive
endpoint chord at order six. The Galois condition certifies
this original profile by a different arithmetic constraint.
