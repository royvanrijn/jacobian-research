# Uniform propagation of zero factorial windows for homogeneous phases

Write \(M_j(F)=\Lambda(F^j)\), with
\(\Lambda(X^\alpha)=\prod_i\alpha_i!\).

**Theorem.** For fixed dimension \(m\) and degree \(D\ge0\) there are
integers \(B\ge1\) and \(H\ge0\) with the following properties for every
homogeneous degree-\(D\) phase over any characteristic-zero field.
A block of \(B\) zero factorial moments at any positive start \(n\)
forces \(M_j(F)=0\) for all \(j\ge n\). If the moments are eventually
zero, they are zero at every \(j>H\).

The bounds are independent of the coefficients and the support within
degree \(D\). Thus the same finite window works for all nonzero phases
in any such family where eventual vanishing forces \(F=0\).
This window can exceed the support size.

## The relative period

The case \(m=1\) or \(D=0\) follows from the single-monomial formula.
For \(r=m-1\) write \(x_m=1-\sum_{i=1}^r x_i\),
\(g(x)=F(x_1,\ldots,x_r,x_m)\), and
\(\Delta=\{x\in\mathbb R^r:x_1,\ldots,x_m\ge0\}\).
Successive beta integrals give
\[
 a_j(F)=\frac{M_j(F)}{(Dj+r)!}=\int_\Delta g(x)^j\,dx.
\]
For complex coefficients the compact simplex gives a convergent germ
\[
 I_F(t)=\sum_{j\ge0}a_j(F)t^j
       =\int_\Delta\frac{dx_1\cdots dx_r}{1-tg(x)}
 \quad (|t|\max_\Delta |g|<1).
 \tag{1}
\]
It is a relative period for the pair
\[
 U=\mathbb A^r\setminus V(1-tg),\qquad
 B_\partial=U\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^m\{x_i=0\}
 \tag{2}
\]
over the rational function field in \(t\).
For nonempty \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), write
\(B_J=U\cap\bigcap_{i\in J}\{x_i=0\}\). Every nonempty \(B_J\) is a
smooth open subset of an affine space. The relative de Rham group
is computed by the finite-column total complex of de Rham complexes
of \(U\) and all \(B_J\), with alternating restrictions in the
horizontal direction. This follows from affine de Rham comparison
and the resolution of the constant sheaf of the union of the facets
by the constant sheaves on their intersections. All the individual
schemes are affine.
[Grothendieck, Theorem 1](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1966__29__95_0.pdf)

The derivation \(\theta=t\partial_t\) acts on the coefficients of these
forms, keeping \(x\) and the phase parameters fixed. It commutes with
the differential in \(x\) and with the face restrictions. Because the
coordinate lift \(t\partial_t\) preserves all the facets, its action
on the total complex gives the Gauss–Manin connection. In (1) the
simplex is fixed at small \(t\), so differentiation of the pairing
also follows directly.
[Katz–Oda, Theorem 1 and §2](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~matilde/GaussManinKatzOda.pdf)

The relative connection is regular singular with rational exponents
at \(t=0\) and \(t=\infty\). Indeed, the column spectral sequence is
horizontal and has the ordinary Gauss–Manin connections of \(U\) and
the \(B_J\) on its first page. These are covered by the open local
monodromy theorem for smooth varieties over the function field of
the complex \(t\)-curve. The category of regular-singular connections
with rational exponents is closed under subquotients and extensions
at each place. For a subquotient take intersections and images of
logarithmic lattices. For an extension choose logarithmic bases on
its ends and lift the quotient basis. Multiplying these lifts by a
sufficiently high power of the local parameter makes the off-diagonal
block logarithmic and shifts the quotient residues by integers.
[Deligne's open local monodromy theorem in Katz, Theorem 14.3](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/nilpconn.pdf)

## The ends of the scalar recurrence

Let \(K\) be a finitely generated field of phase parameters over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}\). In the total complex over \(K(t)\) the top form
\(\omega=dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_r/(1-tg)\) with zero face components
represents a relative class. It is nonzero, since after embedding \(K\)
in \(\mathbb C\) an exact representative would have zero period at
generic small \(t\), whereas \(I_F(0)=1/r!\). The de Rham column
cohomologies have finite dimension, so the cyclic differential module
generated by \([\omega]\) has finite dimension. Its first dependence
among \([\omega],\theta[\omega],\ldots\), after clearing denominators
and removing a common power of \(t\), gives
\[
             L=\sum_{k=0}^b t^k P_k(\theta),
             \qquad P_0P_b\ne0,\qquad P_k\in K[T].
 \tag{3}
\]
This is the minimal-order equation for the cyclic class. Its order
can exceed that of the minimal equation for the simplex period.

Both endpoint polynomials \(P_0,P_b\) are scalar multiples of
polynomials in \(\mathbb Q[T]\). Embed \(K\) into \(\mathbb C\).
The explicit affine total complex commutes with extending the
coefficient field from \(K(t)\) to \(\mathbb C(t)\) by flatness and
the unchanged affine equations. Thus the cyclic rank and the first
dependence in (3) survive. The scalar solutions give horizontal
functionals on the cyclic module, whose dual admits a logarithmic
lattice with rational residual eigenvalues by the preceding argument.

For clarity, take a root \(\lambda\) of \(P_0\) with no positive
integer translate among the roots of \(P_0\). There is a formal
solution \(t^\lambda(1+\sum_{k\ge1}u_k t^k)\) of \(L\), since
the successive equations divide by \(P_0(\lambda+k)\).
Its successive \(\theta\)-derivatives specify a horizontal functional
on the basis of cyclic derivatives. In the logarithmic lattice of
the dual its entries, after removing \(t^\lambda\), are Laurent
series. The lowest Laurent coefficient is nonzero and the associated
exponent, \(\lambda\) plus an integer, is a residue eigenvalue up to
sign. Hence \(\lambda\in\mathbb Q\). Every root of \(P_0\) reaches
such a root by a nonnegative integer translation within its coset,
so every root is rational. At infinity use \(u=1/t\) in \(t^{-b}L\)
with \(\theta=-u\partial_u\); the corresponding polynomial is \(P_b(-T)\).
The embedding of \(K\) is injective and fixes \(\mathbb Q\), so the
monic endpoint polynomials already have rational coefficients over
\(K\).

## Specialization and all starting orders

Let phase coefficients range over an irreducible closed subvariety
\(S\) of the coefficient space defined over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\),
with function field \(K\). In the total complex the relation
\(L\omega=d_{\rm tot}\eta\) is witnessed by a finite tuple of rational
forms over \(K(t)\). Their coefficients have polynomial denominators
in \(t\) and the phase parameters, and powers of \(1-tg\) inverted on
the corresponding strata. There is a nonempty open subset of \(S\)
on which all parameter denominators remain nonzero as polynomials
in \(t\). Shrink it also to keep the scalar factors of the two monic
endpoint polynomials defined and nonzero. The relation then
specializes to every phase in this open subset. For generic small
complex \(t\), Stokes' formula on the simplex and its faces gives
\(LI_F=0\). Thus it holds as a germ at \(0\). A shorter equation at
a specialized phase does not affect this identity.

Repeating on the finitely many irreducible components of the closed
remainder, which have smaller dimension, gives a finite cover by
locally closed strata with operators of the form (3). On each
stratum the monic endpoint polynomials are fixed. Let \(b_*\) be the
maximum of the spans \(b\), let \(N_*\ge1\) exceed every nonnegative
integer root of the monic \(P_0\)'s, and let \(H\ge0\) be at least
every nonnegative integer root of the monic \(P_b\)'s. Comparing
coefficients in the specialized equation gives
\[
 P_0(j)a_j=-\sum_{k=1}^b P_k(j-k)a_{j-k},\qquad a_i=0\ (i<0).
 \tag{4}
\]
For \(j\ge N_*\) this determines the next term. A block of
\(B=N_*+b_*+1\) zeros starting at any \(n\ge1\) therefore forces
every subsequent term to vanish. In the other direction, if \(I_F\)
is polynomial of degree \(h\), the coefficient of \(t^{h+b}\) in
\(LI_F\) is \(P_b(h)a_h\), giving \(h\le H\). The nonzero constant
\(a_0\) ensures that the degree exists when the tail vanishes.

For any phase over a characteristic-zero field, the subfield
generated by its finitely many coefficients over \(\mathbb Q\)
embeds in \(\mathbb C\). The polynomial moment equalities therefore
obey the same bounds there.

## Scope for factorial nonvanishing

The propagation statement includes arbitrary homogeneous supports and
singular phases. It gives a uniform window on a family with an
independent proof against eventual vanishing. For example the
[native-order Hasse–Witt formula](FACTORIAL_HASSE_WITT_NATIVE_ORDERS.md)
supplies this input for smooth homogeneous ternary cubics and smooth
homogeneous quaternary quartics.

The proof also applies to all degree-bounded polynomial phase
integrals on a fixed simplex using \(a_j=\int_\Delta g^j\) directly.
Homogeneity is what identifies this compact integral with the actual
factorial moments. The ordinary inhomogeneous propagation problem
and the actual-support length remain open in this argument.

## Effective bounds and a finite test for eventual vanishing

The finite stratification can be constructed without having to compute
a minimal annihilator at every phase. At a generic stratum point
enumerate candidate polynomials \(P_k\) in \(K[T]\) and forms \(\eta\)
in the explicit affine complex. The coefficient field is computable
as the function field of the given algebraic variety over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}\). Equalities of the rational form coefficients,
and membership of the two monic endpoint polynomials in \(\mathbb Q[T]\),
are decidable by polynomial arithmetic. One can equivalently enumerate
the endpoint polynomials in \(\mathbb Q[T]\) separately. The existence
proof guarantees a candidate with both endpoint coefficients nonzero
and \(L\omega=d_{\rm tot}\eta\). From its denominators and endpoint
scalar factors one computes the nonempty open subset where it
specializes. Exact irreducible decomposition on each closed remainder
terminates by dimension.

In particular for fixed \(m,D\), the locus of eventually zero moments
is defined by \(M_{H+1}=\cdots=M_{H+B}=0\). The implication in each
direction follows from the two bounds in the theorem. A projective
ideal calculation therefore decides whether this locus contains a
nonzero homogeneous phase in that dimension and degree. This is a
terminating certificate search, without a complexity or useful
numerical bound asserted here.

## A nonvanishing family in arbitrary dimension

Let \(S=X_1+\cdots+X_m\), let \(L=\sum_i a_iX_i\) be any linear form,
and let \(q\) be a polynomial of degree at most \(D\) so that
\[
                         F=S^D q(L/S)
 \tag{5}
\]
is homogeneous of degree \(D\). If \(F\ne0\), its factorial moments
are nonzero infinitely often. Consequently there is a finite window
for all such \(F\) at once at fixed \(m,D\), including repeated or
complex values among the coefficients \(a_i\).

To prove the new nonvanishing assertion, work over \(\mathbb C\).
On the simplex the phase is \(q(\ell)\), where
\(\ell(x)=\sum_i a_i x_i\). If \(q\) or \(\ell\) is constant on the
simplex the nonzero assertion follows directly. Otherwise set
\(r=m-1\ge1\) and \(\Pi(y)=\prod_i(y-a_i)\).
For a polynomial \(J(y)\), let \(\mathcal D(J)\) be the coefficient
of \(y^r\) in its remainder modulo \(\Pi\).
Then \(\mathcal D(y^{k+r})=h_k(a_1,\ldots,a_m)\), the complete
homogeneous symmetric function. Indeed both sides satisfy the
recurrence with denominator \(\prod_i(1-a_it)\), with first value
one and zero predecessors. For the left side the recurrence follows
from \(\Pi(y)=0\) in the quotient, with those same initial values
since \(\mathcal D(y^j)=0\) for \(0\le j<r\).
Dirichlet integration of the multinomial expansion gives
\[
 \int_\Delta\ell(x)^k\,dx=\frac{k!}{(k+r)!}h_k(a_1,\ldots,a_m).
\]
For \(H_t(y)=(1-tq(y))^{-1}\), let \(J_t\) be its \(r\)-fold primitive
with the first \(r\) initial values zero at \(y=0\). The last two
identities applied coefficientwise in \(t\) show that
\(I_F(t)=\mathcal D(J_t)\).

If the distinct knots are \(u_\nu\) with multiplicities \(m_\nu\),
Hermite interpolation expresses \(\mathcal D(J_t)\) as a fixed
linear combination of \(J_t^{(k)}(u_\nu)\) with \(0\le k<m_\nu\).
This follows from the Chinese remainder decomposition of
\(\mathbb C[y]/(\Pi)\) into the algebras modulo
\((y-u_\nu)^{m_\nu}\). Since the knots are not all the same,
\(k\le r-1\). For small \(t\), each evaluation is the weighted integral
\[
              \int_0^{u_\nu}
                \frac{(u_\nu-y)^{r-k-1}}{(r-k-1)!}
                \frac{dy}{1-tq(y)}.
 \tag{6}
\]
One can use the straight segment for every knot, since for small \(t\)
the integrands are holomorphic on a fixed disk containing the segments.

The continuation of an unweighted interval period by partial
fractions and logarithms is the method of
[Müger and Tuset, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12148).
The polynomial weights in (6) admit the same continuation.

With \(z=1/t\), polynomial division in \(y\) followed by partial
fractions gives a continuation of (6) as a sum of a polynomial
multiple of \(z\) and terms of the form
\[
 \frac{z\,W(v(z))}{q'(v(z))}
       \bigl(\log(u_\nu-v(z))-\log(-v(z))\bigr)
\]
up to a common sign on the partial fractions. Here \(W\) is the
polynomial weight in (6), and \(q(v(z))=z\). The polynomial
part has the factor \(z\) since the leading coefficient of
\(z-q(y)\) in \(y\) is a nonzero constant. Continue from large \(z\)
to a sector at zero avoiding the finite critical values of \(q\)
and the values \(q(0),q(u_\nu)\). Each inverse branch there tends
to a root of \(q\). If that root has multiplicity \(h\), then
\(z/q'(v(z))=O(|z|^{1/h})\), by local inversion in \(z^{1/h}\).
Either logarithm grows at most logarithmically, including when
the limit root is an endpoint. Hence every term tends to zero.
If \(I_F(t)\) were polynomial in \(t\), its nonzero value
\(I_F(0)=1/r!\) would make this continuation a nonzero polynomial
in \(1/z\), which cannot tend to zero as \(z\to0\). This contradiction
proves absence of eventual vanishing. For \(m=2\) every homogeneous
phase has the representation (5). The
[explicit binary bound](BINARY_HOMOGENEOUS_UNIFORM_WINDOW.md) gives a
numerical width in that dimension.
