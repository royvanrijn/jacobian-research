# Half-valuation quadratic face forcing: an integral divided-power pattern

Let L/Q_2 be a finite extension, O its valuation ring, and v(2)=1.
Assume z in L has v(z)=1/2 and z^2/2 is congruent to1 modulo2O.
Put B=1/z and lambda=2B; thus v(lambda)=1/2,
lambda^2 is zero in O/2O, and 2B^2 is congruent to1 modulo2O.

For
t_m(r,z)=Lambda((r x^2+x+z)^m)+m Lambda((r x^2+x+z)^(m-1))
define
Q_m(A)=t_m(zA,z)/z^m, P_m(V)=2^s2(m) Q_m(V/4).
Divide P_m by its leading unit to make it monic, and take its reciprocal
F_m(Z). Write eta_j(Z)=2^j Z^j/j! and F_m=sum b_j eta_j.

**Lemma 1 (native coefficient pattern).** Every b_j belongs to O.
Modulo2O, its exact pattern is

| m parity | b_(4h) | b_(4h+1) | b_(4h+2) | b_(4h+3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| odd | 1 | 1+lambda | 0 | 1 |
| even | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

Only indices0<=j<=m are included.

**Lemma 2 (higher-factor inheritance).** If m is1 or2 modulo4,
F_m factors, up to units, into a factor of each binary degree e>=4
occurring in m, and one final factor of degree epsilon=m mod4.
The normalized ordinary reciprocals g_e of the higher factors are
monic, have constant coefficient valuation1, and all their roots have
valuation1/e. Write g_e(V)=sum a_j V^j, a_e=1. Then
v(a_j)>=s_2(e-j) for j<e. In addition:

* If m=1 modulo4, v(a_j)>=s_2(e-j)+1 for j=2 or3 modulo4.
* If m=2 modulo4, v(a_j)>=s_2(e-j)+1 for every j not0 modulo4.

The final degree-one factor for epsilon1 has root valuation1.
The final degree-two factor for epsilon2 has both root valuations1/2.

These are native coefficient/factor statements. They do not yet exclude
three consecutive t zeros: the coefficients lie in a ramified field,
and the derivative cancellation issue in Section4 must be handled
separately.

## 1. Exact combined coefficient and its valuation

Put n=m-i. Relative to the coefficient binom(m,i)(2i)! of A^i
in the constant-plus-square moment, the coefficient of Q_m is
U_i(B)=1+sum_(l=1)^n 2(i+l)binom(n,l)(2i+1)_(l-1) B^l,            (1)
where the subscript denotes a rising factorial.

Indeed the first moment contributes binom(n,l)(2i+1)_l B^l.
The added mB moment contributes
n binom(n-1,l-1)(2i+1)_(l-1)B^l.
Since n binom(n-1,l-1)=l binom(n,l), their sum is (1).

Every term of (1) is integral. The l1 term has valuation at least1/2.
For l=2h>=6, the rising product contains the even factors
2(i+1),...,2(i+h-1); their valuation is at least
h-1+v((h-1)!). Including 2B^(2h) gives valuation at least
v((h-1)!)>=1.
For l=2h+1>=5, the analogous bound is
1/2+v(h!)>=3/2.
For l4 the valuation is
v(i+4)+v(i+1)+v(binom(n,4))>=1,
because one of i+4,i+1 is even. Thus only l1,l2,l3 survive modulo2O.

Put U=2B^2, so U is congruent to1 modulo2O. Those three terms give
U_i(B) congruent to
1+i binom(n,2)
 +lambda[(i+1)n+(i+3)(i+1)binom(n,3)] modulo2O.                   (2)

The reciprocal divided-power coefficient b_j, with i=m-j,
is U_i(B) times the odd rational unit
1/[(2m-1)(2m-3)...(2m-2j+1)].                                   (3)
This unit is1 modulo2O. Formula (2), Lucas parity for binom(n,2)
and binom(n,3), and the parity of i prove the table in Lemma1.
No coefficient is asserted to be a unit when its table entry is0.

## 2. Finite-degree splitting over O, including its ramification

The usual finite-degree divided-power Hensel split works over the
complete ring O with the ideal2O; it does not require O/2O to be a field.

For e the highest binary degree and m=e+S, S<e, reduction modulo2O
gives a truncated low divided-power algebra A_e and
eta_e eta_j=eta_(e+j) for j<e.
All low products that cross degree e vanish, by the binary carry in
the multiplication coefficient. Write
Fbar=F_lo+eta_e F_hi.

The constant of F_hi is b_e=1 and its leading coefficient b_m is a unit.
Choose Ubar=F_hi and Gbar=eta_e+F_lo/Ubar.
The constant of Gbar is1. Its leading divided-power coefficient is1.
The fixed-degree correction map has high component V and low component
D Ubar+G_lo V. It is invertible:
V=E_hi, D=(E_lo-G_lo V)/Ubar.
Lifting modulo successive powers of2 converges in fixed finite O-lattices,
with degrees e and S fixed and top coefficient of G fixed.
Thus this argument also applies to the nonreduced ring O/2O.
The residual U retains the same table as F_hi at its degree S.

## 3. The exact highest-bit quotient and inherited extra powers

For e>=4 put t=e/4 and write S=4q+epsilon, epsilon in{1,2}.
In A_e, put X=eta_1, Y=eta_2, XY=eta_3, and
H_q=sum_(j=0)^q eta_(4j), H_full=sum_(j=0)^(t-1) eta_(4j).
The algebra generated by the higher binary bits contains only indices
divisible by4. Both X and Y square to zero modulo2O.

If m=1 modulo4, set
F=1+(1+lambda)X+XY, L=1+(1+lambda)X.
The table gives
F_lo=F H_full,
F_hi=F H_(q-1)+L eta_(4q),
where H_(-1)=0. Since F^(-1)=F and L/F=1+XY,
F_lo/F_hi=H_full/[H_q+XY eta_(4q)].
Its coefficients with indices1 or2 modulo4 vanish modulo2O:
the quotient involves only higher-bit indices and those indices plus3.

If m=2 modulo4, set F=1+X+Y. Then
F_lo=F H_full, F_hi=F H_q,
so F_lo/F_hi=H_full/H_q has only indices divisible by4.

The residual degree S has the same corresponding table. Repeating the
split therefore proves the stated pattern for every higher factor.
A divided-power coefficient at index e-j contributes a unit multiple of
2^(e-j)/(e-j)! to a_j. This proves the base bound s_2(e-j) and the extra
1 in Lemma2. The constant has valuation s_2(e)=1, so each g_e has its
single Newton slope and root valuation1/e.
The final degrees1,2 have respectively the tables
1+(1+lambda)eta_1 and 1+eta_1+eta_2, which give the stated valuations.

## 4. How this enters, and what is still missing

For any positive integer m with v(m(m+1))=1, a positive-valuation
root z of
Pi_m(z)=2z^3+(6m+3)z^2+6m(m+1)z+m(m+1)(2m+1)=0
has v(z)=1/2. Put E=z^2+m(m+1)/3. The exact identity
[3(2m+1)+2z]E=-(16/3)z m(m+1)
gives v(E)=11/2. In particular z^2/2 is congruent to1 modulo2O,
so Lemmas1--2 apply to the actual half-valuation branch.

For an odd index p=1 modulo4, the relevant derivative calculation
in the triple-zero problem asks whether a higher factor can have
v(g_e'(beta))=s_2(S)+2-(S+2)/e, S=p mod e.                        (4)
Over Q_2, integer coefficient valuations would give one derivative
summand at each fractional part and force an index contradiction.
Here coefficient valuations belong to (1/2)Z. Terms at indices j
and j+e/2 can have the same valuation. Their cancellation may raise
valuation by increments finer than1/2, through the algebraic root beta.

Consequently merely listing the two indices with the desired fractional
part modulo1/2 is not a complete proof of impossibility in (4).
One must control lower-valued pair cancellations as well, or prove a
stronger conjugate-coefficient/local-different statement. The inheritance
bounds proved here do not authorize ignoring this issue.
This paragraph identifies a proof gap in that proposed inference;
it does not assert that an admissible factor actually attains (4).

The coefficient and whole-factor statements above are all-range. They do
not supply the monic ordinary Anti-coefficient gate required in the separate
[conditional closure proof](QUADRATIC_HALF_VALUATION_MONIC_ANTI_GATE_CONDITIONAL_CLOSURE.md).
Bounded native controls are supplementary and are not universal premises.
