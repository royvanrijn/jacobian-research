# Factorial windows from simple logarithmic critical points

Let \(F\) be a polynomial of total degree \(D\ge2\) in \(m\)
variables over a characteristic-zero field, and let \(H\) be its
top homogeneous part. Use the product Gamma factorial functional
\(\Lambda(X^\beta)=\prod_i(\alpha_i)_{\beta_i}\) with fixed positive
rational shapes \(\alpha_i\). Ordinary factorial is \(\alpha_i=1\).
Suppose that the following two conditions hold over an algebraic closure.

1. The homogeneous forms \(x_i\partial_i H\), \(1\le i\le m\),
   have no common nonzero zero.
2. Every common zero of \(G_i=x_i\partial_i F\) is simple, in
   the sense that the \(m\)-by-\(m\) Jacobian of the \(G_i\) is invertible.

Put \(R=D^m\) and \(W(a,b)=a+b\,a(a+3)/2\).
For every \(k\ge1\), every positive window of
\[
                            \left\lceil W(kR,m+1)/k\right\rceil
 \tag{1}
\]
factorial moments of \(F^k\) contains a nonzero moment. For \(F\)
itself, an eventually zero arithmetic progression of moment
orders is identically zero on that progression. These conclusions
hold in every dimension and degree satisfying the two tests.
The bounds can exceed the support size.

The first condition is a test for the chosen coordinate
compactification and the second is a finite critical-point test.
The hypotheses are satisfied for generic degree-\(D\) polynomials
in the full coefficient space. For example
\(F=\sum_i(x_i^D-x_i)\) has logarithmic top system with only the origin as a common zero, and
each coordinate of its finite logarithmic critical system has
simple roots. Openness follows from the first condition
excluding points at infinity in the homogenized critical system,
and then the second excluding the projective image of the
critical Jacobian degeneracy locus.

## A finite system from weighted integration by parts

Over \(\mathbb C\) let
\(I_\phi(s)=\sum_{n\ge0}\Lambda(\phi F^n)s^n/n!\).
For \(u=1/s\), recurrence of the Gamma weights gives
\[
    I_{G_i a}=u I_{\, (x_i-\alpha_i)a-x_i\partial_i a}
 \tag{2}
\]
for each polynomial amplitude \(a\). This holds coefficientwise,
with no analytical assumptions on the series.

Choose homogeneous polynomial representatives \(V\) of a
graded basis of the quotient by the top forms
\(P_i=x_i\partial_i H\). Since the \(P_i\) are a homogeneous
regular sequence of \(m\) forms of degree \(D\), the quotient
has Hilbert series
\((1+t+\cdots+t^{D-1})^m\). In particular its dimension is
\(R\) and the largest basis degree is \(L=m(D-1)\).

Division by the perturbed generators \(G_i\) gives
\[
                  \psi=v+\sum_i G_i a_i,\qquad v\in V ,
 \tag{3}
\]
with \(\deg a_i\le \deg\psi-D\), and \(v\) uniquely determined.
For existence, divide the top homogeneous part by the \(P_i\)
and reduce the degree successively using \(G_i\).
For uniqueness, suppose a nonzero \(v\in V\) is in the perturbed
ideal. In a representation \(\sum G_i a_i\), if the highest
degree of the summands exceeds \(\deg v\), the leading parts of
the \(a_i\) give a homogeneous syzygy on the \(P_i\).
A regular sequence has its first syzygies generated by the
Koszul relations. Subtract the corresponding relations of the
\(G_i\) from this representation. Their leading parts remove
the highest degree of the \(a_i\); repeat until the largest
summand degree is at most \(\deg v\). Then the leading part
of \(v\) belongs to the top ideal, contrary to the graded
choice of \(V\). This also proves that (3) represents the
full logarithmic critical algebra.

In differentiating \(I_\phi\) for \(\phi\in V\), first divide
\(\psi=F\phi\) by (3). Applying (2) to its ideal term costs
one factor \(u\) and reduces the degree of the remaining
amplitude by at least \(D-1\). Repeat until the degree is
less than \(D\). Starting with degree at most \(L+D\) requires
at most \(m+1\) steps, because the bound after \(m+1\) is one.
This gives a finite rational system \(Y'=A(s)Y\) with entries
polynomial in \(u\) of degree at most \(m+1\).
Its constant matrix \(A_0\) acts on the amplitudes as
multiplication by \(F\) in the full logarithmic critical algebra.
The description of a leading multiplication matrix is part of the
Brieskorn lattice construction on the complex torus. Here (2) uses
the factorial functional on the affine coordinate space, so the
calculation below includes the critical points on the coordinate
boundary as well.
[Douai–Sabbah, §2.c and Proposition 2.13](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/aif.1974.pdf)

## Positive exponents at every simple critical point

The simplicity hypothesis makes the algebra in (3) a
product of copies of \(\mathbb C\), indexed by its \(R\) points.
In the basis of evaluations its leading matrix is diagonal, with
actions the values \(F(p)\). In the action block with value \(v\)
an amplitude \(\phi\in V\) satisfies \([F\phi]=v[\phi]\).
By the unique representative in (3), choose
\[
                  (F-v)\phi=\sum_i G_i a_i .
 \tag{4}
\]
The next coefficient, projected within this action block,
is obtained by evaluating
\(\sum_i ((x_i-\alpha_i)a_i-x_i\partial_i a_i)\)
at its points. This follows from one application of (2):
further corrections in reducing that amplitude cost a second
factor \(u\).

Fix one such point \(p\). Write \(A=\{i:p_i\ne0\}\)
and \(Z=\{i:p_i=0\}\). For \(i\in Z\) the \(i\)-th row
of the critical Jacobian has just the entry
\(\partial_iF(p)\) on its diagonal. Simplicity implies that
all these entries are nonzero and that the Hessian
\(B=(\partial_i\partial_jF(p))_{i,j\in A}\) on the active
coordinate face is nonsingular. Indeed the remaining active
block of the Jacobian is \(\operatorname{diag}(p_i) B\),
since the active first derivatives vanish at \(p\).
Taking linear terms of (4) in the active coordinates gives
\(a_i(p)=0\) for \(i\in A\).
Taking the other linear terms then gives
\(a_i(p)=\phi(p)\) for \(i\in Z\).

Restrict (4) to the active face and compare its quadratic
terms about \(p\). If \(L_A\) is the matrix of first
derivatives \((\partial_j a_i(p))_{i,j\in A}\) and
\(P=\operatorname{diag}(p_i)_{i\in A}\), this gives
\[
                 B P L_A+L_A^{\mathsf T}P B=\phi(p)B .
\]
Multiplying by \(B^{-1}\) and taking traces yields
\(\operatorname{tr}(P L_A)=|A|\phi(p)/2\).
The residue contribution evaluated at \(p\) is therefore
\[
 \sum_i \bigl((p_i-\alpha_i)a_i(p)-p_i\partial_i a_i(p)\bigr)
             =-\left(\sum_{i\in Z}\alpha_i+\frac{|A|}{2}\right)\phi(p).
 \tag{5}
\]
For an empty active set use the two empty matrix terms and
trace zero. Formula (5) does not depend on which division
coefficients in (4) are chosen. It shows that every diagonal
action block has strictly negative rational residue
eigenvalues in the \(s\)-equation, even when several critical
points have the same value.

Apply the formal block and scalar-output argument of the
[separated polynomial theorem](FACTORIAL_SEPARATED_POLYNOMIAL_POWER_WINDOWS.md).
Each formal fundamental output column has positive order in
\(u\) after removing its exponential action. The same holds
for the direct sum representing the root-of-unity filter.
For the filter retaining multiples of \(k\), the dimension is
at most \(kR\), the common denominator is \(s^{m+1}\), and
the output has constant coefficient one. The scalar width
bound and truncation argument give (1). A general residue
class uses the weighted filter and the absence of a nonzero
polynomial solution. The finite coefficient field embeds
into \(\mathbb C\), preserving the hypotheses and conclusions.
