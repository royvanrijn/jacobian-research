# Exact zero patterns for cyclic monomial blocks

## Scope

This is an all-order closure for an unbounded family of ordinary
factorial polynomials, with arbitrary monomial degrees, numbers of
variables, and numbers of terms. The coefficients have a specified
root-of-unity symmetry, and the constituent monomials use disjoint
variable blocks. Those restrictions are essential to the argument;
this note does not establish unrestricted Strong Factorial.

Let `K` be any characteristic-zero field, let `r>=1`, and let the ordinary factorial
functional be `Lambda(X^alpha)=prod_i alpha_i!`. For each `ell=1,...,r`,
choose a nonconstant monomial template `M_ell`, an integer `q_ell>=1`,
a primitive `q_ell`-th root of unity `zeta_ell` in `K`, and a positive
rational number `rho_ell`. Make `q_ell` copies `M_(ell,j)` of the
template, using pairwise disjoint variables across **all** copies and
templates. For `lambda in K^*`, put

\[
 F=\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^r\rho_\ell
             \sum_{j=0}^{q_\ell-1}\zeta_\ell^j M_{\ell,j},
 \qquad S=\langle q_1,\ldots,q_r\rangle\subseteq\mathbb N.
 \tag{1}
\]

Here `S` is the additive semigroup containing zero generated by the
listed integers. The positivity of the rational `rho_ell` concerns
explicit rational coefficients in a formal identity; the field `K`
and the polynomial `F` are not assumed ordered or real.

**Theorem.** For every integer `n>=0`,

\[
              \Lambda(F^n)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\notin S.
 \tag{2}
\]

If `n in S`, then `Lambda(F^n)/lambda^n` is a strictly positive
rational number. In particular, every window of `min q_ell`
consecutive moment orders contains a nonzero moment. The ordinary
support of `F` has exactly `sum q_ell` terms, so this proves its
Strong Factorial assertion, at every starting order.

The statement remains true over a field not containing the displayed
roots whenever (1) is interpreted after a field extension and its
polynomial descends to that field. Nonzero rational constants stay
nonzero under every characteristic-zero extension.

## 1. A formal Kaluza lemma

Suppose `a_0=1` and all `a_n` are positive rationals, with
`a_(n+1)/a_n` nondecreasing. In the formal series ring define

\[
 \phi(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nt^n,\qquad
 1-\phi(t)^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge1}u_nt^n=:U(t).
\]

Then `u_n>=0` for every `n`, and all coefficients of `log phi` are
strictly positive. Here is a coefficient proof that does not assume a
positive analytic radius of convergence. Write `r_n=a_(n+1)/a_n`.
The identity `phi U=phi-1` gives `u_1=a_1>0` and, for `n>=2`,

\[
 u_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u_i a_{n-1-i}
                         (r_{n-1}-r_{n-1-i})\ge0.
 \tag{3}
\]

For (3), use the convolution identity for `a_(n-1)`, multiply by
`r_(n-1)`, and subtract the terms defining `u_n`. Every difference is
nonnegative, so induction applies. Since

\[
 \log\phi=-\log(1-U)=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{U^k}{k},
\]

the coefficient `b_n=[t^n]log phi` satisfies the explicit bound

\[
                         b_n\ge a_1^n/n>0.
 \tag{4}
\]

These operations are formal: a coefficient of a fixed degree receives
only finitely many contributions. No analytic convergence is needed.

### Literature and overlap

This is the coefficient-level version of Kaluza's reciprocal sign
criterion. The precise analytic statement is Theorem 1.3 of
Baricz, Vesti and Vuorinen, *On Kaluza's sign criterion for reciprocal
power series*, arXiv:1010.5337v3 (2011): positive log-convex
coefficients imply nonpositive nonconstant reciprocal coefficients,
under a positive convergence-radius hypothesis. The statement and
its hypotheses were inspected in the
[primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1010.5337v3).
Our factorial series can have radius zero, which is why (3) supplies
the needed formal proof instead of silently importing that analytic
hypothesis. No novelty claim is made for this elementary consequence.

## 2. Monomial factorial coefficients meet the hypothesis

For a nonconstant monomial `M=prod_i X_i^(d_i)`, with `d_i>=0`, set

\[
                  a_n=\frac{\Lambda(M^n)}{n!}
                      =\frac{\prod_i(d_in)!}{n!}.
 \tag{5}
\]

Choose any `i_0` with `d_(i_0)>0`. The exact ratio is

\[
 \frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}
 =\frac{\prod_i\prod_{j=1}^{d_i}(d_in+j)}{n+1}.
 \tag{6}
\]

Cancel the factor `d_(i_0)(n+1)` in the numerator. The remaining
expression is the positive constant `d_(i_0)` times a product of
positive nondecreasing affine functions of `n`. Hence (5) is
log-convex, including the degree-one case where the ratio is constant.
By (4), every coefficient of its formal logarithm is positive rational.

## 3. Root-of-unity filtering gives the entire zero set

Disjoint variables make the factorial functional multiplicative.
The multinomial theorem gives the exact formal exponential generating
function

\[
 \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{\Lambda(F^n)}{n!\lambda^n}t^n
   =\prod_{\ell=1}^r\prod_{j=0}^{q_\ell-1}
                  \phi_\ell(\rho_\ell\zeta_\ell^j t).
 \tag{7}
\]

Write `log phi_ell=sum_(m>=1) b_(ell,m)t^m`, where all `b_(ell,m)>0`
by Section 2. The root-of-unity sum is `q_ell` at multiples of
`q_ell` and zero otherwise. Thus (7) equals

\[
 \exp\!\left(
   \sum_{\ell=1}^r q_\ell
     \sum_{k\ge1}b_{\ell,q_\ell k}
                   \rho_\ell^{q_\ell k}t^{q_\ell k}
       \right).
 \tag{8}
\]

All coefficients inside the exponential are positive at the displayed
degrees. Expansion of the exponential has a positive rational
coefficient exactly at the sums of these degrees, namely the elements
of `S`. No cancellation is possible in this rational expression.
This proves (2) over `Q(zeta_1,...,zeta_r)` and then over `K`.

For a single cycle of length `q`, the exact condition is simply
`q | n`. Its nonzero moments even satisfy

\[
 \frac{\Lambda(F^{qk})}{(qk)!\lambda^{qk}}
       \ge \frac{(a_1\rho)^{qk}}{k}\quad(k\ge1),
 \tag{9}
\]

because the coefficient of the logarithm alone has that lower bound.

## 4. Powers, products, and monomial multipliers

For every integer `m>=1`, (2) gives

\[
                 \Lambda((F^m)^n)=0\iff mn\notin S.
 \tag{10}
\]

Set `q_* = min q_ell`. Each multiple of `q_*/gcd(q_*,m)` has
`mn in S`, so every window of that length contains a nonzero moment
of `F^m`. With `L=sum q_ell`, disjoint nonconstant monomials are
multiplicatively independent: two distinct exponent tuples give
different monomials. All multinomial coefficients in `F^m` are
nonzero in characteristic zero, hence its support has exactly
`binom(L+m-1,m)` terms. The proved window is no longer than that
support size, as required for SFC.

Multiplying by a nonzero monomial in additional disjoint variables
multiplies each moment by a nonzero factorial scalar and changes
neither the zero set nor the support size.

More generally, let `d>=1` and let `F_1,...,F_d` be independent polynomials of form
(1), on pairwise disjoint variables, and take positive integer powers
`m_i`. The moment of `prod_i F_i^(m_i)` is nonzero exactly when
`m_i n in S_i` for every `i`. It is nonzero at all multiples of

\[
 L_0=\operatorname{lcm}_i
       \left(q_{*,i}/\gcd(q_{*,i},m_i)\right).
\]

The support size is `prod_i binom(L_i+m_i-1,m_i)`, which is at least
`prod_i q_(\*,i)/gcd(q_(\*,i),m_i)` and therefore at least `L_0`.
This proves SFC for these products and for their disjoint monomial
multipliers as well. General product preservation of SFC is not used.

## 5. Boundaries and failed shortcuts

The proof does not establish the same window for arbitrary coefficient
choices or for monomials sharing variables. In particular, positivity
of a formal series alone is insufficient: `phi(t)=exp(t)` has positive
coefficients, but `prod_(j=0)^(q-1) phi(zeta^j t)=1` for every `q>=2`.
Every positive coefficient then vanishes after cyclic filtering. Its
coefficient sequence `1/n!` fails the required log-convexity. This is
a countercontrol to the generalized-kernel shortcut, not an ordinary
factorial counterexample.

A separate possible route would prove consecutive coprimality of
`sum_(k=0)^n a_k a_(n-k) z^k` for all positive log-convex sequences.
Decreasing self-reciprocal coefficients localize its roots on the unit
circle, but that observation alone does not separate the roots for
different `n`. Numerical searches and optimization are exploratory;
no general interlacing theorem is imported or claimed here.

## 6. Exact verification

`verify_sfc_cyclic_monomial_blocks.py` checks the two coefficient
recurrences for (3)–(4), performs direct cyclic products in exact
cyclotomic quotient rings, and compares their vanishing coefficients
with the semigroup criterion. It checks powers and independent
products, and includes the exponential countercontrol. The recorded
finite cases test these identities; the all-order result is the proof
in Sections 1–4. No floating-point root locations are proof inputs.
