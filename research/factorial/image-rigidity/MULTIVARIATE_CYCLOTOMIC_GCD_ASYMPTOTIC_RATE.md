# The asymptotic gcd of the two cyclotomic pair products

Fix integral polynomials \(F,G\) of positive degrees with
nonzero constant coefficients. They can be the original
ordinary adjacent factorial parents. For each odd prime \(q\)
use the integral self products
\[
 I_q(F)=a^{(q-1)(m-1)}
              \prod_{i<j}\Psi_q(\alpha_i,\alpha_j),\qquad
 \Psi_q(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}x^{q-1-k}y^k,
\]
where \(a\) is the leading coefficient of \(F\),
\(m=\deg F\), and \(\alpha_i\) are its roots counted
with multiplicity. Define \(I_q(G)\) likewise. Let \(S\)
contain the primes of both leading and constant coefficients.
For an integer \(z\ne0\) let \(|z|_{S^c}\) be the part of
\(|z|\) supported outside \(S\).

The limit of \(q^{-1}\log|\gcd(I_q(F),I_q(G))|_{S^c}\)
along primes has an exact formula in terms of the two root
quotient multisets. It gives a global height bound for a
common factor and a criterion reducing all possible common
factors to rational binomial factors. It also identifies when
the same exponential growth can arise from different quotient
directions.

## The limit and its height consequence

Take one quotient \(\alpha_i/\alpha_j\) from every unordered
root pair of each parent. Discard roots of unity from the two
lists. Partition the remaining quotients into classes \(C\)
under multiplicative dependence modulo roots of unity, meaning
that nonzero integral powers agree up to a root of unity.
The subgroup of each class generated by its quotients is
cyclic modulo torsion. Choose a lift \(t_C\) of a generator.
Replace a quotient by its inverse as necessary to put it
in the form \(\zeta t_C^a\) with \(a>0\) and \(\zeta\) a root
of unity. For a root of unity \(\xi\), let \(m_F(C,\xi)\)
count the quotients of \(F\) in this class satisfying
\(\xi^a=\zeta\), with their original multiplicities. Count
\(m_G(C,\xi)\) the same way. Only finitely many \(\xi\)
occur, since they solve finitely many fixed power equations.

Then
\[
 \lim_{\substack{q\to\infty\\q\ {\rm prime}}}
   \frac1q\log|\gcd(I_q(F),I_q(G))|_{S^c}
   =\sum_C h(t_C)\sum_\xi
                \min\bigl(m_F(C,\xi),m_G(C,\xi)\bigr)=:\mathcal L .
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Here \(h\) is the absolute logarithmic Weil height.
The limit does not change on adding any fixed finite
set of primes to \(S\).

In particular, if \(H\) is any rational common factor
with roots \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_d\), then
\[
                       \sum_{i<j}
                          h(\lambda_i/\lambda_j)\le\mathcal L .
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
The root pair multiset of \(H\) occurs in each parent.
A quotient \(\zeta t_C^a\) contributes to exactly \(a\)
of its labels, and \(h(\zeta t_C^a)=a h(t_C)\).
This proves (2), accounting also for repeated roots.

For an irreducible common quadratic with nonreal roots, let
\(D\) be the reduced denominator of its reciprocal trace.
The norm-one direction \(\gamma=\lambda_1/\lambda_2\)
has both Archimedean conjugates of modulus one. Its negative
valuations occur at split primes and their contribution to
the height is \((\log D)/2\).
Thus (2) gives \(\log D\le2\mathcal L\).
If \(\mathcal L=0\), every ratio in every common factor is
a root of unity. An irreducible common factor of degree
at most \(d_0\) then divides \(z^L-c\) with \(c\in\mathbb Q^\times\)
and \(L\) depending only on \(d_0\). This is the
[binomial branch of the fresh-prime reduction](MULTIVARIATE_HIGHER_DEGREE_FRESH_QUOTIENT_PRIMES.md#reduction-to-rational-binomial-factors).

## Decomposition at finite places

Work in a fixed number field containing all the roots and all
the finitely many torsion labels. Outside primes of the
endpoints, all parent roots are units. The identity
\(\Psi_q(x,y)=y^{q-1}\Phi_q(x/y)\) shows that the self
product has the same valuations there as its product of
\(\Phi_q\) over the quotient list. Inverting any quotient
does not change these valuations by reciprocity.
For all sufficiently large primes \(q\), and each fixed
exponent \(a\) in the list, the identity
\[
               \Phi_q(\zeta t^a)=
                       \prod_{\xi^a=\zeta}\Phi_q(\xi t)        \tag{3}
\]
follows by factoring \(Z^a-1\) in numerator and denominator
of the geometric sum. The \(q\)-th power map permutes
the \(a\) choices in the numerator since \(q\nmid a\).
Thus the local exponent contributed by the label \((C,\xi)\)
is \(m_F(C,\xi)\) times the valuation of \(\Phi_q(\xi t_C)\)
for \(F\), and the corresponding multiple for \(G\).

At any fixed finite place where a quotient is a unit,
\(\Phi_q\) of that quotient is a unit for all sufficiently
large primes \(q\). Indeed the residue field has fixed size,
so it cannot contain a nontrivial \(q\)-th root of unity;
if the residue is one, the value reduces to \(q\), a unit
outside the index prime. This also applies to all torsion
quotients. At all other places outside a fixed finite set,
torsion orders reduce faithfully and a torsion quotient
again gives no contribution for \(q\) exceeding its order.
We may therefore discard fixed primes dividing the orders
of the torsion labels without changing the limit.

For the remaining finite places of residue characteristic
\(p\ne q\), two different labels of the same class cannot
both contribute a positive valuation. Otherwise their two
reductions \(\xi t_C\) would both have order \(q\),
and their ratio would be a nontrivial root of unity of
fixed order prime to \(q\), which is impossible. At the
index prime \(q\), for large \(q\) each nonzero
\(v_{\mathfrak q}(\Phi_q(\xi t_C))\) equals
\(v_{\mathfrak q}(q)\). In fact
\(\Phi_q(X)\equiv(X-1)^{q-1}\bmod q\) forces residue
one. Expanding \((1+s)^q-1\) then gives the first
term \(qs\) as unique term of least valuation, since
\((q-1)v_{\mathfrak q}(s)>v_{\mathfrak q}(q)\) for
\(q-1\) exceeding the fixed field degree.
The index contribution to the logarithmic gcd is
therefore \(O(\log q)\).

For labels \(x=\xi t_C\) and \(y=\xi' t_{C'}\) in
different classes, the numbers \(x,y\) are
multiplicatively independent. All their powers lie
in a fixed finitely generated group. The normalized
sum of \(\min(v_{\mathfrak p}(x^q-1),
v_{\mathfrak p}(y^q-1))\log N\mathfrak p\) over
places outside the fixed support is \(o(q)\) by
[Corvaja–Zannier, Corollary 1 and inequality (1.3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311030).
Hence the corresponding sum for \(\Phi_q(x),\Phi_q(y)\)
is \(o(q)\) as well. Restricting a nonnegative
sum to untracked places only decreases it.

At each good finite place the exponent in the gcd of
the two integer self products is the smaller of the two sums of
label contributions. It is bounded below by the sum of
the minima for matching labels. It is bounded above
by the sum of the minima for all cross label pairs,
using \(\min(\sum_i a_i,\sum_j b_j)\le
\sum_{i,j}\min(a_i,b_j)\) for nonnegative entries.
The off-diagonal terms sum to \(o(q)\): different
labels in the same class do not meet at a good place,
and different classes satisfy the preceding bound.
Thus the matching label terms account for the limit.

For fixed nontorsion \(x=\xi t_C\), its cyclotomic
value contributes \(q h(x)+O(\log q)\) outside the
fixed support, after normalizing by the field degree.
Indeed the remainder on each Archimedean embedding
in
\[
          \log|\Phi_q(x)|=(q-1)\log^+|x|+O(\log q)
\]
is bounded by complex logarithmic forms applied to
\(x^q-1\) or \(x^{-q}-1\), as in the
[higher-degree fresh-prime bound](MULTIVARIATE_HIGHER_DEGREE_FRESH_QUOTIENT_PRIMES.md).
At a place in the fixed support where the valuation
of \(x\) is negative, the leading term gives exactly
\((q-1)v_{\mathfrak p}(x)\), and at positive valuation
the constant term gives zero. A unit at such a place
contributes zero eventually by the finite-field argument
above. The product formula now gives the stated
\(q h(x)+O(\log q)\). Finally valuations of rational
integers in the splitting field give their logarithmic
gcd when the ideal sum is divided by the field degree.
Since \(h(x)=h(t_C)\), this proves (1).

## Exact examples and scope

Take the ordinary mirrored rows \((13,12),(12,13)\)
at orders \(3,4\). The primitive parents have arrays
\((13,37,37,13)\) and \((221,833,1225,833,221)\).
All root quotients of the cubic are units at every
place over \(17\). At \(17\) the quartic has two
roots of valuation \(1/2\) and two of valuation
\(-1/2\). Its two inverse root pairs come from the
two roots of \(221X^2+833X+783\), which lie between
\(-2\) and \(-1\). The trace field is
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{1717})\). The two squareclasses of
the inverse-pair discriminants \(X^2-4\) are
independent in this field: each is negative in both
real embeddings and their product is \(3333/221^2\),
which is nonsquare in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{1717})\).
The splitting field therefore admits independent
inversions within the two inverse pairs. For every
quartic root quotient some conjugate has nonzero
valuation at a place over \(17\): an inverse pair
already has opposite valuations, and for roots from
different pairs one can invert either root.
No nontorsion quotient of the cubic can be
multiplicatively dependent with any quartic quotient,
because the same embedding preserves the cubic's
unit property. Thus \(\mathcal L=0\) in this ordinary
example. The remaining cyclotomic angles can be
excluded by the exact small power tests in the
[cyclotomic product example](MULTIVARIATE_QUADRATIC_CYCLOTOMIC_PRODUCT_PRIMES.md).

For \(F=(z-1)(z-2)\) and \(G=(z-3)(z-12)\), the
quotients can be oriented as \(2,4\). With \(S=\{2,3\}\)
the gcd outside \(S\) is \(\Phi_q(2)\) for every odd
prime \(q\), by (3). Thus \(\mathcal L=\log2\), even
though the two quotient directions differ.
For \(G=(z-3)(z+12)\) instead, the quotients are
\(2,-4\). Their label sets are disjoint, so their
prime-order gcd outside \(S\) is one and
\(\mathcal L=0\). These are tests of the power and
torsion conditions for integral polynomials.

Formula (1) concerns each fixed input profile. The
Subspace Theorem does not give a uniform convergence
cutoff over arbitrary factorial rows. A bound on the
overlap of commensurable root quotient classes in
unrestricted ordinary factorial parents remains open.
