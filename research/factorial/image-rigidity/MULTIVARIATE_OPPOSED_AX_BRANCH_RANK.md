# Common-factor degree on opposed factorial height branches

Fix an ordinary two-monomial core, two fixed positive gaps \(d,e\),
and adjacent orders \(n,N=n+1\), with \(n\ge2\). Append \(r_+\)
copies of \((L,L+d)\) and \(r_-\) copies of \((Y+e,Y)\). Use
the fixed signed gap \(h=dr_+-er_-\) and the local height branches
of the [opposed diffuse branch theorem](MULTIVARIATE_OPPOSED_DIFFUSE_PUISEUX_HEIGHT_BRANCHES.md).
A positive Gaussian contact has two strictly positive limiting
curvature shares in the interior, where
\(-1/e<\lambda_*/T_*<1/d\). Its bias is \(\lambda_*/T_*\).

**Theorem.** Near every positive interior contact of rational
bias, all sufficiently high ordinary profiles are free of a
common root. Near every positive interior contact of irrational
bias, all sufficiently high ordinary collisions have a greatest
common divisor of degree exactly two. Both conclusions allow
multiple and tangential Gaussian contacts. The first conclusion
is local to the stated height and multiplicity family, with
fixed core, gaps, and signed gap. At a positive boundary contact,
any branch with infinitely many ordinary collisions must satisfy at
least two independent multiplicative relations among the normalized
factorial multipliers defined below.

The degree bound comes from a general germ inequality. If
the coefficient multipliers give a full-rank list of rational
functions, a shared factor of degree \(\ell\) requires at least
\(\ell+1\) algebraically independent height and count functions.
The integer height argument at rational bias combines this
inequality with an auxiliary polynomial and an affine height
exclusion.

## A bound from differential transcendence

Work in the differential field of germs of meromorphic functions
at a regular point of a one-parameter complex branch. The derivative
has constant field \(\mathbb C\). Let \(K\) be the field generated
over \(\mathbb C\) by the varying heights and by the nonconstant
common count parameter \(r\). Write \(t=\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb C}K\).
After making a common argument change and normalizing both
constant terms to one, suppose the two polynomials have exact
degrees \(n,N\) and their \(m=n+N\) nonconstant coefficients
are of the form
\[
                  G_i\exp(r\log F_i),\qquad F_i,G_i\in K^\times.
\]
Assume the \(F_i\) are multiplicatively independent modulo
\(\mathbb C^\times\). The logarithms are taken at the regular
point. Set \(H_i=\log F_i\), \(J_i=rH_i\).

Suppose the polynomials over the meromorphic germ field have
greatest common divisor of degree at least \(\ell\).
Over an algebraic closure of the field generated by the lower
polynomial's coefficients over \(K\), there are only finitely
many monic degree-\(\ell\) divisors of that polynomial. One
of them divides the upper polynomial over a common algebraic
closure. For any such divisor \(U\), the classes of
\(z,\ldots,z^\ell\) are linearly independent modulo \(U\).
Indeed, \(z\) is invertible there because \(U(0)\ne0\), and
\(1,\ldots,z^{\ell-1}\) are a basis. Divisibility therefore
gives \(\ell\) independent linear equations on the upper
nonconstant coefficients after an algebraic field extension.
This yields
\[
            \operatorname{trdeg}_K K(\exp J_1,\ldots,\exp J_m)
                   \le m-\ell .
\]

Let \(\delta\) be the dimension over \(\mathbb Q\) of the
constant relations among the \(2m\) functions \(H_i,J_i\).
Projection of the relations to the \(J\) coordinates is injective
because the \(H_i\) are independent modulo constants. A basis
of relations can thus be written \((A+rB)H=c\), where \(B\)
has row rank \(\delta\) and \(c\) is constant. Take a
\(\delta\)-column minor on which \(B\) has full rank. The
same minor of \(A+rB\) is a polynomial in \(r\) with nonzero
leading coefficient, hence is nonzero in \(K\). It follows that
\(\operatorname{trdeg}_K K(H)\le m-\delta\).

For each of the \(2m\) logarithms \(x\) and its exponential
\(y\), the identity \(D x=D y/y\) holds. Select
\(2m-\delta\) logarithms independent modulo constants, so
their derivatives are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).
Ax's one-derivation theorem gives transcendence degree at
least \(2m-\delta+1\) for these logarithms and exponentials.
[Ax, On Schanuel's conjectures, (SD)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1971/93-2/p03)

On the other hand, \(\exp H=F\) lies in \(K\), and \(J=rH\).
Adjoining all logarithms and exponentials to \(K\) gives the
upper bound \(t+(m-\delta)+(m-\ell)\). Hence
\[
                              \ell\le t-1.                 \tag{1}
\]
This argument works with any number of height parameters under
the stated multiplicative independence hypothesis.

The same proof applies to any list of \(s\) multiplicatively
independent \(F_i\), with \(\ell\) replaced by the codimension
of the algebraic target for \(\exp J\) in its \(s\)-dimensional
torus over \(K\). Only the transcendence-degree upper bound
for \(\exp J\) uses the common factor. The inequality then says
that the target has codimension at most \(t-1\). Compare the
[multiple-count codimension theorem](MULTIVARIATE_MULTIPLICITY_AX_CODIMENSION.md),
which allows several independent count parameters over a
one-variable algebraic height field.

## Characters of the factorial ramps

Write \(r_\pm=c_\pm r+s_\pm\), where
\(c_+=e/g,c_-=d/g\) and \(g=\gcd(d,e)\). On each branch use
\(u=1/r\), \(p=r/L\), and \(q=r/Y\). Define the ramps
\[
 A_{j,k}(L)=\prod_{a=1}^{kd}(1+a/(jL)),\qquad
 B_{j,k}(Y)=\prod_{a=1}^{ke}(1+a/(jY)).
\]
The common physical change in equation (1) of the
[branch proof](MULTIVARIATE_OPPOSED_DIFFUSE_PUISEUX_HEIGHT_BRANCHES.md#the-exact-complex-one-parameter-germ)
and division by each parent constant give the required coefficient
form, with
\[
       F_{j,k}= A_{j,k}(L)^{c_+}
              \left(\frac{B_{j,j-k}(Y)}{B_{j,j}(Y)}\right)^{c_-},
                  \qquad j=n,N,\quad 1\le k\le j.
\]
The other factors \(G_{j,k}\) consist of the normalized core
coefficients, \(j^{hk}\), and the offset powers with exponents
\(s_\pm\). These are nonzero elements of \(\mathbb C(L,Y)\).
Each \(F_{j,k}\) tends to one as the two heights diverge.

As functions of formal independent height variables, these
characters are multiplicatively independent modulo constants.
To see this, replace them by their first differences
\(F_{j,k}/F_{j,k-1}\), with \(F_{j,0}=1\). The two lists
generate the same group by telescoping. If \(d\ge2\), each
increasing block of \(d\) consecutive factors has an offset
\(a/j\) with \(j\nmid a\). Its zero is unique to that block
among the \(L\)-factors in the two orders, since
\(\gcd(n,N)=1\). Its divisor forces the block exponent in
any multiplicative relation to be zero. The same argument
with the decreasing blocks applies if \(e\ge2\). If \(d=e=1\),
the increasing factor has a nonintegral offset for \(k<j\),
and the decreasing factor has a nonintegral offset for \(k>1\).
Their exceptions do not meet because \(j\ge2\). This proves
independence in the formal height variables.

If rank fails on a branch, an integer product of these
characters is constant and its limit is one. Separating the
\(L\) and \(Y\) factors gives
\[
                      U(L)=V(Y),\qquad U,V\in\mathbb Q(X),
                      \qquad U(\infty)=V(\infty)=1 .
\]
Both rational functions are nonconstant. If either were
constant, the other would be constant since its height ranges
over infinitely many values, and the resulting formal relation
would violate independence in the two height variables.

At an interior contact the ratio \(Y/L\) tends to
\[
              \rho=\frac{T_*+e\lambda_*}{T_*-d\lambda_*}>0 .
\]
Write the expansions at infinity as
\(U(L)=1+aL^{-b}+O(L^{-b-1})\) and
\(V(Y)=1+cY^{-f}+O(Y^{-f-1})\), with \(a,c\) nonzero
rational numbers and \(b,f\ge1\). The finite positive limit
forces \(b=f\) and \(\rho^b=c/a\). In particular rank failure
forces \(\rho\) to be algebraic. Dividing the exact identity
by \(L^{-b}\) and writing \(t_0=1/L\), \(z_0=Y/L\)
gives an analytic equation near \((0,\rho)\).
Its derivative in \(z_0\) at that point is nonzero, since
the leading equation is \(a=c z_0^{-b}\). The implicit
function theorem gives
\[
                       Y=\rho L+\kappa+O(L^{-1})
\]
for a constant \(\kappa\). If \(\rho\) is rational and an
infinite sequence of height pairs is integral, the values
\(Y-\rho L\) belong to a fixed discrete rational lattice.
Along that sequence they equal \(\kappa\) for all sufficiently
large heights.

## Application to exact integer collisions

An accumulating sequence of ordinary collisions lies,
after taking a subsequence, on one of the finitely many
holomorphic branches in \(u^{1/M}\). If the exact greatest
common divisor has degree at least \(\ell\) along the sequence,
the corresponding Sylvester rank minors vanish on the entire
branch by the identity theorem. Their entries are holomorphic
through \(u=0\) in the exact physical normalization. The
constant and leading coefficients stay nonzero. In particular,
the greatest common divisor on the branch has degree at least two
if there are infinitely many ordinary collisions. An adjacent
pair includes an even order, whose moment is strictly positive
for real \(z\) since the two ordinary monomials are distinct
and the factorial measure has full support on the positive
orthant. Any common roots at integer profiles therefore occur
in conjugate pairs with matching multiplicities.

At a rational-bias contact the
[auxiliary-polynomial theorem](MULTIVARIATE_OPPOSED_AUXILIARY_POLYNOMIAL_RATIONAL_BIAS.md#an-integer-auxiliary-polynomial)
gives a nonzero polynomial relation among \(L,Y,r\) on any
branch with an accumulation of ordinary collisions. Thus
\(t\le2\) in (1). Full character rank would force
\(\ell\le1\), a contradiction. If the rank fails, the
preceding argument applies with \(\rho\) rational. The
sequence eventually satisfies the exact rational affine
height relation \(Y=\rho L+\kappa\). The
[affine height exclusion](MULTIVARIATE_OPPOSED_INDEPENDENT_HEIGHT_RATIONAL_BIAS.md#first-marks-exclude-the-forced-affine-family)
rules this out for all sufficiently large heights, regardless
of the counts. This excludes all branches approaching the
interior rational-bias contact.

At an irrational interior bias the
[contact algebraicity argument](MULTIVARIATE_OPPOSED_DIFFUSE_PUISEUX_HEIGHT_BRANCHES.md)
shows that \(\rho\) is transcendental. Explicitly,
\(w_*=\exp(-T_* /(2nN))\) and
\(v_*=\exp(-\lambda_* /(2nN))\) are algebraic and
\(0<w_*<1\). If \(\lambda_*/T_*\) were algebraic irrational,
Gelfond–Schneider would contradict the algebraicity of
\(v_*=w_*^{\lambda_*/T_*}\). Thus an irrational bias is
transcendental, as is its nonconstant rational fractional
linear transform \(\rho\). The characters must have full
rank. Now \(K=\mathbb C(L,Y,r)\) gives \(t\le3\), so (1)
gives \(\ell\le2\). Any sequence with greatest common divisor
of degree at least four would contradict this bound. Since
the degree of an exact ordinary common factor is even, all
sufficiently high collisions have greatest common divisor
of degree exactly two.

## Boundary branches need at least two character relations

At a boundary contact both heights still tend to infinity,
although one limiting curvature share is zero. The bias is
rational. If ordinary collisions accumulate, the auxiliary
polynomial again gives \(t\le2\), and full character rank is
impossible by (1). Suppose there is just one independent
multiplicative relation. The generated group of characters,
modulo constants, is free of rank \(m-1\). Take an integral
basis \(F'_1,\ldots,F'_{m-1}\). All the characters tend to
one, so the constant factors relating the two lists are one.
Use their logarithms near one. Both exponentiated lists
\(\exp(r\log F_i)\) and \(\exp(r\log F'_i)\) are then
integer monomials in each other. After the \(G_i\) scales,
the coefficient pair lies in a translate \(W\) of a
codimension-one subtorus, defined by a single nontrivial
monomial equation.

Over an algebraic closure of \(K\), the common-factor locus
of degree at least two in the coefficient torus is
irreducible of dimension \(m-2\). Indeed, parameterize the
two parents by \(U A,U B\), where the three constant terms
are one and the degrees are \(2,n-2,N-2\). This parameter
space is open in affine space of dimension \(m-2\); the
restriction that all parent coefficients are nonzero is
a nonempty open condition. Every pair with a common
degree-two divisor occurs, and each fiber is finite because
there are finitely many normalized divisors of the lower
parent. Closedness inside the torus also follows from the
Sylvester rank minors.

More generally, no nonconstant monomial in the coefficients
of such a pair is constant on the degree-\(\ell\) locus,
for \(1\le\ell\le n\). To prove this, take the coefficients
\(U_1,\ldots,U_\ell\) of the common factor to be independent
indeterminates, and let \(A\) have degree \(q\ge1\). Each
nonconstant coefficient of \(U A\) is an affine-linear
polynomial in \(A_1,\ldots,A_q\) over the field generated
by the \(U_i\). At indices \(k\le\ell\) its constant term
is \(U_k\) and its coefficient of \(A_1\) is \(U_{k-1}\),
where \(U_0=1\). The ratios \(U_{k-1}/U_k\) are distinct as
rational functions, so none of these polynomials are
proportional. At indices \(k>\ell\) the constant term is
zero and its first nonzero variable index is \(k-\ell\).
These polynomials are again pairwise nonproportional.
The same argument applies independently to \(B\) when its
degree is positive. Looking at the distinct linear divisors
forces all monomial exponents from those parents to vanish.
If one parent is \(U\) itself, its remaining coefficients
are the independent \(U_1,\ldots,U_\ell\), which force the
remaining exponents to vanish as well.

In particular the degree-two locus is not contained in
\(W\). Its intersection with \(W\) has dimension at most
\(m-3\), hence codimension at least two within \(W\).
In the \(m-1\) basis characters this is a target of
codimension at least two for their exponentials. The
codimension form of (1) bounds it instead by \(t-1\le1\).
This contradiction rules out a branch with exactly one
independent relation. Boundary branches with two or more
relations and quadratic collisions at irrational interior
bias remain open.
