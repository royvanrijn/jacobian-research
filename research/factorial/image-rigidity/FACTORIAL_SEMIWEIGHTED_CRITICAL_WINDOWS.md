# Factorial windows from isolated weighted principal parts at critical points

Use the Gamma factorial functional
\(\Lambda(X^\beta)=\prod_i(\alpha_i)_{\beta_i}\) with fixed positive
rational shapes \(\alpha_i\). Ordinary factorial is the case
\(\alpha_i=1\).

**Theorem.** Let \(F\) be a polynomial over a characteristic-zero
field. Give the variables positive integer weights \(w_1,\ldots,w_m\)
and suppose the top weight of \(F\) is \(d>\max_i w_i\).
Let \(H\) be its weighted top part. Assume the following conditions,
over an algebraic closure of the coefficient field.

1. The forms \(X_i\partial_i H\) have only the origin as a common zero.
2. At every zero \(p\) of \(X_i\partial_i F\) with \(F(p)=0\),
   use formal coordinates \(y\) centered at \(p\) and adapted to the coordinate divisors
   through \(p\). Thus, after ordering the coordinates, each
   \(X_i\) with \(p_i=0\) is \(y_i\) times a formal unit. Give
   the \(y_i\) positive rational weights \(\omega_i\). In these
   coordinates require
   \[
       F(X(y))-F(p)=f_0(y)+f_{>1}(y),
   \]
   with \(f_0\) of weighted degree one and all terms of \(f_{>1}\)
   of weight strictly above one. Define \(D_i=\partial_{y_i}\) if
   \(p_i\ne0\) and \(D_i=y_i\partial_{y_i}\) if \(p_i=0\).
   The ideal generated by the \(D_i f_0\) must have only the origin
   as a common zero.

Set
\[
 R=\prod_{i=1}^m\frac d{w_i},\qquad
 L=\sum_{i=1}^m(d-w_i),\qquad
 q=1+\left\lfloor\frac{L}{d-\max_i w_i}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 W(a,q)=a+q\,\frac{a(a+3)}2.
\]
For every \(k\ge1\), each positive window of length
\[
                       \left\lceil W(kR,q)/k\right\rceil
\]
of factorial moments of \(F^k\) contains a nonzero moment. For
\(F\) itself, if its moments are eventually zero on an arithmetic
progression of orders, then they vanish identically on that
progression.

The local conditions allow multiple critical points and higher
weighted terms which change the critical algebra action. The
proof constructs a full set of formal solutions with positive
reciprocal orders for the part of the differential system
associated with critical points on the zero fiber.
The global degree and dimension control the length of the window.

## The global finite system

Work over \(\mathbb C\). Write \(G_i=X_i\partial_iF\), \(P_i=X_i\partial_i H\),
\(u=1/s\), and
\[
                 I_a(s)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{\Lambda(aF^n)}{n!}s^n .
\]
The Gamma factorial recurrence, applied to each monomial of an
amplitude \(a\), gives the formal identity
\[
 I_{G_i a}
       =u I_{(X_i-\alpha_i)a-X_i\partial_i a}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(\Lambda(X_i h)=\Lambda((X_i\partial_i+\alpha_i)h)\)
on polynomials, and one can apply it coefficientwise to
\(h=a e^{sF}\). Also \(I_a'=I_{Fa}\) for amplitudes independent
of \(s\).

By the first hypothesis the \(P_i\) form a regular sequence.
The Hilbert series of the graded quotient is
\(\prod_i(1-t^d)/(1-t^{w_i})\), by the successive exact
sequences for multiplication by \(P_i\). Its dimension is
\(R\) and its highest weight is \(L\). Alternatively each
\(P_i\) must be nonzero on the \(i\)-th punctured coordinate
axis, so \(w_i\mid d\) and each factor of this Hilbert series
is itself a polynomial. Choose a homogeneous complement \(V\)
to the top ideal and a homogeneous basis of \(V\) including one.

An amplitude \(\psi\) of weighted degree \(e\) has a division
\[
                       \psi=v+\sum_i G_i a_i,\qquad v\in V ,
 \tag{2}
\]
with \(a_i\) of weighted degree at most \(e-d\). To construct
it, divide the successive highest weights by the homogeneous
top ideal and use the corresponding \(G_i\) to remove those
terms. The representative \(v\) is unique. Indeed, if a sum
\(\sum_i G_i a_i\) belongs to \(V\), a weight above the weight
of the sum gives a syzygy of the top sequence.
Write that syzygy as a Koszul syzygy of the \(P_i\) and subtract
the same antisymmetric combination of the \(G_i\) from the
coefficients \(a_i\). The sum is unchanged and the largest
summand weight drops. When that weight reaches the weight of
the sum, its leading part belongs to the homogeneous ideal,
which has zero intersection with the chosen complement.
Thus the critical algebra has dimension \(R\).

In the moment system, using (1) after (2) multiplies the ideal
contribution by \(u\) and lowers the upper bound on its
amplitude weight by at least \(d-\max_i w_i>0\).
For \(I_{F\phi}\) with \(\phi\) a basis element, the starting
weight is at most \(L+d\). After at most \(q\) reductions
all terms of the remainder have weight below \(d\).
We obtain a system
\[
                   Y'=A(s)Y,\qquad
            A(s)=A_0+u A_1+\cdots+u^q A_q ,
 \tag{3}
\]
of size \(R\), with \(I_1\) an output row.
The leading matrix \(A_0\) is multiplication by \(F\) in the
critical algebra, since each use of (1) costs a factor \(u\). The same division
expresses \(I_a\) for an arbitrary polynomial \(a\) in this
basis by a polynomial row in \(u\). These reductions apply to
any coefficientwise linear functional satisfying (1).

The construction of the multiplication and differential
matrices has a related torus version in the Brieskorn lattice
of a convenient nondegenerate Laurent polynomial. The affine
coordinate faces and the local Gamma densities in the present
calculation also include points on the coordinate divisors.
[Douai–Sabbah, §2.c and Proposition 2.13](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/aif.1974.pdf)

## The local polynomial functionals

Fix a point \(p\) as in condition 2. Write \(v=F(p)\) and use
the local weights \(\omega_i\) and operators \(D_i\) there.
Set \(\delta_i=0\) at active coordinates \(p_i\ne0\), and
\(\delta_i=\alpha_i\) at boundary coordinates \(p_i=0\).
The homogeneous polynomials \(g_i=D_i f_0\) are a regular
sequence by the local isolation condition. Their degrees
\(\eta_i\) are \(1-\omega_i\) at active coordinates, and one
at boundary coordinates. These are positive. In fact an
active coordinate has no linear term in \(f_0\), and if its
weight were at least one, \(f_0\) would be independent of it,
contrary to isolation.

Let \(\psi_j\) be a homogeneous complement basis of the
polynomial ring modulo the homogeneous ideal \((g_i)\).
The same \(\psi_j\) form a basis for the vector-space quotient
of the polynomial ring by the sum of the images of the
operators
\[
                         L_i^{(0)}=D_i+g_i+\delta_i .
 \tag{4}
\]
Indeed the leading part of \(L_i^{(0)} a\) is \(g_i a\) on
a homogeneous input, since the derivative and scalar terms
have weight exactly one less when present. Successive highest
weight division proves spanning. For independence, suppose
a nonzero combination of the \(\psi_j\) is expressed as
\(\sum_i L_i^{(0)} a_i\). If the largest summand weight is
above that of the combination, the leading coefficient
tuple is a Koszul syzygy of the \(g_i\). The \(L_i^{(0)}\)
commute, because the \(D_i\) commute and
\(D_i g_j=D_j g_i\). Subtracting an antisymmetric combination
of the \(L_i^{(0)}\) from the coefficients therefore preserves
the sum and lowers its largest summand weight. When the
weights reach that of the combination, its leading part
would belong to \((g_i)\), a contradiction.

Let \(J_j\) be the dual polynomial functionals for this
quotient, so \(J_j(\psi_l)=\mathbf 1_{j=l}\) and
\(J_j\) kills the images in (4). Define the Euler field
\(E=\sum_i\omega_i y_i\partial_{y_i}\) and the positive
rational number
\[
              \nu=\sum_{p_i\ne0}\omega_i+
                           \sum_{p_i=0}\alpha_i\omega_i .
\]
For an active coordinate apply \(L_i^{(0)}\) to \(y_i h\).
For a boundary coordinate apply it to \(h\). Sum with
weights \(\omega_i\) and use \(E f_0=f_0\) to get
\[
                            J_j((E+\nu+f_0)h)=0 .
 \tag{5}
\]

The number of these functionals is the length of the
logarithmic critical algebra at \(p\).
To see this, pass to the formal series ring in \(y\).
The derivations \(D_i\) generate the logarithmic tangent
fields in the adapted coordinates. So do the original
\(X_i\partial_i\) under the coordinate change, since \(X_i\)
is a unit at each active coordinate and the boundary
divisors are preserved. Therefore the local ideal in either
coordinate system is generated by \(D_i(F\circ X)\) in
the \(y\)-coordinates. The lowest weighted parts of its
generators are \(g_i\).
If the lowest weighted summands in a representation cancel,
their coefficients give a Koszul syzygy of the regular sequence
\(g_i\). Subtract the same antisymmetric combination of
the full generators, which raises the lowest summand weight.
Iterating up to the weight of the given ideal element proves
that its lowest part belongs to \((g_i)\). Thus the weighted
initial ideal is exactly \((g_i)\).
Ascending weighted division of an arbitrary series now gives
the finite complement spanned by the \(\psi_j\). The coefficients
used during division converge in the formal series ring since
each correction has increasing weight. The lowest-part identity
also proves independence of the complement. This proves the
length assertion for the possibly nonreduced local algebra.
In the global critical algebra, multiplication by \(F\) has
only the eigenvalue \(F(p)\) on the local algebra at \(p\),
since the maximal ideal of each finite local algebra is
nilpotent. Thus the sum of these local dimensions over the
points with \(F(p)=0\) is the dimension of the generalized
zero eigenspace of \(A_0\).

## Formal solutions from the local principal parts

In the local coordinates express the Gamma density as
\[
 \left(\prod_i X_i^{\alpha_i-1}e^{-X_i}\right)
                 dX_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dX_m
       =U(y)\left(\prod_{p_i=0}y_i^{\alpha_i-1}\right)
                 dy_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_m .
 \tag{6}
\]
Here \(U\) is a formal unit. Its constants at units of
fractional power can be chosen arbitrarily among the compatible
roots, and the other coefficients follow by the binomial series.
The Jacobian is a unit since the coordinates are invertible.

Use a common ramification of the formal parameter \(u\) for all
weights and all \(\nu\)'s in the finitely many local constructions.
In (6) substitute \(y_i\mapsto u^{\omega_i}y_i\). The
unit-free density contributes the factor \(u^\nu\).
Set
\[
 K(u,y)=\frac{F(X(u^\omega y))-v}{u}-f_0(y),\qquad
 h_a(u,y)=a(X(u^\omega y))U(u^\omega y)e^{K(u,y)} .
 \tag{7}
\]
All terms of \(K\) have positive \(u\)-order. Each coefficient
of these series is polynomial in \(y\), since the \(\omega_i\)
are positive, and each coefficient of \(e^K\) uses finitely
many terms. In particular \(J_j\) applies coefficientwise to
\(h_a\). Define the local expression
\[
                  \mathcal I_{p,j}(a)=
                       e^{v/u}u^\nu J_j(h_a(u,y)).
 \tag{8}
\]
The exponential is only a formal action symbol, with
\(\partial_s e^{v/u}=v e^{v/u}\).

These expressions satisfy the same integration by parts
as \(I_a\) in (1). Here are the details. In the rescaled
coordinates let \(T\) be the expression of \(X_i\partial_i\),
so \(T\) is a Laurent series of logarithmic vector fields with
coefficients polynomial in \(y\), of bounded below \(u\)-order.
For such a field write \(T=\sum b_l D_l\).
With respect to the unit-free density \(\rho_0\) from (6),
\[
 (T+\operatorname{div}_{\rho_0}T+T f_0) h
           =\sum_l (D_l+\delta_l+D_l f_0)(b_l h).
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus applying \(J_j\) gives zero coefficientwise.
For \(h=h_a\) the left side of (9) equals
the rescaled \(U e^K\) times
\[
 \left(X_i\partial_i+\alpha_i-X_i+\frac{G_i}{u}\right)a
\]
evaluated at \(X(u^\omega y)\). This follows from the
product rule, \(\operatorname{div}_{\rho} X_i\partial_i
=\alpha_i-X_i\) for the original Gamma density, and
\(T(K+f_0)=G_i/u\) from (7).
The constant scale factor \(u^\nu\) of the density has
no derivative in the \(y\)-coordinates. This proves (1)
for (8).

For the parameter derivative, direct differentiation of (7)
gives \(u\partial_u h_a=E h_a-K h_a\). Applying
\(\partial_s=-u^2\partial_u\) to (8) and using (5) gives
\[
 \partial_s\mathcal I_{p,j}(a)
       = e^{v/u}u^\nu J_j\bigl((v+u f_0+uK)h_a\bigr)
       = \mathcal I_{p,j}(Fa).
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus the evaluations of (8) on the global basis of \(V\) solve
(3). After dividing out \(e^{v/u}\), each of their entries
has \(u\)-order at least \(\nu>0\).

The columns from the zero-fiber points are independent over the
formal Puiseux field. For each such pair \((p,l)\), prescribe a finite jet of a global
polynomial amplitude \(a_{p,l}\) so that at \(p\)
\[
               a_{p,l}(X(y))U(y)
                   =\psi_l(y)+\text{terms of weight above }\deg_\omega\psi_l.
\]
At every other point prescribe the amplitude to vanish to
weight above \(\deg_\omega\psi_l\), using that point's own
weights. Sufficiently high ordinary jets imply the desired
weighted truncations because all weights are positive.
The formal coordinate change and the invertible \(U\) let us
translate each finite condition to ordinary jets in \(X-p\).
The ordinary polynomial Chinese remainder theorem realizes
them simultaneously.

Form the square matrix of these amplitude evaluations by the
local expressions (8). In each column remove
\(e^{v/u}u^\nu\) for that column's point, and in row
\((p,l)\) divide by \(u^{\deg_\omega\psi_l}\).
By (7), the resulting matrix has constant term the identity:
the leading evaluations at \(p\) are \(J_j(\psi_l)\), and
all evaluations at other points have positive order.
Hence it is invertible. The reduction after (3) expresses
each of the \(a_{p,l}\) rows in terms of the global basis,
also on these local functionals by (9).
Hence the solution columns on the basis of \(V\) are independent.
Their number is the dimension of the generalized zero eigenspace
of \(A_0\). The other generalized eigenspaces need no local
hypothesis.

For comparison, Brieskorn lattices for an isolated boundary
singularity give local Gauss–Manin modules for one smooth
boundary. The formal construction here uses a union of
coordinate boundaries and the indicated positive Gamma
powers. Its independence and positive orders follow directly
from (4)–(10).
[Kourliouros, Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.05619)

## The zero-window bound

Use the scalar elimination and window argument of the
[separated polynomial theorem](FACTORIAL_SEPARATED_POLYNOMIAL_POWER_WINDOWS.md).
For clarity, for a rational system of size \(a\) with output
row \(b\), form the derivative rows
\(b_0=b,\ b_{j+1}=b_j'+b_j A\) and take their first dependence
at order \(r\le a\). In the present system \(s^q A\) and
\(s^q b\) have polynomial entries of degree at most \(q\).
Writing \(b_j=L_j/(s^q)^{j+1}\) gives
\(\deg L_j\le(j+1)q\).
Cofactor elimination gives coefficients of the derivative
operator of degree at most
\(q((r+1)(r+2)/2-1)\). Conversion to Euler derivatives by
multiplication by \(s^r\) gives width at most \(W(a,q)\).

This scalar equation has no nonzero polynomial solution.
Over \(\mathbb C((u))\), a system with leading matrix \(A_0\)
and only nonnegative powers of \(u\) splits formally by the
generalized eigenvalues of \(A_0\). After a constant block
decomposition, use a gauge \(1+O(u)\). At each step the
off-block correction is obtained by inverting
\(A_{0,i}X-XA_{0,j}\) between two blocks with distinct
eigenvalues. This map is a nonzero scalar plus a nilpotent
operator, because the two nilpotent multiplications commute.
The derivative of the gauge raises order and hence does not
affect that step. A horizontal map between blocks with distinct
leading eigenvalues vanishes: the first nonzero Laurent
coefficient of such a map would be killed by the same
invertible operator, since the derivative raises order.

Apply this argument to the cyclic row submodule \(V_b\)
spanned by the derivative rows \(b_j\), with differential
action \(v\mapsto v'+vA\). Its intersection with the module
of power series rows is a saturated free lattice spanning
\(V_b\), stable under the differential action. Saturation
gives an injection modulo \(u\) into the ambient lattice
modulo \(u\), so its leading eigenvalues are among those of
\(A_0\). The same splitting and absence of cross-block maps
show that each block of \(V_b\) embeds into the corresponding
ambient block.

For a polynomial solution \(P\), its derivatives define a
horizontal map on \(V_b\) to the trivial line by
\(b_j\mapsto\partial_s^j P\), using the first dependence.
The map vanishes on the blocks with nonzero leading eigenvalue,
by the argument for cross-block maps with target eigenvalue zero.
On the ambient zero block, the local columns already constructed
are horizontal maps to the trivial line after Puiseux extension.
They vanish on all other blocks and are independent, with number
equal to the zero block rank. They therefore identify that block
with a trivial differential module. Over the Puiseux field,
a differential submodule of a trivial module admits a constant
basis: take a reduced row echelon basis of its subspace.
The derivatives of the basis vectors lie in that subspace,
but vanish at all pivot positions, so are zero.
Thus the map on the zero block of \(V_b\) extends to the ambient
zero block by linear algebra over \(\mathbb C\).
It is a constant linear combination of the local functionals.
Their outputs have positive \(u\)-order, whereas a nonzero
polynomial in \(s\) has order at most zero.
A block of zero coefficients of length the scalar width
at a positive order would make the nonzero polynomial
truncation below the block a solution. Indeed applying
the Euler-form operator to that truncation produces a
polynomial whose degree is below the first possible
nonzero coefficient allowed by the omitted tail.
This proves the window for \(I_1\).

The direct sum of the \(k\) systems with parameter \(\zeta s\)
for the \(k\)-th roots of unity retains all the stated
degree and zero-block properties, with size at most
\(kR\). Averaging outputs keeps the moment orders divisible
by \(k\) and gives constant coefficient one. A block
of \(\lceil W(kR,q)/k\rceil\) zero moments of \(F^k\)
would give at least \(W(kR,q)\) consecutive zero degrees in
that filtered series, beginning at a positive degree.
Weighted root-of-unity filters for other residues similarly
exclude a nonzero eventually zero series. The hypotheses
and conclusions hold over the original field by embedding
the field generated by the finite phase data and the
countably many coefficients of the formal changes into
\(\mathbb C\).

## Examples and scope

If a point of the logarithmic critical system is simple,
the active Hessian is nonsingular and every boundary
normal derivative is nonzero. Weights \(1/2\) on active
coordinates and one on boundary coordinates at that point
give the principal part consisting of the active quadratic
form and the boundary linear terms. Thus condition 2 holds
there. At the opposite extreme, a weighted homogeneous
\(F\) satisfying condition 1 has its only critical point
at the origin and again satisfies condition 2. The critical
multiplicity there is \(R\).

For a genuinely higher-weight local perturbation take
\[
              F=x^3+y^3+x^5+y^5+\varepsilon x^2y^2
\]
with \(\varepsilon\ne0\) sufficiently small. The top
logarithmic forms are \(5x^5,5y^5\).
At the origin the weights \(1/3\) give \(f_0=x^3+y^3\),
with principal ideal \((x^3,y^3)\).
The local action of \(F\) on the logarithmic critical
algebra is nonzero: \(F-(G_x+G_y)/3\) has leading term
\(-\varepsilon x^2y^2/3\), outside that initial ideal.
At a nonorigin point on either axis, the active second
derivative is nonzero since \(3+5y^2=0\) gives
\(6y+20y^3=-6y\) on the \(y\)-axis, and the normal
quadratic coefficient there is \(\varepsilon y^2\ne0\).
The ordinary quadratic principal part therefore has
isolated logarithmic critical ideal, with the normal
square in that ideal. The analogous computation holds
on the other axis. In the torus, divide the equations
\(G_x=G_y=0\) by \(x^2,y^2\) to get
\[
 5x^3+3x+2\varepsilon y^2=0,\qquad
 5y^3+3y+2\varepsilon x^2=0 .
\]
At \(\varepsilon=0\) there are nine distinct simple solutions.
They continue as nine distinct simple solutions for all
sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). The leading cubics have
only the origin as a common zero, so the system has length
nine at every \(\varepsilon\) by the degree division argument
of (2). Thus those continued solutions account for all torus
points. The family satisfies both conditions even though
the origin has a nonzero nilpotent logarithmic critical
action and the nonorigin axis points have multiple normals.

Only the logarithmic critical points on the zero fiber need
isolated weighted principal ideals, including any such points
on coordinate faces. The condition is vacuous if the zero
fiber contains no logarithmic critical points. The explicit bound can exceed the monomial
support size.
