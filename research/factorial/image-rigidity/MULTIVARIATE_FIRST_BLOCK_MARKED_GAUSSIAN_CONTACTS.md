# Gaussian contacts at an isolated upper first-block mark

Fix a finite ordinary two-monomial factorial core and adjacent
orders \(n,N=n+1\), \(n\ge2\). In each order write \(b_{j,k}>0\)
for its exact coefficient, including the binomial factor. Put
\[
 H_j(q,z)=\sum_{k=0}^j b_{j,k}q^{K_jk(j-k)}z^k,\qquad
     K_n=N,\quad K_N=n,\qquad q=e^{-W/(2nN)},\quad W>0 .
\]
This is the Gaussian pair for the core before applying positive
physical argument scales. An angular contact is a common root
argument of these polynomials. Excluding it excludes physical
contact for every diffuse bias and every integer total gap.

Assume there is a prime \(p>N\) where the coefficient valuations,
after discarding affine functions of \(k\) independently in the two
orders, are
\[
       (0,\ldots,0)\quad\text{at }n,\qquad
       (0,E,\ldots,E)\quad\text{at }N,\qquad E\ge1.              \tag{1}
\]
This holds if an isolated \(p\)-factor of depth \(E\) occurs in
a first increasing factorial block of the upper parent and all
other stripped factors in both orders are units. In particular
\(p=NA+1>n(A+d)\) in a single increasing row \((A,A+d)\)
gives (1) with \(E=1\) when \(p>N\) and \(Nd<p\).
Additional rows with factorial arguments below \(p\) are allowed.
The criterion only uses the valuation shapes (1).
Reversing both exponent columns permits a decreasing mark.

Write \(Q=q^2\). If there is an angular contact, \(q\) is
algebraic by the
[finite core angular eliminant](MULTIVARIATE_DIFFUSE_PHYSICAL_EXCEPTION_POINTS.md#the-finite-set-of-physical-reference-contacts).
The necessary restrictions below apply in every embedding into
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_p\), with \(v_p(p)=1\).

**Theorem.** Put \(t=v_p(q)\), and let \(e\) be the local
ramification index of \(\mathbb Q(Q)\) at the place in question.

- \(t>0\) is impossible.
- \(t=0\) requires \(\gcd(eE,N)>1\).
- \(t<0\) requires an element of the explicit menu
  \[
      t=-\frac{E}{b\{n(b+1)-2d\}},\qquad
                   1\le b\le n-1,\quad 1\le d\le n-b.         \tag{2}
  \]
  There is also a congruence of positive depth at each of
  these candidates, given below. The denominator in (2)
  before reduction is always even.

In particular, when \(E=1\) there is no angular contact with
rational \(q\). An odd algebraic degree for \(q\) is excluded when
\(N\) is a power of two. For general \(N\), an odd degree smaller
than its least odd prime divisor is excluded when \(\gcd(E,N)=1\)
and \(E\) is odd. The power-of-two conclusion also holds for
any odd \(E\). If \(E=1\) and \(Q\) is rational, every prime
satisfying (1) occurs exactly once in its reduced denominator.
Moreover
\[
       v_p\!\left(Q\,\frac{R_n}{R_N}-1\right)\ge n-1,\qquad
            R_j=\frac{b_{j,0}b_{j,j}}{b_{j,1}b_{j,j-1}} .      \tag{3}
\]
These conclusions constrain the positive reference with no tail
bias assumption. An effective ordinary transfer is given below.

## Clock and field conventions

The affine terms in (1) can be removed by independent scalar
and argument dilations by powers of \(p\). Their coefficients
are integers by comparing two adjacent indices. The dilations
preserve all quotients of distinct roots. The even polynomial
remains strictly positive on the real axis at \(W>0\), since
the Gaussian transformation is an average over positive
lognormal argument rescalings of the ordinary even moment.
A shared argument must therefore be nonreal. Dividing both
contacting roots by their conjugate roots gives an exact
equality of off-diagonal quotients, so it can be examined
in any embedding at \(p\).

The two quotient computations can be made over the local field
generated by \(Q\). In the odd order all \(K_j k(j-k)\) are
even. In the even order, replace \(z\) by \(q^{-K_j}z\);
the new exponents \(K_j k(j-k-1)\) are even. This substitution
changes no root quotient. It contributes only an affine change
of valuation, which does not affect any quotient valuation
or an off-face gap. In particular the unit case uses the
ramification index of \(Q\) rather than that of a square root.

If \(t>0\), the Gaussian valuations add a positive concave
quadratic to the interior indices. In each order the interior
lies strictly above the endpoint chord. After local rescaling
both reductions are endpoint binomials, and all their roots
are units with simple reductions since \(p>N\).
Their off-diagonal quotients reduce to nontrivial roots of
unity of orders dividing \(n\) and \(N\), respectively.
The two sets are disjoint.

## The unit clock and tame relative degrees

Assume \(t=0\). Work over the completed maximal unramified
extension \(K\) of the completion of \(\mathbb Q(Q)\).
The residue field is algebraically closed and the value
group is \(e^{-1}\mathbb Z\). After removal of the affine
valuation terms, the lower coefficient valuations are zero,
so all lower roots are units. The upper polynomial at
\(z=p^{-E/N}X\), before an irrelevant scalar, has the form
\[
              c_0+c_N X^N+
                   \sum_{k=1}^{N-1}c_k p^{E(1-k/N)}X^k
\]
with \(p\)-adic unit \(c_k\in K\). The two-clock argument
rescaling in the preceding section is included in these
coefficients. Let \(\lambda_i\in K\) be the binomial roots.
They have distinct residues. The unique first error term
is the one at \(k=N-1\), of valuation \(E/N\).
Hensel lifting and expansion at \(\lambda_i\) give the
associated root \(X_i\) in the form
\[
    X_i/\lambda_i
       =1+C\,\lambda_i^{N-1}p^{E/N}
                                     +O_{\!v}(2E/N),
      \qquad C=-\frac{c_{N-1}}{N c_N\lambda_i^N}\in K^\times.
\]
Here \(C\) is independent of \(i\) and is a unit.
The symbol \(O_{\!v}(a)\) denotes an error of valuation at
least \(a\). The higher terms and the quadratic root error
have valuation at least \(2E/N\). For distinct \(i,j\),
the residue of \((\lambda_i/\lambda_j)^{N-1}\) is not one.
Writing \(\omega=\lambda_i/\lambda_j\), we get
\[
                     v_p(X_i/(\omega X_j)-1)=E/N.             \tag{4}
\]

If this quotient matches a lower quotient \(\alpha/\alpha'\),
then \(v_p(\alpha-\omega\alpha')=E/N\), because both lower
roots are units. Each lower root generates a tame extension
of \(K\) of relative degree \(r,r'\le n\). With a uniformizer
\(\varpi\) for \(K\), these extensions are
\(K(\varpi^{1/r})\) and \(K(\varpi^{1/r'})\).
Indeed the residue field stays algebraically closed, the
degree equals the ramification index, and these indices are
prime to \(p\). A uniformizer in either extension can be
adjusted by a unit whose required root exists by Hensel to
give the stated generator. Equivalently this is the standard
tame form of [Abhyankar's lemma, Stacks Project 0EXW](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EXW).

In the common power basis for
\(\varpi^{1/\operatorname{lcm}(r,r')}\), the support of
\(\alpha-\omega\alpha'\) uses only fractional exponents
from \(r^{-1}\mathbb Z\) or \({r'}^{-1}\mathbb Z\).
The nonzero coordinates have distinct fractional valuations,
so the minimum comes from one of those groups.
Consequently the reduced denominator of \(eE/N\) must
divide \(r\) or \(r'\). If \(\gcd(eE,N)=1\), this denominator
is \(N>n\), which proves the unit restriction.

## The negative clock and its congruences

Write \(t=-s<0\). The lower coefficient ordinates in (1)
after the clock change are \(-Ns\,k(n-k)\).
Its Newton polygon has singleton edges with root valuation
differences \(2Ns\,d\), \(1\le d<n\).
The upper ordinates are
\[
               0\quad(k=0),\qquad E-ns\,k(N-k)
                           \quad(1\le k\le N).
\]
Choose the largest minimizer \(b\) of \(E/k-ns(N-k)\)
among \(1\le k\le N\). Its hull begins with the edge
\([0,b]\) and then has singleton edges because the ordinates
at positive indices are strictly convex. The first edge
root valuation is \(ns(N-b)-E/b\); that of the singleton
edge ending at \(k>b\) is \(ns(N+1-2k)\).

Two roots from the first edge give quotient valuation zero,
which does not occur in the lower order. Two singleton
roots give a difference \(2ns\,d'\) with \(1\le d'<N\),
which cannot equal \(2Ns\,d\) since \(n,N\) are coprime.
Thus \(b<N\), and one upper root must come from each of
the two kinds of edges. Comparing their valuations gives
\[
     2Ns\,d=ns(2k-b-1)-E/b,\qquad
                 s=E/\{b[n(2k-b-1)-2Nd]\}.                  \tag{5}
\]
The upper first-edge threshold satisfies
\(E/[b(b+1)]\le ns\), and for \(b>1\) also
\(ns\le E/[b(b-1)]\). These are the comparisons of
the secant slope at \(b\) with its two neighbors.
For \(b=1\) use just the first bound and positivity of
the denominator in (5). Substituting (5) gives
\[
                    k-b-1\le Nd/n\le k-b .
\]
Since \(Nd/n=d+d/n\) lies strictly between \(d\) and
\(d+1\), we have \(k=b+d+1\). This yields (2).
The two inequalities are now strict, so the first edge
has no collinear internal points. The denominator
\(b\{n(b+1)-2d\}\) is even because \(b(b+1)\) is even.

For each candidate set \(k=b+d+1\). For some
\(i\in\{1,\ldots,n-d\}\) the matching quotient must
use the two lower singleton roots at steps \(i,i+d\).
Put
\[
 u_{j,l}=-b_{j,l-1}/b_{j,l},\qquad
 C_{b,d,i}=
       \frac{\bigl[(u_{n,i}/u_{n,i+d})\,u_{N,k}\bigr]^b}
                    {-b_{N,0}/b_{N,b}},\qquad
              m=\tfrac12b\{n(b+1)-2d\}.
\]
A necessary condition is
\[
                     v_p(C_{b,d,i} Q^m-1)\ge \Delta,         \tag{6}
\]
where \(\Delta>0\) is the minimum off-face depth of the
three singleton edges and the initial upper edge.
The depths are read from the displayed valuations and
\(t\), subtracting the supporting chord on each edge.

Indeed for a root on an exact binomial face, dividing its
root equation by either face term leaves the two-term
root equation to relative error of valuation at least the
off-face depth. In particular the root on singleton
step \(l\) equals \(u_{j,l}q^{K_j(2l-j-1)}\) to that
precision. The root on the initial upper edge, raised to
power \(b\), equals
\((-b_{N,0}/b_{N,b})q^{-n b(N-b)}\) to that precision.
Multiply the lower quotient by the upper singleton
root and take its \(b\)-th power. Equality of the two
quotients then gives (6), since the resulting exponent
of \(q\) is \(b\{n(b+1)-2d\}\).
Independent affine scalar and argument normalizations
cancel in this comparison.

For \(E=1\) and \(Q\in\mathbb Q\), the unit case is impossible
and (2) forces the denominator in (2) to divide two.
It is even, so it equals two. This happens only at
\(b=1,d=n-1\): at \(b\ge2\) it is at least
\(b\{n(b-1)+2b\}>2\). Thus \(v_p(Q)=-1\).
In (6) the lower edges are the first and last, and the
upper edges are also the first and last. Now
\(C_{1,n-1,1}=R_n/R_N\).
At \(s=1/2\) the lower second differences are \(N\)
and the upper ones are \(n\) except for \(n-1\) at the
first internal index. Their endpoint-adjacent off-face
depths have minimum \(n-1\). This gives (3).
A square of a rational \(q\) cannot have valuation \(-1\).

For the odd-degree assertion about \(q\), apply the unit
test over the square clock field or over the field of \(q\)
itself. In the latter field the ramification index must
share a divisor with \(N\) if \(\gcd(E,N)=1\).
At every negative place the ramification index of \(q\)
is even when \(E\) is odd, by (2).
The global degree is the sum of the local ramification
indices times their residue degrees. It is therefore
even if \(N\) has no odd prime divisor. If it is odd for
a general \(N\), at least one summand is odd, and this
can only come from a unit place with an odd prime divisor
of \(N\) in its ramification index. That summand is at
least the smallest such divisor.

## A discriminant charge for every isolated mark prime

If \(E\) is odd and \(\gcd(E,N)=1\), every place over
the marked prime ramifies in \(F=\mathbb Q(q)\). For unit
places this is the unit-clock test, and for negative places
it follows from the even reduced denominator in (2).
There are no positive places. If \(D_F=[F:\mathbb Q]\),
then
\[
                v_p(\operatorname{Disc}F)
                    \ \ge\ \left\lceil D_F/2\right\rceil .
\]
To see this, let \(e_i,f_i\) be the local ramification and
residue degrees. The reduction of the integral trace pairing
on \(\mathcal O_F/p\mathcal O_F\) kills its nilradical:
multiplication by a nilpotent times any element is a
nilpotent linear map, whose trace is zero. The dimension of
the nilradical is \(D_F-\sum_i f_i\). Because every
\(e_i\ge2\) and \(\sum_i e_i f_i=D_F\), this dimension is
at least \(D_F/2\). The integral trace matrix has at
least that many Smith entries divisible by \(p\), giving
the claim. In particular the product of the distinct
depth-one marked primes, raised to
\(\lceil D_F/2\rceil\), divides the absolute discriminant
of the clock field. A candidate unramified at a single
such prime fails the angular contact test.

## Transfer to arbitrary ordinary tails

For a fixed finite core, the tests apply to the finite list
of positive reference clocks from the angular eliminant.
If all candidate clocks on a compact positive strength
range fail them, the Gaussian references separate there
uniformly in angle. The
[ordinary diffuse transfer](MULTIVARIATE_ARBITRARY_CORE_DIFFUSE_EXTENSION.md#uniform-transfer-to-the-added-ordinary-rows)
then excludes sufficiently diffuse appended ordinary tails
without a constraint on tail count, gaps, or bias.
This also applies around any fixed positive strength whose
clock is excluded at the marked pair.

For instance, let \(J\) be at least the
[absolute ordinary terminal cutoff](MULTIVARIATE_EFFECTIVE_ABSOLUTE_EVENTUAL_ANGLES.md),
and suppose the fixed core supplies a depth-one prime (1)
at each \(3\le N<J\). Put
\(L=\operatorname{lcm}_{\,2\le n<J-1}(n(n+1))\).
For any rational \(0<r<1\) the prescribed strength
\(W_0=-2L\log r\) has rational clock at each of these
pairs. There are effective neighborhoods of \(W_0\) and
diffuseness tolerances on which the actual ordinary extensions
satisfy SFC(2) in every degree. The first pair follows from
real positivity, and the cutoff supplies the high pairs.
For an unrestricted core, the availability of the necessary
marks or the exclusion of the remaining algebraic clocks
still needs a separate argument.
