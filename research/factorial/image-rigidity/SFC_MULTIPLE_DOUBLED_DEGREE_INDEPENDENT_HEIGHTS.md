# Independent common heights with several doubled degree classes

Fix distinct ordinary exponent offsets \(b_j\) in \(d\) variables,
with \(s\) columns. Group them by total degree \(d_j=|b_j|\).
Suppose each group contains at most two columns, and write \(h\)
for the number of pairs. Assume that the differences within all
pairs lie on a single real line. In two variables this holds
for every collection of pairs of equal total degree.

**Theorem.** Let \(I\) consist of \(s\) distinct positive orders,
at least \(h\) of which are even. There is a computable threshold
\(H\), depending on the offsets and \(I\), such that the ordinary
moments at those orders have no common nonzero coefficient
direction after adding any common integer vector \(L\) with
every \(L_i>H\). The heights \(L_i\) vary independently.
The conclusion holds over every characteristic-zero field.

In two variables this gives a finite exact decision for all
independent nonnegative common translations of the fixed
support and order set. In particular it applies at every
prescribed consecutive start and for arbitrary support size,
since any such window contains at least \(h\) even orders.
This extends the
[common-ray result with several pairs](SFC_COMMON_TRANSLATION_DOUBLED_DEGREE_BLOCKS.md)
and the
[independent-height result with one pair](SFC_INDEPENDENT_COMMON_HEIGHT_DECISION.md#2-a-doubled-degree-at-independent-scales).
The collinearity assumption permits a determinant of degree
one in each node instead of a resultant of quadrics.

## 1. A squarefree even-index coefficient cannot cancel

Let \(x_m\) be any choice of root of
\(\operatorname{He}_m(\sqrt m\,x)\) for each \(m\) in a finite
set of distinct positive indices. Call a polynomial
multiaffine when each of its variables occurs to degree at most
one.

**Theorem.** Let \(D(X)\) be a rational homogeneous multiaffine
polynomial in the indices in question. If some monomial using
only even indices has nonzero coefficient, then
\(D((x_m)_m)\ne0\).

Fix such a monomial and let \(E\) be its even index set.
Suppose the evaluation vanishes. For every index \(m\ge10\)
with \(x_m\ne0\), the
[scaled Hermite isolation theorem](SFC_SCALED_HERMITE_ROOT_ISOLATION.md#isolation-in-a-compositum)
implies that \(x_m\) is outside the field generated by all
the other chosen nodes, since its square already has degree
\(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) over that field. Separating constant and
linear coefficients in that variable shows that both evaluated
coefficients vanish. Select the coefficient of \(X_m\) when
\(m\in E\) and the constant coefficient otherwise. If \(x_m=0\),
take the constant coefficient, since \(m\notin E\).
Continue until only indices at most nine remain. The evaluated
polynomial still vanishes, and the coefficient of the remaining
even monomial is unchanged.

For the remaining variables use the valuations and unramified
unit cases of the
[small-index Hermite calculation](SFC_SCALED_HERMITE_ROOT_ISOLATION.md#low-indices).
At \(7\) the nodes of indices at most six lie in an unramified
extension. The nonzero nodes at indices \(7,8\) have valuations
\(-1/3,1/8\). If a nonzero node at \(9\) is selected, choose a
place at which it has valuation \(1/6\). Such a place exists
by Schur's irreducibility of the polynomial of the squared
nodes at index nine and the Newton slope with this valuation.
Pass to an embedding of the entire coefficient field extending
that place. Zero nodes at odd indices are set to zero.
The eight subset sums of
\[
                         -1/3,\quad 1/8,\quad 1/6
\]
are distinct modulo integers. Expand in the three variables
of indices \(7,8,9\). After evaluating the coefficients in
the lower-index nodes, each nonzero term from this expansion
has its own valuation class modulo integers. The sum can
vanish only if all those coefficients vanish. Retain the
coefficient which uses the required exponent of \(X_8\) and
omits the odd indices \(7,9\).

Now at \(5\) the nodes of indices at most four are unramified.
The indices \(5,6\) have nonzero-node valuations
\(-1/4,1/6\); the four subset sums are distinct modulo
integers. The same argument removes them, retaining the
required exponent of \(X_6\). At \(3\) the index \(4\)
has valuation \(1/4\), whereas the lower nodes are unramified.
Retain the required exponent of \(X_4\). Finally the nodes
at indices \(1,3\) are rational, and \(x_2=\pm1/\sqrt2\)
separates constant and linear terms over \(\mathbb Q\).
The selected even monomial uses exactly the homogeneous
degree of the original polynomial. Thus at the end its
coefficient is a nonzero constant, a contradiction.

## 2. The matrix at the independent-height limit

With no paired group, the distinct-degree argument of the
[independent-height theorem](SFC_INDEPENDENT_COMMON_HEIGHT_DECISION.md#1-exact-normalization-and-the-case-of-distinct-degrees)
applies. Assume at least one pair below.

For positive real heights \(L_i\), use the
[reciprocal-height normalization](SFC_INDEPENDENT_COMMON_HEIGHT_DECISION.md#1-exact-normalization-and-the-case-of-distinct-degrees).
At each fixed \(m\in I\), the normalized phase is
\(\sum_j C_j m^{d_j}Y^{b_j}\), where the \(Y_i\) are independent
Gammas of shape \(mL_i+1\) divided by \(mL_i\), with unit
rate before division, and the coefficient change to \(C\)
is independent of \(m\).

Use \(A_g\) for the sum of the coefficients in each degree
group \(g\). For a paired group use \(B_g\) for the coefficient
multiplying a difference of its two monomials. Write its
offset difference as \(\lambda_g v\), with \(v\) fixed
nonzero integral and \(\lambda_g\ne0\) rational. Let
\[
             \epsilon=\max_i L_i^{-1/2},\qquad
             \sigma^2=\sum_i v_i^2/L_i .
\]
Factoring the coordinatewise common monomial in a pair,
then expanding the remaining powers at \(Y=\mathbf1\), gives
\[
 \|Y^{b'_g}-Y^{b_g}-\lambda_g v\cdot(Y-\mathbf1)\|_m
                   =O(\epsilon\sigma).
\]
Indeed the nonlinear terms of the reduced difference involve
only coordinates where \(v_i\ne0\). Each such coordinate
fluctuates by \(O(\sigma)\) in the fixed moment norms, and all
coordinates fluctuate by \(O(\epsilon)\). Multiplication by
the common monomial changes the linear term by
\(O(\epsilon\sigma)\) as well. These are the same uniform
moment estimates as in the single-pair proof. The scaled
linear form \(v\cdot(Y-\mathbf1)/\sigma\) converges in moments
to a centered real Gaussian \(Z_m\) of variance \(1/m\)
as all heights grow, with no constraint on their ratios.

Let \(P(m)=\sum_g A_g m^{d_g}\). The norm of the fluctuation
from this constant is \(O(\epsilon\|A\|+\sigma\|B\|)\).
At a moment zero, expanding the power and applying Hölder's
inequality bounds \(|P(m)|\) by a constant times this norm.
The degree-group evaluation matrix at the selected orders
has full column rank by Descartes' rule of signs. Therefore
\(\|A\|=O(\sigma\|B\|)\) after absorbing the
\(O(\epsilon\|A\|)\) term for large heights. In a hypothetical
escaping sequence, normalize \(\|B\|=1\) and pass to limits
of \(A/\sigma\) and \(B\), denoted \(a_g,b_g\).
After dividing the normalized phases by \(\sigma\),
their limits in moments are
\[
 p_m+q_m Z_m,\qquad
          p_m=\sum_g a_g m^{d_g},\qquad
          q_m=\sum_{g\ \mathrm{paired}}\lambda_g b_g m^{d_g}.
\]
The vectors of limiting coefficients are not both zero.

At a vanishing limiting moment there is a scaled Hermite
node \(x_m\) with \(p_m=\mathrm i x_m q_m\).
If \(q_m=0\), the zero moment gives \(p_m=0\), so any
available node works. The resulting linear system has rows
\[
          \big(m^{d_g}\big)_{g}\ ,\
          \big(x_m m^{d_g}\big)_{g\ \mathrm{paired}},
\]
absorbing \(-\mathrm i\lambda_g\) into the contrast coefficients.
Its square determinant is homogeneous multiaffine of degree
\(h\) in the nodes. Choose \(h\) even orders \(E\).
The coefficient of \(\prod_{m\in E}X_m\) is, up to sign,
the product of the power evaluation determinants for the
paired degrees on \(E\) and for all degrees on \(I\setminus E\).
Both are nonzero by the same positive-order interpolation
argument. Section 1 makes the full determinant nonzero,
contrary to the nonzero limiting vector. This excludes any
escape with every height tending to infinity.

The normalization has rational polynomial coefficients in
the reciprocal heights. Searching for a positive real corner
with no simultaneous projective zero is effective by the
[real quantifier-elimination test](SFC_INDEPENDENT_COMMON_HEIGHT_DECISION.md#4-effective-search-in-two-independent-heights).
With two independent heights, each remaining collision lies
on one of finitely many horizontal or vertical fibers.
The homogeneous resultant on a fiber is a univariate polynomial
in its free common height. A nonzero polynomial gives a finite
list of integer zeros, and an identically zero one gives a
counterexample at any integer point of that fiber.
This proves the decision and transfers nonvanishing to
every characteristic-zero field. The uniform bound as the
offsets and the starting order grow is a separate problem.
