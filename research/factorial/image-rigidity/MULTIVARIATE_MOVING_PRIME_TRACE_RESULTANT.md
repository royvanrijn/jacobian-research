# Moving-prime resultant bounds from reciprocal traces

At a shared Newton slope, truncated parent polynomials can force
root quotients near the roots of a fixed algebraic polynomial.
This applies to nonbinomial faces and allows the primes and
truncations to change. A single integer resultant then bounds the
total certified depth at those primes, together with real
approximation to the same algebraic targets at a diffuse contact.

Let \(H\) be an irreducible rational common factor of consecutive
ordinary moment polynomials \(P_n,P_N\), \(N=n+1\), \(n\ge2\).
Write its nonzero roots as \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d\).
They are distinct and nonreal. For \(i<j\) set
\[
 t_{ij}=\alpha_i/\alpha_j+\alpha_j/\alpha_i,\qquad
 T_H(Y)=\prod_{i<j}(Y-t_{ij}).
\]
By symmetry \(T_H\in\mathbb Q[Y]\). Its least denominator is
the same \(D_H\) as for the
[ordered quotient polynomial](MULTIVARIATE_DEEP_BINOMIAL_PROJECTIVE_QUOTIENTS.md#exact-quotient-denominator).
Indeed at a finite place, writing \(s_i=v_p(\alpha_i)\), one has
\(v_p(t_{ij})=-|s_i-s_j|\) if \(s_i\ne s_j\) and
\(v_p(t_{ij})\ge0\) otherwise. Selecting exactly all roots
of negative valuation gives the unique minimum-valuation
term of that elementary symmetric sum. Thus
\[
                       v_p(D_H)=\sum_{i<j}|s_i-s_j|.              \tag{1}
\]

## A local certificate from arbitrary truncated faces

Fix a prime \(p\), a shared root valuation \(s=a/b\) in lowest
terms, and a uniformizer \(\pi\) with \(\pi^b=p\). Use the valuation
ring \(\mathcal O\) of \(\mathbb Q_p(\pi)\), normalized by
\(v_p(p)=1\). For either parent set
\[
 \mu_j=\min_k (b v_p(c_{j,k})+ak),\qquad
 F_j(X)=\pi^{-\mu_j}P_j(\pi^a X).
\]
Choose either or both parents. At a chosen parent retain a subset
of the polynomial indices containing all the minimizers, with
least and greatest retained index the left and right minimizers.
Divide the retained polynomial by the left monomial and its
leading coefficient to obtain a monic \(A_j\in\mathcal O[X]\)
with unit constant coefficient. Let \(L\) be a positive integer
such that all discarded coefficients of every chosen parent
\(F_j\) lie in \(\pi^L\mathcal O\). This uses the actual
valuation of each discarded term, and permits retaining
higher-valuation coefficients between the endpoints.

For a fixed \(f\in\mathbb Z[Y]\) of degree \(m\ge1\) form
\[
         M_f(X,Z)=(XZ)^m f(X/Z+Z/X)\in\mathbb Z[X,Z].
\]
In \((\mathcal O/(\pi^L))[X,Z]\) let \(\mathcal I\) be the ideal
generated by the \(A_j(X),A_j(Z)\) of the chosen parents.
The following certificates apply to **every** pair of roots
of \(H\) of this same valuation \(s\).

1. If \(M_f\in\mathcal I\), then \(v_p(f(t_{ij}))\ge L/b\).
2. If \((X-Z)M_f\in\mathcal I\) and some chosen \(A_j\bmod\pi\)
   is squarefree, the same depth \(L/b\) holds.
3. If \((X-Z)M_f\in\mathcal I\) without the squarefree hypothesis,
   the target \(\widetilde f(Y)=(Y-2)f(Y)^2\) satisfies
   \(v_p(\widetilde f(t_{ij}))\ge 2L/b\).

In fact \(x_i=\alpha_i/\pi^a\) is a unit and
\(v_p(A_j(x_i))\ge L/b\) for every chosen parent, by its
exact equation \(F_j(x_i)=0\). This proves the first
certificate directly. For the second, the reduction of
the normalized parent is a unit scalar times \(X^\ell A_j(X)\)
for the left index \(\ell\). It has simple roots at every
nonzero root of the reduction of \(A_j\). If two distinct
\(x_i,x_j\) reduced to the same such root, the divided
difference of the normalized parent at them would reduce
to its nonzero derivative there, although it vanishes
exactly. Hence \(x_i-x_j\) is a unit. The last certificate
follows from
\[
 t_{ij}-2=(x_i-x_j)^2/(x_i x_j).
\]
This argument imposes no tame-prime restriction.
The local test is finite. With one chosen parent the
ordinary two monic divisions give a remainder test. With
both parents one can use those same divisions first,
in a finite free module over \(\mathcal O/(\pi^L)\), then
test membership in the submodule generated by the
multiples of the other parent's polynomials in the
quotient. For a quadratic truncation with coefficients
\(a_0,a_1,a_2\), the first divisions already certify the
second case for \(f(Y)=Y-\theta\) whenever
\[
               a_1^2-(\theta+2)a_0a_2\equiv0\pmod{\pi^L}
\]
and the reduction of the quadratic is squarefree. In
the quotient by \(X^2+uX+v,\ Z^2+uZ+v\), where
\(u=a_1/a_2,\ v=a_0/a_2\), the remainder of
\((X-Z)(X^2+Z^2-\theta XZ)\) is
\((u^2-(\theta+2)v)(X-Z)\).

## The total depth and real-factor bound

For each prime and each shared slope \(s\) choose any certified
depth \(\kappa_{p,s}\ge0\) for one fixed target \(f\).
This means all equal-slope pairs above satisfy
\(v_p(f(t_{ij}))\ge\kappa_{p,s}\). These certificates may use
different parents and truncations at different primes or slopes.
Set \(\kappa_{p,s}=0\) when no certificate is used. Write
\(r_{p,s}=\#\{i:v_p(\alpha_i)=s\}\).

If no actual trace is a zero of \(f\), there is a nonzero integer
\[
             I_f=\operatorname{Res}(D_HT_H,f)
                          =D_H^m\prod_{i<j} f(t_{ij}).
\]
For different-slope pairs, integrality of the coefficients of
\(f\) gives \(v_p(f(t_{ij}))\ge -m|s_i-s_j|\).
The total of these negative bounds cancels the
\(m v_p(D_H)\) term by (1). Same-slope pairs therefore give
\[
           v_p(I_f)\ge
            \left\lceil\sum_s\binom{r_{p,s}}2\kappa_{p,s}
            \right\rceil.                                      \tag{2}
\]
The product of \(p\) to these exponents divides \(I_f\)
over any set of certified primes, including moving primes.

Normalize \(P_n\) by a positive scale of its argument and
a constant scalar so both endpoint coefficients equal one.
By log convexity of factorials, all resulting coefficients
are at most the respective binomial coefficients. The
usual monic root bound and its reciprocal place every
normalized root in \([2^{-n},2^n]\) in modulus. Thus every
trace is bounded in absolute value by \(2^{2n+1}\), regardless
of the ordinary profile. This bounds \(\prod |f(t_{ij})|\)
in terms of \(n\) and \(f\) alone.

At a fixed positive diffuse contact, let the matched factor
roots differ from the reference roots by \(O(\eta^\gamma)\)
on the compact annulus of the
[projective factor comparison](MULTIVARIATE_DIFFUSE_PROJECTIVE_FACTOR_SIEVE.md#consequences-at-finite-physical-diffuse-contacts).
Here \(\eta\) is the largest tail relative gap and \(\gamma\)
is its root-matching exponent. If a reference trace \(t_{ij,*}\)
is a zero of \(f\) of multiplicity \(m_{ij}\), its matched
factor is \(f(t_{ij})=O(\eta^{\gamma m_{ij}})\).
Use \(m_{ij}=0\) at the other reference traces. Coincident
reference roots give a sharper rate at target \(2\) by
\(t_{ij}-2=(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)^2/(\alpha_i\alpha_j)\).
Using (2) and the real product of these fixed factors gives
\[
 \sum_p \left\lceil\sum_s\binom{r_{p,s}}2\kappa_{p,s}\right\rceil
                     \log p
   +\gamma\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}\log(1/\eta)
                \le m\log D_H+O_{n,f,C,*}(1).                    \tag{3}
\]
The rate term is omitted for an unrestricted ordinary profile.
The finite allocation check can use the
[truncated Newton width bounds](MULTIVARIATE_PHYSICAL_TRUNCATED_NEWTON_FACES.md#common-root-widths-at-higher-precision)
for each possible \(r_{p,s}\). The right hand side can use the
[oriented block gcd bound](MULTIVARIATE_ORIENTED_BLOCK_DENOMINATOR_GCD.md)
or the exact valuation-dispersion formula for the same allocation.
There is no fixed-prime-set hypothesis.

Exact target traces can be removed. Let \(B(Y)\) be the
monic rational polynomial on the traces \(t_{ij}\) with
\(f(t_{ij})\ne0\), and \(D_B\) its least denominator.
This is rational by Galois invariance. Gauss's lemma
gives \(D_B\mid D_H\) by multiplying the primitive integer
forms of the removed and retained monic factors.
For monic \(f\) all removed values are algebraic integers,
so \(D_B=D_H\). Use
\(\operatorname{Res}(D_BB,f)\), which is nonzero even
if \(B=1\), and in (2) count only nonexact pairs having
equal slopes. The negative valuation computation still
works for \(B\), because its least denominator is the
sum of the absolute spreads for its own unequal-slope
pairs by the argument proving (1). Omit the corresponding
exact pairs from the real term in (3). In particular this
allows both finite target attainment and positive
remaining depth in the same collision hypothesis.

The [binomial-face estimate](MULTIVARIATE_DEEP_BINOMIAL_PROJECTIVE_QUOTIENTS.md#face-depth-and-the-all-degree-inequality)
supplies depths for the target given by
\(\rho^\ell+\rho^{-\ell}-2\) in the reciprocal trace
variable. The certificate here also supplies depths
from nonbinomial faces and arbitrary algebraic targets.
A supply of the certified moving primes, or suitable
bounds for the denominator budget in (3), is still
needed for exclusion over all diffuse tails.

## An ordinary nonbinomial example with unbounded depth

Fix \(p=31\), the orders \(3,4\), and a positive integer
\(E\). Use five unit-gap rows of signs \(+,+,+,-,-\).
Take the first two smaller exponents to be
\[
 a=31^E-1,\qquad b=(u_E31^{3E}-3)/4,
\]
where \(u_E\in\{1,3\}\) makes the numerator a multiple
of four. Their residues are \(30,7\bmod31\).
The remaining smaller exponents initially have residues
\(19,1,18\bmod31\), respectively, so their two ramps
consist of units at \(p\).

Write \(C_{j,k}=c_{j,k}/c_{j,0}\). Since the last two
rows are decreasing, their contributions to \(C_{j,k}\)
are the reciprocals of the last \(k\) factors of their
stripped ramps. The binomial factor is \(\binom jk\).
Direct substitution of the five bases gives
\[
       C_{3,1}\equiv C_{4,1}\equiv1,\qquad
       C_{3,2}\equiv C_{4,2}\equiv7 \pmod{31}.
\]
The three rational functions of the last three bases
\[
       C_{3,1}-C_{4,1},\qquad C_{3,2}-C_{4,2},\qquad
                    C_{4,1}^2-9C_{4,2}
\]
have unit denominators in these residue classes and
vanish modulo \(31\). Their Jacobian at the listed
residues is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}15&0&0\\11&6&14\\15&20&30\end{pmatrix}
                            \pmod{31},
 \qquad \det\equiv19\pmod{31}.
\]
For example each derivative of \(C_{j,k}\) at an increasing
base \(x\) is \(C_{j,k}\sum_{t=1}^k j/(jx+t)\).
At a decreasing base it is
\(-C_{j,k}\sum_{t=j-k+1}^j j/(jx+t)\).
At each precision step change the three bases by \(31^q\)
times a vector modulo \(31\) and solve the invertible
linear system from this Jacobian modulo \(31\).
This lifts the three congruences to modulus \(31^E\),
and the bases can be chosen nonnegative.

At order \(3\) the valuation ordinates are
\((0,0,0,E)\). At order \(4\) they are
\((0,0,0,3E,4E)\). Indeed the first base hits the last
factor in each order with depth \(E\), while the
second base hits only the third factor at order \(4\)
with depth \(3E\).
The only shared root valuation is zero and its width
is two. By even positivity any possible common factor
would therefore be quadratic. Its two roots are
\(p\)-adic units and all its denominator dispersion
is at other primes. The order-\(3\) truncated quadratic
has squarefree reduction \(1+X+7X^2\). The lifts give
its reciprocal trace \(7\bmod31^E\).
Thus the second certificate applies to \(f(Y)=Y-7\)
with depth \(E\). There is no exact target pair, since
the reciprocal trace of a nonreal conjugate pair is
real in \([-2,2)\). Equation (2), using that interval
directly in the real product, requires
\[
                                  D_H\ge31^E/9 .
\]
Both local faces have three terms and agree modulo
\(31^E\) after making them monic. The local truncated
gcd therefore retains width two even at that precision.
Here the new obstruction is the denominator cost of a
nonbinomial target. For example the quotient residue
does not satisfy \(\rho^{12}=1\): the recurrence for
\(\rho^k+\rho^{-k}\) with trace \(7\bmod31\)
gives \(12\bmod31\) at \(k=12\), different from \(2\).
