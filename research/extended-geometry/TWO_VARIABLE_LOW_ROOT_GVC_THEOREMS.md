# Low-root binary symbols in two-variable GVC

## 1. Scope and results

This note proves two balanced homogeneous GVC results in two variables.

> **Theorem 1.1 — two-root symbols in every degree.** Let \(\Lambda\) be a
> homogeneous constant-coefficient operator of order \(d\) whose binary
> symbol has at most two distinct roots over an algebraic closure.  Let
> \(P\) be homogeneous of degree \(d\).  If
> \[
>  \Lambda^m(P^m)=0\qquad(m\geq1),
> \]
> then, for every \(Q\),
> \[
>  \Lambda^m(QP^m)=0\qquad(m>\deg Q).
> \tag{1.1}
> \]

> **Theorem 1.2 — the quartic three-root orbit.** Let \(d=4\), and suppose
> the symbol of \(\Lambda\) has root partition \((2,1,1)\).  The first five
> pure moments already force the same conclusion (1.1).  On this orbit the
> first five moments cut out exactly the one-sided Segre nullcone.

> **Theorem 1.3 — the generic squarefree quartic orbit.** There is a
> nonempty Zariski-open set of squarefree binary-quartic symbol orbits such
> that the first six pure moments cut out exactly the four one-sided
> annihilator lines on the Segre cone.  In particular, every symbol in
> this open set satisfies (1.1) under the first-six-moment premise.

Together with the
[balanced cubic theorem](TWO_VARIABLE_CUBIC_GVC_THEOREM.md), these results
initially left only the squarefree binary-quartic symbol in balanced degree
four.  The later [split-symbol theorem](SPLIT_SYMBOL_GVC_THEOREM.md)
subsumes the all-order GVC conclusion in every balanced degree.  The
results here remain stronger finite-cutoff and explicit-radical statements.
They do not treat nonhomogeneous pairs or the full nonseparable SIC space.

## 2. All degrees with at most two symbol roots

After scalar extension and a linear change, write

\[
 \Lambda=\partial_x^r\partial_y^{d-r},
 \qquad 0\leq r\leq d.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Write

\[
 P(x,y)=x^d p(t),\qquad t=y/x,
\]

and form the Laurent polynomial

\[
 h(t)=t^{-(d-r)}p(t).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Full contraction extracts one coefficient:

\[
 \Lambda^m(P^m)
 =(rm)!((d-r)m)!\,[t^{(d-r)m}]p(t)^m
 =(rm)!((d-r)m)!\operatorname{CT}(h^m).
 \tag{2.3}
\]

The Duistermaat--van der Kallen constant-term theorem says that if every
positive power of a Laurent polynomial has zero constant term, then the
origin is outside its Newton polytope.  In one variable, all exponents of
\(h\) are therefore strictly positive or all are strictly negative.

If they are positive, every exponent of \(t\) in \(p\) is greater than
\(d-r\).  Hence every monomial of \(P\) has \(x\)-degree at most \(r-1\),
and

\[
 \deg_x(QP^m)\leq\deg Q+(r-1)m<rm
\]

for \(m>\deg Q\).  The factor \(\partial_x^{rm}\) kills the product.

If all Laurent exponents are negative, every monomial of \(P\) has
\(y\)-degree at most \(d-r-1\), and

\[
 \deg_y(QP^m)\leq\deg Q+(d-r-1)m<(d-r)m.
\]

The factor \(\partial_y^{(d-r)m}\) kills the product.  The endpoint cases
\(r=0,d\) are included: only the compatible one of the two alternatives
can occur.  This proves Theorem 1.1 after descending the resulting
polynomial identity to the original characteristic-zero field.

The same inequalities allow a fixed dual-variable factor in the SIC
multiplier, because it only adds derivatives.  Thus the corresponding
rank-one tensor lies in the pair-linear one-sided nullcone.

## 3. The quartic \((2,1,1)\) orbit

Normalize the symbol and polynomial to

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A(u,v)&=u^2v(u+v),\\
 P(x,y)&=ax^4+bx^3y+cx^2y^2+dxy^3+ey^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The first moment is

\[
 \mu_1=2(3b+2c),
\]

so put \(c=-3b/2\).  Let \(I\subset\mathbb Q[a,b,d,e]\) be generated by
the primitive parts of the restricted moments \(\mu_2,\mu_3,\mu_4,\mu_5\).
The expected one-sided locus has three components:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 J_1&=(a,b),
 &P&=y^3(dx+ey),\\
 J_2&=(b,d,e),
 &P&=ax^4,\\
 J_3&=(b+4a,d+4a,e-a),
 &P&=a(x-y)^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Put \(J=J_1\cap J_2\cap J_3\).  Exact reduction over \(\mathbb Q\) gives

\[
 I\subseteq J.
\tag{3.3}
\]

A degree-compatible Gröbner basis of \(J\) has five generators.  The fourth
power of every one of those five generators reduces to zero modulo \(I\):

\[
 j_i^4\in I,\qquad 1\leq i\leq5.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Each \(J_i\) is a prime linear ideal, so \(J\) is radical.  Equations
(3.3)--(3.4) prove

\[
 \sqrt I=J.
\tag{3.5}
\]

## 4. Eventual mixed vanishing

On \(J_1\), every monomial of \(P\) has \(x\)-degree at most one, while
\(\Lambda^m\) contains \(\partial_x^{2m}\).  Hence this factor kills
\(QP^m\) for \(m>\deg Q\).

On \(J_2\), the factor \(\partial_y^m\) annihilates \(P=ax^4\).  On \(J_3\),
the factor \((\partial_x+\partial_y)^m\) annihilates
\(P=a(x-y)^4\).  Since the operator factors commute, the Leibniz rule gives
zero for \(m>\deg Q\) in both cases.  This proves Theorem 1.2.

## 5. The generic squarefree quartic orbit

Normalize a squarefree symbol with ordered roots to

\[
 A_\lambda(u,v)=uv(u-v)(u-\lambda v),
 \qquad \lambda\ne0,1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The four expected one-sided polynomial lines are

\[
 x^4,\qquad y^4,\qquad (x+y)^4,\qquad(\lambda x+y)^4.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Each is annihilated by one of the four linear factors of the operator, so
every pure moment vanishes identically on these four sections over the
\(\lambda\)-line.

At the exact anchor \(\lambda=2\), write

\[
 P=ax^4+bx^3y+cx^2y^2+dxy^3+ey^4.
\]

The first moment gives \(b=2c-2d\).  Let
\(I\subset\mathbb Q[a,c,d,e]\) be generated by the restricted moments
\(\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_6\).  Let \(J\) be the intersection of the four prime
line ideals corresponding to

\[
 x^4,\quad y^4,\quad(x+y)^4,\quad(2x+y)^4.
\]

Exact degree-compatible reduction gives

\[
 I\subseteq J,\qquad
 \dim I=\dim J=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{mult}(I)=\operatorname{mult}(J)=4.
\tag{5.3}
\]

A reduced Gröbner basis of \(J\) has six generators
\(j_1,\ldots,j_6\), and the exact power certificate is

\[
 j_i^5\in I,\qquad 1\le i\le6.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus \(\sqrt I=J\), while equality of the multiplicities shows that the
projective special fiber consists of four reduced points.

Now form the projective moment family

\[
 X\subset\mathbb P^4_{[a:b:c:d:e]}
       \times(\mathbb A^1_\lambda\setminus\{0,1\})
\]

defined by \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_6\).  Its projection to the
\(\lambda\)-line is proper.  The fiber at \(\lambda=2\) has length four,
so upper semicontinuity of fiber dimension and of
\(h^0(\mathcal O_{X_\lambda})\) gives an open neighborhood \(U\) of
\(\lambda=2\) on which every fiber is zero-dimensional of length at most
four.  The four disjoint sections (5.2) lie in every fiber over the
squarefree locus, so every fiber over \(U\) has length at least four.
Consequently it has length exactly four and equals those four sections.
This proves Theorem 1.3.

The theorem is generic in the cross-ratio: it leaves a finite set of
possible exceptional squarefree orbits.  Determining that exceptional
set, or proving it empty, requires a uniform localized certificate over
\(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\).

## 6. Reproduction

Run

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_two_variable_quartic_three_root_gvc.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_two_variable_quartic_squarefree_generic.py
```

The checker derives the first five apolar moments, eliminates the first
linear equation, verifies \(I\subseteq J\), and checks all five
fourth-power certificates in (3.4) with exact Singular reduction.  The
all-degree two-root result uses the external constant-term theorem in the
written proof and no bounded computation.  The squarefree checker verifies
the four symbolic annihilator sections and the exact degree-four,
fifth-power radical certificate at \(\lambda=2\); the open-family argument
is the written proof above.
