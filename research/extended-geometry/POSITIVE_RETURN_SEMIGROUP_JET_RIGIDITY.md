# Positive return-semigroup jet rigidity

## 1. Scope and outcome

**Current status.**  This general rigidity theorem remains valid.  The
Hall-specific promotion statement it leaves behind belongs to the parked
GVC2ALL route; the later
[Hall-envelope theorem](BINARY_GVC_ENVELOPE_CLOSURE.md) proves unrestricted
binary GVC without it.

This note isolates a general theorem which occurs before the rectangular Hall
geometry.  Let \(A\) be any integral return matrix.  If its nonnegative return
monoid contains a vector which is positive in every coordinate, then the full
exponential return jet of a positive coefficient point has no invisible
directions except the coefficient-torus directions coming from the rows of
\(A\).

The theorem has three useful consequences.

1. It applies to every finite positive return configuration, not only a
   translated-binomial rectangle.
2. Although the premise contains all jet orders, Noetherianity shows that a
   finite set of period and jet orders already suffices for each fixed
   configuration.
3. Every finite-order twist of the untwisted positive point with the same
   complete period sequence is coefficient-torus flat, with no exceptional
   prime.  This includes arbitrary mixed-prime orders and is stronger than an
   eventual prime-power statement.

This is an earlier-chain rigidity theorem, but it is not the Hall
promotion theorem.  It does not compare two nontrivially phased baseline
points, turn a signed sum of packets into a positive return sum, or expose one
common high-digit quotient from a prime-dependent Hall shell.  Those are now
the precise surviving obstructions within that parked route.

## 2. Full return jets

Let \(A\in\mathbb Z^{r\times s}\), with columns
\(a_1,\ldots,a_s\), and put

\[
 S_A=\{n\in\mathbb N^s:A n=0\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Assume that there is an \(n^0\in S_A\) with \(n_i^0>0\) for every \(i\).
Equivalently, after restricting to the affine span of the columns, the origin
lies in the relative interior of their convex hull.  Fix positive real
numbers \(c_1,\ldots,c_s\).  For \(b\in\mathbb C^s\), define

\[
 Z_{b,N}(t)=
 \sum_{\substack{n\in S_A\\ |n|=N}}
 \frac{N!}{\prod_i n_i!}\,c^n
 \exp\!\bigl(t\langle b,n\rangle\bigr).
\tag{2.2}
\]

It is also the moving constant term

\[
 Z_{b,N}(t)=\operatorname {CT}_X
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^s c_i e^{t b_i}X^{a_i}\right)^N.
\tag{2.3}
\]

> **Theorem 2.1 (positive return-semigroup full-jet rigidity).**  Under the
> positive-return hypothesis,
> \[
>  Z_{b,N}(t)=Z_{0,N}(t)
>  \quad\text{for every }N\geq1
> \tag{2.4}
> \]
> as formal power series in \(t\) if and only if
> \[
>  b\in\operatorname {im}(A^{\mathsf T}:\mathbb C^r\longrightarrow
>  \mathbb C^s).
> \tag{2.5}
> \]

### Proof

Fix \(N\).  Group the finite sum (2.2) by the distinct complex numbers
\(\lambda=\langle b,n\rangle\).  Every grouped coefficient is a sum of
strictly positive real numbers.  The functions \(e^{\lambda t}\) for
distinct \(\lambda\) are linearly independent: evaluating the first
derivatives at zero gives their Vandermonde matrix.  Thus (2.4) forces

\[
 \langle b,n\rangle=0
 \qquad(n\in S_A).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Now take any \(z\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\).  For sufficiently large \(L\), both
\(Ln^0\) and \(Ln^0+z\) belong to \(S_A\).  Subtracting their two instances
of (2.6) gives \(\langle b,z\rangle=0\).  The integral kernel of an integral
matrix spans its complex kernel, so \(b\) annihilates
\(\ker_{\mathbb C}A\).  This is equivalent to (2.5).

Conversely, if \(b=A^{\mathsf T}y\), then
\(\langle b,n\rangle=\langle y,An\rangle=0\) on every return.  Each summand
in (2.2) is unchanged, proving (2.4).  \(\square\)

The positive return is doing two separate jobs.  Positivity of the weights
prevents distinct exponential classes from cancelling.  Strict positivity
of \(n^0\) makes the group completion of the nonnegative return monoid equal
to the full lattice kernel.  No freeness or unique factorization of \(S_A\)
is required.

## 3. A finite jet certificate

Suppose now that the \(c_i\) are positive rational numbers.  For \(k\geq1\)
write

\[
 D_{N,k}(b)=
 \left.\frac{d^k}{dt^k}Z_{b,N}(t)\right|_{t=0}
 =\sum_{\substack{n\in S_A\\ |n|=N}}
 \frac{N!}{\prod_i n_i!}\,c^n\langle b,n\rangle^k.
\tag{3.1}
\]

> **Corollary 3.1 (finite full-jet certificate).**  For every fixed
> \((A,c)\) satisfying the hypotheses, there is a finite set
> \(F\subset\mathbb N_{>0}^2\) such that
> \[
>  D_{N,k}(b)=0\quad((N,k)\in F)
>  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
>  b\in\operatorname {im}A^{\mathsf T}
> \tag{3.2}
> \]
> over every algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

### Proof

Let \(I\) be the ideal in \(\mathbb Q[b_1,\ldots,b_s]\) generated by all
polynomials \(D_{N,k}\), and let \(J\) be the linear prime ideal defining
\(\operatorname {im}A^{\mathsf T}\).  Theorem 2.1 says
\(V_{\mathbb C}(I)=V_{\mathbb C}(J)\), hence the Nullstellensatz gives
\(\sqrt{I\,\mathbb C[b]}=J\,\mathbb C[b]\).  Faithful flatness of
\(\mathbb C/\mathbb Q\) contracts this equality to \(\sqrt I=J\).  The
polynomial ring is Noetherian, so finitely many of the displayed generators
already generate \(I\).  Taking their indices gives \(F\), and their common
zero set is \(V(J)\).  \(\square\)

More concretely, after choosing rational linear generators
\(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_m\) of \(J\), the same proof supplies integers
\(M>0,D>0\) and polynomial identities

\[
 D\ell_j^M=\sum_{(N,k)\in F}H_{j,N,k}D_{N,k}
 \quad(1\leq j\leq m)
\tag{3.3}
\]

with integral coefficients after clearing denominators.  These identities
give finite arithmetic reductions of the full-jet theorem, although exact
torsion rigidity at the positive point has the simpler argument below.

## 4. All torsion twists of the positive point

Let \(h\geq1\), let \(u\in(\mathbb Z/h)^s\), let \(\zeta\) be a primitive
\(h\)-th root of unity, and, for Laurent variables
\(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_r)\), put

\[
 F_u(X)=\sum_{i=1}^s c_i\zeta^{u_i}X^{a_i},
 \qquad P_{u,N}=\operatorname {CT}_X F_u(X)^N.
\tag{4.1}
\]

> **Theorem 4.1 (torsion rigidity at a positive point).**  Fix an integral
> matrix \(A\), a strictly positive integral return \(n^0\), and positive
> real coefficients \(c_i\).  For every \(h\geq1\) and every
> \(u\in(\mathbb Z/h)^s\),
> \[
>  P_{u,N}=P_{0,N}\quad(N\geq1)
> \tag{4.2}
> \]
> implies that there is an \(\eta\in(\mathbb C^*)^r\) such that
> \[
>  \zeta^{u_i}=\eta^{a_i}\qquad(1\leq i\leq s).
> \tag{4.3}
> \]
> Hence \(F_u(X)=F_0(\eta_1X_1,\ldots,\eta_rX_r)\), so the collision is a
> flat coefficient-torus symmetry.  If \(\Delta_A\) is the product of the
> nonzero Smith invariant factors of \(A\) and
> \(\gcd(h,\Delta_A)=1\), then one may take
> \(\eta_j=\zeta^{y_j}\), equivalently
> \(u=A^{\mathsf T}y\pmod h\).

### Proof

For a fixed \(N\), write the two sides of (4.2) as

\[
 \sum_{\substack{n\in S_A\\|n|=N}}W_n\zeta^{\langle u,n\rangle}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \sum_{\substack{n\in S_A\\|n|=N}}W_n,
 \qquad
 W_n=\frac{N!}{\prod_i n_i!}c^n>0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The right side is the triangle-inequality maximum for the left side.  Equality
therefore forces

\[
 \zeta^{\langle u,n\rangle}=1
 \qquad(n\in S_A).
\tag{4.5}
\]

As in Theorem 2.1, write every \(z\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\) as the difference
\((Ln^0+z)-Ln^0\) of two nonnegative returns.  Equation (4.5) shows that
\(\langle u,z\rangle=0\pmod h\).  Thus \(u\) belongs to the annihilator of
\(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\) modulo \(h\).

Put \(\tau_i=\zeta^{u_i}\).  The preceding congruence says
\(\tau^z=1\) for every \(z\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\).  The monomial map
\[
 \phi_A:(\mathbb C^*)^r\longrightarrow(\mathbb C^*)^s,\qquad
 \eta\longmapsto(\eta^{a_1},\ldots,\eta^{a_s})
\tag{4.6}
\]
has image exactly the subtorus cut out by those kernel characters.
Indeed, Smith coordinates reduce this assertion to surjectivity of the power
maps \(x\mapsto x^{d_i}\) on \(\mathbb C^*\).  Thus
\(\tau=\phi_A(\eta)\), proving (4.3) for every \(h\).

In the same Smith coordinates, \(\eta\) can be chosen \(h\)-torsion exactly
when the relevant invariant factors can be inverted modulo \(h\); in
particular this holds when \(\gcd(h,\Delta_A)=1\), and gives the final
congruence assertion.  \(\square\)

There is therefore no exceptional torsion prime in characteristic zero.
The twist labels may vary arbitrarily with \(h\), and no bound on their
digits, prime factors, or prime-power exponents is used.  The saturation
index records only whether the witnessing torus parameter can be chosen
inside the same group of \(h\)-th roots.

## 5. The smallest Cartesian square has a four-jet certificate

Corollary 3.1 is nonconstructive about the required jet orders.  The first
two-dimensional translated-binomial rectangle already admits a small exact
certificate.  Put

\[
 H_b(\epsilon;t,w)=
 \sum_{j,k=0}^2\binom2j\binom2k
 e^{\epsilon b_{jk}}t^jw^k,
 \qquad
 M_{b,N}(\epsilon)=[t^Nw^N]H_b(\epsilon;t,w)^N.
\tag{5.1}
\]

> **Proposition 5.1 (exact centered-square fourth-jet certificate).**  If
> \[
>  M_{b,N}^{(k)}(0)=0
>  \qquad(1\leq N\leq3,\ 1\leq k\leq4),
> \tag{5.2}
> \]
> then
> \[
>  b_{jk}=B(j-1)+C(k-1).
> \tag{5.3}
> \]

### Exact certificate

Subtracting a flat label does not change any return row.  Use it to impose
\(b_{01}=b_{10}=0\), and write the remaining labels in row-major order as

\[
 (a,b,c,d,e,f,g)=(b_{00},b_{02},b_{11},b_{12},b_{20},b_{21},b_{22}).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Over \(\mathbb Q[a,b,c,d,e,f,g]\), the ideal generated by (5.2) has a
degree-reverse-lexicographic standard basis of size eleven and quotient
dimension forty.  The exact basis contains

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &d+f,\qquad c,\qquad a+b+3d+e+3f+g,\qquad g^4,\\
 &4f^4+2f^2g^2-bg^3-eg^3,\\
 &e^3-3e^2f-4bf^2-ef^2-f^3+e^2g-2efg-7f^2g
   +2bg^2+3eg^2-fg^2+g^3,\\
 &2b^2+3be+2e^2+bf-ef+5f^2+bg+eg+g^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

At a common zero, these equations successively give
\(g=f=e=b=0\), followed by \(d=c=a=0\).  Thus the gauge slice has only the
origin, which proves (5.3).  The exact replay also records how the affine
dimension drops with the available jets:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\text{largest jet order}&1&2&3&4\\ \hline
\text{affine dimension}&4&2&1&0.
\end{array}
\tag{5.6}
\]

In particular, quadratic equations alone retain complex isotropic ghosts;
the real sum-of-squares Hessian argument cannot simply be transported to an
algebraically closed residue field.  Cubic jets still leave a curve.  The
quartic jet is the first one in this exact smallest-square calculation which
kills the entire complex gauge slice.

## 6. What this removes, and what it does not

Theorem 2.1 subsumes the positive-Hessian kernel calculation: the latter is
only the second derivative of the full exponential return partition
function.  Theorem 4.1 is stronger than fixed-label adelic separation because
the label and its torsion order may both vary.  Thus prime-varying labels and
mixed-prime torsion are not obstructions once a Hall component has been
promoted to a twist of one fixed positive return configuration with the
untwisted component on the other side.

There is also an exact paired-point version once independent coefficient
polarizations are available.  Let \(c_i,\alpha_i,\beta_i\in\mathbb C^*\) and
introduce independent marks \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_s\):

\[
 \mathcal P_{\alpha,N}(Y)=\operatorname {CT}_X
 \left(\sum_i c_i\alpha_iY_iX^{a_i}\right)^N.
\tag{6.1}
\]

> **Theorem 6.1 (polarized paired-point rigidity).**  Under the positive
> return-vector hypothesis,
> \[
>  \mathcal P_{\alpha,N}(Y)=\mathcal P_{\beta,N}(Y)
>  \quad(N\geq1)
> \tag{6.2}
> \]
> if and only if \(\beta/\alpha\) lies in the coefficient torus
> \(\operatorname {im}\phi_A\).  In fact equality at finitely many values of
> \(N\), depending only on \(A\) and a chosen positive return, suffices.

Indeed, the coefficient of \(Y^n\) in (6.1) is the nonzero scalar

\[
 \frac{N!}{\prod_i n_i!}\,c^n\alpha^n
\tag{6.3}
\]

when \(n\in S_A\) and is zero otherwise.  Thus (6.2) says
\((\beta/\alpha)^n=1\) on every nonnegative return.  The positive return
upgrades this to every \(z\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\), and the torus argument in
Theorem 4.1 gives \(\beta/\alpha\in\operatorname {im}\phi_A\).  Conversely,
a coefficient-torus ratio is one on every return monomial.  For finiteness,
choose a lattice basis \(z_1,\ldots,z_m\) of \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\) and write
each \(z_j\) as \((L_jn^0+z_j)-L_jn^0\); only the finitely many total degrees
of those displayed returns are needed.

For those finitely many homogeneous polynomials, coefficient equality is
equivalent to equality of a finite mixed Euler-derivative jet at
\(Y=(1,\ldots,1)\).  Thus Theorem 6.1 asks the Hall tower to inherit only a
finite list of common marked derivatives for each fixed return matrix, not an
infinite family of new variables.

Consequently paired phases are not a toric rigidity problem either, provided
the shell supplies enough common marks to recover the individual return
monomials.  The Hall shell does not currently supply arbitrary independent
\(Y_i\): it supplies a signed scalar sum together with the adjacent-channel
Cartesian derivative tower.

Accordingly the implication left in that route is not factorial or toric
rigidity.  It is the following promotion problem.

> A tied Cartesian Hall shell must either expose a fixed positive component
> against the untwisted point, or supply enough common polarizations to
> separate two phased components and signed cross-packet sums, all over one
> prime-independent high-digit quotient.

Theorem 4.1 closes the first alternative after exposure.  Proposition 5.1
shows concretely that four cyclotomic coefficients suffice on the smallest
mixed rectangle, and Theorem 6.1 closes paired baselines after full marking.
What remains within the route is to inherit either of those separating
structures from the signed Hall shell over one common high quotient.  This
note supplies no binary counterexample; the unrestricted theorem is the
independent Hall-envelope result.

### Why the earlier operator reductions do not remove this step

De Bondt's shifted-power reduction is genuinely useful here: after the pure
vanishing statement has been promoted, it is enough to prove the tails with
multiplier \(P^d\), rather than carry an arbitrary polynomial multiplier
through the shell.  His reduction of a general constant-coefficient operator
to a sum of pure powers is stable rather than binary: it introduces auxiliary
variables.  It therefore does not preserve the two-dimensional Hall return
matrix, its packet support, or its Graver complexity, and cannot justify
replacing the binary operator by a two-endpoint symbol before promotion.
See M. de Bondt,
[A reduction for the Generalized Vanishing Conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2836).
The homogeneous binary case remains safe because its symbol splits into
linear factors, but the filtered corrections are exactly what create the
present nonfree shell.
