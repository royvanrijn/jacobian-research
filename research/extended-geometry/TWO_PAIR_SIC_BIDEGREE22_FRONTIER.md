# The two-pair bidegree-\((2,2)\) theorem

## 1. Status

This note proves two results about direct compression of the four-term
three-pair Image-Mathieu counterexample:

1. its natural four-parameter linear compression to two pairs contains no
   counterexample; and
2. every bihomogeneous two-pair polynomial of bidegree \((2,2)\) satisfies
   the Special Image Conjecture.

The global theorem is an exact characteristic-zero radical calculation.
The earlier support census through seven terms is retained because it gives
small explicit one-sided normal forms and an independent sparse regression.
This result proves only the complete bidegree-\((2,2)\) stratum.  The later
[bidegree-\((4,4)\) witness](TWO_PAIR_IMAGE_MATHIEU_COUNTEREXAMPLE.md)
shows that unrestricted \(\operatorname{SIC}(2)\) is false.

Use contraction pairs \((W,Z),(V,Y)\), with

\[
 \mathcal E_2(W^aV^bZ^cY^d)
 =\partial_Z^a\partial_Y^b Z^cY^d.                         \tag{1.1}
\]

## 2. The natural linear compression is one-sided

Substitute linear forms for the homogenizing pair in the three-pair
witness:

\[
 t=cZ+dY,\qquad \tau=aW+bV.
\]

This gives the bihomogeneous four-parameter family

\[
 f_{a,b,c,d}
 =(aW+bV)\bigl(cZ+(d-1)Y\bigr)
  \bigl(WZ+V(cZ+dY)\bigr).                                 \tag{2.1}
\]

Put

\[
 A=ac+b(d-1).                                               \tag{2.2}
\]

Direct exact contraction gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal E_2(f_{a,b,c,d})
   &=(2d+1)A,\\
 \mathcal E_2(f_{a,b,c,d}^2)
   &=4(6d^2+3d+1)A^2.                                     \tag{2.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

If both expressions vanish and \(A\ne0\), then the first equation forces
\(d=-1/2\), while the quadratic factor in the second equation becomes

\[
 6(-1/2)^2+3(-1/2)+1=1,
\]

a contradiction.  Hence \(A=0\).

Let

\[
 \ell=aW+bV,\qquad r=cZ+(d-1)Y.
\]

The condition \(A=0\) says that the differential linear form \(\ell\)
annihilates \(r\).  In nondegenerate adapted pair coordinates write
\(\ell=U_1\) and \(r=X_2\).  The remaining bilinear factor has the form

\[
 \alpha U_1X_1+\beta U_1X_2+\gamma U_2X_1+\delta U_2X_2.
\]

The only term of \(f\) that can survive the first contraction is
\(\gamma U_1U_2X_1X_2\).  Since \(A=0\), the first identity in (2.3) gives
\(\gamma=0\).  Thus every nonzero monomial of \(f\) has positive weight
for

\[
 \operatorname{wt}(U_1)=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{wt}(X_1)=-1,\qquad
 \operatorname{wt}(U_2)=\operatorname{wt}(X_2)=0;
\]

the three possible weights are \(1,2,1\).  For every fixed multiplier
\(g\), the weight of \(gf^m\) is therefore eventually positive, whereas a
nonzero contraction requires weight zero.  Consequently

\[
 \mathcal E_2(gf^m)=0
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\).  Degenerate cases are zero or have the
same immediate one-sided description.

> **Theorem 2.1.** The natural linear compression family (2.1) contains no
> counterexample to \(\operatorname{SIC}(2)\).

## 3. Exact sparse bidegree-\((2,2)\) census

Use the nine-monomial basis

\[
 M_{ij}=W^{2-i}V^iZ^{2-j}Y^j,\qquad 0\leq i,j\leq2.        \tag{3.1}
\]

For

\[
 f=\sum_{(i,j)\in S}a_{ij}M_{ij},
\]

every \(f^m\) has equal total dual and coordinate degree.  Hence
\(\mathcal E_2(f^m)\) is a scalar.  If
\((n_{ij})_{(i,j)\in S}\) records a term in the multinomial expansion,
put

\[
 \sum n_{ij}=m,\qquad s=\sum i\,n_{ij}.
\]

The term survives exactly when

\[
 \sum(i-j)n_{ij}=0,
\]

and its contribution is

\[
 \frac{m!}{\prod n_{ij}!}(2m-s)!\,s!
 \prod a_{ij}^{n_{ij}}.                                   \tag{3.2}
\]

For every exact support \(S\) of size at most seven, the checker normalizes
one coefficient to one and adjoins the inverse of the product of all other
coefficients.  Singular then computes over \(\mathbb Q\) whether the
localized ideal generated by the first six contractions is the unit ideal
through support size six; size seven uses the first eight contractions.
Through five terms the complete result is

\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r|l}
 |S|&\text{charts}&\text{survivors}&\text{surviving weights }i-j\\ \hline
 1&9&6&\{-2\},\{-1\},\{1\},\{2\}\\
 2&36&6&\{-2,-1\},\{-1,-1\},\{1,1\},\{1,2\}\\
 3&84&2&\{-2,-1,-1\},\{1,1,2\}\\
 4&126&0&--\\
 5&126&0&--
\end{array}                                                \tag{3.3}
\]

Every surviving support is strictly one-sided.  Therefore all its pure
contractions vanish for weight reasons, and for every fixed \(g\) its mixed
contractions vanish eventually.

For six terms, \(84\) exact supports reduce to eight one-dimensional
localized charts.  Simultaneous pair reversal and transposition reduce them
to four representatives.  If the first coefficient is normalized to one,
their reduced Laurent curves have the following parametrizations:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{support}&\text{coefficient vector}\\ \hline
(0,1,2,3,4,5)&(1,-2q,q^2,q^{-1},-2,q)\\
(0,1,2,6,7,8)&(1,q,q^2/4,-4q^{-2},-4q^{-1},-1)\\
(0,1,3,5,7,8)&(1,q,-q^{-1},q,-q^{-1},-1)\\
(1,2,3,5,6,7)&(1,q,q^{-2},-1,-q^{-3},-q^{-2}),
\end{array}
\qquad q\ne0.                                             \tag{3.4}
\]

Here a support tuple lists the row-major indices of \(M_{ij}\).  Singular
proves equality of each reduced curve with the radical of the first-six-
moment ideal on the localized chart.  The required powers of the five
displayed radical generators are respectively

\[
 (1,2,2,2,1),\quad
 (1,2,2,2,2),\quad
 (1,2,2,1,1),\quad
 (6,6,6,6,6).                                             \tag{3.5}
\]

The four families factor as

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &W(W+q^{-1}V)(Z-qY)^2,\\
 &\frac{(qW-2V)(qW+2V)(qY+2Z)^2}{4q^2},\\
 &\frac{(qW-V)(qY+Z)(VY+WZ)}q,\\
 &\frac{(qW-V)(Z+qY)(q^2WY+VZ)}{q^3}.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{3.6}
\]

In each line one displayed dual factor annihilates the displayed coordinate
factor.  Adapted pair coordinates put the remaining
bilinear factor on the diagonal; the only weights are \(1,2,1\).
Consequently all eight six-term charts are hidden one-sided families.

For seven terms, \(36\) exact supports reduce under moments through order
eight to twelve one-dimensional charts.  Four representatives cover them
under the same symmetries.  Their reduced Laurent curves are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{support}&\text{coefficient vector}\\ \hline
(0,1,2,3,4,6,8)&
 (1,\frac43q,\frac13q^2,-\frac4{3q},-\frac43,
       \frac1{3q^2},-\frac13)\\
(0,1,2,3,4,7,8)&
 (1,\frac32q,\frac12q^2,-q^{-1},-1,
       -\frac1{2q},-\frac12)\\
(0,1,2,4,5,6,7)&
 (1,3q,2q^2,-2,-2q,-q^{-2},-q^{-1})\\
(0,1,2,4,5,6,8)&
 (1,4q,3q^2,-4,-4q,-q^{-2},1),
\end{array}
\qquad q\ne0.                                             \tag{3.7}
\]

The powers proving that their six displayed prime Laurent ideals equal the
radicals of the localized moment ideals are

\[
 (5,5,5,5,6,5),\quad
 (6,6,3,6,6,6),\quad
 (1,6,6,6,6,6),\quad
 (6,6,6,3,6,6).                                           \tag{3.8}
\]

Every family in (3.7) factors as

\[
 (qW-V)(Z+qY)B_q(W,V,Z,Y).                                \tag{3.9}
\]

Use adapted coordinates

\[
\begin{aligned}
 U_1&=qW-V,&U_2&=qW+V,\\
 X_1&=Z-qY,&X_2&=Z+qY.
\end{aligned}
\]

Up to nonzero scalar factors, the four bilinear quotients become

\[
\begin{aligned}
 U_1X_1+U_1X_2+U_2X_2,\qquad&
 U_1X_1+U_1X_2+2U_2X_2,\\
 -U_1X_1+U_1X_2+2U_2X_2,\qquad&
 -U_1X_1+U_1X_2+U_2X_2.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{3.10}
\]

The balancing term \(U_2X_1\) is absent in every case.  Hence these twelve
charts also have only the positive weights \(1,2,1\).

> **Theorem 3.1.** Let \(f\) be bihomogeneous of bidegree \((2,2)\) in two
> contraction pairs and suppose that \(f\) has at most seven expanded
> monomials.  If its first six pure contractions vanish when \(f\) has at
> most six terms, or its first eight pure contractions vanish when \(f\)
> has seven terms, then
> \[
>  f\text{ is pair-linearly one-sided}.
> \]
> Consequently \(f\) satisfies
> \[
>  \mathcal E_2(gf^m)=0
> \]
> for every fixed \(g\) and all sufficiently large \(m\).  In particular,
> no such \(f\) is a counterexample to \(\operatorname{SIC}(2)\).

This is stronger than merely excluding all-order counterexamples: six
initial pure contractions suffice through six terms, and eight suffice at
seven terms.

## 4. The global bidegree-\((2,2)\) nullcone

The sparse calculation illustrates why a finite survivor is not yet a
counterexample.  One rational point that survived an initial moment census
was

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &W^2ZY-W^2Y^2+WVZ^2-WVY^2+V^2Z^2-V^2ZY\\
 &\qquad=-(V+W)(Y-Z)(VZ+WY).
\end{aligned}                                              \tag{4.1}
\]

With

\[
 U_1=V+W,\quad U_2=W-V,\quad
 X_1=Z+Y,\quad X_2=Z-Y,
\]

the right side is a scalar multiple of

\[
 U_1X_2(U_1X_1-U_2X_2),
\]

which is strictly one-sided.

We now close the eight- and nine-term charts simultaneously.  Write

\[
 f=\sum_{i,j=0}^2x_{3i+j}M_{ij}.                            \tag{4.2}
\]

A finite flag chart of the pair-linear one-sided locus is

\[
 f_0=V'Z'(AW'Z'+BV'Z'+CV'Y'),                              \tag{4.3}
\]

with the pairing-preserving shear

\[
 W'=W,\qquad V'=V-qW,\qquad Z'=Z+qY,\qquad Y'=Y.            \tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed \(W'Z'+V'Y'=WZ+VY\).  Expanding (4.3) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_0&=q(-A+Bq),&
x_1&=q^2(-2A+2Bq+C),&
x_2&=q^3(-A+Bq+C),\\
x_3&=A-2Bq,&
x_4&=-2q(-A+2Bq+C),&
x_5&=-q^2(-A+2Bq+2C),\\
x_6&=B,&
x_7&=2Bq+C,&
x_8&=q(Bq+C).
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{4.5}
\]

Let \(J\subset\mathbb Q[x_0,\ldots,x_8]\) be the kernel of this
parametrization.  Exact elimination gives the following twelve generators:

\[
\begin{aligned}
j_1={}&2x_0+x_4+2x_8,\\
j_2={}&2x_2x_6+2x_5x_7-x_4x_8+2x_8^2,\\
j_3={}&2x_1x_6+2x_5x_6+x_4x_7-2x_3x_8+2x_7x_8,\\
j_4={}&x_4^2-4x_3x_5-4x_1x_7-8x_5x_7-16x_8^2,\\
j_5={}&2x_2x_3+x_4x_5+2x_2x_7-2x_1x_8+2x_5x_8,\\
j_6={}&x_1x_3+2x_3x_5+2x_1x_7+3x_5x_7+2x_4x_8+8x_8^2,\\
j_7={}&x_2x_4x_7-2x_1x_5x_7-2x_5^2x_7+x_1x_4x_8
       +2x_4x_5x_8+4x_2x_7x_8-4x_1x_8^2,\\
j_8={}&2x_5^2x_6-x_4x_5x_7+2x_2x_7^2+2x_3x_5x_8
       +2x_1x_7x_8+12x_5x_7x_8-4x_4x_8^2+16x_8^3,\\
j_9={}&x_4x_5x_6-2x_3x_5x_7-2x_5x_7^2+x_3x_4x_8
       +4x_5x_6x_8+2x_4x_7x_8-4x_3x_8^2,\\
j_{10}={}&2x_3x_5x_6-x_3x_4x_7+2x_5x_6x_7-2x_4x_7^2
       +2x_3^2x_8+4x_4x_6x_8+2x_3x_7x_8
       -4x_7^2x_8+16x_6x_8^2,\\
j_{11}={}&x_1x_4x_5+2x_4x_5^2-2x_1x_2x_7-2x_2x_5x_7
       -2x_1^2x_8-4x_2x_4x_8-2x_1x_5x_8
       +4x_5^2x_8-16x_2x_8^2,\\
j_{12}={}&x_1x_5^2x_7+2x_5^3x_7-x_2^2x_7^2
       -2x_1x_2x_7x_8-8x_2x_5x_7x_8-x_1^2x_8^2
       +4x_5^2x_8^2-16x_2x_8^3.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{4.6}
\]

The kernel \(J\) is prime.  The finite \(q\)-chart is dense in the
projective flag line, so its image closure is exactly the full pair-linear
one-sided nullcone.

Let

\[
 I=(\mathcal E_2(f),\mathcal E_2(f^2),\ldots,
       \mathcal E_2(f^6)).                                 \tag{4.7}
\]

The moments are constructed independently from (3.2).  Exact Gröbner
reduction over \(\mathbb Q\) proves

\[
 I\subseteq J,\qquad
 j_1\in I,\qquad
 j_r^7\in I\quad(2\le r\le12).                             \tag{4.8}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \sqrt I=J.                                                \tag{4.9}
\]

> **Theorem 4.1.** Let \(f\) be any bihomogeneous polynomial of bidegree
> \((2,2)\) in two contraction pairs over a characteristic-zero field.  If
> \(\mathcal E_2(f^m)=0\) for \(1\le m\le6\), then after scalar extension
> to an algebraic closure, \(f\) is pair-linearly one-sided.  Therefore
> \[
>   \mathcal E_2(gf^m)=0
> \]
> for every fixed \(g\) and all sufficiently large \(m\).  In particular,
> the Special Image Conjecture holds on the entire bidegree-\((2,2)\)
> stratum.

This includes all nine exact eight-term supports and the full nine-term
support.  The theorem is now the sharp safe low-degree boundary preceding
the bidegree-\((4,4)\) counterexample; full bidegree \((3,3)\) remains
undecided.

Run

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_two_pair_sic_bidegree22_frontier.py
```

The script derives the scalar moments from (3.2), independently checks the
moment generator against direct symbolic contraction, eliminates the
four-parameter nullcone chart, verifies all twelve global power
certificates in (4.8), runs all \(501\) localized charts of sizes one
through seven over \(\mathbb Q\), verifies the eight six-term and twelve
seven-term radical certificates, verifies the hidden one-sided
factorizations and the linear-compression identities, and writes
`artifacts/generated-results/two_pair_sic_bidegree22_frontier.json`.
