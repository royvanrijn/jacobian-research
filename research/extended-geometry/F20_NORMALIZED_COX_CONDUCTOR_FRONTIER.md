# The normalized Cox-conductor frontier for the corrected \(F_{20}\) cover

## Status and outcome

This note computes the global normalization and conductor that were left
open by the natural multi-Rees calculation.  Write

\[
 R=\mathbf Q[s,t,X]/(P),
\]

where \(P\) is the corrected Lecacheux polynomial.  Its normalization is a
finite three-generated \(R\)-module, its conductor is generated by three
explicit polynomials, and the conductor square reduces global descent to one
equalizer.  The length-\(57\) pre-normalization residue splits into
five exact collision packets.

There is also a sharp negative conclusion.  Both nontrivial global
normalization generators have order two at the triple-\(E_1\) valuation.
Thus normalization does **not** supply the primitive value-one column needed
by \(D_d\).  That column first appears as an exceptional Cox variable on a
controlled transform or regular resolution.  Extending the natural
degree-\((3,1,1)\) product to the normalization still does not contain
\(P_X\).

On the normalized \(q\)-conductor cover, the slope-residual discriminant is
the square \(L(w)^2\), and the distinguished derivative residue \(L(w)\) is
anti-invariant.  This settles the total parity in the conductor equalizer,
but it does not yet distribute a residue cocycle among the three individual
columns \(D_d,D_q,D_r\).
Consequently inverse-adjugate polynomiality and affine-space recognition are
still not reached.

The exact checker is
[`verify_f20_normalized_cox_conductor.py`](../scripts/verify_f20_normalized_cox_conductor.py),

## 1. General conductor and exceptional-value gates

Let \(A\) be a reduced Noetherian domain whose normalization \(B\) is finite,
and put

\[
 \mathfrak c=\operatorname{Ann}_A(B/A).
\]

The conductor is an ideal of both \(A\) and \(B\).  The standard conductor
square gives

\[
 A\simeq B\mathop{\times}_{B/\mathfrak c}A/\mathfrak c. \tag{1.1}
\]

Hence a section on the normalization descends precisely when its two
restrictions agree on the conductor.  Valuation equality is only the first
part of this condition; unit and residue data survive in the lower row of
(1.1).

Now let \(v\) be an exceptional divisorial valuation centered on
\(\operatorname{Spec}B\), and let

\[
 S_v(B)=\{v(f):0\ne f\in B\}\cap\mathbf N.
\]

> **Exceptional-value gate.** If a desired effective Cox column has
> coefficient \(e\notin S_v(B)\), no ordinary integral ideal on
> \(\operatorname{Spec}B\) realizes that column.  A realization must pass to
> a modification on which the valuation is represented by an exceptional
> divisor and then use its divisorial or reflexive ideal.  In particular,
> normalization alone is insufficient whenever \(1\notin S_v(B)\) and the
> target coefficient is one.

Indeed, the value of a finitely generated ideal is the minimum of the values
of its generators.  The assertion follows immediately.  This gate is
independent of the presentation of a Rees algebra and therefore precedes
elimination.

Together, (1.1) and the exceptional-value gate give the correct global
pipeline:

\[
 \text{normalize}\longrightarrow
 \text{resolve/controlled transform}\longrightarrow
 \text{form the exceptional Cox algebra}\longrightarrow
 \text{solve the conductor equalizer}.             \tag{1.2}
\]

## 2. The exact global normalization

Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_1&=4Xt-2st+2X-2s+1,\\
 c_2&=X^2-Xs-2X+s,\\
 c_3&=2s^2t-2Xs+2s^2+2X+3s-4.
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{2.1}
\]

The last generator satisfies \(c_3=-h_q\), with \(h_q\) as in the natural
incidence ideal.  Exact `normal.lib` computation, followed by equality tests
in the original quotient presentation, gives

\[
 \mathfrak c=(c_1,c_2,c_3),                         \tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
 \overline R
 =R+R\frac{dc_1}{c_2}+R\frac{dc_3}{c_2}.            \tag{2.3}
\]

An affine presentation of \(\overline R\) has five variables and twelve
relations.  Formulae (2.2)--(2.3), rather than that larger presentation, are
the useful input to the next Cox construction.

At either conjugate triple-\(E_1\) center, use

\[
 s=2i+\tau^4,\qquad
 t=-\frac34+\frac i2+z\tau^4,\qquad
 X=1+i+\tau^2(b+\tau Y),\qquad b^2+i=0.             \tag{2.4}
\]

The conductor orders are

\[
 \bigl(v(c_1),v(c_2),v(c_3)\bigr)=(2,4,2),          \tag{2.5}
\]

while \(v(d)=4\).  Therefore

\[
 v(dc_1/c_2)=v(dc_3/c_2)=2.                        \tag{2.6}
\]

The original local incidence ring has no positive value below two, and the
module description (2.3) shows the same for its affine normalization at
this exceptional valuation.  The coefficient
\(D_d(E_{1,\mathrm{ram}})=1\) is consequently an exceptional Cox degree,
not a regular function supplied by normalization.

## 3. Primary packets of the finite residue

Let

\[
 J=I_d^3I_qI_r\subset R,\qquad C=(J:P_X).
\]

The earlier computation gave \(\operatorname{length}(R/C)=57\) and base
projection length \(33\).  Exact primary decomposition now assigns every
piece to a known collision packet:

| collision packet | root primary data \((\ell,\deg k)\) | root length | base primary data | base length |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(q\)-node | \((2,1),(4,2)\) | 6 | \((3,1)\) | 3 |
| \(r\)-cusp | \((12,1)\) | 12 | \((6,1)\) | 6 |
| conjugate triple orbit | \((20,2)\) | 20 | \((14,2)\) | 14 |
| transverse \(q\)-\(r\) point | \((1,1)\) | 1 | \((1,1)\) | 1 |
| three \(q\)-\(r\) tangencies | \((6,3),(12,3)\) | 18 | \((9,3)\) | 9 |
| **total** |  | **57** |  | **33** |

Here \(\ell\) is the rational vector-space length of the primary quotient
and \(\deg k\) is the degree of its associated prime.  Thus the conjugate
triple packet contributes length ten at each geometric center, and the
tangency packet contributes length six at each of its three centers.  No
new support appears; the decomposition replaces one large residue ledger by
five local gluing problems.

## 4. The conductor cover and derivative parity

Normalize the \(q\)-curve by

\[
 t=\frac{y^2-9}{8},\qquad
 s=\frac{4(y+2)}{(y+1)(y+3)},\qquad
 X=\frac{2}{y+3}.                                  \tag{4.1}
\]

The connected slope cover is rational:

\[
 y=\frac{5+3w^2}{1-w^2},\qquad w\longmapsto-w.     \tag{4.2}
\]

Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_-&=w^2-2w+5,&A_+&=w^2+2w+5,\\
 B_-&=w^3-3w^2-w-5,&B_+&=w^3+3w^2-w+5,\\
 C&=w^4+10w^2+5.
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{4.3}
\]

The finite conductor collisions factor as follows:

| packet | factor on the \(w\)-line |
|---|---|
| \(q\)-node | \(C\) |
| conjugate triples | \(A_-,A_+\) |
| transverse \(q\)-\(r\) point | \(w\) |
| \(q\)-\(r\) tangencies | \(B_-,B_+\) |
| \(r\)-cusp | off the \(q\)-conductor |

The boundary restrictions are

\[
 d(w)=\frac{A_-^2A_+^2}{16(w^2+3)^2},              \tag{4.4}
\]

and

\[
 r(w)=\frac{4w^2A_-A_+B_-^2B_+^2}
 {(w-1)^6(w+1)^6(w^2+3)^2}.                        \tag{4.5}
\]

Deform \(s\) normally and write the repeated root as
\(X=2/(y+3)+\epsilon Y\).  The discriminant of the first nonzero quadratic
slope residual becomes \(L(w)^2\), where

\[
 L(w)=\frac{w(3w^2+5)A_-A_+}{32(w-1)(w+1)}.        \tag{4.6}
\]

Since \(L(-w)=-L(w)\), the total derivative residue is anti-invariant.  The
previous unit pullback, completed by the selector \(w-1\), has determinant
\(-1\).  Thus the integral unit lattice and the total parity are compatible
with conductor descent.  What remains is a line-bundle gluing problem: split
this anti-invariant residue among the individual \(D_d,D_q,D_r\) sections
with product \(P_X\).

## 5. Retesting the compact degree

Extend the natural product \(J=I_d^3I_qI_r\) to \(\overline R\).  In the
five-variable normalization presentation its Gröbner basis has size \(43\),
and exact reduction gives

\[
 P_X\notin J\overline R.                            \tag{5.1}
\]

This is stronger than the pre-normalization control but has the same narrow
scope: it excludes the natural incidence ideals after integral closure.  It
does not test the still-unconstructed exceptional divisorial ideals.

## 6. The next global multi-Rees/Cox object

Let \(\pi:\widetilde X\to\operatorname{Spec}\overline R\) be a certified
regular controlled transform carrying the sixty-three colored divisors.
The next object is

\[
 \mathcal C_{\widetilde X}
 =\bigoplus_{a,b,c\ge0}
 H^0\!\left(\widetilde X,
 \mathcal O_{\widetilde X}(-aD_d-bD_q-cD_r)\right)
 U^aV^bW^c.                                        \tag{6.1}
\]

It must contain a primitive exceptional generator of value one at each
triple-\(E_1\) color, together with multiplication maps realizing

\[
 3D_d+D_q+D_r=\operatorname{div}(P_X).              \tag{6.2}
\]

The subsequent
[`F20_EXCEPTIONAL_COX_ATLAS.md`](F20_EXCEPTIONAL_COX_ATLAS.md) constructs
the controlled charts and primitive exceptional variables for all
forty-eight positive exceptional colors.  It verifies literal
degree-((3,1,1)) cancellation on every chart and gives a parity-compatible
three-frame factorization of \(L(w)\) on the punctured conductor.  The
remaining order of work is now exact:

1. compute the Čech transition maps among the thirteen controlled chart
   types, the generic ramification charts, and the normalization chart;
2. test whether the resulting multidegree-((3,1,1)) cocycle is globally
   trivial, so that its local sections glue literally to \(P_X\);
3. only for that survivor, test entrywise inverse-adjugate polynomiality and
   affine-space recognition.

The global normalization, conductor square, primary packets, local
exceptional Cox atlas, and punctured three-frame residue product are proved.
A global regular SNC resolution with certified overlaps, its global Cox
algebra, a polynomial Keller map, and affine-space completion remain open.

## 7. Reproduction

Requires the repository Python environment and Singular 4.4-compatible
`normal.lib` and `primdec.lib`:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_f20_normalized_cox_conductor.py \
  --output artifacts/generated-results/f20_normalized_cox_conductor.json
```

The checker verifies (2.2)--(2.6), the two packet decompositions, the cover
factorizations (4.3)--(4.5), the odd derivative residue (4.6), and the
failure (5.1).  The expensive independent `norTest` routine is not part of
this certificate; the result uses Singular's exact normalization,
conductor, primary-decomposition, and ideal-equality routines.
