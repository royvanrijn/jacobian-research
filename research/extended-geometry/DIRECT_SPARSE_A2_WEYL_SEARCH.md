# Direct sparse search in the second Weyl algebra

## Status

The second Dixmier conjecture is not settled here.  This note records a
finite, direct search in `A_2`; it does not descend a plane Keller map and
does not assume that a useful leading-symbol tuple is a Keller map.

Write

\[
A_2(K)=K\langle x_1,x_2,d_1,d_2\rangle,\qquad
[d_i,x_j]=\delta_{ij},
\]

in PBW order \(x_1^{a_1}x_2^{a_2}d_1^{b_1}d_2^{b_2}\).  The experiment
searches

\[
(P_1,P_2,Q_1,Q_2)
\quad\text{with}\quad
Y_i\longmapsto Y_i+c_iM_i,                         \tag{1}
\]

where \(Y=(x_1,x_2,d_1,d_2)\), each correction is either absent or one PBW
monomial, and the Bernstein degree and coefficient alphabet are bounded.
All six Weyl relations are imposed by exact PBW multiplication.

This is deliberately a small first slice.  It tests the normal-order engine,
the distinction between Weyl relations and generation, non-Keller leading
symbols, reduction modulo \(p\), and the induced center maps before a
larger support search is attempted.

## Characteristic-zero result

The recorded characteristic-zero run uses

\[
2\leq\deg_B M_i\leq4,\qquad
c_i\in\{-2,-1,1,2\}.                                \tag{2}
\]

There are 65 possible correction monomials and 261 choices per image,
including no correction.  The exact compatibility search finds 657 Weyl
tuples, one of which is the identity.  For every one of the 656 nonidentity
tuples, the four ambient generators occur in the span of ordered image words
of degree at most four:

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\text{first recovery word degree}&2&3&4\\ \hline
\text{number of tuples}&96&224&336.
\end{array}                                         \tag{3}
\]

Thus every tuple in this finite ansatz generates the full \(A_2\).  There is
no counterexample in (2).

Every nonidentity tuple has a non-Keller top-symbol tuple under the ordinary
Bernstein leading-term operation.  For example,

\[
(x_1,x_2,d_1,d_2-2x_2^2)
\]

is an elementary Weyl automorphism, but its four separate highest-degree
symbols are \((x_1,x_2,d_1,-2x_2^2)\), whose commutative Jacobian is zero.
Consequently:

\[
\boxed{\text{a non-Keller leading-symbol tuple is allowed, but is not by
itself evidence of non-surjectivity.}}               \tag{4}
\]

This confirms the motivating filtered angle while also supplying its first
cheap false-positive screen.

## Modular control and center behavior

In characteristic two, already at correction degree two and coefficient
alphabet \(\{1\}\), the search finds 624 nonidentity Weyl tuples.  Of these,
100 recover the ambient generators by image words of degree at most five,
15 are proved to generate proper subalgebras by their center maps, and 509
remain unresolved by the bounded generation test.

The simplest certified proper modular tuple is

\[
P_1=x_1,\qquad P_2=x_2,\qquad
Q_1=d_1,\qquad Q_2=d_2+d_2^2.                       \tag{5}
\]

Indeed,

\[
[Q_2,P_2]=[d_2+d_2^2,x_2]=1+2d_2=1
\quad\text{in characteristic two}.                 \tag{6}
\]

Put \(D_2=d_2^2\), one of the four ambient center variables.  The induced
center map fixes the other three variables and sends

\[
D_2\longmapsto (d_2+d_2^2)^2=D_2+D_2^2.             \tag{7}
\]

Its image is proper: on fraction fields the last univariate coordinate has
degree two.  Hence the algebra generated by (5) is proper as well.  This is
not a characteristic-zero candidate, since (6) acquires the nonzero term
\(2d_2\) over characteristic zero.

The 15 proper cases are certified only when the center map is a tensor
product of four nonconstant univariate maps in distinct center variables
and the product of their degrees is greater than one.  The 509 remaining
cases are labelled unresolved, not proper.

Over \(\mathbf F_3\), with the same correction-degree bound and all nonzero
coefficients, the search finds 40 nonidentity tuples; all recover the full
algebra within word degree two.  The sharp difference between \(p=2\) and
\(p=3\) at degree two is the expected Frobenius warning rather than evidence
for a characteristic-zero counterexample.

[`search_sparse_a2_weyl_endomorphisms.py`](../scripts/search_sparse_a2_weyl_endomorphisms.py).
Run:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/search_sparse_a2_weyl_endomorphisms.py \
  --max-degree 4 --coefficients=-2,-1,1,2 --recovery-degree 8

.venv/bin/python scripts/search_sparse_a2_weyl_endomorphisms.py \
  --max-degree 2 --coefficients all --prime 2 --recovery-degree 5 \
  --max-records 10000 \
  --output artifacts/generated-results/sparse_a2_weyl_endomorphism_search_p2.json

  --max-degree 2 --coefficients all --prime 3 --recovery-degree 5 \
```

## Secondary control search

This direct PBW branch is now a control calculation, not the primary
rank-two strategy.  The primary branch uses the exact Ore/Darboux
localization, quantizes the marked-root incidence family before
specializing its classical symbol, solves the Hamiltonian connection, and
treats polynomiality as boundary saturation and conductor descent.  See
[the unified deformation complex](UNIFIED_DEFORMATION_COMPLEX.md#6-incidence-first-rank-two-quantization).

If the direct finite search is enlarged, the useful enlargement is not a
higher coefficient alphabet.  It is:

1. allow two PBW correction monomials per image at Bernstein degree at most
   three;
2. solve coefficient equations over several good primes, retaining only
   support components stable across primes;
3. reconstruct those components over \(\mathbb Q\);
4. for each characteristic-zero Weyl tuple, compute an Ore-Gröbner
   elimination certificate for whether \(x_1,x_2,d_1,d_2\) lie in the image
   subalgebra;
5. keep the non-Keller top-symbol condition as metadata, not as a gate.

This targets genuinely nonhomogeneous Weyl behavior while preventing
small-characteristic Frobenius families such as (5) from dominating the
search.  Cross-prime rank agreement is only a discovery screen; reconstructed
components still require exact characteristic-zero equations and
saturation certificates.
