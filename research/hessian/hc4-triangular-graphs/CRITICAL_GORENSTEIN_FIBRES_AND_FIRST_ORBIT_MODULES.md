# Critical Gorenstein fibres and the first orbit module

Canonical claims HC4MYKI0/1/2. [MATH_STATUS.json](../../MATH_STATUS.json)
is the mathematical authority. This note closes the complete-intersection
case of the remaining h-dependent plinth problem, in all degrees. It
replaces a reduced-fibre input by Gorensteinness at the labelled critical
values and gives an equivalent small-generator test for ker(delta^2).
No native action or unconditional Gorensteinness statement is supplied.

## 1. The actual hypotheses

Use [KF0](FLAT_PLINTH_FAMILIES_AND_ONE_VALUE_RECOGNITION.md). Let X be a
smooth irreducible factorial affine complex fourfold with nonzero LND
delta on R, and actual embedded invariant ring

    A=ker(delta)=B[h], B=C[b1,b2],
    J=pl(delta), sqrt(J)=I_Z A,

where the three generators are algebraically independent and Z is a
nonempty fixed finite reduced subset of A2. The full J may depend on h.
KF proves T=A/J finite free over D=C[h], with support-component ranks L_i.
There is one actual Cech class xi with annihilator J. Source intersection
uses factorial kernel closure; these facts are retained, not new assumptions.

At b_i and a closed value c let y=(b_i,c) in Spec A, s=h-c, and

    C_i,c=(T/(h-c)T)_(b_i),
    I_i,c=the ACTUAL specialization of J in B_(b_i).

This is not the annihilator of a specialized Cech class or the plinth of
a specialized action. It is a local Artinian algebra of length L_i.
Its type means dim_C Soc(C_i,c). For an Artinian local C-algebra,
Gorenstein means type one.

## 2. Actual Gorenstein specialization gives two local slices: HC4MYKI0

Suppose p in X maps to y and is an isolated critical point of h.
Assume C_i,c is Gorenstein. Then

    L_i=1.                                             (1)

In fact the same conclusion follows if J is generated by two elements
in A_y; under the flatness above these two hypotheses are equivalent.
The isolated point is then necessarily Morse.

### Lifting generators uses flatness, not an action base-change claim

Tensor 0 -> J -> A -> T -> 0 over D with D/(s). Flatness of T gives

    J/sJ = I_c inside A/sA.

After localization at b_i, Nakayama therefore identifies

    mu_(A_y)(J_y)=mu_(B_(b_i))(I_i,c).                 (2)

The two-variable Koszul identity proved in
[KE0](FINITE_PLINTH_DESCENT_AND_VANISHING_CYCLE_RECOGNITION.md) applies to
every finite local quotient of the regular plane:

    mu(I_i,c)=1+dim Soc(C_i,c).                        (3)

A type-one fibre consequently gives two generators F,G of J after
shrinking to a principal neighbourhood W of y, avoiding other support
lines. Their height is two in the regular threefold W, so they form a
regular sequence. Conversely such a sequence stays a regular sequence
after specializing s, because s is regular on T; (3) then gives type one.
There is no claim that J descends from B or is generated by two elements
away from this neighbourhood.

### The whole source is recovered on this neighbourhood

Choose local slices p0,q0 in R_W with

    delta(p0)=G, delta(q0)=-F,
    a=F p0+G q0 in A_W.

On D(F),D(G) they give the actual polynomial local-slice rings, and
the Cech class is a/(FG), up to the harmless sign convention. Hence

    J:a=J.                                            (4)

Its image in A_W/J is a nonzerodivisor. Since the radical of J is
the single fixed line b=b_i, its restriction a_i(h)=a(b_i,h) is not
identically zero: otherwise a is nilpotent modulo J, contradicting (4).
The exact whole-source presentation is

    R_W=A_W[P,Q]/(F(b,h)P+G(b,h)Q-a(b,h)).              (5)

Here P maps to p0 and Q to q0. For completeness, F,G are coprime
in A_W and remain coprime in the factorial source by kernel closure.
Thus their simultaneous source vanishing has codimension at least two.
The displayed hypersurface is a domain by the primitive linear relation.
Outside F=G=0 it is smooth and agrees with R_W on both slice charts.
Over F=G=0 its base lies in the finite zero set of the nonzero a_i(h),
and its P,Q fibres have dimension two. Its removed set therefore has
codimension at least two as well. The hypersurface is Cohen--Macaulay
and regular in codimension one, hence normal. Both rings are the
intersection of their F and G localizations in the same field, proving
(5). This extends KE's source-intersection argument to h-dependent F,G.

### The isolated-critical-point obstruction survives h-dependence

Since F,G vanish along the fixed line for all nearby h,

    F_h(b_i,c)=G_h(b_i,c)=0.                           (6)

On (5), put L=FP+GQ-a. A critical point of h has F=G=0 and

    a_h(b_i,c)!=0,
    M (P,Q)^t=a_b(b_i,c),
    M=(grad_b F,grad_b G) at (b_i,c).                  (7)

These follow from dh being proportional to the nonzero differential dL.
The h component of dL is -a_h by (6). If rank M<2 and (7) has one
solution, its solutions form an affine space of dimension at least one.
All those points satisfy L=0, are smooth (the same a_h is nonzero),
and are critical for h. They pass through p, contradicting its isolation.
Thus M has rank two. The specialized ideal has independent linear
generators and C_i,c=C. Since its length is L_i by flatness, (1) follows.

One also obtains local source coordinates (b1,b2,P,Q) by the implicit
function theorem in h. At the critical point its Hessian has mixed
block M/a_h and zero P,Q block, so

    det Hess(h)=det(M)^2/a_h^4 !=0.                   (8)

It is necessarily Morse. No fixed point linearization, primitive-cycle
transversality assumption or generic Gorenstein-to-special inference was
used. The actual action and its polynomial slice rings are essential.

### Isolation is essential, even for a genuine A4 action

For every integer m>=2, on the actual source C[U,v,P,Q] set

    h=UP+v^m Q, delta=v^m partial_P-U partial_Q,
    u=U+vh.

This is a primitive LND with actual kernel C[U,v,h]=C[u,v,h]. The
U-inverted chart and independence of v,h modulo U remove invariant
denominators. The two slice charts have class h/(U v^m),whose annihilator
is (U,v^m) in C[U,v,h]; their codimension-two source intersection proves
the exact plinth. In the chosen quotient frame it is

    J=(u-vh,v^m), sqrt(J)=(u,v),
    T_c=C[u,v]/(u-cv,v^m).

It is a genuine h-dependent family of Gorenstein length m for every c.
The whole source is A4 and the action is globally algebraic. But the
critical set of h has support U=v=P=0,Q arbitrary,an affine line.
Equivalently (5) reads (u-vh)P+v^m Q-h=0 and (7) has rank one at0.
Thus a Gorenstein plinth fibre is not alone enough: the theorem uses
the isolated critical point of the SAME function. This is an arbitrary-m
control, not a remaining native frontier or a bounded search.

## 3. A critical-type recognition theorem: HC4MYKI1

Return to actual A4 and a native h whose N finite Morse cycles form the
whole integral H3 basis, including coincident critical values. KF gives
n=N and labels each support point b_i by its unique Morse point p_i,
with value c_i. For the SAME candidate fixed-support action the following
are equivalent:

1. J=I_Z A, hence the complete cotangent source frame is recovered.
2. Each actual C_i,c_i is Gorenstein (socle dimension one).
3. J is locally generated by two elements at every (b_i,c_i).
4. The normalized residue embeddings of
   [KH1](WEIGHTED_PERIOD_RECOVERY_AND_THE_GLOBAL_NILPOTENCE_GAP.md) are
   saturated at their labelled Morse values.

The implication 2 -> 1 is (1) component by component, followed by KF/KD.
The reverse is immediate. The other equivalences are (2)--(3) and KH1.
The values tested are the labelled CRITICAL values; generic Gorensteinness
was already automatic and does not supply this certificate. Different
components may use different values; collisions retain separate local
algebras. It is enough that the whole actual plinth fibre be Gorenstein
at every distinct critical value, though the componentwise test is sharper.

This is an exact pure-alignment criterion for the native frontiers once
the candidate kernel and fixed support are supplied. Unit existence still
requires the independent E_N conjunct from KB. The candidate action and
its critical type-one fibres are not constructed here.

There are useful uniform restrictions on any remaining thick component:

    L_i>1 implies type(C_i,c_i)>=2,
                    mu(J_(b_i,c_i))>=3,
                    L_i>=3.                          (9)

The last inequality requires no classification: a length-two local algebra
has a one-dimensional maximal ideal with square zero, and hence type one.
Such a component is ruled out by KI0. Since the generic component is
Gorenstein by KE's inverse-system pairing, every thick component would
have to undergo an actual Gorenstein-to-non-Gorenstein type jump at its
own labelled critical value. The entire h-dependent locally complete-
intersection case is therefore closed. Only genuinely non-complete-
intersection critical plinth fibres remain as possible excess components.

In particular the TOTAL constant length has the gap

    L=N or L>=N+2.                                   (10)

Thus one actual ideal fibre of length at most N+1 already suffices to
recover the whole source. For S_rho this means at most four, for K2 at
most three. This is an all-N consequence of (9), not a bounded search.
The fibre must still be the actual ideal fibre. Nothing here rules out
lengths N+2 or greater or proves critical Gorensteinness automatically.

## 4. A three-generator first-orbit-module test: HC4MYKI2

Let F1=ker(delta^2) as an A-module. Its elements have action degree at
most one. The exact sequence

    0 -> A -> F1 --delta--> J -> 0                   (11)

makes it finite over A, of rank two: J is a finite rank-one ideal.
At any quotient point y with a source point p over it, evaluation at p
shows that 1 survives in F1/m_y F1. Tensoring (11) with the residue
field therefore gives an INJECTIVE copy of C on the left and

    mu_(A_y)((F1)_y)=1+mu_(A_y)(J_y).                (12)

One must not assume that all of (11) stays left exact; it is evaluation
that proves this particular injection. At a labelled native Morse point,
(2)--(3) yield

    mu((F1)_(b_i,c_i))=2+type(C_i,c_i).              (13)

Consequently another exact minimum-length criterion is

    mu((F1)_(b_i,c_i))<=3 for every i.                (14)

The number is then exactly three. Any thick component instead requires
at least four generators there. No bound on a polynomial source degree,
slice degree or action degree is assumed. The missing global statement
can be sought directly as this generator bound, or as critical
Gorensteinness, rather than by deeper Morse-period expansions.

### Rank-two local freeness would be the wrong target

Even the actual standard model

    R=C[u,v,P,Q], h=uP+vQ,
    delta=v partial_P-u partial_Q, A=C[u,v,h]

has first orbit module

    F1=A{1,P,Q},
    0 -> A --(-h,u,v)^t--> A^3 -> F1 -> 0.           (15)

Indeed its exact plinth is (u,v), retained in
[JU0](QUOTIENT_HOLES_TORSOR_RESIDUES_AND_LOCAL_PERIOD_BARRIER.md).
Lifting its two ideal generators proves the displayed generation, and
coprimality of u,v makes every relation a multiple of -h+uP+vQ=0.
At u=v=h=0 all relation coefficients vanish, so its minimal generator
number is three, while its generic rank is two. It is NOT locally free
at that point, despite the source being A4 and h an ordinary Morse quadric.
Outside that quotient point a relation coefficient is a unit and it is
locally free. The three-generator test (14), rather than freeness, matches
the desired global geometry exactly.
