# Generic source lines have transverse collision pairs

**Theorem DirectionalConductorCollisionScheme.** Let \(F=(P,Q)\) be
a complex polynomial plane Keller map, and suppose a source frame has
\(P\) monic of positive longitudinal degree. On every source line of
the frame, the divided differences of the two restricted generators
define a finite ordered collision scheme. The first projection has
zeroth Fitting ideal generated by the exact directional conductor on
that line. In particular, the length of the ordered collision scheme is
the conductor degree. If the restricted generator degrees are \(m,n\ge2\),
this length is at most
\((m-1)(n-1)-(\gcd(m,n)-1)\), with equality for coprime degrees.

**Theorem GenericSourceLineTransverseCollisions.** For a complex plane
Keller map there is a nonempty Zariski-open set of affine source lines
on which distinct source points mapping to the same target point have
distinct image tangent lines. On these source lines, for each source
point \(r\), the order of vanishing of its conductor equals the number
of other points on the line mapping to \(F(r)\). For a noninvertible
map there are at least eleven unordered matching pairs on each line
of this open set after intersecting with the monic directions. More
precisely the unordered count is half the conductor degree and equals
the normalization defect.

## The divided differences and the conductor

Fix one line in a monic frame and use a longitudinal variable \(x\).
Write \(p(x),q(x)\) for the restricted generators. The
[exact coordinate-order theorem](GENERIC_COORDINATE_ORDER_CONDUCTOR_AND_CLOSED_COLLISIONS.md)
gives the finite birational order \(\mathbb C[p,q]\) with normalization
\(\mathbb C[x]\) and exact conductor generated by \(f_V(p,q)/p'\),
where \(f(U,V)\) is its monic minimal relation in \(V\).
Use variables \(x,t\) for the two points and put
\[
 D_p(x,t)=\frac{p(t)-p(x)}{t-x},\qquad
 D_q(x,t)=\frac{q(t)-q(x)}{t-x}.
\]
The scheme cut out by \(D_p,D_q\) misses the diagonal, because
their diagonal values are \(p'(x),q'(x)\) and the Keller condition
makes these derivatives nowhere vanish simultaneously.

If \(p\) has degree one then \(D_p=1\), and the order is the full
normalization. Otherwise \(\mathbb C[x,t]/(D_p)\) is finite free over
\(\mathbb C[x]\) with rank \(\deg p-1\), by monicity.
The norm determinant for multiplication by \(D_q\) is the
\(D\)-resultant. In the birational case its quotient identity with
\(f_V/p'\) is the monic instance of
[van den Essen–Yu, Proposition 1.1](https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/156137/1/content.pdf).
Indeed, over the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb C(x)\) the roots
\(t_j\ne x\) of \(p(t)=p(x)\) are simple. The corresponding values
\(q(t_j)\), together with \(q(x)\), are the distinct conjugates of
\(q(x)\) over \(\mathbb C(p)\). The product of \(D_q(x,t_j)\) is
the derivative \(f_V(p,q)\) divided by \(p'(x)\), since the signs
in the numerator and denominator cancel. Thus the determinant
generates the conductor on the first line coordinate. The cokernel
of multiplication is precisely the coordinate ring of the collision
scheme. Its zeroth Fitting ideal is generated by the determinant,
and its length is the degree of this determinant by Smith normal
form over \(\mathbb C[x]\).

When \(m,n\ge2\), homogenize \(D_p,D_q\) to degrees \(m-1,n-1\)
in the projective \((x,t)\)-plane. The homogenizations have no
common component since their affine intersection is finite and
neither contains the line at infinity. Their common points at
infinity have \(x,t\ne0\) and \(t/x\) a nontrivial common root of
unity of orders dividing \(m,n\). Each contributes at least one
to the projective intersection multiplicity. Bézout gives the
stated bound after subtracting these contributions, with no
subtraction when \(m,n\) are coprime.

## Generic transversality

The ordered distinct-pair scheme of the whole map is the complement
of the diagonal in \(\mathbb A^2\times_F\mathbb A^2\). It is smooth
of pure dimension two when nonempty, since \(F\) is étale and the
projection to either source point is its base change. There is a
morphism from this scheme to the dual projective plane that sends
two distinct points to their joining line. The homogeneous
coordinates of the map are the cross product of the two source
points in affine projective coordinates, so they do not vanish
simultaneously.

On each irreducible component that dominates the dual plane, the
morphism has differential of rank two at its generic point. The
extension of function fields is finite separable in characteristic
zero. The rank-deficient locus therefore has dimension at most
one and its image has closure of dimension at most one. The images
of nondominant components also have proper closure. Remove all
these closures and the point parametrizing the line at infinity.
For every remaining affine line the joining-line fibre is a
reduced zero-dimensional scheme.

The fibre for a line is scheme-theoretically the distinct-pair
scheme on that line. Two distinct points give independent affine
projective coordinate vectors, so the conditions that a fixed line
contain both cut out the fibre of their joining-line map. In line
coordinates the divided differences differ from the actual equality
equations by the unit \(t-x\). Since the diagonal contains no common
zero, the reduced fibre is the finite affine scheme of \(D_p,D_q\).
At each point of this complete intersection its Jacobian in
\(x,t\) is invertible. Off the diagonal its columns are scalar
multiples of the two longitudinal image tangent vectors, so those
vectors are linearly independent. This proves transversality for
every pair, including pairs at multiple image points.

Intersect the open set with the directions where the leading part of
\(P\) is nonzero, and choose the line coordinate with \(P\) monic.
For a reduced collision scheme, the order of the Fitting determinant
at \(x=r\) is the length of its module localized at \(r\). This is one
for each distinct mate of \(r\). The total length is consequently
twice the number of unordered pairs. Multiple image points can
contribute several mates to an endpoint even though every pair is
transverse. By [the current global bound](GLOBAL_CONDUCTOR_TWENTY_EXCLUSION.md)
and exact specialization, on a further nonempty open set of lines
the length is at least twenty-two. Indeed the generic direction has
this global bound and the generic intercept preserves its leading
conductor coefficient. Equivalently one can take the common generic
degree of the resulting resultant in the direction and intercept
parameters, which holds away from its leading coefficient locus.
The normalization defect is half this degree.
