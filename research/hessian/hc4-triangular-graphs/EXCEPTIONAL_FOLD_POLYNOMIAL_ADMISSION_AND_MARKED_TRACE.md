# Polynomial admission and marked traces at an exceptional fold

Let an actual complex polynomial plane Keller map have the
[exceptional polar fold](EXCEPTIONAL_POLAR_FOLD_WHOLE_CHARTS_AND_BIRATIONAL_IMAGES.md)
in its full finite normalization \(S\). Normalize its source frame and
chart by \(a=b^{-2}+bt\). Thus \(v_E(a)=-2,\ v_E(b)=1,\ e_E=2\), the entire
chart line \(b=0\) is the omitted prime \(E\), and its map to the target
image \(\Gamma\) is birational. Write \(J=\operatorname{Jac}_{a,b}(P,Q)\)
for the resulting nonzero constant.

## The simultaneous polynomial admission module

Set \(u=ab^2=1+b^3t\). In \(\mathbb C[b,b^{-1},u]\) a polynomial in both
\(\mathbb C[a,b]\) and \(\mathbb C[t,b]\) has a unique finite expression
\(\sum_{k} b^k p_k(u)\) with the exact admission rule
\[
u^{\max(0,\lceil-k/2\rceil)}
(u-1)^{\max(0,\lceil-k/3\rceil)}\mid p_k(u).
\tag{1}
\]
In the original plane the coefficient of \(u^i\) in weight \(k\)
contributes the monomial \(a^i b^{k+2i}\). In the chart the coefficient of
\((u-1)^j\) in that weight contributes \(t^j b^{k+3j}\). Distinct weights
cannot cancel either monomial. The two conditions combine because
\(u\) and \(u-1\) are coprime.

Define
\[
m_n=\max\{m\ge0:\lceil m/2\rceil+\lceil m/3\rceil\le n\},
\qquad q_n=\lceil m_n/3\rceil,\qquad
F_n=b^{-m_n}u^{n-q_n}(u-1)^{q_n}.
\]
The admitted elements of \(a\)-degree at most \(N\) have
\(\mathbb C[b]\)-basis \(F_0,\ldots,F_N\). Indeed \(F_n\) is admitted,
and the \(a^n\) coefficient of any admitted element of degree \(n\) is
divisible by \(b^{2n-m_n}\), the exact leading coefficient of \(F_n\).
Subtraction then descends in \(a\)-degree and also proves independence.
The two ceilings give \(m_{n+5}=m_n+6\), so \(F_{n+5}=\ell F_n\) with
\(\ell=b^{-6}u^3(u-1)^2=a^3(u-1)^2\). In particular the full intersection
is a free module of rank five over \(\mathbb C[b,\ell]\), with basis
\[
1,\ u,\ w,\ k,\ w^2,\qquad w=a(u-1),\qquad k=ba^2(u-1).
\]
For example the cutoffs at \(N=2,3,4\) yield respectively the first
three, four and five basis elements. The first ten values of \(m_n\)
are \(0,0,2,3,4,6,6,8,9,10\). Restriction to \(E\) sees a monomial
\(t^j\) only from weight \(-3j\); the \(t\)-degree of the restriction
is at most \(\lfloor m_N/3\rfloor\).

## An exact degree obstruction and the first possible initial forms

For an actual exceptional fold,
\[
\max(\deg_a P,\deg_a Q)\ge9.
\tag{2}
\]
If the maximum is nine, an affine target transformation gives
restrictions to \(E\) of degrees two and three, respectively, and
lowest weight blocks that are nonzero constant multiples of \(k^2\)
and \(k^3\), respectively. This is a necessary condition at that
degree.

In fact \(\Gamma\) is an irreducible nonproperness component. Finiteness
makes the image of \(E\) closed. Approaching \(b=0\) in the whole
chart with \(t\) fixed escapes to source infinity and gives every
point of that image. The nonproper value set is contained in the
finite image of the source complement in the normalization, a proper
closed set of dimension at most one. A restriction of degree one of
an affine target combination would make \(\Gamma\) a polynomial graph,
isomorphic to the affine line. This is excluded by the remark on
printed page 43 of
[Chau, *Two remarks on non-zero constant Jacobian polynomial maps of C²*](../../jacobian/affine-complements/sources/global-boundary-topology/chau-2003-two-remarks.pdf).

Write \(p_0(t)=P|_E,\ q_0(t)=Q|_E\). By the whole-chart theorem they
generate \(\mathbb C(t)\) as a field. If both have degree at most two,
cancelling their leading terms where necessary gives a linear
combination of degree one: the alternative is a field generated by
one quadratic or by constants. This excludes a maximum \(a\)-degree
at most seven. Through maximum nine the restriction degrees are at
most three. Cancelling a common highest degree and using the same
argument leaves only \((\deg p_0,\deg q_0)=(2,3)\) after affine target
change.

Let \(b^{-m}p(u)\) and \(b^{-n}q(u)\) be the lowest weight blocks of
the transformed pair. Then \(m\ge6,\ n\ge9\). The weighted bracket is
\[
\operatorname{Jac}_{a,b}(b^i p,b^j q)
=b^{i+j+1}\bigl(j p' q-i p q'\bigr).
\]
The pair of lowest blocks uniquely contributes the smallest possible
weight \(-m-n+1<0\). The constant Jacobian therefore forces
\(p^n/q^m\in\mathbb C^*\).

At maximum \(a\)-degree at most eight, \(n=9\) and the cubic
restriction forces \(\operatorname{ord}_1 q=3\). An \(m=9\) block of
\(P\) would need order at least four at 1 and at least five at 0,
impossible through degree eight. Thus \(6\le m\le8\). The relation at
1 gives \(9\operatorname{ord}_1p=3m\), so \(m=6\). At 0 it gives
\(9\operatorname{ord}_0p=6\operatorname{ord}_0q\); (1) requires
\(\operatorname{ord}_0q\ge5\), so that order is at least six. Hence \(q\)
has degree at least nine. This proves (2).

At maximum nine a block of weight \(-10\) must be proportional to
\(u^5(u-1)^4\) by (1). If just one of \(m,n\) is 10, the orders at
0 and 1 force the other weight magnitude to be divisible by both 2
and 5, a contradiction. If both are 10, cancel the weight \(-10\)
block of \(Q\) by a multiple of \(P\). The restrictions still have
degrees two and three, leaving exactly one block of weight \(-10\),
which has just been excluded. Hence \(n=9\) and \(m\le9\). Order
three for \(q\) at 1 excludes \(m=9\), because \(P\) has no cubic
restriction. The preceding order calculation gives \(m=6\) and
\(q\propto u^6(u-1)^3\), \(p\propto u^4(u-1)^2\). These are the stated
blocks \(k^3,k^2\) after restoring the powers of \(b\). At higher
degrees (1) still gives simultaneous admission for each polynomial;
the unrestricted constant-Jacobian compatibility remains open.

## A marked trace with exact double pole

Let \(H\) be a reduced equation of \(\Gamma\) in \(B=\mathbb C[P,Q]\).
Use the full semilocal normalization \(S_{(H)}=S\otimes_B B_{(H)}\)
over the target DVR and a target derivation \(D\) transverse to
\(H=0\). There exists a marker \(\chi\in S\) for which
\(\operatorname{Tr}_{\operatorname{Frac}(S)/\operatorname{Frac}(B)}(\chi D(a))\)
has **exactly a double pole** at \(H\). The same is true with \(a\)
replaced by any \(\lambda a+\mu b+\nu\) with \(\lambda\ne0\).

To compute the pole choose the local unit \(\eta=H/b^2\) at \(E\).
The residue degree at \(E\) is one, so choose \(c\in B_{(H)}\) lifting
\(\bar\eta\). Since \(a=b^{-2}+bt\),
\(v_E(a-c/H)\ge-1\). The tame derivative estimates from
[the codifferent preimage theorem](CODIFFERENT_PREIMAGES_UNDER_TARGET_DERIVATIONS.md)
give \(v_E(D(a-c/H))\ge-3\) and \(v_E(D(b))\ge-1\). Consequently
\[
\overline{H^2D(a)}\big|_E=-\bar c\,\overline{D(H)}\ne0.
\tag{3}
\]
In the full finite semilocal order choose a marker congruent to 1 at
\(E\) and vanishing to sufficiently high order at every other prime
above \(H\), by the Chinese remainder theorem for their powers.
Then \(H^2\chi D(a)\) is integral there and has zero residue at every
other prime. The order is finite free over the target DVR, so its
trace reduces to the trace of multiplication on its special fibre.
For each Artinian local factor the length filtration contributes
\(e_{E'}\) copies of the trace on its residue field. Using \(e_E=2\)
and residue degree one gives
\[
\overline{H^2\operatorname{Tr}(\chi D(a))}
=-2\,\bar c\,\overline{D(H)}\ne0.
\]
Clearing the marker's localization denominator by a target unit
preserves the pole and produces a global marker in \(S\). The term
\(D(b)\) contributes zero to (3), which proves the affine-coordinate
assertion. This witnesses failure of ordinary codifferent membership
for these velocities in the presence of the fold. The local
calculation uses every prime above \(H\) in the full normalization.

## Marked residues at every polar omitted prime

Let \(F\) be any actual complex plane Keller map, \(S\) its full finite
normalization, and \(f\) any source polynomial with pole order \(q>0\)
at an omitted prime \(E\). Write \(H\) for the reduced equation of its
target image and take a regular target derivation \(D\) transverse to it.
Let \(e=e_E,\ d_E=[\mathbb C(E):\mathbb C(H=0)]\), and put
\[
n=\lfloor q/e\rfloor+1,\qquad r=q-e\lfloor q/e\rfloor .
\]
There is a marker in \(S_{(H)}\) for which the exact target residue is
\[
\overline{H^n\operatorname{Tr}(\chi D(f))}
=-q\,d_E\,\overline{D(H)}.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular a marker in \(S\) gives a pole of exactly order \(n\).
The computation holds at each prime individually even if coincident image
curves have other primes with larger pole orders.

Indeed choose a uniformizer \(\pi\) at \(E\) and write
\(H=\eta\pi^e,\ f=c\pi^{-q}\) with units \(\eta,c\). The derivative
estimate for regular functions gives \(v_E(D\eta),v_E(Dc)\ge1-e\).
Differentiating \(H=\eta\pi^e\) shows that the leading term of
\(D\pi\) is \(D(H)/(e\eta\pi^{e-1})\); the error has valuation at least
\(2-e\). Thus \(H^n\pi^r D(f)\) has residue
\(-\tfrac qe\,\bar\eta^{\,n-1}\bar c\,\overline{D(H)}\).
Choose \(\chi/\pi^r\) with residue \((\bar\eta^{\,n-1}\bar c)^{-1}\)
at \(E\), and choose \(\chi\) to vanish to sufficiently high orders
at all other primes above \(H\). These finitely many local
congruences are simultaneous by the semilocal Chinese remainder
theorem. Then the special-fibre trace used above contributes
\(e\) times the residue field trace of
\(-\tfrac qe\,\overline{D(H)}\), giving (4). Clearing the marker
denominator by a target unit preserves the pole order.
For any finite \(B\)-generating family of \(S\), at least one of its
marked traces therefore has pole order at least \(n\). The known
[common denominator formula](CODIFFERENT_PREIMAGES_UNDER_TARGET_DERIVATIONS.md)
takes the maximum over all primes above this \(H\).

In a source-finite frame \((x,y)\), the transverse coordinate \(y\)
has a pole at each omitted prime. If \(y\) were regular there,
integrality of \(x\) over \(B[y]\) and normality of the prime DVR
would make \(x\) regular there too. The map from that DVR to the
full normalization would then factor through the open source since
\(S\subset\mathbb C[x,y]\), contrary to the prime being omitted.
Consequently (4) measures a specific obstruction for the source
velocity \(D_{Q^*}(y)\) on any such prime when \(Q^*\) is chosen in
coordinates making \(D_{Q^*}\) transverse. For an index-three,
residue-degree-one prime it gives order \(\lfloor q/3\rfloor+1\)
and residue \(-q\,\overline{D(H)}\). Establishing automatic landing
still requires an actual-source argument eliminating the corresponding
omitted boundary.
