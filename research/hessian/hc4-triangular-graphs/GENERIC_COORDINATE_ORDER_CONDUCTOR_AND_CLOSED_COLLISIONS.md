# Generic coordinate orders and the exact closed collision conductor

Let K=(P,Q) be an actual complex polynomial plane Keller map, normalized
to Jac(P,Q)=1. In every source frame in which P is monic of positive
degree in x over C[y], the coordinate order

    A=C[P,Q,y]

has finite birational normalization R=C[x,y]. Its conductor in R is the
principal ideal generated by F_T(P,Q,y), where F(U,V,T) is the irreducible
equation for A, normalized to be monic in V (the Q variable).

The ZERO SET of this conductor is exactly the locus of source points
having a DISTINCT source mate with the SAME (P,Q,y). This includes
special closed points. If K is noninvertible, every parallel source line
y=c meets this locus, but no such line is an entire component of it.
Its defining polynomial has positive EVEN degree on EVERY such line,
with an explicit norm-square certificate. The lowest degree two gives
exactly a sheared nodal-curve product. Automatic trace integrality
and JC2 remain open.

## 1. One generic source coordinate recovers the raw source over S

Write B=C[P,Q], F=Frac(B), L=C(x,y), and let S be the full finite
normalization of B in L. Assume P has positive x-degree M and nonzero
constant leading coefficient; scale P and inversely scale Q to make P monic while keeping Jac=1. Then x satisfies
the monic polynomial P(X,y)-P over B[y]. Thus R is finite over B[y],
and also over S[y].

The canonical normalization lift Spec(R)->Spec(S) is an OPEN immersion.
The open-immersion/finite-intermediate lemma from Section6.2 of
[the first-derivative reconstruction proof](REPLICA_DEGREE_AND_SOURCE_DERIVATIVE_FIELD_RECONSTRUCTION.md)
therefore gives

    R=S[y].                                                 (1)

For completeness, a finite intermediate C with S subset C subset R
has Spec(R)->Spec(C) surjective by lying-over. All points of Spec(C)
therefore map into the specified source open of Spec(S). Factoring
through that open gives R->C inverse to C->R; hence C=R.
Apply this to C=S[y]. This uses no normality assertion about B[y].

The monicity assumption holds in every generic constant source
direction: if P has total degree D and top homogeneous part P_D,
the x^D coefficient in a frame with direction v is the constant P_D(v).
There are at most D exceptional projective directions.

In particular y has a pole at EVERY omitted prime of Spec(S).
Otherwise both y and every element of R=S[y] would be regular there;
the resulting valuative centre would belong to the given source open,
contradicting omission. The statement concerns a source-finite generic
coordinate, not an arbitrary preselected coordinate.

## 2. The coordinate order is finite birational over a polynomial base

The field equality L=F(y) follows from the elementary first-column
field theorem in Section6 of the cited proof, without a new
coordinate-primitive or polynomial Luroth input. Indeed the target
derivations partial_P and partial_Q extend uniquely inside the finite
separable field E=F(y). In L their values on y are

    partial_P(y)=-Q_x, partial_Q(y)=P_x.

Thus E contains F(P_x,Q_x)=L.

Put D=C[P,y], a polynomial ring. Both A=D[Q] and R=D[x] are finite
over D, and their fraction fields are the SAME L. The element x has
monic minimal equation P(X,y)-P of degree M over Frac(D). The element Q
is also primitive over Frac(D), because Frac(A)=L. Its monic minimal
polynomial lies in D[V], since Q is integral over the normal D.
Write it as

    F(U,V,T), monic in V, deg_V F=M,
    F(P,Q,y)=0.                                             (2)

Hence A=D[V]/(F), with normalization exactly R. It is a hypersurface
order, which may be nonnormal. It is NOT the full finite normalization
S over B. In particular (1) does not replace S by a hypersurface.

## 3. Exact principal conductor, by finite trace duality

Use the field trace L/Frac(D). The elementary monogenic complementary
module formulas are

    R_D^#=(1/P_x)*R,
    A_D^#=(1/F_V(P,Q,y))*A.                                  (3)

They hold for monogenic finite free orders; normality of A is not needed.
One can verify the formula by the dual basis from polynomial division:
for a monic separable polynomial f of degree M and root alpha,

    Tr(alpha^j/f'(alpha))=0 for 0<=j<M-1,
    Tr(alpha^(M-1)/f'(alpha))=1.

These identities follow by partial fractions of 1/f, and their
triangular coefficient transcriptions give the complete dual basis.

Let C=(A:R)={c in L:cR subset A}; since 1 is in R it is an ideal in A
and in R. Formula(3) gives the EXACT equivalences

    c in C
      iff (c/F_V)*R subset A_D^#
      iff Tr((c/F_V)*R*A) subset D
      iff c/F_V belongs to R_D^#.

Here R*A=R. Consequently

    C=(F_V/P_x)*R.                                          (4)

Differentiate (2), using dy=P_x*dQ-Q_x*dP and the independent forms
dP,dQ. This gives

    F_U(P,Q,y)=Q_x*F_T(P,Q,y),
    F_V(P,Q,y)=-P_x*F_T(P,Q,y).                              (5)

Thus, putting kappa=F_T(P,Q,y),

    (A:R)=kappa*R.                                          (6)

All equations are equalities in the source field or in actual embedded
rings. The constant sign in(4) does not change the ideal.

The polynomial kappa is nonzero: (2) is a separable inverse equation
in the T variable over C(P,Q). It records the NONNORMALITY of the
coordinate order, not the finite ramification divisor of S/B.
Its zero scheme can have arbitrarily high multiplicities; no
squarefreeness or radical-conductor assertion is made.

## 4. The conductor has exactly the closed same-line collision support

The finite birational normalization map

    nu:X=Spec(R)->W=Spec(A)

is UNRAMIFIED. Indeed B subset A subset R gives a surjection
Omega_(R/B)->Omega_(R/A), and the first module is zero because K is etale.

Fix any closed point w of W. Its finite fibre is a reduced disjoint
union of complex points: a finite unramified C-algebra is etale.
If the fibre has just ONE point, R/m_w*R=C. After localizing at w,
Nakayama applied to the finite module R/A gives R=A there.
Conversely if R=A near w, its normalization fibre has one point.

The conductor is the annihilator of R/A. At a normal point its
localization is the unit ideal; at a point with two or more source
preimages it is proper. In fact if a conductor element were a unit
at just one preimage, then cR subset A would make R_c=A_c.
The other preimages have the same value of c in their common complex
residue field and remain above w, contradicting that isomorphism.
Therefore ALL preimages of such a point lie in V_R(kappa).

Since a point of W is specified by its (P,Q,y) coordinates, this proves

    kappa(x0,y0)=0
      iff there is (x1,y0)!=(x0,y0) with
          P(x1,y0)=P(x0,y0), Q(x1,y0)=Q(x0,y0).              (7)

This holds at EVERY closed source point. There is no unverified
point-supported correction to a generic collision statement.
The residue-field and unramified hypotheses matter: a cuspidal
normalization can have a one-point nonnormal fibre when unramifiedness
fails.

## 5. Every source line imposes a nonempty finite conductor fibre

For each complex c, the restriction of K to L_c={y=c} is a polynomial
immersion: P_x,Q_x cannot vanish simultaneously because Jac(P,Q)=1.
Its map to its image is finite, since at least one coordinate polynomial
is nonconstant. After normalizing that image it is a finite unramified
map A1->C to a smooth affine curve.

Such a map has degree one. One way to see this uses the same complex
covering principle as finite affine-space covering triviality: its
simply connected source A1 is a universal cover. A nontrivial finite
deck group would act freely on A1, but a finite group of affine
automorphisms has a fixed point (average any orbit). Thus the map
is generically injective. Equivalently one can compactify and apply
Riemann--Hurwitz, retaining the single point at infinity upstairs.

It follows from(7) that kappa(x,c) is NOT identically zero. Only finitely
many points of a line can have a same-line collision.

Now assume K is noninvertible. Gwozdziewicz's
[Injectivity on one line, Theorem1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/alg-geom/9305008v1)
states that a complex plane Keller map injective on one affine line is
an automorphism. Its statement and proof were rechecked; it retains
the author's Abhyankar--Moh and Newton-polygon inputs, without an
independent reproduction of those imported theorems.

The contrapositive supplies a collision on EVERY L_c. Therefore

    kappa(x,c) is nonzero and nonconstant for EVERY c in C.    (8)

Writing kappa=sum_j kappa_j(y)*x^j gives useful algebraic restrictions:

    no factor y-c divides kappa;
    gcd_(j>=1)(kappa_j(y))=1;
    V(kappa)->A1_y is surjective with nonempty finite fibres.  (9)

These conditions are necessary for an actual noninvertible Keller
source in EVERY source frame satisfying the stated monicity.
They exclude coordinate-order candidates with vertical conductor
components, with a conductor fibre missing from the pencil, or with
only a nonzero constant restriction on one source line.
They do not classify every allowed conductor or prove its absence.

## 6. Index squares and even conductor degree on EVERY source line

Let C(U,T) be the M-by-M inclusion matrix of the D-basis
1,Q,...,Q^(M-1) of A into the D-basis 1,x,...,x^(M-1) of R.
Its columns are computed by reducing Q^j modulo the MONIC polynomial
P(X,T)-U. Put Delta(U,T)=det(C), which is nonzero because both bases
span the same generic field.

The two trace Gram matrices satisfy G_A=C^t*G_R*C. The monogenic
discriminant identities therefore give

    Norm_(L/C(P,y))(F_V/P_x)=Delta(P,y)^2,
    Norm_(L/C(P,y))(kappa)=(-1)^M*Delta(P,y)^2.                (12)

This is an EXACT polynomial square certificate over D, with a supplied
finite matrix defining its square root. It concerns the finite
coordinate order A over D, not the target trace defect Lambda/S.

It specializes at EVERY y=c, not merely at generic c. Section5's
finite polynomial-immersion argument makes Q(x,c) primitive over
C(P(x,c)). Hence Delta(U,c) is nonzero. Thus the specialized basis
inclusion is injective and identifies A/(y-c) with C[P_c,Q_c] inside
C[x]. No unsupported general commutation of normalization with
specialization is used: monicity and the line-specific field degree
prove this specialization directly.

Over the PID C[U], Smith normal form gives

    delta(c)=dim_C(C[x]/C[P_c,Q_c])=deg_U Delta(U,c).

Here the quotient is a VECTOR-SPACE quotient of the two rings. It is
finite-dimensional. For any nonzero polynomial r(x), the norm from
C(x) to C(P_c) has U-degree deg_x r: it is the resultant
Res_X(P_c(X)-U,r(X)), and reversing the resultant expresses it as a
product of the deg_x r factors P_c(alpha)-U, counted with multiplicity.
Applying this to kappa(x,c) in(12) proves

    deg_x kappa(x,c)=2*delta(c) for EVERY c in C.               (13)

In particular this degree is EVEN on EVERY source line. Under
noninvertibility it is at least TWO. Every degree drop at a special
parameter is even. This excludes, for example, an odd generic
conductor degree, any odd special-fibre degree, or a degree drop to one.
It does not require simple double collisions or a radical conductor;
higher tangencies and multiple coincident sheets retain the square.

### The exact lowest positive-degree form

Suppose K is noninvertible and deg_x kappa=2 globally. Write
kappa=a(y)x^2+b(y)x+c(y). By(13), no special fibre can have degree less
than two, so a(y) is a nonzero CONSTANT. By(7), every zero of a fibre
has a distinct mate in that same fibre. A quadratic cannot therefore
have just one distinct root. Its discriminant b(y)^2-4*a*c(y) never
vanishes, so it too is a nonzero CONSTANT.

After a polynomial shear and constant scale u=(x-h(y))/s, s!=0, one gets

    kappa*R=(u^2-1)*R,
    P(1,y)=P(-1,y), Q(1,y)=Q(-1,y).

The conductor and its full closed collision description identify the
ENTIRE raw coordinate order:

    A={r in C[u,y]:r(1,y)=r(-1,y)}
     =C[y,p=u^2-1,q=u*(u^2-1)],
    q^2=p^2*(p+1).                                          (14)

Indeed A contains y and its conductor (u^2-1)R. Modulo that conductor,
its functions have the same values at the two branches, and all
diagonal values C[y] already belong to A. This proves equality,
not just inclusion into a pinching order.

Thus the lowest positive conductor degree reduces to an explicit
nodal-curve product. Excluding an actual Keller target pair inside
this order would need another argument; no such exclusion is asserted.

## 7. Unramified normalization controls with arbitrarily thick conductor

For every integer k>=1 take source R_0=C[t,y] and

    p=t^2-1, q=t*p^k+y, A_k=C[p,q,y],
    F(U,V,T)=(V-T)^2-(U+1)*U^(2k).                         (10)

Then A_k is the hypersurface order with finite normalization R_0,
since t^2=p+1. Over D_0=C[p,y],

    A_k=D_0 direct-sum D_0*p^k*t,
    R_0=D_0 direct-sum D_0*t,
    (A_k:R_0)=p^k*R_0.                                    (11)

This last equality follows directly by multiplying a+b*t by 1,t and
requiring both t coefficients divisible by p^k. It independently
checks the trace-dual calculation.

The normalization is UNRAMIFIED everywhere: dp/dt=2t and
d(t*p^k)/dt have no common zero (at t=0 the second is (-1)^k).
Its exact closed collision locus is t=1 or t=-1, paired with each other
on EVERY y-line. Its fibres are reduced, but its conductor has
multiplicity k. For k=2 it is a tacnode control; higher k retain
arbitrarily high tangency. Thus reduced normalization fibres and
exact collision support do not make the conductor ideal radical.

The target plane map (p,q) is NOT Keller: its source Jacobian is 2t.
Here F_T=-2t*p^k has an EXTRA zero at t=0, which is not a collision.
The corrected finite-duality ratio is

    F_V/P_x=p^k,

as in(4). Therefore one cannot drop the constant-Jacobian hypothesis
when replacing this ratio by F_T. These controls use an actual source
coordinate y and a finite unramified normalization, while isolating
the precise missing Keller condition.

Polynomial automorphism controls P=H^m+y, Q=-H, H=x+h(y), m>=2,
have Jac=1 and monic relation F=V^m+(-1)^m*(T-U).
Their conductor F_T is a unit, as required.

### Higher multiple points and persistent generic collinearity

The controls extend to ANY monic squarefree f(t) of degree M>=2:

    P=f(t), Q=t*f(t)^k+y, A=C[P,Q,y], k>=1.

Then R=C[t,y] is the finite birational normalization of A.
The derivatives f'(t) and d(t*f(t)^k)/dt have no common zero, so this
normalization is unramified. The defining polynomial is

    F(U,V,T)=U^(kM)*(f((V-T)/U^k)-U),

expanded as a polynomial, monic in V. Its exact conductor and index are

    (A:R)=f(t)^(k(M-1))*R,
    Delta(U,T)=U^(kM(M-1)/2).                                (15)

The conductor follows either from the trace-dual ratio F_V/P_t or
directly from the basis change Q^j=(t*U^k+y)^j, j<M.
Its norm is the square Delta^2. The finite conductor degree is
k*M*(M-1), including odd numbers of branches M>=3.

For example f=t^3-t and k=1 give F=(V-T)^3-(V-T)U^2-U^4,
Delta=U^3 and conductor f(t)^2. The three source points t=-1,0,1
are identified on each y-line, with conductor order two at each branch.

For target values (U,V), every generic source fibre lies on the line

    y=V-U^k*t.

Thus triple-collinearity is IDENTICAL when M>=3, although the direction
of that line varies with the target. It is not excluded by nonconstancy
of the source-difference direction map. After a generic constant source
shear ell=y+alpha*t, the conductor is
(f(t)^k-alpha)^(M-1) and the same persistent multiple collisions remain.
The finitely many alpha at which f'(t) and f(t)^k-alpha have a common
zero are omitted from that unramified control.

The plane target map has Jacobian f'(t), which is nonconstant. These
controls therefore do not refute a future argument exploiting the
actual Keller condition to exclude persistent collinearity. They do
block inferring generic reduced conductor from finite unramified
normalization or from a nonconstant difference-direction map alone.

## 8. What remains for trace integrality

The full finite normalization S, its finite trace dual Lambda, and the
nonnormal coordinate order A=B[y] remain different rings/modules.
Formula(6) gives a new exact actual-source constraint on A and its
entire closed collision locus. The exact index square forces even
conductor degree on every source line and classifies its degree-two
boundary. Target integrality of P_x,Q_x and regularity of
Tr(Lambda*Lambda) remain unproved.

The finite trace-dual and conductor proofs above are algebraic.
The line-fibre restriction uses the attributed one-line theorem.
The checker supports monogenic trace matrices, conductor ratios,
Jacobian identities, reduced closed collision fibres and nonradical
tangency controls. Finite controls do not prove the all-degree theorem.
Automatic trace integrality, HC4 and JC2 remain open. Independent
mathematical review, formal verification and external review are absent.
