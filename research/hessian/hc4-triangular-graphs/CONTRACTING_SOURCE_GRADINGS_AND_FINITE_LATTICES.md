## 1. LN0: spectral components recover every source multiplication

Let R=C[z_1,...,z_n], B=C[P_1,...,P_n] subset R. Assume only that
the Jacobian matrix of P at0 is invertible. In this section P need NOT
be globally Keller. Give the source coordinates arbitrary POSITIVE
integer weights w_i and let

    Delta=sum_i w_i*z_i*partial_(z_i).

Let V subset R be a nonzero finite B-submodule with Delta(V) subset V.
Then V is a nonzero R-IDEAL and R is FINITE over B.

This is a theorem about the ACTUAL embedded module. No freeness,
projectivity, normality, or finite-flat presentation of V is required;
V need not contain1. The contracting fixed point can be any source
point after translating coordinates, or the origin in ANY polynomial
source coordinate system.

First Delta is diagonal and locally finite on R. Polynomial interpolation
in the finitely many weights of an individual v extracts each homogeneous
component v_d. Hence V is a graded C-subspace. If v_d is homogeneous
and b=sum b_e is in B, the components of b*v_d are exactly b_e*v_d.
They all belong to V. Thus V is stable under multiplication by EVERY
homogeneous component of EVERY element of B.

Let H be the subalgebra of R generated by those homogeneous components.
We prove H=R using positivity and the invertible first jet. Order the
distinct source weights increasingly. At the smallest weight w,
the w-components of P_i-P_i(0) are the linear combinations

    sum_(j:w_j=w) (partial_j P_i)(0)*z_j.

The indicated columns of the invertible Jacobian have full column rank,
so they recover all coordinates of that weight. Inductively, at a higher
weight w the same component is this linear expression plus a polynomial
in the ALREADY recovered lower-weight coordinates. Every nonlinear
monomial of weight w uses only variables of weight strictly smaller
than w, since all weights are positive. Subtract it and use the same
full-column-rank argument. This recovers EVERY z_j in H.

Therefore V is closed under all source coordinate multiplications and
is an R-ideal. This step DOES NOT assume Delta(B) subset B.

Choose B-generators v_1,...,v_N of V. Multiplication by any r in R
maps V to V, hence is represented on those generators by an N-by-N
matrix C_r over B. The adjugate identity makes

    det(r*I-C_r)*V=0.

R is a domain and V!=0, so this monic polynomial vanishes at r.
Every r is integral over B. In particular the finitely many z_j are
integral, so R is finite over B. This is the elementary determinant
trick, written out here; it is not a new freeness theorem.

### An explicit ordinary-Euler operator version

For ordinary total degree and D>=max_i deg(P_i), define

    pi_1(T)=product_(0<=r<=D,r!=1)(T-r)/(1-r).

For multiplication operators m_b on R,

    ad_Delta(m_b)=[Delta,m_b]=m_(Delta b),
    pi_1(ad_Delta)(m_(P_i-P_i(0)))=m_(linear part of P_i).

The inverse first-jet matrix then recovers m_(z_j). These are finite
operator identities independent of the degrees of elements of V. They
explain why one source Euler field plus target multiplication is enough;
there is no proposed hierarchy of higher-order inverse ODEs.

## 3. An all-degree control: finite orbits do not give finite module closure

For EVERY m>=1 let

    R=C[x,y], P=x, Q=x^m*y, B_m=C[P,Q],
    Delta_1=(x-1)*partial_x+y*partial_y.

The first jet at(1,0) is invertible, but the global Jacobian is x^m,
NOT a scalar unit. The etale source is x!=0, not the whole plane, so
this is NOT a Keller counterexample.

Every chosen polynomial has a finite-dimensional C-linear Delta_1-orbit.
In source coordinates X=x-1,y,

    Q=(1+X)^m*y.

Its degree1 component is y. The B_m-module generated by the Euler
components of the displayed algebra generators is exactly

    V_0=B_m+B_m*y,

a FINITE module. But it is NOT Delta_1-stable:

    Delta_1(Q*y)=(m+2)*Q*y-m*x^(m-1)*y^2.            (3)

The last monomial is not in V_0. Indeed a monomial x^a*y^b in V_0
must satisfy a>=m*max(b-1,0). For b=2 the exponent a=m-1 violates
this condition. There is no cancellation between distinct monomials.

The smallest B_m-module stable under Delta_1 and containing1 is ALL R:
LN0's spectral-component argument makes it an R-ideal. This closure is
NOT finite over B_m; its fibre over P=Q=0 contains the entire y-line,
so the map is not finite. Equivalently y is not integral over B_m.
For every k, the degree-k component of Q^k=(1+X)^(mk)*y^k is y^k;
the B-multiples cannot be controlled by the finite orbits of Q alone.

This proves in EVERY m that finite source-generator orbits do not justify
a finite invariant target module. No degree cutoff or extrapolation
from computed examples is used.

The same family shows why the FIRST JET at the contracting fixed point
cannot be dropped. For Delta_0=x*partial_x+y*partial_y, B_m itself is
a nonzero finite B_m-module and is stable, but R is not finite over B_m.
The fixed point is now(0,0), where the Jacobian vanishes. LN0 does not
apply at that centre. Its conclusion is properness, not invertibility,
