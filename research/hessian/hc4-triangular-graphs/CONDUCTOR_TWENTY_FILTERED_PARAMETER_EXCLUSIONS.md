# Five conductor-twenty exclusions by parameter equations

**Theorem ConductorTwentyFilteredParameterExclusions.** Let
\(F=(P,Q)\) be a complex polynomial plane Keller map with \(P\)
monic of positive degree in the longitudinal source coordinate \(x\).
Its reduced generic directional order cannot have any of the
characteristic sequences
\[
 (14,4,9),\quad(15,6,5),\quad(21,6,2),\quad(8,6,9),\quad(10,6,5).
\]
The contradiction holds over the generic parameter field. It requires
no hypothesis about specialized conductor degrees.

## A common parameter identity

Write \(K=\mathbb C(y)\), and let \(W,V\) be the reduced actual
generators of degrees \(m,n\), respectively, with \(m<n\).
Use the
[one-place approximate-root theorem](sources/assi-garcia-sanchez-1407.0490v1.pdf),
Propositions 2 and 4. Its monic coefficient hypotheses and the one-place
normalization for the directional order are as in the
[cubic–quintic proof](CONDUCTOR_EIGHT_CUBIC_QUINTIC_EXCLUSION.md).
The reduced pair in each case can be normalized by polynomial target
changes over \(K\). As in the
[conductor-eighteen parameter proof](CONDUCTOR_EIGHTEEN_FILTERED_PARAMETER_EXCLUSIONS.md),
if \(\lambda\) is their determinant, put \(x=\rho z\) with
\(\rho^{m+n}=\lambda\) and scale the two outputs by
\(\rho^{-m},\rho^{-n}\), respectively. Continue with \(W,V\) for
the scaled monic generators. The target determinant is one and its
parameter derivative gives a polynomial target vector field of
divergence zero. Its Hamiltonian \(H\) satisfies
\[
 W_zV_y-W_yV_z=J\rho+\partial_z H(W,V),\qquad
 J=\operatorname{Jac}_{x,y}(P,Q)\ne0 .
\tag{1}
\]
The degrees of the polynomials in the evaluated order belong to
the semigroup generated by the specified characteristic sequence.

## The three square-root cases

For \((n,m,r)=(14,4,9),(15,6,5),(21,6,2)\) the last
approximate root has degree \(r\) in the source and is monic of
degree \(e=2n/m\) in \(W\). The weights \(mi\), \(0\le i<e\),
are distinct modulo \(n\). Thus terms above weight \(2n\)
cannot cancel in this approximate root. Its top terms are
\(W^e-V^2\). The lower terms involving \(V\) are linear in it,
with coefficients polynomial in \(W\). Complete its square and
translate \(W\) to depress the remaining degree-\(e\) polynomial:
\[
 V^2=A(W)+T,\qquad A(t)=t^e+\sum_{i=0}^{e-2}c_i t^i,\qquad
 \deg_z T=r .
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(T\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of the approximate root;
the parameter rescaling already described preserves the identity
and its degree data.

In the monic Laurent branch at infinity,
\(V-A(W)^{1/2}=T/(V+A(W)^{1/2})\) has highest exponent \(r-n\).
Its contribution to the Jacobian on the left side of (1)
has highest exponent at most \(m-1+r-n<0\) in all three cases.
The polynomial part of that Jacobian is therefore exactly
\[
 \left[\frac{W_z}{2A(W)^{1/2}}\sum_{i=0}^{e-2}c_i' W^i\right]_{\ge0}.
\tag{3}
\]
Here derivatives of \(W\) inside \(A(W)\) cancel by the chain rule.
A nonzero term \(c_i'\) has leading degree \(mi+m-1-n\).
The following comparisons are made in descending degree. At each
row the contributions listed above it for the same sequence
have already vanished. Equation (1) gives the upper bound
on the degree of the evaluated \(H\); the last column is the
largest semigroup value within that bound.

| Sequence | \(i\) | Degree in (3) | Upper bound on \(\deg H\) | Largest allowed degree |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \((14,4,9)\) | 5 | 9 | 10 | 9 |
| | 4 | 5 | 6 | 4 |
| | 3 | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| \((15,6,5)\) | 3 | 8 | 9 | 6 |
| | 2 | 2 | 3 | 0 |
| \((21,6,2)\) | 5 | 14 | 15 | 14 |
| | 4 | 8 | 9 | 8 |
| | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2 |

In every row \(\partial_z H\) has degree strictly below the degree
of the coefficient term, so that coefficient derivative vanishes.
All omitted terms of (3) have negative order. The Jacobian on the
left of (1) is therefore zero, which requires \(H(W,V)\) to have
degree one. That degree is absent in each semigroup.

## The two cubic cases

For \((n,m,r)=(8,6,9),(10,6,5)\) let \(e=3n/m\), respectively
four and five. The degree-\(r\) approximate root is monic of
degree \(e\) in \(W\), with leading terms \(W^e-V^3\). As before,
the weights \(mi\) for \(0\le i<e\) are distinct modulo \(n\),
so terms of weight above \(3n\) cannot cancel. Depress the
cubic in \(V\) by a triangular shear and translate \(W\) to
remove the degree-\((e-1)\) term of its monic polynomial.
The normalized pair satisfies
\[
 V^3+A(W)V=B(W)+T,\qquad
 A(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor2n/m\rfloor}a_i t^i,\quad
 B(t)=t^e+\sum_{i=0}^{e-2}b_i t^i,\quad\deg_z T=r .
\]
Apply \(W_z\partial_y-W_y\partial_z\), which annihilates \(W\).
The identity becomes
\[
 (3V^2+A(W))\operatorname{Jac}_{z,y}(W,V)
 = W_z \left(\sum_i b_i' W^i-\sum_i a_i'W^i V\right)
   +\operatorname{Jac}_{z,y}(W,T).
\tag{4}
\]
The denominator on the left has degree \(2n\). The last term on
the right has degree at most \(m-1+r<2n\), so it contributes
nothing to the polynomial part after division. The derivative
terms for \(a_i\) and \(b_i\) have respective Laurent degrees
\(mi+m-1-n\) and \(mi+m-1-2n\). Their nonnegative degrees are
all distinct for each sequence.

| Sequence | Coefficient | Degree after division | Upper bound on \(\deg H\) | Largest allowed degree |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \((8,6,9)\) | \(a_2\) | 9 | 10 | 9 |
| | \(a_1\) | 3 | 4 | 0 |
| | \(b_2\) | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| \((10,6,5)\) | \(a_3\) | 13 | 14 | 12 |
| | \(a_2\) | 7 | 8 | 6 |
| | \(b_3\) | 3 | 4 | 0 |
| | \(a_1\) | 1 | 2 | 0 |

Again (1) forces all listed coefficient derivatives to vanish,
in descending order. This leaves a zero polynomial Jacobian in
(4), which contradicts the nonzero constant \(J\rho\) in (1)
because neither semigroup contains degree one.
