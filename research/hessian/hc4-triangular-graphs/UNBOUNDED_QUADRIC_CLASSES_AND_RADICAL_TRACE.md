# Unbounded quadric plane classes and nonconstant radical-trace obstructions

15 November 2026. Claims **HC4MYIX0/1/2**.
The preceding [IW0/1/2](QUADRIC_MIDDLE_CYCLE_AND_FREE_PROJECTION_CLOSURE.md)
closes every constant-trace embedding retaining the intrinsic free variables.
This note both identifies a precise obstruction to extending that cycle
argument and closes new nonconstant-trace cases in the rank-one chart.

## IX0: actual affine planes have every integral middle class

Use the smooth affine four-quadric

    V={X^2+w1 Y1+w2 Y2=1}.

Let alpha be the infinite-order class of
P+={w1=w2=0,X=1} in integral H_4^BM(V,Z), as proved in IW0.
For every integer m>=1 there is a genuine closed embedding

    e_m(t,s)=(w1=t^m, w2=s, X=1+t^(m+1),
              Y1=-2t-t^(m+2), Y2=0)                       (1)

of A2 into V, and its fundamental class is exactly m alpha.

**Closedness in every degree.** Put r=X-1 and

    V_m(r)=sum_(k=0)^(m-1) (-r)^k/2^(k+1).

The finite geometric identity is

    (2+r)V_m(r)=1-(-r)^m/2^m.

On (1), r=w1 t, Y1=-(2+r)t and r^m=w1^(m+1). Consequently the ambient
polynomial

    t=-Y1 V_m(r)+(-1)^m w1^m r/2^m,        s=w2            (2)

recovers both parameters. This proves a surjective coordinate-ring map,
hence an actual closed embedding, without any finite degree search.
Direct expansion gives the quadric equation.

**The exact class.** Let
M+={w1=0,Y2=0,X=1} and
P-={w1=w2=0,X=-1}.
On the integral threefold {w1=0} in V, the principal divisor of w2 has
cycle P+ + P-, so [P-]=-alpha. Write
a_i=(w_i+Y_i)/2, b_i=(w_i-Y_i)/(2i).
Rotation of (X,b2) through pi fixes the remaining coordinates, preserves
the sum-of-squares equation and takes P- onto M+. This is a proper
homotopy of complex linear maps, just as in IW0. Thus [M+]=-alpha.

On the integral threefold D={Y2=0}=Q2 times A1_(w2), consider

    F_m=(X-1)^m-w1^(m+1).

Away from w1=0 its zero set is precisely e_m(A2) with t=(X-1)/w1,
and is generically reduced. At the generic point of M+, X+1 is a unit and

    X-1=-w1 Y1/(X+1),
    F_m=w1^m[(-Y1/(X+1))^m-w1].

The bracket is a unit there, so the multiplicity is m. On w1=0 the only
possible zero has X=1, so there is no additional component. Therefore

    div_D(F_m)=e_m(A2)+m M+,
    [e_m(A2)]=-m[M+]=m alpha.                              (3)

Reflection X->-X sends alpha to -alpha, so it supplies all negative
integer multiples too. Class zero also occurs: the plane

    w1=1, X=0, w2=s, Y2=t, Y1=1-st

is a genuine closed A2. It is the principal divisor X=0 on the integral
threefold {w1=1} in V, so its middle class is zero.

Thus arbitrary closed affine planes do not have a unit or even nonzero
middle class. Contractibility, smoothness and polynomial source recovery
do not supply that conclusion. IW works because its planes are sections
over the retained free pair with one fixed sign. For m>1, (1) has retained
projection (t,s)->(t^m,s), which is ramified; it is not such a section and
is not a Keller counterexample.

**Why passing to all levels loses the IW class.** The total family

    {(v,T): Q(v)=T}

is the graph of Q and is isomorphic to A5. The plane P+ on the level
T=c^2 has zero absolute class there: it is the principal divisor X-c
on the integral A3 given by w1=w2=0, with X,Y1,Y2 free.
The same statement holds after excluding the critical level T=0:
the containing threefold is then Gm_X times A2.
Thus the fibre class used by IW becomes zero under this proper
closed inclusion. Putting different levels into one total space does
not preserve the nonzero absolute cycle certificate. A relative theory
would require additional data and an additional proof.

## IX1: the radical zero curve gives actual Keller reconstruction

Let K=(K1,K2) be complex plane Keller, and suppose an actual closed
embedding

    j(z,w)=(w1,w2,X(z,w),Y1(z,w),Y2(z,w))

satisfies, with no helper degree bound,

    X^2+w1 Y1+w2 Y2=D(z)+w dot K(z).                        (4)

Set f(z)=X(z,0), a_i(z)=partial_(w_i)X(z,0), and y_i(z)=Y_i(z,0).
The exact trace and first jet are

    D=f^2,                 K_i=y_i+2f a_i.                 (5)

The closed zero-section is the actual embedding
z->(f,y1,y2) into A3. Its source coordinate ring R=C[z] is therefore
generated by those three elements. Modulo f, (5) gives

    R/(f)=C[the images of K1,K2].                           (6)

Equivalently the restriction

    K: V(f) -> A2

is a closed immersion, including its scheme structure if f is not
square-free. In particular K is injective on the entire reduced curve
f=0, not merely on one generic branch.

If f=0 has any irreducible component isomorphic to A1, then K is a
polynomial automorphism. Indeed this is a closed embedded A1 in the
source plane. The retained Abhyankar-Moh-Suzuki plane-line rectification
takes it onto a coordinate line. Compose K with that source plane
automorphism; the resulting pair remains Keller and is injective on the
line. The retained Gwozdziewicz theorem HC4MYGL0 supplies its inverse.

This uses precisely the plane-line rectification input already explicit in
[GR0](CRITICAL_COMPONENTS.md), together with
[GL0](CRITICAL_COORDINATE_JET.md), not an affine-three-space rectification.
For example every nonconstant f=P(u), where u is a polynomial source
coordinate and P is arbitrary, closes: any root of P gives a closed
coordinate line. Multiple roots do not affect this conclusion.
The cases f identically zero and f a nonzero constant are respectively
the earlier critical and full noncritical constant-trace closures.

Hence a hypothetical nonautomorphic rank-one retained-free embedding
has nonconstant f and **no actual affine-line component in f=0**.
A rational normalization, a punctured line or Euler characteristic one
does not substitute for that hypothesis. Higher free jets cannot remove
the exact closed-curve condition (6).

## IX2: a target-descended trace is forced onto singular parametric curves

Continue with (4), assume f is nonconstant, and suppose

    D=f^2 belongs to B=C[K1,K2].                            (7)

The retained no-abelian-intermediate theorem HW0 gives f in C(K):
otherwise C(K)(f)/C(K) would be a nontrivial quadratic Galois extension.
The same retained coprime-pullback denominator argument used in IW1 gives

    R intersect C(K)=B,       f=a(K),       D=a(K)^2         (8)

for a nonconstant target polynomial a(T1,T2).
Thus even descent of the square of the normal trace forces descent of
the normal trace itself; no choice of a square root in the source field
is silently adjoined.

Let A be the reduced target curve a=0 and C the reduced source curve
a(K)=0. By IX1, K:C->A2 is a closed embedding. Its image is exactly A.
To justify surjectivity, every irreducible factor a_i has a nonconstant
pullback a_i(K); each source prime factor dominates the target divisor
a_i=0 by HW0. The closed image therefore contains a dense subset of
every target component and hence every component. Both curves are
reduced, so the closed embedding is an isomorphism

    K: C -> A.                                              (9)

In particular **every target point on a=0 has exactly one source preimage**.

Now suppose K is nonautomorphic, with generic field degree d>1.
At any target point outside its nonproperness set S_K, a proper
quasi-finite Keller map is finite etale on a neighbourhood and has
exactly d points in its fibre. A finite etale rank on a nonempty open
neighbourhood agrees with the generic degree. Thus (9) implies

    A is contained in S_K.                                 (10)

The retained qualitative Jelonek-Lason theorem, arXiv:1411.5011v2
Theorem 3.2, makes S_K a union of polynomially parametrized curves.
A finite target open is obtained by clearing algebraic-equation
denominators, so S_K lies in a proper target curve. At a generic point
of any component of A, the supplied nonconstant polynomial curve must
lie in that component. Every component of A consequently has affine
normalization A1, by the retained normalization/Hurwitz argument of IC1.

Moreover every such component must be singular. If a component were
smooth, its normalization A1 would be that component itself. Its inverse
image under (9) would be a closed A1 component of f=0, contradicting IX1.

These are all-degree necessary conditions, and they give actual new
inverse certificates: under (4) and (7), K is an automorphism whenever
a=0 has either

- a component not polynomially parametrizable by A1 (equivalently its
  affine normalization is not A1), or
- a smooth irreducible component.

For example a=T1*T2-1 closes by its nonconstant units: every polynomial
map A1->V(a) is constant. Arbitrary positive powers of that polynomial
have the same reduced curve and close as well. Smooth coordinate
curves close by IX1. A singular cusp T2^2=T1^3 passes these necessary
curve tests and is not declared closed.

For every beta on a=0, the perturbed cotangent potential
f^2+w dot (K-beta) has exactly one critical point, at the unique z over
beta and w=0: its free derivatives are K-beta, and grad(f^2)=0 there.
Thus if a(0)=0 the original potential has exactly one critical point.
This excludes its unit-ideal endpoint in that case without excluding
the singleton endpoint or proving that such a nonautomorphic
representative exists.

## Sources, boundaries and replay

The topology and principal-divisor foundations of IX0 are the explicitly
retained IW inputs. IX1 uses the retained plane-line rectification and
one-line theorem; IX2 uses HW0 and the qualitative nonproperness theorem
already imported in IC1. No new unproved theorem is imported here.

New literature inspection of
[Blanc-van Santen](https://arxiv.org/html/1702.00779v2), Introduction
and Theorems 1-3, provides context about inequivalent affine-space
embeddings in quadrics of dimensions two and three. It does not prove
our four-quadric class formula or a general rectification statement.
The class formula (1)-(3) is proved directly. A current-source inspection
of [graded Keller maps](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20210v1) concerns
equivariant maps; no grading is assumed or imported for (4).

The [source context](sources/radical-trace-context.json) pins all used
prior proofs and primary inputs. Run

    .venv/bin/python research/hessian/hc4-triangular-graphs/verify_radical_trace.py

for [exact controls](receipt-radical-trace.json); see also
[scoped validation](validation-radical-trace.json). Controls do not
prove the infinite class formula by sampling m, or certify the external
geometric inputs. The unbounded arguments are written above.

Nonconstant normal traces without affine-line zero components, singular
target-descended parametric curves, higher helper ranks, intrinsic
projections not known to be ambient, actual endpoint representatives
and unrestricted HC4/JC2 remain open.
