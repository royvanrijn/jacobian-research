# Primitive helpers and the normality of the Legendre hypersurface

15 November 2026. A single extra polynomial helper cannot repair a
nonautomorphic Keller map even away from a source subset of codimension
at least two. For every primitive helper, the implicit hypersurface
must be nonnormal along a divisor reached by actual source points.
For a Keller gradient, the Legendre energy supplies a canonical
primitive helper; its implicit hypersurface is normal if and only if
the gradient is invertible.

We also give the complete polynomial compensation identity in the
remaining noncritical rank-one chart. It isolates the higher free
dependence and syzygy term without treating them as a degree search.
Claims HC4MYIV0/1/2 are recorded in
[MATH_STATUS.json](../../MATH_STATUS.json). None of these assertions
makes a primitive field element into a global source generator, or
makes the Legendre hypersurface of an arbitrary embedding normal.
Both plane endpoints and unrestricted HC4/JC2 remain open.

## 1. Complete compensation data (HC4MYIV0)

Take Q=X²+w1Y+w2Z, c!=0, and a plane Keller pair K(z).
Every polynomial substitution retaining w with

~~~
Q composed with j=c²+w dot K(z), X(z,0)=c
~~~

has the following complete form. Put H=X-c. Since H belongs to
(w1,w2), choose polynomials A1(z,w),A2(z,w) with H=w dot A.
The potential identity gives

~~~
w1(Y+(2c+H)A1-K1)+w2(Z+(2c+H)A2-K2)=0.
~~~

Coprimality of w1,w2 makes their syzygy module generated by
(w2,-w1): reduction modulo w1 makes the second coefficient
divisible by w1, and substitution gives the first coefficient.
Thus for a polynomial C(z,w),

~~~
X=c+H,
Y=K1-(2c+H)A1+w2 C,
Z=K2-(2c+H)A2-w1 C.                               (1)
~~~

Conversely every A1,A2,C gives the exact potential identity, in
every degree. These data are not unique. All changes with fixed H
are exactly

~~~
A -> A+(w2,-w1)R,
C -> C+(2c+H)R,                                   (2)
~~~

and leave all five coordinate functions unchanged.

For an actual closed embedding, IU0 additionally requires

~~~
(K1-2c A1(z,0), K2-2c A2(z,0))
       to be a polynomial source coordinate pair. (3)
~~~

This zero-section obligation is not global immersion or ring
generation. IU2 is the special case A=(K-z)/(2c), independent of w,
and C=0; its global immersion already forces the inverse. Arbitrary
higher free dependence remains in (1). A shear preserving w1Y+w2Z
is not automatically an ambient automorphism when its coefficient
depends on coordinates that it moves.

## 2. One primitive helper and its source singular divisor (HC4MYIV1)

Let F:A^n_x->A^n_T be complex Keller, n>=1, R=C[x],
k=C(F), L=C(x). Let a in R be a primitive field helper:

~~~
L=k(a).
~~~

Let q(T,S) be its primitive irreducible polynomial relation over
C[T]. The closure of (F,a) is Z=V(q), with C[Z]=C[F,a].
This primitive-field hypothesis is essential to the normality and
descent criteria below. Define

~~~
p=q_S(F,a) in R, p!=0.                             (4)
~~~

Separability makes p nonzero. Polynomial Nambu derivations
delta_i(F_j)=delta_ij give

~~~
q_(T_i)(F,a)=-p delta_i(a).
~~~

Therefore the pullback of the full hypersurface Jacobian ideal is
exactly

~~~
(q_S,q_(T_1),...,q_(T_n))R=(p).                     (5)
~~~

A zero of p is a singular point of the implicit image, although
(F,a) has injective differential everywhere because DF is invertible.
Differential immersion and locally closed embedding must be distinguished.

### Descent, normality and codimension-one recovery

If p=b(F) for a polynomial b, q_S-b(T) vanishes at (F,a).
The kernel is (q), while this difference has smaller S degree.
Hence q_S=b(T). Characteristic zero makes deg_S q=1, and
primitivity gives L=k.

Here the birational Keller inverse can be proved by the retained HW0
coprime-pullback argument. A source divisor maps onto a target divisor
under an etale quasi-finite Keller morphism. Thus coprime target
polynomials have coprime pullbacks: their codimension-two intersection
cannot contain that divisor image. Write a rational inverse coordinate
as u(T)/v(T) in lowest terms. Its polynomial source value makes v(F)
divide u(F), so v(F) is a unit and v is constant. Every inverse
coordinate is polynomial. The same argument gives

~~~
R intersection k=C[F].                             (6)
~~~

Even p^m in C[F] for any positive integer m suffices. The intermediate
extension k(p)/k is cyclic Galois, since all roots of S^m-p^m lie in
k(p) and C contains the roots of unity. HW0 excludes a nontrivial
abelian Galois intermediate extension inside a Keller source field.
Thus p lies in k, and (6) reduces to the previous case.

In particular p in C* forces the inverse. Under nonautomorphy p must
be nonconstant, and p=0 has a source prime divisor D. The map (F,a)
is quasi-finite, since F is. The closure of its image of D therefore
has dimension n-1; by (5) it is a singular divisor of Z. Normal
Noetherian varieties are regular in codimension one. Consequently
normality of Z, or regularity at the images of all source prime
divisors, forces the inverse. The failure cannot be confined to
missing points at the boundary or to source codimension at least two.

If (F,a) is a locally closed embedding near every codimension-one
source point, its image there is open in its irreducible closure.
It identifies a smooth source neighbourhood with that open subset,
so p is a unit at every such point. A polynomial with that property
is a nonzero constant. This weaker embedding condition also forces
the inverse. Local embedding on a nonempty source open already gives
generic birationality, so this condition can be applied without first
supplying a separate primitive-field certificate.

There is also a componentwise saturation criterion. For every
irreducible component D of p=0, suppose D is the only source prime
divisor over the generic point of the closure of F(D). The target
irreducible equation then has square-free pullback with only component
D, by HW0. Factoring p gives p in C[F], so the inverse follows.
Under nonautomorphy at least one component must share its generic
target image with another source divisor. Saturation of the union
alone is not this componentwise condition.

The recent source for the characteristic-zero weak-type-one
mechanism is [Borisov--Gabber--Vasiu,
Definition 22.2 and Proposition 27.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05746v1).
The derivative, divisor and descent arguments used here are reproduced
above with explicit hypotheses. No positive-characteristic assertion
from that paper is imported, and no new priority is claimed.

## 3. The canonical Legendre hypersurface (HC4MYIV2)

Let h be a complex Keller potential in any dimension, F=grad h,
and G=x dot F-h. IT1 proves delta_i G=x_i. This also proves
that G is primitive: differentiate its separable minimal equation,

~~~
delta_i G=-q_(T_i)(F,G)/q_S(F,G) in k(G).
~~~

Every source coordinate is in k(G), hence C(x)=C(F)(G).
This is [Adamus--Hajto, Theorem 4.15](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36736v1),
reproduced here with its attribution.

Call V(q), the closure of (grad h,G), the Legendre hypersurface.
Its derivative p=q_S(F,G) satisfies the stronger exact identities

~~~
q_(T_i)(F,G)=-p x_i.                               (7)
~~~

The following are equivalent:

- grad h is a polynomial automorphism;
- its Legendre hypersurface is normal;
- that hypersurface is regular at the image of every source prime divisor;
- (grad h,G) is a locally closed embedding near every source
  codimension-one point;
- p is a nonzero complex constant;
- some positive power of p belongs to C[grad h].

IV1 proves the nontrivial implications. In the automorphic case G=B(F)
for a polynomial B, so the primitive relation is a scalar multiple of
S-B(T), and all the properties hold. If p=c in C*, q=cS+b(T),
and (7) gives the actual inverse

~~~
x_i=-b_(T_i)(F)/c.                                 (8)
~~~

This is an exact implicit-equation certificate, not a claim that a
bounded search finds it for arbitrary graphs.

### Generic stable fibres already carry the collision divisor

In the actual cotangent form h=D(z)+w^T K(z), use the polynomial
coordinates eta=grad D+DK^T w. Then F=(eta,K) and
G=z dot eta-D(z). Put k0=C(K), L0=C(z), d=[L0:k0].
Let z_1,...,z_d be the distinct embeddings into an algebraic closure
Omega0 of k0. Over the generic plane target field the minimal energy
equation can be made monic and is exactly

~~~
q(eta,S)=product_i (S-eta dot z_i+D(z_i)).            (9)
~~~

Its coefficients are in k0[eta]: the labels are affine in the
independent eta variables and the product is fixed by Galois.
This normalization only multiplies the original primitive equation
by a nonzero element of k0 on these generic fibres. At sheet i,

~~~
p_i(eta)=product_(j!=i)
       (eta dot (z_i-z_j)-D(z_i)+D(z_j)).            (10)
~~~

Each factor has nonzero linear part. For d>1 the geometric generic
stable source plane already has a nonempty singular-preimage divisor,
of total eta degree d-1: affine lines counted with multiplicity.
Different factors need not be nonproportional. This is not a singularity
of the actual closed HC4 embedding j. That image is still isomorphic
to the smooth source; the Legendre hypersurface is a different image.

## Controls and exact remaining obligations

For F=(s,t²), a=t, the primitive implicit image S²-T2=0 is smooth,
but F is not Keller. Its rational Nambu derivative delta_2 a=1/(2t)
prevents the polynomial divisibility (5). The normalization map
t->(t²-1,t(t²-1)) has injective differential everywhere and identifies
t=+/-1 at a node. Neither is a Keller counterexample.
