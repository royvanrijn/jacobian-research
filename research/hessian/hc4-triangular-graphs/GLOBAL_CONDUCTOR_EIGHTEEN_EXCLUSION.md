# Exclusion of global directional conductor degree eighteen

**Theorem KellerDirectionalConductorEighteenExclusion.** Let
\((P,Q)\) be an actual noninvertible complex plane Keller map in
a source frame \((x,y)\) with \(P\) monic of positive degree in \(x\).
Its exact global directional coordinate conductor has even \(x\)-degree
at least twenty. The coordinate-order normalization defect is at least
ten on the generic line and on every line where the global leading
conductor coefficient is nonzero. On arbitrary specialized lines the
lower bounds are two for conductor degree and one for normalization
defect.

## Exclusion of the four remaining embeddings

The [degree-sixteen theorem](GLOBAL_CONDUCTOR_SIXTEEN_EXCLUSION.md)
gives even global degree at least eighteen. Its
[classification and reductions](GLOBAL_CONDUCTOR_SIXTEEN_EXCLUSION.md#characteristic-sequences-at-degree-eighteen)
leave four reduced embeddings at degree eighteen. Each is excluded by
a parameter identity for the actual Keller generators.

| Sequence | Branch exclusion | Finite parameter control |
|---|---|---|
| \((10,3)\) | [Cubic–decic](CONDUCTOR_EIGHTEEN_CUBIC_DECIC_EXCLUSION.md) | Three initial collision resultants exclude parameter poles. A fixed singular tangent determinant excludes homogeneous scaling. |
| \((7,4)\) | [Quartic–septic](CONDUCTOR_EIGHTEEN_QUARTIC_SEPTIC_EXCLUSION.md) | Two parameter invariants force immersed pole shapes. The homogeneous case again gives a fixed singular order. |
| \((10,4,11)\) | [Quartic–decic](CONDUCTOR_EIGHTEEN_QUARTIC_DECIC_EXCLUSION.md) | The degree-nine collision equation controls gaps in three coefficient pole regimes. |
| \((9,6,7)\) | [Sextic–nonic](CONDUCTOR_EIGHTEEN_SEXTIC_NONIC_EXCLUSION.md) | The approximate-root identities and local collision equations control the degenerations of the square-leading branches. |

Thus degree eighteen is excluded and the global bound is twenty.
By [exact coordinate-order specialization](GENERIC_COORDINATE_ORDER_CONDUCTOR_AND_CLOSED_COLLISIONS.md),
the specialized degree is twice the normalization defect on every line.
It equals the global degree on the lines specified in the theorem.
The same specialization theorem gives the arbitrary-line bounds.

The [degree-twenty theorem](GLOBAL_CONDUCTOR_TWENTY_EXCLUSION.md)
uses the next characteristic-sequence classification to raise the
global bound to twenty-two.
