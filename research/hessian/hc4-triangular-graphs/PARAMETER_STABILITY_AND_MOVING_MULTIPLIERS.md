# Parameter stability and moving multiplier suspensions

15 November 2026. Plane polynomial-output irreducibility survives
every algebraic extension of the parameter field. This removes the
correlated-factor obstruction for multipliers depending on `T`.
The remaining global questions are a common free filtration and the
fibres at zeros of the multiplier's coefficient content.

Let `K=(f,g)` be complex plane Keller, `kappa=C(T)`,
`F=C(T,V)` and `L=C(s,t)`, with `T=f,V=g`. Claims
`HC4MYIM0/1/2/3` are recorded in
[MATH_STATUS.json](../../MATH_STATUS.json). All polynomial, cover,
parameter and stable embedding degrees below are unrestricted.
The unrestricted source-field conclusions remain plane-only.
Neither plane endpoint nor unrestricted HC4/JC2 is resolved.

## 1. Irreducibility after every parameter extension (HC4MYIM0)

For every nonconstant `P in C(T)[Z]` and every finite algebraic
`E/kappa`, the polynomial

```
P(f,Z)+g is irreducible over L E.             (1)
```

Equivalently it remains irreducible over `L kappa_bar`, where
`kappa_bar` is an algebraic closure of `kappa` in a common algebraic
closure of `F`. A factor over that union has coefficients in a
finite extension, so the finite statement implies the latter one.
The retained generative-polynomial transfer HC4MYIL0 then gives
the same assertion for every positive-joint-degree polynomial in
arbitrarily many variables `Z_1,...,Z_k`.

### 1.1 Bad inertia persists in the smaller source subgroup

Enlarge `E` to a finite Galois extension of `kappa`. Let `M/F`
be the splitting field of `P+V`, and let `N_bad` be the normal
subgroup of `Gal(M/F)` generated by actual inertia over target
curves with no nonconstant polynomial `A1` parametrization. Every
such curve is horizontal: a vertical irreducible curve is the line
`T=c`, which has a polynomial parametrization.

Form `M_E=M E`, finite Galois over `F`. At a horizontal target
prime the parameter extension `E(V)/kappa(V)` is unramified:
the finite separable scalar extension

```
kappa[V] -> E[V]
```

is finite etale, and its localizations include every horizontal
prime of the original target. For a chosen inertia group `I` of
`M/F`, take a prime above it in `M_E/F`. Its inertia `I_E` maps
surjectively to `I` by the newly explicit primary input
[Stacks 0BU7, Lemma 15.114.11](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BU7).
Its image in the unramified cover `E(V)/F` is trivial, so it
fixes `E`.

The retained plane HC4MYII0/3 applies to `M_E/F` with the
**original** source `L`. Since the target curve is nonparametrizable,
it puts `I_E` inside the source image over `L`. An element of
that source image which fixes `E` is in the source image over
`L E`: this is the subgroup fixing `L E` in the Galois extension
`M_E L/L`. Projecting to `M` therefore proves

```
N_bad subset H_E,
H_E=image Gal(M L E/L E) in Gal(M/F).         (2)
```

This holds for every prime over every such target curve. We have
not applied a plane nonproperness theorem to a parameter-cover
source. At vertical parameter branch curves the inertia need not
fix `E`; no such assertion is used.

### 1.2 Integral monic models remain valid after enlargement

The proof of HC4MYIK0/1 gives the following useful strengthening
of its stated base-field consequence. If `R in kappa[U]` admits
an integral algebraic affine monic model, then `R(f,U)+g` is
irreducible over `L E` for every finite `E/kappa`.

Indeed choose a finite Galois parameter field containing `E`, the
affine input change and all coefficients of that model. Enlarging
the parameter field preserves their integrality over `C[T]`.
The actual finite normal parameter curve and smooth source base
change then satisfy HC4MYIK0, whose conclusion is irreducibility
over that enlarged source field itself. Descent to its subfield
`L E` gives the assertion. The generic monic model plus `V` is
irreducible by linearity in `V`, and its total space is the smooth
parameter curve times an affine line. Thus no extra normality or
irreducibility hypothesis is introduced by enlarging the field.

The source-cover units still need not be constant. The retained
proof uses their finite-free norms on the original polynomial
source, and all conjugate monic models, exactly as before.

### 1.3 Use the same original polynomial block quotient

The target subgroup `N_bad` and its root blocks are those from
the original HC4MYIK3 proof and do not depend on `E`. If it is
transitive, (2) already proves (1). Otherwise its block quotient
is a polynomial decomposition

```
P=R composed with S, R,S in kappa[Z],        (3)
```

whose outer polynomial `R` has every finite critical value integral
over `C[T]`. These are the retained one-variable Luroth, unique-pole
and critical-curve arguments in HC4MYIK3, applied to the original
target cover. HC4MYIK2 supplies an integral centered monic model
for `R`. Section 1.2 therefore makes `R(f,U)+g` irreducible over
`L E`. The source group `H_E` acts transitively on the blocks and,
by (2), contains the group transitive inside each block. It is
transitive on all original roots, proving (1).

Generative transfer HC4MYIL0 now proves (1) for
`P in C(T)[Z_1,...,Z_k]` of positive joint degree. This is root
transitivity after parameter extensions, not full preservation of
an arbitrary splitting group or two-variable target cover.

## 2. Moving multiplier rows have zero source invariants (HC4MYIM1)

Let `X` and `Z` be disjoint variable sets, and allow any nonzero
`A(T,X) in C(T)[X]` and any nonconstant `P(T,Z) in C(T)[Z]`.
No leading-coefficient condition is needed for this generic statement.
Let `P=R(S)` be a closed generative decomposition over `kappa`,
with `m=deg R`. Over `kappa_bar`, factor

```
A=c product_(j=1)^b a_j(T,X)^(e_j), e_j>=1,  (4)
```

with distinct geometric irreducible factors; a multiplier in
`kappa*` gives `b=0`. Their finitely many coefficients belong
to a finite Galois parameter extension `E/kappa`.

Over an algebraic closure `Omega` of `F` the generic ring

```
R_Omega=Omega[X,Y,Z,W]/(A(T,X)Y-P(T,Z)-V)     (5)
```

is smooth and has the exact lattice

```
Cl(R_Omega)=direct_sum_(j=1)^b
                     Z^m/Z(1,...,1).        (6)
```

This is the direct localization proof of HC4MYIL1 over the
generic parameter field: invert `A`, eliminate `Y`, and use the
removed divisors `(a_j,S-alpha_i)`. Their only relations are
`div(a_j)=sum_i D_(j,i)`, independently of `e_j`. The proof of
smoothness also applies verbatim: a singular point would have
`A=0`, `P+V=0`, and all `Z` derivatives of `P` zero, impossible
at the transcendental generic value. Factors depending only on
`T` are generic field units and add no row to (6).

The target Galois group may now permute both rows and outer roots.
Pass to the subgroup

```
H_E=Gal(Omega/L E).                          (7)
```

It fixes every row, and acts transitively on the outer roots by
Section 1 applied to `R`. Each root-lattice fixed subgroup is
zero by the retained sum-of-coordinates argument. Hence

```
Cl(R_Omega)^H_E=0,
Cl(R_Omega)^(Gal(Omega/L))=0.                (8)
```

The second assertion follows because the original source group
contains (7). Thus moving multiplier factors and repeated factors
do not leave a source-fixed component class. This conclusion
uses the stronger parameter-stable irreducibility theorem; outer
transitivity over `L` alone would be insufficient.

For example the simultaneous row/root swap in the old two-copy
control acts as `(v_1,v_2)->(-v_2,-v_1)` and fixes `(1,-1)`.
Once the rows are split, such a subgroup has trivial root action,
contradicting Section 1 for a genuine plane Keller source. The
abstract representation control is retained; it is not a Keller
realization or a counterexample to (8).

## 3. Separate the global filtration from the content fibres (HC4MYIM2)

Now take polynomials `A in C[T,X]`, `A!=0`, and
`P in C[T,Z]`, `deg_Z P>0`, with disjoint `X,Z`. Define
the coefficient content

```
delta_A(T)=gcd in C[T] of all X coefficients of A. (9)
```

For `p=#X>=1`, `k=#Z>=1`, let `r>=p+k-1` and include
`r-p-k+1` unused variables. Consider

```
Pi=(T,A(T,X)Y-P(T,Z)).                       (10)
```

### 3.1 The exact affine height-one criterion

Every affine height-one target fibre of (10) is geometrically
integral **if and only if**, at each root `c` of `delta_A`,

```
P(c,Z) is nonconstant and closed over C.     (11)
```

Equivalently its generic level `P(c,Z)+V` is geometrically
irreducible over `C(V)`. This is a condition on the full generic
level, not on every special complex level. If `delta_A` is
constant, the condition is empty.

Indeed `P+V` is nonzero over the residue field of every target
prime `pi`, by the same positive-joint-degree argument as in
HC4MYIL2. If `A` remains nonzero, disjoint variable sets make
it coprime to `P+V`; primitive linearity in `Y` proves geometric
integrality. If `A` becomes zero, `pi` must be `T-c` for a root
of (9), and the fibre is a polynomial extension of
`C(V)[Z]/(P(c,Z)+V)`. A nonconstant closed polynomial has
geometrically integral generic level by the direct field proof
of HC4MYIL0. A composite polynomial instead splits into the
distinct generative factors of its outer equation after an
algebraic closure. A constant `P(c,Z)` makes the fibre empty.
This proves both directions.

There is a useful separate closure in the constant case: if
`A(c,X)=0` and `P(c,Z)=b` is constant, no plane Keller pair can
be carried at all. A carried equation would give

```
g+b=(f-c)H(s,t,w),
J(f,g)=(f-c)J(f,H),                          (12)
```

contradicting the nonzero constant Jacobian. This argument needs
no leading or filtration hypothesis. If a nonconstant composite
level violates (11), no Keller realization is asserted; the
global proof below simply does not apply.

### 3.2 A global frame gives the carried inverse

Assume (11) and supply a common free nonnegative filtration over
`B_0=C[T,V]` for the ambient presentation

```
B_0[X,Y,Z,W]/(A(T,X)Y-P(T,Z)-V),             (13)
```

with only `1` in degree zero, exhaustive multiplicative finite-free
bounded spans, and a generic geometric associated graded domain. Then
every closed stable embedding carrying a plane Keller pair through
(10) forces its polynomial inverse.

Section 2 gives generic normality and zero source-fixed class
subgroup. The selected geometric image component, and then every
target conjugate component, has class zero. With the frame and
all height-one integral fibres, the retained global principal-component
proof of HC4MYHW1 and HC4MYHZ2 gives the common component pencil,
primitive denominator saturation and a degree-one minimal label.
The birational Keller endpoint yields the polynomial inverse.
No target localization remains in this conclusion.

### 3.3 Two explicit all-degree leading certificates

One sufficient frame certificate is a highest-total-degree leading
`X` monomial of `A`, for a supplied graded monomial order, with
nonzero constant complex coefficient. Another is the analogous
leading `Z` monomial of `P`. Either is enough; the content-fibre
condition (11) remains separate.

Put `a=deg_X A>=0` and `d=deg_Z P>0`. Use weights

```
weight(X_i)=weight(Y)=d,
weight(Z_i)=a+1, weight(W_i)=1.              (14)
```

Both top terms in (13) have weight `(a+1)d`. If the certificate
is in `A`, put `Y` first in ties; if it is in `P`, put the `Z`
block first. The relation has a monic leading monomial with
constant coefficient, giving the global free standard-monomial
basis. Monic reduction never increases (14). Its generic graded
equation

```
A_a(T,X)Y-P_d(T,Z)=0                        (15)
```

is primitive linear in `Y` over the generic target field, since
the displayed nonzero coefficients involve disjoint variable
sets. Thus it is a domain. The proof allows a nondomain graded
fibre at a special parameter level, provided its affine fibre
still satisfies (11).

For example `A=1+TX`, `P=Z^m`, every `m>=2`, uses the monic
`P` certificate and has constant content. At `T=0` its affine
fibre is `Y-Z^m-V=0`, integral, while the chosen graded fibre
has equation `Z^m=0`. This is admitted by the affine-fibre
version of the global denominator proof. It differs from the
retained denominator-trap control `A=T,P=Z^m`, whose affine
height-one fibre itself splits and violates (11).

For a moving-row example, `A=X^2-T` has monic leading term and
splits over `C(T,sqrt(T))`. Section 2 kills its correlated row/root
invariants for every polynomial `P(T,Z)` of positive joint degree.

The conclusion is also stable under arbitrary ambient and target
polynomial automorphisms, by the same carried-pair argument as
HC4MYIL2. No source Hessian invariance is used. Neither leading
certificate is asserted necessary: a different verified global
frame can satisfy Section 3.2.

## 4. A uniform adapted frame beyond both original leading certificates (HC4MYIM3)

Let `B(X) in C[X_1,...,X_p]` be nonconstant of joint degree
`a>=1`, let `h(T) in C[T]` be nonzero, and let `b(Z) in C[Z]`
have degree `m>=2`, with leading coefficient `beta!=0`. Every
carried closed stable plane embedding through

```
Pi=(T,(1+h(T)B(X))Y-h(T)b(Z)-Z), r>=p,      (16)
```

forces the Keller inverse. All parameter, multiplier, root,
source and embedding degrees and all variable counts are
unrestricted. For nonconstant `h`, both original top coefficients
can be nonunits, so Section 3.3 need not apply to that presentation.

Introduce the actual ambient polynomial

```
H=B(X)Y-b(Z),
Y=V+Z-h(T)H.                               (17)
```

The ambient ring over `C[T,V]` is isomorphic to

```
C[T,V][X,H,Z,W]/(
 (1+h(T)B(X))H+b(Z)-B(X)Z-B(X)V).           (18)
```

The two formulas in (17) are polynomial and inverse in these
presentations: the relation in (18) is exactly
`H=B(X)(V+Z-hH)-b(Z)`. This is an isomorphism over the original
target ring, not a target localization or a claim that `H` alone
is an ambient coordinate.

Assign

```
weight(X_i)=m-1, weight(Z)=a+1,
weight(H)=m+a, weight(W_i)=1.                (19)
```

The highest weight is `m(a+1)`. The graded equation is

```
h(T)B_a(X)H+beta Z^m=0.                     (20)
```

All other terms in (18) have strictly smaller weight. In particular
`B Z` has weight at most `am+1<m(a+1)`. The two terms in (20)
are coprime as coefficients of the primitive linear equation in
`H`, giving a generic domain. Put `Z` first in ties: the monic
leading term `Z^m` supplies the common global free basis of
monomials with `Z` exponent less than `m`.

The multiplier `1+h(T)B(X)` has constant coefficient content in
`C[T]`. If `h(c)=0` it becomes one; if `h(c)!=0` its positive
`X` coefficients do not all vanish. Thus (11) is automatic.
Section 2 supplies zero generic source invariants, and the adapted
frame (18)--(20) now invokes Section 3.2. This proves (16) for
every `m>=2`, without adding a chain of degree-specific cases.

For HC4 take `p=2`, `B=X_1 X_2` and
`P=h(T)b(Z)+Z`. The embedding

```
(s,t,u,v) -> (T,X_1,X_2,Y,Z)
          = (s,0,u,t+P(s,v),v)               (21)
```

is closed and carries the identity pair. The nondegenerate ambient
quadratic `T X_2+Y Z+X_1^2` pulls back to
`s u+t v+v P(s,v)`, whose four-variable Hessian determinant is one.
This supplies actual unit-Hessian controls in the moving-coefficient
class; no nonautomorphic Keller pair is constructed.

### A general unimodular parameter frame

The same argument has a larger form. Let `B(T,X)` have positive
generic joint `X` degree `a`. Allow any number of `Z` variables,
and let `b(T,Z)` have joint degree `m>=2` with a highest-degree
graded leading monomial of nonzero constant complex coefficient.
Take `A_0,h in C[T]` with `h!=0` and `gcd(A_0,h)=1`, and
any polynomial remainder `C_0(T,Z)` of joint degree at most
`n`, where `0<=n<m`. Require
the coefficient content of `A=A_0+h B` to be constant. Then every
carried plane output

```
(T,(A_0(T)+h(T)B(T,X))Y-h(T)b(T,Z)-C_0(T,Z)) (22)
```

closes, in every admitted stable dimension and all degrees.
Parameter variation in all lower coefficients of `b` is allowed.

Choose `u,v in C[T]` with `u A_0+v h=1`. Put

```
H=B Y-b, S=V+C_0,
J=u H-v Y.
```

The target equation is `S=A_0 Y+h H`. The parameter matrix

```
(S,J)^tr = [[A_0,h],[-v,u]] (Y,H)^tr
```

has determinant one, and its polynomial inverse gives

```
Y=u S-h J, H=v S+A_0 J.                     (23)
```

Eliminating `Y,H` gives an isomorphic presentation over the
original target ring with the single relation

```
(A_0+h B)J+b-(u B-v)(V+C_0)=0.              (24)
```

Its graded leading `Z` monomial has unit coefficient. Use weights
`m-n` on every `X_i`, `a+1` on every `Z_i`, `m+a n` on `J`,
and one on unused variables. The highest equation is
`h B_a J+b_m(T,Z)`, primitive
linear in `J` because the two displayed coefficients involve
disjoint variable sets. A `Z`-first order gives a common global
free basis avoiding the chosen leading monomial of `b`. All extra
parameter coefficients have degree zero in this filtration.
Therefore (24) is a global free frame.
The constant-content hypothesis makes the height-one condition
empty, so Section 3.2 proves the inverse.
The crucial degree gap is positive: the product `B C_0` has
weight at most `am+n`, whereas the top weight is `m(a+1)`.
Their difference is `m-n`; the unmultiplied `J` term is lower
by `a(m-n)`. This proves the frame uniformly, including the
maximal remainder degree `n=m-1`.

When `B` is fixed and nonconstant, constant content is automatic:
its positive coefficients give a nonzero constant multiple of `h`,
while `gcd(A_0,h)=1`. More generally it is automatic if the
positive-`X` coefficients of `B(T,X)` have gcd one in `C[T]`.
For arbitrary moving `B` it is a genuine condition. For example
`A_0=T`, `h=T-1`, `B=(T-2)X-2` give multiplier content `T-2`;
coprimality of `A_0,h` alone does not remove that content fibre.
This is a coefficient-boundary control, not a Keller realization.

Equations (22)--(24) include (16) with `A_0=1,C_0=Z`.
They use a parameter-unimodular matrix, not a sequence of
degree-specific corrections or a claim that every missing global
frame can be repaired this way.

For one root variable there is a particularly broad consequence.
Let `P(T,Z)` have degree `m>=2` and leading coefficient `h(T)`.
If `A(T,X)` has constant coefficient content, positive `X`
degree, and `h` divides every positive-`X` coefficient of `A`,
then the output `(T,A Y-P)` closes with all lower coefficients
of `P` arbitrary. Write `A=A_0+h B`, with `A_0` its constant
`X` coefficient. Constant content makes `gcd(A_0,h)=1`.
Divide each coefficient of `P` by `h` in `C[T]`. This gives
`P=h b+C_0`, with `b` monic of degree `m` and
`deg_Z C_0<m`. Thus (22)--(24) apply directly. This condition
does not require the original top multiplier or root coefficient
to be a unit.

## 5. Evidence and remaining boundary

The [checker](verify_parameter_stability.py),
[receipt](receipt-parameter-stability.json) and
[validation](validation-parameter-stability.json) separate exact finite
controls for the written all-degree arguments from whole-repository
checks. The [source record](sources/parameter-stability-import.json)
pins the newly used Galois-tower inertia-surjectivity lemma and
the retained nonproperness, norm, generic-factor and global proofs.

Generic correlated multiplier/root monodromy is no longer an
obstruction for disjoint moving multipliers. A global filtration
without either leading certificate or another adapted frame,
nonconstant composite content fibres, overlapping variable sets,
other output geometries, compatible observables for arbitrary HC4
graphs, actual unit-Hessian endpoint representatives, both plane
endpoints and unrestricted HC4/JC2 remain open. Formal verification,
independent replay, external review and novelty priority are not claimed.
