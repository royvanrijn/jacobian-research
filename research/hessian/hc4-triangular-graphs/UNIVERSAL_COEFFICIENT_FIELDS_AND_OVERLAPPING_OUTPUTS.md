# Independent coefficient fields and overlapping polynomial directions

15 November 2026. Polynomial-output irreducibility extends to
polynomials whose coefficients belong to any independent extension
of `C(T)`, including the function field of a multiplier component.
This removes the disjoint-variable restriction from the generic
class argument when an independent polynomial direction survives
on every multiplier component. A generic positive filtration and
the exact content-fibre criterion then give the carried inverse.
Monic root terms and a polynomial Bezout leading certificate supply
such filtrations uniformly in every degree.

Claims HC4MYIO0/1/2 are recorded in
[MATH_STATUS.json](../../MATH_STATUS.json). The unrestricted
irreducibility assertions remain plane-only. Both plane endpoints
and unrestricted HC4/JC2 remain open.

## 1. Arbitrary independent coefficient fields (HC4MYIO0)

Let `(f,g)` be complex plane Keller, `k=C(T)`, `F=k(V)` and
`L=C(s,t)`, with `T=f,V=g`. For any extension field `D/k`, put

```
L_D=Frac(L tensor_k D).
```

The tensor product is a domain: HC4MYHX0 makes `k` relatively
algebraically closed in `L`, and characteristic zero makes this
finitely generated field extension regular. The tensor construction
keeps the coefficient field independent of the original source.
Then for every nonconstant polynomial `P in D[Z_1,...,Z_q]`,

```
P(Z)+g is irreducible over L_D.                    (1)
```

Field, source, coefficient,
polynomial and variable degrees are unrestricted. This strengthens
HC4MYIM0 in two ways: the coefficients themselves may be algebraic
over `C(T)`, and an arbitrary independent coefficient extension is
allowed. HC4MYIM0 alone concerned a polynomial already over `C(T)`.

### 1.1 First allow algebraic coefficients

Take a univariate `P in E[U]`, where `E/k` is finite, and enlarge
`E` to finite Galois. Write `F_E=E(V)` and let `N/F_E` be the
splitting field of `P+V`. Let `M/F` be a finite Galois closure
containing `N`. In `G=Gal(N/F_E)`, define `B_bad` to be the
normal subgroup generated by all actual inertia at primes of
`E[V]` whose image in `A2_(T,V)` has no nonconstant polynomial
`A1` parametrization.

Such image curves are horizontal. The parameter extension
`E(V)/k(V)` is finite etale there, since `k[V]->E[V]` is
finite etale. For a chosen inertia in `N/F_E`, lift it to inertia
in `M/F_E` using the retained
[Stacks 0BU7 Galois-tower lemma](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BU7).
At a horizontal prime this upper inertia equals the inertia of
`M/F`: its image in `E(V)/F` is trivial, so it already fixes `E`.

Apply HC4MYII0/3 to `M/F` and the **original plane source `L`**.
For a nonparametrizable image curve every such upper inertia lies
in the source image over `L`. Since it fixes `E`, it lies in the
source image over `L E`. Restricting to `N` is surjective onto
the chosen relative inertia, giving

```
B_bad subset H_E=image Gal(N L E/L E) in G.         (2)
```

All primes and conjugate inertia groups are included. No plane
nonproperness theorem is applied to the parameter-cover source.
No inertia at a vertical parameter branch curve is declared trivial.

If `B_bad` is transitive, (2) proves irreducibility. Otherwise
repeat the ordinary one-variable Luroth and unique-pole block-field
argument of HC4MYIK3 over the coefficient field `E`. It gives

```
P=R composed with S,  R,S in E[U],
```

with the `B_bad` orbits as the outer blocks. The outer degree
is at least two. The normal subgroup `B_bad` fixes the outer
splitting field. Therefore no finite critical branch divisor of
`R` on the parameter cover can have a nonparametrizable image
in the original target: inertia there would be among the generators
of `B_bad`, yet would have a nontrivial outer image. Surjectivity
of inertia in the splitting-field tower justifies that last assertion.

Let `b` be any finite critical value of `R`, algebraic over `E`
and hence over `k`. Its image curve in the original target admits
a polynomial parametrization `(T(x),V(x))`, with nonconstant
`T(x)` because the curve is horizontal. The variable `x` is
integral over `C[T(x)]` by its polynomial equation, whose highest
coefficient is a nonzero complex constant. Thus `V(x)` is integral
over `C[T(x)]`. The defining irreducible image curve is the
minimal relation of `b` over `C(T)`, so `b` and all its conjugates
are integral over `C[T]`.

The **valuation lemma** in HC4MYIK2 is valid over any valued
characteristic-zero field; its proof did not require coefficients
in `C(T)`. Apply it to `R in E[U]` after centering and scaling
the input by a root of its leading coefficient. At every place over
finite `T`, integral critical values give integral centered monic
coefficients. A finite Galois parameter field `E'/k` containing
these coefficients and `E` therefore supplies a model

```
Q in O(D')[U], monic,
D' the finite normal curve over A1_T in E'.
```

The root cover `Q+V` has total space `D' times A1`, hence is
smooth and normal. HC4MYIK0, as stated over the actual source
`L E'`, proves its irreducibility. Undoing the input change proves
`R+g` irreducible over `L E'`, hence over `L E`. The source group
is transitive on the blocks and contains `B_bad`, transitive within
each block. This proves `P+g` irreducible over `L E`.

Degree-one polynomials are immediate. The argument may be applied
after **any** finite algebraic enlargement of `E`. Consequently,
for `k0=k_bar` and `L0=Frac(L tensor_k k0)`, every nonconstant
univariate polynomial in `k0[U]` stays irreducible after adding `g`
over `L0`: any hypothetical factor would already be defined over
one finite algebraic parameter extension.

### 1.2 Spread a hypothetical factor over a finite coefficient algebra

Now let `D/k0` be arbitrary. A hypothetical proper factorization
of `P(U)+g`, `P in D[U]`, over `L_D` uses only finitely many
coefficients of `D`. The field `L0` is a finite separable extension
of `k0(g)`. Fix a basis of that extension. Since `L0/k0` is regular,
the same basis is a basis after independent constant extension
to `D(g)`.

Express the factor coefficients in this basis, with rational
functions of `g` having coefficients in `D`. Adjoin their finitely
many numerator and denominator coefficients, and the coefficients
of `P`, to a finite-type `k0`-subalgebra of `D`. Invert one nonzero
coefficient of each denominator polynomial in `g`, and coefficients
that keep both factors of positive degree and keep `P` nonconstant.
Multiplication in the fixed basis has structure constants in
`k0(g)`. Clearing their fixed denominators makes the factorization
a finite collection of polynomial identities over this algebra.

The resulting localized coefficient algebra is a nonzero finite-type
`k0`-algebra. The explicitly imported
[Stacks 00FV Hilbert Nullstellensatz](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00FV)
gives a `k0`-valued closed point, since `k0` is algebraically closed.
Specialization there preserves every denominator and both positive
factor degrees. It produces a proper factorization of a nonconstant
polynomial in `k0[U]` plus `g` over `L0`, contradicting Section 1.1.

For an arbitrary extension `D/k`, enlarge it to a field containing
an algebraic closure of `k`. The preceding argument proves
irreducibility after that enlargement, hence before it. This proves
the univariate statement (1). The generative-polynomial transfer
HC4MYIL0, whose field argument works over any characteristic-zero
coefficient field, proves (1) for arbitrarily many polynomial variables.

Independence is indispensable. If one instead identifies a coefficient
with the **original** source `g` and takes `P=U^2-g`, then
`P+g=U^2`. The tensor construction uses an independent copy `g_D`,
and gives `U^2+g-g_D` instead. No coefficient depending on the
original source variables is being allowed silently. Nor is full
splitting-group preservation inferred from (1).

## 2. Generic class vanishing with overlapping coefficients (HC4MYIO1)

Take `A in k[X]` nonzero and `P in k[X,Z]`, with `p,q>=1`
independent variables `X,Z`. The polynomial `P` may depend
arbitrarily on the multiplier variables `X`. Require:

```
For every geometric irreducible factor a_j of A,
P modulo a_j has positive joint Z degree.           (3)
```

Here the factors are over `k_bar`. If `A` is a generic parameter
unit there are no rows and (3) is empty. For

```
R_Omega=Omega[X,Y,Z,W]/(A Y-P-V),
Omega an algebraic closure of k(V),
```

the ring is smooth, its class group is a direct sum of row lattices

```
Cl(R_Omega)=direct_sum_j Z^(m_j)/Z(1,...,1),          (4)
```

and its subgroup fixed by the plane source is zero. The number
`m_j` is the number of geometric components of the generic level
of the restriction of `P` to `a_j=0`; it need not be the same
for different rows and is not the joint degree of `P`.

### 2.1 Divisors and their only relations

Factor `A=c product a_j^(e_j)` over `k_bar`. Its factors have
coefficients in a finite Galois extension `E/k`. The equation
`A Y-P-V` is primitive linear in `Y` over `Omega`, because
(3) prevents any `a_j` from dividing `P+V`. It is irreducible.
It is smooth as well: for the polynomial function `A Y-P`
over `k_bar`, each irreducible component of its critical locus
has constant value in characteristic zero. There are finitely
many such values, all in `k_bar`, so the transcendental generic
value `V` is smooth.

Inverting `A` eliminates `Y`, giving the UFD
`Omega[X,1/A,Z,W]`. The removed prime divisors are the components
of `(a_j,P+V)`. Every such component dominates `a_j=0` in the
`X` variables. Here is the needed check, since overlap makes it
nonautomatic. A vertical divisor component would lie over a
codimension-one component of the common zero set of all positive
`Z` coefficients of `P modulo a_j`. Those loci are defined over
`k_bar`. On such a component the constant coefficient of `P`
would have to equal `-V` identically, impossible for a regular
function defined over `k_bar`. A subset of higher codimension
cannot support a divisor of this principal level equation. Thus
no component is lost by passing to the generic `X` point.

At that point the field is `E_j=Frac(E[X]/a_j)`, and the
restriction `P_j` is a nonconstant polynomial in `E_j[Z]`.
Its generic level `P_j+V` is geometrically reduced in characteristic
zero. This follows, for example, from the same critical-value
argument over an algebraic closure of `E_j`. The generic point
of `a_j=0` is smooth. Hence the quotient by `a_j` is reduced
at every removed generic divisor point, and in the ambient normal
DVR this means `valuation_D(a_j)=1`. Consequently

```
div(a_j)=sum_(i=1)^(m_j) D_(j,i),                   (5)
```

independently of `e_j`. Every principal relation supported on
the removed divisors is a unit in `Omega[X,1/A,Z,W]`, and these
units are exactly `Omega* product a_j^(n_j)`. Thus (5) gives
all relations and proves (4). In particular the class group is
torsion-free.

### 2.2 The source is transitive within every split row

The subgroup fixing `L E` fixes each multiplier row. By (1),
`P_j+g` is irreducible over the independently extended source
field `L_(E_j)`. The component-dominance argument just given
also works after source base change: the common coefficient-zero
loci are defined over `E`, whereas `g` remains transcendental
over `E`. Thus all components still dominate the multiplier
component, and irreducibility at its function field proves
transitivity of the source subgroup on the `m_j` geometric
components in that row.

For any transitive permutation action, the fixed subgroup of
`Z^m/Z(1,...,1)` is zero by the retained sum-of-coordinates
argument. Therefore every row has zero fixed subgroup under the
source subgroup fixing `E`, and so

```
Cl(R_Omega)^Gal(Omega/L)=0.                         (6)
```

This proves generic class vanishing despite arbitrary overlap of
the `X` coefficients. It does not yet supply a positive generic
filtration or control every affine height-one fibre.

## 3. A carried inverse and uniform generic filtration certificates (HC4MYIO2)

Return to polynomial `A in C[T,X]`, `P in C[T,X,Z]`, and
assume (3). Let `delta_A(T)` be the gcd of all `X` coefficients
of `A`. The exact affine height-one criterion is now

```
At every zero c of delta_A,
P(c,X,Z) is nonconstant and closed over C.          (7)
```

At horizontal target primes, (3) prevents a multiplier factor
from dividing `P+V` after residue extension. At a vertical prime
`T=c` where `A(c,X)!=0`, its factors are defined over `C`, and
the independent transcendental `V` prevents divisibility by any
of them. These fibres are primitive linear hypersurfaces in `Y`.
If `A(c,X)=0`, the fibre is instead a polynomial extension of
`C(V)[X,Z]/(P(c,X,Z)+V)`, which is geometrically integral exactly
under (7), by the retained generative-field proof. This proves
both directions of the criterion, under (3).

Condition (7) also proves faithful flatness: at every `T=c` the
second observable is nonconstant, so every closed target fibre
is nonempty of the expected dimension. The regular-sequence and
miracle-flatness proof of HC4MYIN1 applies unchanged.

Suppose in addition that the generic ambient ring has a positive
filtration as required by HC4MYIN0. Then every genuine closed
stable embedding carrying a plane Keller pair through

```
Pi=(T,A(T,X)Y-P(T,X,Z)), r>=p+q-1,                  (8)
```

forces that pair invertible. Generic smoothness and zero source-fixed
classes come from Section 2; faithful flatness and height-one
geometric integrality have just been proved. HC4MYIN0 gives the
global pencil and inverse. No common global free filtration is
required. This is a conditional general overlap theorem, not a
claim that every overlap has a positive generic filtration.

### 3.1 Leading forms already coprime

One direct certificate is any positive weighting of `X,Y,Z`
for which the highest equation `A_top Y-P_top` is primitive
linear in `Y`, and hence a domain after geometric generic
extension. All coefficients here lie in `F`, so a leading
coefficient is a field unit. The single-relation initial-ideal
argument supplies the required finite-dimensional generic
filtration. This allows overlap whenever the relevant leading
forms are coprime; disjointness is sufficient but unnecessary.

### 3.2 A polynomial Bezout leading coefficient

Here is a uniform certificate even when the original leading forms
are not coprime. Suppose

```
P(T,X,Z)=a(T,X)b(T,Z)+Q(T,X,Z),
deg_Z b=d>=1, deg_Z Q<d,
u A+v a=1 for some u,v in C(T)[X].                  (9)
```

The degree is joint in all `Z` variables. The polynomial `b`
is independent of `X`; all lower `Z` coefficients of `Q` may
depend arbitrarily on `T,X`. The Bezout condition is comaximality
of the ideals `(A,a)` over `C(T)[X]`; for multiple `X` variables
having no common irreducible factor is not by itself enough.

Work only over the generic target field. Set

```
S=V+Q=A Y-a b,
J=v Y+u b.
```

The determinant-one matrix and its inverse give

```
[[S],[J]] = [[A,-a],[v,u]] [[Y],[b]],
Y=u S+a J,  b=-v S+A J.
```

Thus the original generic ring is isomorphic to

```
F[X,J,Z,W]/(A J-b-v(V+Q)).                          (10)
```

Both maps are polynomial over `F[X]` and inverse by (9).
This constructs a generic presentation, and makes no claim that
its coefficients are integral over `C[T,V]`.

Write `ell=deg_X A`. Give every `X` variable weight one, every
`Z` variable weight `M`, and `J` weight `d M-ell`, with unused
variables of weight one. Choose one integer

```
M>max(ell, deg_X(v Q), deg_X v, 0),                 (11)
```

omitting degrees of zero polynomials. Then all weights are positive.
Every term in `v Q` has weight at most
`deg_X(v Q)+(d-1)M<d M`; the `v V` term is lower as well.
The generic highest equation in (10) is

```
A_ell(T,X) J-b_d(T,Z),                             (12)
```

primitive linear in `J` by the disjoint leading variable sets.
It is a geometric domain. This proves the generic filtration
uniformly, with no degree scan or bound on any coefficient degree.

Condition (9) also implies (3): `a` is a unit modulo each
multiplier factor, so `P modulo a_j` retains the positive leading
`Z` term `a b_d`. Together with (7), it therefore proves the
carried inverse in (8) in every degree and admitted dimension.
When `a` is any nonzero polynomial in `T` alone, it is a unit
over `C(T)` and (9) is automatic. In particular this closes
every output monic in one root variable, with arbitrary overlapping
lower coefficients, every primitive multiplier, and the admitted
nonprimitive content levels. No assertion that (9) is necessary
is made; Section 3.1 or another verified generic filtration can
cover additional cases.

### 3.3 A genuine overlapping HC4 control

For every `m>=2` and arbitrary polynomial `C(T,X2,Z)` with
`deg_Z C<m`, take

```
A=1+X1 X2, a=X1, b=Z^m,
Q=Z+X1 C(T,X2,Z), u=1, v=-X2.
```

The leading coefficient `a` is nonconstant, yet `u A+v a=1`.
The HC4-size closed embedding

```
j(s,t,u0,v0)=(s,0,u0,t+v0,v0)
```

in coordinates `(T,X1,X2,Y,Z)` carries the identity pair through
(8), with inverse `(T,Y-Z,X2,Z)` on the image. The independent
nondegenerate ambient quadratic `T X2+Y Z+X1^2` pulls back to
`s u0+t v0+v0^2`, whose actual four-variable Hessian determinant
is one. Arbitrary extra lower overlapping terms and all root
degrees are admitted. This is a compatibility control with an
invertible gradient, not a nonautomorphic Keller construction.

## 4. Boundaries and evidence

The residual polynomial direction (3) is essential to the generic
class conclusion. If `A=X` and `P=X Z`, the generic equation
is `X(Y-Z)=V`; it has the nonconstant unit `X` and no positive
filtration with only constants in degree zero. If `A=X` and
`P=Z^2+X Z^3`, the degree on its multiplier component is two,
even though the joint polynomial degree is four: the row count
must come from the restriction, not the ambient degree.

Comaximality in (9) is a certificate, not synonymous with coprimality
of two polynomials in several variables: `X1,X2` have gcd one
but their ideal is proper. Nor does failure of (9) prove absence
of another generic filtration. Nonconstant composite content
levels remain outside (7); constant content levels still forbid
a carried Keller pair by the earlier Jacobian-divisibility argument.

Remaining cases include overlaps without a residual polynomial
direction, overlaps lacking any verified positive generic filtration,
nonconstant composite content levels, other output geometries,
compatible observables for arbitrary HC4 graphs, actual endpoint
representatives, both plane endpoints and unrestricted HC4/JC2.
Ambient and target polynomial automorphism orbits inherit the
proved carried-output conclusions. Nonlinear source Hessian
invariance is not used, and universal normal forms are not claimed.
