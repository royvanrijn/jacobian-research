# Miyanishi parity lead: quotient-locus audit

Status: **external theorem not adopted by this audit**. This is a source and applicability note, not a new HC4 closure or a disproof of the published theorem. HC4/JC2, centered-even HC4, and the actual finite trace-pairing implication remain open in this thread. LU0/1 is independent of this audit. External review remains deferred.

M. Miyanishi, *Equivariant Jacobian Conjecture in dimension two*, Transformation Groups 28 (2023), 951–971, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00031-022-09727-7), states that an equivariant étale plane endomorphism for a small finite linear group of even order is invertible. The subgroup generated by minus the identity would give the odd-plane case. The [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06709), version 1, has 26 pages; its metadata abstract has broader group scope than the paper. Use the small-group statement.

The preprint defines quasi-étale endomorphisms by an étale self-map of the smooth locus (p.1). Its introductory remark (2), p.2, asserts descent from equivariant plane maps. Lemma 2.1, p.7, starts with a quasi-étale quotient map and obtains a lift with singleton origin fibre. Lemma 1.1 and the scalar-case blow-up argument use that singleton condition. The main proof concludes on p.25. These observations locate a bridge to verify; they do not establish an error in the theorem.

## Exact whole-plane question

Let (G\ne1\) be a small finite subgroup of (\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb C)\), let (\pi:\mathbb A^2\to Y=\mathbb A^2/G\), and let (F\) be a (G\)-equivariant Keller endomorphism. Write (o=\pi(0)\). Every nonidentity element of (G\) has no eigenvalue 1: in dimension two such an element would be a pseudoreflection. Thus (G\) acts freely off 0, (Y^{\rm sm}=Y\setminus\{o\}\), and (F(0)=0\).

There is always an induced morphism (\phi:Y\to Y\). Its restriction is an étale **endomorphism of the smooth locus** if and only if

\[
F^{-1}(0)=\{0\}.
\]

Indeed, preservation of the smooth locus is exactly absence of a nonzero source point mapping to 0. If that absence holds, both quotient maps on the punctured planes are finite étale torsors. Equivariance makes the resulting torsor diagram Cartesian, so étaleness of (F\) descends to (\phi\). Conversely, a nonzero point in the origin fibre gives a smooth quotient point mapped to (o\), violating preservation immediately. Near the origin the equivariant analytic inverse function theorem identifies quotient germs; that local identification does not exclude other origin preimages.

For the odd case (G=\{1,-1\}\), the exact question is therefore whether every whole-plane odd Keller map has singleton origin fibre, or whether another argument justifies the published conclusion without this descent step. This audit supplies neither assertion. Do not replace the whole-plane condition by a local equivariance argument.

## Connected affine control for the local inference

On the connected smooth affine surface

\[
U=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[t,y,1/(t(t^2+1))]
\]

take the free involution (\sigma(t,y)=(-t,-y)\), the target involution ((X,Y)\mapsto(-X,-Y)\), and

\[
F_U(t,y)=(t-t^{-1},y).
\]

This map is equivariant and étale: its Jacobian is ((t^2+1)/t^2\), a unit on (U\). Both ((1,0)\) and ((-1,0)\) map to the target origin. The quotient (U/G\) is smooth because the action is free. Its map to the quadric cone

\[
\mathbb A^2/G=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[a,b,c]/(ac-b^2)
\]

sends a smooth point to the vertex. In local étale coordinates at ((1,0)\), the pullbacks (a=(t-t^{-1})^2\), (b=(t-t^{-1})y\), (c=y^2\) all have zero differential. Thus quotient étaleness also fails there.

This is an actual connected affine control against a purely local descent inference. **It is not a polynomial plane self-map or a counterexample to Miyanishi's statement**: the source is a proper principal open and the first component has a denominator. Whole-plane geometry could supply the missing bridge. A new small symbolic check verifies these displayed identities; it does not test the unknown whole-plane assertion.

## What parity would give, conditionally

Call a potential centered-even up to an affine term when

\[
q(Z)=h(a+Z)-b\cdot Z,\qquad q(-Z)=q(Z).
\]

Translation and subtraction of a linear term preserve the constant Hessian condition and gradient invertibility. Retained HCTANG2 has its existing written-reproduction assurance limits. Its noninverse alternative uses a **constant linear** source frame, so central inversion commutes with that change. Write its normal form as

\[
q(z,w)=D(z)+w\cdot K(z),\qquad \det DK\in\mathbb C^*.
\]

Comparison of coefficients of (w\) in (q(-z,-w)=q(z,w)\) gives (K(-z)=-K(z)\); the constant coefficient gives (D(-z)=D(z)\). Hence an adopted odd-plane inversion theorem would exclude this noninverse alternative. This is a conditional deduction, not an adoption.

Conversely, any odd plane Keller map (K\) gives an even four-variable potential (H(z,w)=w\cdot K(z)\), with Hessian determinant ((\det DK)^2\). Its gradient is ((DK(z)^T w,K(z))\). If (K\) is invertible, this formula has a polynomial inverse. If that gradient has a polynomial inverse, restriction to outputs ((0,v)\) forces (w=0\) and supplies a polynomial inverse of (K\). This direct cotangent branch needs no Tang classification. Thus the all-degree parity connection is precise, but does not settle unrestricted HC4.
