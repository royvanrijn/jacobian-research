\section{Two peripheral models for the fragment groups}

Let
\[
 H=\langle a,t\mid t^2=a^3\rangle,
 \qquad h=a^3=t^2.
\]
The element $h$ is central and infinite. Two choices of peripheral
section will be used:
\[
 e_P=a^{-1}t,\qquad e_Q=ta.
\]
Both are primitive sections in suitable orientable Seifert models over
$D(2,3)$. Explicitly, start with a pair of pants times a circle and
fill two boundary tori. For $H_P$, use cone generators $t,a^{-1}$ with
squares/cubes $h,h^{-1}$; for $H_Q$ use $t,a$ with powers $h,h$.
The primitive filling slopes have multiplicities two and three.
The remaining boundary bases are $(e_P,h)$ and $(e_Q,h)$ respectively.
These are specified peripheral models; no particular named torus-knot
meridian convention is presumed.

Let the cable piece have base annulus with one cone point of order two:
\[
 C=\langle k,s\mid[k,s^2]=1\rangle,\qquad
 f=s^2,\qquad a_C=sk.
\]
Its two boundary bases are $(k,f)$ and $(a_C,f)$. This follows directly
from the pair-of-pants presentation with cone generator $s$, fiber $f$,
and base boundary words $k,(sk)^{-1}$.

All these boundary maps are injective. For $H$, quotienting by $h$
gives $C_3*C_2$, and either section projects to a cyclically reduced
word of length two, hence has infinite order. Since $h$ has infinite
order, $(e,h)$ generates a free abelian group of rank two. For $C$,
quotienting by $f$ gives $\mathbb Z*C_2$. The images of $k$ and $sk$
have infinite order, and $f$ is infinite, so both displayed commuting
pairs give embedded $\mathbb Z^2$. The normal-form theorem for
amalgamated products then makes all piece and edge maps injective.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:models}
The group $P$ is the torus amalgam of $H_P$ and $C_P$ defined by
\[
 k_P=h_P,\qquad f_P=e_P h_P.
\]
For $\epsilon=1$ or $-1$, the group $Q$ is the torus amalgam of $H_Q$
and $C_Q$ defined by
\[
 k_Q=h_Q^\epsilon,\qquad f_Q=e_Qh_Q^{-1}.
\]
The two choices of $\epsilon$ specify different outer boundary markings
on the same abstract group $Q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In $P$ set
\[
 s=u^2v^{-1},\qquad t=u^{-2}s^2,\qquad h=u^3.
\]
The change $(u,v)\leftrightarrow(u,s)$ is a free-basis change, with
$v=s^{-1}u^2$. The source relator becomes, up to cyclic conjugation
and inversion, $t^2u^{-3}$; hence
\[
 P=\langle u,t,s\mid t^2=u^3,\ s^2=u^2t\rangle.
\]
Now $u^2t=(u^{-1}t)h=e_Ph$. This is exactly the claimed amalgam:
the additional cable relation $[h,s^2]=1$ is already implied because
$h$ commutes with $u,t$.

In $Q$ set $t=y^2x^2$ and $h=x^3$. Its source relator is equivalent
to $t^2=x^3$, so
\[
 Q=\langle x,t,y\mid t^2=x^3,\ y^2=tx^{-2}\rangle.
\]
Since $tx^{-2}=(tx)h^{-1}=e_Qh^{-1}$, adjoining the cable piece with
$s_Q=y$ gives the second amalgam. Again its commutation relation is
redundant, so these are isomorphisms, not just quotient maps.
\end{proof}

\section{The graph manifold and the rank obstruction}

Glue the four standard orientable Seifert pieces in the chain
\[
 \underset{D(2,3)}{H_P}\;---\;
 \underset{A(2)}{C_P}\;---\;
 \underset{A(2)}{C_Q}\;---\;
 \underset{D(2,3)}{H_Q}
\]
using these torus maps, and call the resulting manifold $M_\epsilon$.
The underlying graph is a tree, so choose orientations on successive
pieces to make every boundary gluing orientation reversing. This works
for either endpoint sign. All boundary tori are paired, so $M_\epsilon$
is closed, connected and orientable. The Seifert pieces are irreducible;
gluing along their incompressible boundary tori preserves irreducibility
by the usual innermost-circle sphere argument. Van Kampen and the exact
identifications above give
\[
 \pi_1(M_1)\cong G_A,\qquad \pi_1(M_{-1})\cong G_B.
\]
Only group isomorphism is needed for the rank argument. No source-cover
homeomorphism, literal tangle sphere or numerical triangulation
identification is assumed.

The four orbifold Euler characteristics are
$-1/6,-1/2,-1/2,-1/6$. Thus none of the pieces is a solid torus,
$T^2\times I$, or the exceptional Klein-bottle bundle with ambiguous
boundary fiber slopes. Boundary fibers are unique. The three gluing
tori are incompressible and cannot be deleted from a Seifert/atoroidal
decomposition: adjacent Seifert fibrations cannot extend across a torus
where the fiber intersection is one, and that torus would remain an
essential non-boundary-parallel torus in the union. A Seifert union with
horizontal gluing tori would instead give torus products or twisted
torus bundles on cutting, incompatible with these negative-base pieces.
Thus boundary fiber uniqueness also applies to any putative larger
Seifert union. The classical JSJ criterion, equivalently the minimality
and uniqueness argument in the
read Hatcher sections, therefore gives exactly four canonical pieces.

If either $G_X$ had rank two, $M_\epsilon$ would violate the necessary
Boileau--Weidmann list, which allows at most three JSJ pieces. Rank zero
or one is excluded by the embedded nonabelian vertex group $H$ (also
by the retained nonabelian quotient). The exact three-generator source
presentations give the opposite bound. Hence $\rank G_A=\rank G_B=3$.

\begin{proof}[Completion of Theorem~\ref{thm:knots}]
For a knot group generated by $m$ meridians, quotient by meridian squares
and take its even subgroup. This is the double branched-cover group
and is generated by the $m-1$ products with a fixed meridian. Thus
\[
 1+\rank\pi_1(\Sigma_2(K))\le\mr(K)\le b(K).
\]
Mutation transfers the now exact cover rank to all four knots in each
block. The eight independent four-seed traces satisfy every geometric
propagation step on the literal source PDs; the retained
Blair--Kjuchukova--Velazquez--Villanueva theorem converts them to
$b(K)\le4$. Therefore $4\le\mr(K)\le b(K)\le4$ for each named knot.
No mutation invariance of bridge number or meridional rank is used.
\end{proof}
