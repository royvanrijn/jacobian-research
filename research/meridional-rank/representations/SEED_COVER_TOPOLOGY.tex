The four retained simple-three-cover seeds have double branched covers
with a hyperbolic piece. They therefore lie outside the graph-manifold
double-cover hypothesis of Boileau--Jang--Weidmann (2018).
The same conclusion holds for all twenty-four targets of the new seven-sheeted cover certificates.
This excludes one precise existing family. Separate exact audits now
exclude sharp finite Coxeter quotients and the fishnet and plat gcd
criteria on the twenty-four new cases, and standard twisted diagrams
on the alternating ones. Global priority, infinite-Coxeter and
rational-tangle extensions, and positive periodic parameters remain open.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:outside}
For every knot in the following table, the double branched cover
$\Sigma_2(K)$ is a closed orientable irreducible manifold containing an
incompressible separating torus. One component obtained by cutting
along that torus has interior homeomorphic to the listed finite-volume
cusped hyperbolic manifold:

\section{Exact diagram and cover construction}

The input consists of the twenty-eight literal thirteen-crossing PDs in
\path{seed_cover_topology_inputs.json}. They agree literally with the
corresponding retained panel entries. A separate comparison binds them
to the independently packaged Hoste--Thistlethwaite census:
the full signed Gauss walk agrees up to cyclic shift, reversal,
and global spatial reflections. These ambiguities only reverse
orientation or mirror the knot, and do not change the assertion.

For each literal diagram, Spherogram constructs its exterior with the
diagram meridian peripheral basis. Fill the meridian with coefficient
two, and take the unique cyclic cover of degree two of this orbifold
filling. The meridian has nontrivial image in $C_2$, so its square lifts
primitively. Filling the lifted cusp produces the ordinary
double cover branched over the diagram knot. SnapPy's resulting
triangulation is converted to a closed Regina triangulation.

Every transformation here is combinatorial: exterior construction,
cyclic cover, meridian filling, and bistellar simplification.
The replay fixes the random seed and records both the exterior
isomorphism signature with peripheral data and the resulting closed
signature. Random choices affect the triangulation, not the underlying
manifold. The actual replay must reconstruct the pinned signatures;
a changed signature fails rather than silently substituting a
different triangulation. No floating-point volume, group presentation
similarity, homology coincidence, or numerical isometry test is
an acceptance condition.

Regina certifies that the resulting closed triangulation is valid,
connected, orientable and irreducible. It then enumerates vertex normal
surfaces in quadrilateral coordinates. The input supplies a surface index and its full quadrilateral vector. The indicated entry must agree
with the full pinned quadrilateral vector, be connected, orientable and
two-sided, and have Euler characteristic zero. Thus it is an embedded
torus. Regina's incompressibility algorithm returns true.
Its precondition of closed irreducibility has already been checked.

Cutting along that torus produces two components, each with one torus
boundary. The algorithm simplifies both components, idealizes their
boundaries, and simplifies again. For the selected component the result
is an exact triangulation from the certified hyperbolic census.

\section{The hyperbolicity import}

Hyperbolicity is imported from a rigorously certified census.
Burton's author preprint \cite[Corollary 1.2, printed p.~2]{burton}
states that the Callahan--Hildebrand--Thistlethwaite--Weeks census
contains neither non-hyperbolic entries nor omissions through eight
tetrahedra. Its preceding discussion credits Moser and
Hoffman--Ichihara--Kashiwagi--Masai--Oishi--Takayasu for the
absence of false positives. The present proof uses that imported
certificate at its stated scope; it does not rerun the census-wide
interval computations.

The author's supporting archive contains exact isomorphism signatures
of all minimal triangulations. The exact extracted
\path{4-or.dat} and \path{5-or.dat} files are retained as
\path{sources/burton-hypcensus-4-or.dat} and
\path{sources/burton-hypcensus-5-or.dat}, with hashes and archive URL.
The m036 row lists exactly these four signatures:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\texttt{eLPkaccddjbkan}& \texttt{eLPkaccddjnkaj}\\
\texttt{eLPkbcddddjwnw}& \texttt{eLPkbcddddkokg}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each of the selected four seed components matches one of these.
The twenty-four further components match the m121 or m189 rows in
the five-tetrahedron file. For example the selected $13a_{373}$ component
has signature \texttt{fLLQccecddephrwwn}, in the m121 row.
The chosen closed triangulations have between sixteen and twenty tetrahedra.
Isomorphism signatures encode the exact face pairings, so their equality
is a combinatorial identification.

A closed irreducible graph manifold remains a graph manifold after
cutting along incompressible tori. Equivalently, a hyperbolic component
separated by an incompressible torus persists when one refines to the JSJ
decomposition; redundant Seifert tori cannot absorb a hyperbolic component.
This standard JSJ consequence follows from the gluing criterion
and geometrization decomposition in
\cite[Proposition 1.6.2 and Theorem 1.7.6]{afw}.
Our hyperbolic component therefore excludes graph-manifold membership.
The published BJW18 theorem explicitly assumes a
graph-manifold double branched cover \cite[Theorem 1.2]{bjw}.

\section{Reproducibility and limits}

The replay is \path{verify_seed_cover_topology.py}, using
SnapPy 3.3.2, Spherogram 2.4.1,
\texttt{snappy\_15\_knots} 1.2.1,
\texttt{snappy\_manifolds} 1.4, and Regina 7.4.
It records all twenty-eight successful cases and rejects deliberately changed
knot names and hyperbolic census names.
The input pins the full quadrilateral coordinates and intermediate
triangulations. \path{seed_cover_topology_verification.json} records the
actual internal replay and source digests.

The exact topological predicates are Regina's \texttt{isIrreducible()}
and \path{NormalSurface.isIncompressible()}. Its API states the latter's
preconditions explicitly: a connected compact embedded normal surface
inside a valid, closed, irreducible triangulation.
All are checked before invocation. The surface is taken from the vertex
normal-surface enumeration, rather than constructed from an unchecked
coordinate vector. \texttt{cutAlong()} performs actual cutting without
crushing; \texttt{finiteToIdeal()} collapses the torus boundaries.
These library algorithms and the geometric meaning of the branched
cover construction remain trusted computational imports.
The API descriptions were checked against installed Regina 7.4.

The computations rely on the implementations of these algorithms, the
named census, and Burton's imported certified census theorem.
They are not formal verification or independent human specialist review.
The four existing sharp-four equalities remain those of the retained
simple-three-cover certificates. The twenty-four additional meridional
lower certificates are separate seven-sheeted group computations;
Theorem~\ref{thm:outside} concerns only actual-cover topology.
No conclusion about all $N>0$ members of the periodic twist families
follows from these twenty-eight finite cases.
