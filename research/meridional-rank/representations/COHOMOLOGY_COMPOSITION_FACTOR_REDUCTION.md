# Irreducible factors suffice for the cohomology meridional-rank bound

19 November 2026.

Let F be any field. Let G be a finitely generated group normally
generated by m, with abelianization G_ab isomorphic to Z. In particular
these hypotheses hold for a knot group and a meridian. Every module
below is a finite-dimensional F-linear G-module, with no assumption of
semisimplicity or absolute irreducibility.

For a module V define

 delta(V)=dim_F Z1(G,V)-dim_F V,
 q(V)=rank_F(rho_V(m)-I).

If q(V)>0, the Gao--Gu--Li--Wang cohomology certificate, clipped at the
elementary lower bound1, is

 C(V)=max(1, 1+ceil(delta(V)/q(V))).                    (1)

Set C(V)=1 when q(V)=0. This convention is justified below, rather
than interpreting a quotient with zero denominator.

**Theorem CohomologyCompositionFactorReduction.** For every nonzero
finite-dimensional module E, and every composition series with
irreducible factors S1,...,St,

 C(E) <= max_i C(Si).                                 (2)

Thus some irreducible subquotient gives at least the same lower bound
from this particular cohomology method. No semisimplicity assumption
on E, splitting of its composition series, or field extension is
required. The conclusion concerns(1), not arbitrary invariants of
representations, augmentation rank, or actual meridional rank.

If delta(E)>0, a positive-rank irreducible factor necessarily exists and
one satisfies

 delta(Si)/q(Si) >= delta(E)/q(E)>0.                  (3)

When delta(E)=0 and all factors are trivial, the certificate is just1.
There need not be ANY positive-rank irreducible factor in that case.

## 1. The two exact inequalities

**Lemma CocycleDefectSubadditivity.** For an exact module sequence

 0 -> V -> E -> W -> 0,

 delta(E)<=delta(V)+delta(W).

Proof. Inclusion and projection give a left-exact sequence

 0 -> Z1(G,V) -> Z1(G,E) -> Z1(G,W).                  (4)

Indeed a cocycle whose projection is zero is a V-valued cocycle.
Conversely every V-valued cocycle is an E-valued cocycle. No claim of
surjectivity at the right of(4) is made. These spaces are finite
dimensional: values on a finite generating set determine a cocycle.
Thus dimZ1(E)<=dimZ1(V)+dimZ1(W), while module dimensions add.
Subtracting them proves the lemma. This uses no higher-cohomology
vanishing and no geometric hypothesis. QED.

**Lemma MeridianRankSuperadditivity.** In the same sequence,

 q(E)>=q(V)+q(W).

Proof. Let L=rho_E(m)-I. The image of L on V is im(rho_V(m)-I),
of dimension q(V), and lies in im L intersect V. The projection of
im L onto W is im(rho_W(m)-I), of dimension q(W). The rank-nullity
formula for this projection of im L gives the claimed lower bound.
Equivalently, an upper block triangular matrix can have rank strictly
greater than the sum of the diagonal-block ranks. QED.

For a composition series induction gives

 delta(E)<=sum_i delta(Si),
 q(E)>=sum_i q(Si).                                  (5)

Neither of these inequalities should be silently replaced by equality.

## 2. Zero-rank and negative-defect cases

If q(S)=0 for an irreducible S, m acts as the identity. Its normal
closure is G, so EVERY element of G acts trivially. Irreducibility
therefore forces dimS=1. Since G_ab=Z,

 Z1(G,S)=Hom(G,F)=F,

including every positive characteristic. Hence delta(S)=0 and C(S)=1.

For a nontrivial irreducible S there is no nonzero invariant vector.
The coboundary map v -> [g -> rho(g)v-v] is therefore injective.
Consequently dimZ1(G,S)>=dimS, so delta(S)>=0.

If q(E)=0, normal generation makes E a trivial module. Its cocycle
space is Hom(G,E), of dimension dimE, and delta(E)=0. Thus its
certificate convention C(E)=1 is correct.

For q(E)>0, put

 alpha=max({0} union {delta(Si)/q(Si):q(Si)>0}).

Every zero-rank factor has zero defect. Therefore(5) gives

 delta(E)<=alpha sum_i q(Si)<=alpha q(E).             (6)

If delta(E)<=0, the clipped certificate C(E) is1, already no larger
than each factor's clipped certificate. If delta(E)>0, alpha>0 and
the maximum is attained by a positive-rank factor; division by q(E)
gives(3). Taking ceilings, which preserves order, gives(2).

The zero must not be omitted from alpha in a proof for arbitrary
modules with negative defects: division by a larger positive
denominator reverses the useful numerical comparison for negative
numerators. The clipping and the argument above cover that case
without assuming a sign for delta(E).

An instructive zero-defect nonsplit example is the braid-relation
group G=<a,b | aba=bab> over F2, with a=b=J2(1). Its two composition
factors are trivial, q(E)=1 and dimZ1(G,E)=dimE=2. There is no
positive-rank irreducible factor, and C(E)=1. This prevents the
incorrect strengthening of(3) from delta(E)>0 to delta(E)>=0.

## 3. The reverse uniform-bound equivalence

**Corollary UniformIrreducibleCertificateCapEquivalence.** Fix G,F,m as
above and ANY real number B. The following are equivalent:

 (i) C(V)<=B for every finite-dimensional G-module V;
 (ii) C(S)<=B for every irreducible G-module S;
 (iii) B>=1 and, for every nontrivial irreducible S,

       delta(S) <= (floor(B)-1) q(S).                (7)

The zero module may be included in(i), with C(0)=1; this does not
change the assertion because the trivial one-dimensional module
already has certificate1.

Proof. Statement(ii) follows from(i) because an irreducible module
is a finite-dimensional module. Conversely(ii) and Theorem(2)
give(i) for every nonzero module, and the trivial irreducible module
also supplies C(0)<=B. If B<1, both universal statements are false:
the trivial one-dimensional module has C=1. If B>=1, positive-rank
modules satisfy

 C(V)<=B
 iff 1+ceil(delta(V)/q(V))<=floor(B)
 iff delta(V)<=(floor(B)-1)q(V).

Zero-rank modules have delta=0 and C=1. The preceding equivalence
and the zero-rank irreducible calculation prove(ii) iff(iii). QED.

In particular an INTEGER cap B uses the exact coefficient B-1.
For a noninteger real cap it is floor(B)-1; replacing it by B-1
would be false. For example delta1,q2 gives C2, which exceeds the
real cap3/2 even though delta/q=1/2=B-1. The verifier includes an
actual extension representation with those values.

Equivalently, the supremum of this clipped certificate over all
finite-dimensional modules equals its supremum over irreducible
modules, in the extended interval[1,infinity]. This is a universal
bound equivalence; a particular extension need not have the same
normalized defect or certificate as its individual factors.

## 4. Root-affine and other trivial-quotient extensions

**Corollary TrivialExtensionCannotImproveCohomologyCertificate.**
If W is a trivial module of dimension s and

 0 -> V -> E -> W -> 0,

then delta(E)<=delta(V) and C(E)<=C(V). The same holds for an
extension with a trivial submodule and quotient V.

Indeed dimZ1(G,W)=s, so delta(W)=0. Apply the two exact inequalities.
If delta(V)<0 both clipped certificates are1; otherwise the
numerator cannot increase and the positive denominator cannot
decrease. If q(V)=0, delta(V)=0 and the same conclusion follows.

In particular any root-affine construction

 rho_hat(g)= [ rho(g)  evaluations of selected cocycles ]
             [   0                    I             ]

has a trivial quotient. It cannot improve(1), even if its meridian
displacement rank increases. If that rank stays equal, the simpler
inequality delta(rho_hat)<=delta(rho) already proves the assertion.
This statement permits dependent cocycles, coboundaries and arbitrary
finite iterations; it does not require choosing a basis of H1 or of
the root-valued cocycle space.

This rules out these root-affine and trivial-factor extensions as
a route to a larger certificate. It does not rule out a different irreducible module,
a different coloring, a new knot, or an invariant outside(1).

## 5. Primary source and exact scope

Gao--Gu--Li--Wang, *Meridional ranks of iterated Whitehead doubles*,
arXiv:2611.15673v1, Lemma6.2, printed pages38--39, proves over an
arbitrary field that for a representation of a group generated by r
conjugates of m,

 dimZ1(G,V)-dimV <= (r-1) rank(rho(m)-I).

Solving this integer inequality for r gives(1), with the elementary
clipping r>=1. Remark6.3 identifies delta with dimH1-dimH0.
The present reduction follows from the elementary cocycle projection
and block-rank arguments supplied above. The primary lemma is used
only to identify which meridional-rank certificate is being compared.
No novelty of these elementary exact-sequence arguments is asserted.

The retained primary PDF is reused and hash-pinned in the source
ledger. No duplicate source download is needed.

## 6. Exact finite controls

The standard-library verifier uses the explicit braid-relation group
<a,b | aba=bab>. It normally generates by a: setting a=1 forces b=1.
Its abelianization is Z because the relation gives a=b there. These
facts suffice for the finite controls without importing an additional
knot-identification theorem.

For matrices A,B satisfying ABA=BAB it computes the complete cocycle
space directly from

 (I+AB-B) z(a) + (A-I-BA) z(b)=0.                     (8)

It checks inclusion/projection kernel equality in(4), the possible
failure of surjectivity, the defect and meridian-rank inequalities,

needed when dividing inequalities with negative numerators. It is
labeled as an algebraic edge control, not a claimed knot-module

## 7. The first possible displacement for a protected knot closure

Suppose \(G\) is the group of a knot closing a \(b\)-strand braid with \(k\ge1\) disjoint protected \(U\) blocks and a tail fixing both outer free meridians of every block, in the [two-sided protected theorem](TWO_SIDED_PROTECTED_OBSTRUCTIONS.md). Set \(c=b-k\). The [rank-one cohomology bound](RANK_ONE_COHOMOLOGY_COVECTOR_BARRIER.md#2-consequence-for-disjoint-protected-u-blocks) gives \(C(S)\le c\) for every module with \(q(S)=1\), over every field. A trivial factor contributes certificate one. Thus Theorem (2) bounds \(C(E)\) by \(c\) whenever all its composition factors have \(q(S)\le1\), even if extensions increase \(q(E)\).

More generally, if \(C(E)>c\), then \(\delta(E)>0\), since \(c\ge1\). Applying (3) to any composition series gives an irreducible factor \(S\) with \(C(S)\ge C(E)\) and \(q(S)\ge2\). If \(q(E)\le2\), superadditivity (5) gives \(q(S)=q(E)=2\) and forces \(q=0\) for every other factor. By normal generation all the other factors are trivial. The same equality of displacement ranks in (3) gives \(\delta(S)\ge\delta(E)\). Conversely an irreducible rank-two candidate is already a module of displacement at most two. This gives an exact reduction for every target certificate exceeding \(c\).

For \(q(S)=2\), the integer formula \(C(S)=\max(1,1+\lceil\delta(S)/2\rceil)\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C(S)\ge c+1 &\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \delta(S)\ge 2c-1,\\
 C(S)\ge c+2 &\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \delta(S)\ge 2c+1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed \(\lceil x\rceil\ge n\) for integer \(n\) is equivalent to \(x>n-1\). The second threshold supplies a sufficient rank-two certificate for augmentation gap at least two, since the same protected theorem gives \(\operatorname{ar}_{\mathbb C}(K)\le c\). The reduction leaves the existence of such a representation and the geometry of any resulting knot open.
