# An infinite prime five-bridge family with augmentation gaps

Date: 18 November 2026.

Put

\[
\begin{split}
U={}&\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3^{-2}\sigma_2\sigma_3^{-2}
       \sigma_2\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^3\sigma_3^{-1},\\
\delta={}&\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_4\sigma_3^{-1},\qquad
J_k=\widehat{U\delta^{4+36k}}\quad(k\ge0).
\end{split}
\]

**Theorem.** Every `J_k` is a knot and satisfies

\[
\operatorname{ar}_{\mathbb C}(J_k)\le4<
\operatorname{mr}(J_k)=\operatorname{br}(J_k)=5.
\]

For all sufficiently large integers `k`, `J_k` is hyperbolic and therefore prime. Infinitely many different knot types occur among these prime members. The member `J_0` is also rigorously certified hyperbolic by the separate retained certificate. No effective exceptional threshold, minimal crossing formula, exact augmentation rank, or pairwise distinctness of every parameter is asserted.

The proof uses one explicitly marked two-cusped hyperbolic parent. The finite input and rigorous interval verification establish that parent; disk-twist surgery, hyperbolic Dehn filling and volume estimates establish the infinite conclusions.

## 1. Uniform alternating quotient and augmentation bound

We use `T(a,b)=(b,b^{-1}ab)` for a positive braid generator. Let

\[
x=((01)(23),(14)(23),(05)(16),(12)(67),(08)(79)).
\]

The first four entries generate `A_8` on the old eight symbols. The two-new-symbol extension lemma in [ALTERNATING_TARGET_PRIME_FAMILIES.tex](ALTERNATING_TARGET_PRIME_FAMILIES.tex), proof of Theorem `thm:targets`, shows that the fifth extends the generated group to `A_10`. The literal tuple returns under `U delta^4`.

Put `g=sigma_3 sigma_4 sigma_3^{-1}`. By Lemma 2 of [UNBOUNDED_PRIME_AUGMENTATION_GAP_AUDIT.md](UNBOUNDED_PRIME_AUGMENTATION_GAP_AUDIT.md), the fourth matrix `C` is fixed by `sigma_2,g`, and their action on the framed triple

\[
(M_2,M_3,CM_5C^{-1})
\]

is the usual action of the two adjacent generators of `B_3`. If the ordered product of a triple is `p`, then `(sigma_1 sigma_2)^3` conjugates all three entries by `p^{-1}`. This is an identity in a free group, verified by the exact control.

At the end of `U`, the framed triple from `x` has product

\[
p=(0\ 8\ 5)(1\ 6\ 9\ 4)(2\ 3)(7),
\]

of order 12. Thus `delta^36` fixes the entire prefix tuple. Since `delta^4` returns it to `x`, the tuple returns under `U delta^(4+36k)` for every `k>=0`. It supplies a full `A_10` quotient taking meridians to double transpositions.

The permutation of `delta` is a three-cycle on its moving strand positions. The exponent changes by multiples of 36, so every `J_k` has the same five-cycle closure permutation as `J_0` and is a knot. Four double transpositions supply only eight edges on ten symbols and cannot generate a transitive group. Consequently `mr(J_k)>=5`, while the five-strand presentation gives `mr(J_k)<=br(J_k)<=5`.

Every tail `delta^(4+36k)` belongs to the protected subgroup generated by `sigma_2,g`. The all-field paired-span Theorem 3 of the audit therefore gives `ar_C(J_k)<=4`. That theorem extends the four-strand local folds from [PRIME_AUGMENTATION_GAP_FAMILY.tex](PRIME_AUGMENTATION_GAP_FAMILY.tex) and the reduced `13a_392` paired-span obstruction from [GENERIC_KCH_EXTENSIONS.tex](GENERIC_KCH_EXTENSIONS.tex). Its application does not infer augmentation rank from an alternating finite quotient.

## 2. The explicit twist-circle parent

The braid relation gives

\[
\sigma_3\sigma_4\sigma_3^{-1}
=\sigma_4^{-1}\sigma_3\sigma_4,
\qquad
\delta=\sigma_4^{-1}(\sigma_2\sigma_3)\sigma_4.
\]

Hence the braid defining `J_k` is conjugate to

\[
Q_k=\sigma_4 U\sigma_4^{-1}(\sigma_2\sigma_3)^{4+36k}.
\]

Set `Q=Q_0`, regard it as a six-strand braid fixing the last strand, and put

\[
P=\sigma_5\sigma_4\sigma_3\sigma_2^2
                  \sigma_3\sigma_4\sigma_5^{-1}.
\]

Let `L=closure(QP)=J_0 union C_0`, with `C_0` the added sixth component. The parent is `M=S^3 minus L`. The full literal augmented braid has 32 crossings. Its source input retains its PD code, component edges and complete triangulation.

**Lemma (marked circle).** The component `C_0` is an unknot bounding an embedded disk pierced positively once by exactly the strands in positions two, three and four in the augmentation slab. Its disk longitude is the preferred zero-framing longitude in `S^3`.

**Proof.** For `i<6`, let

\[
A_{i,6}=\sigma_5\cdots\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i^2
              \sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_5^{-1}.
\]

Literal cancellation gives `P=A_(2,6) A_(3,6) A_(4,6)`. These are the point-pushing loops of the sixth puncture around the indicated old punctures, with a common stem. Their product is the boundary loop of a disk enclosing precisely the three consecutive punctures two through four.

The sixth closing arc is brought into this slab through the exterior of the old braid and its closing arcs. Sliding its cap down the common stem removes the detour across the fifth strand: the first and last detour crossings have opposite signs and the sixth component passes over the fifth at both, so they cancel by a planar overpass isotopy. The remaining oval passes over the three selected strands in one direction and under them on return. It is the boundary of the transverse three-punctured disk; the braid outside this slab is untouched.

Here is the exact diagram control. In order, the eight suffix crossings have strand roles

```text
(5,C0),(4,C0),(3,C0),(2,C0),(C0,2),(C0,3),(C0,4),(C0,5)
```

and over-strand roles

```text
C0,C0,C0,C0,2,3,4,C0.
```

The first and last crossings are the cancelling detour. There are no self crossings of `C_0`, so its disk push-off has zero self-linking. Its linking number with the old knot is three. The literal PD wiring independently binds these roles to the retained circle component, which has eight edges; the other component has 56 edges. ∎

## 3. Surgery identity for every parameter

Use the meridian and disk longitude `(mu,lambda)` of `C_0`. Write the meridians at the start of the augmentation slab as `(x,a,b,d,e,c)`, with `c` the circle meridian, and put `w=abd`. Direct free reduction of the action of `P` gives

\[
(x,\ c^{-1}ac,\ c^{-1}bc,\ c^{-1}dc,
\ c_{\rm out}c^{-1}ec\,c_{\rm out}^{-1},\ c_{\rm out}),
\qquad
c_{\rm out}=(wc)^{-1}c(wc).
\]

Closing the circle gives `c_out=c`, equivalently `[c,w]=1`; the fifth old strand then returns to `e`. The disk boundary is `w`, with no self-framing factor in `c`. This confirms the longitude and surgery sign.

**Lemma (all-parameter filling).** Filling the circle cusp along the primitive slope

\[
\mu-q\lambda=(1,-q),\qquad q\in\mathbb Z,
\]

produces the exterior of

\[
\widehat{Q(\sigma_2\sigma_3)^{3q}}.
\]

The old knot meridian is preserved. In particular

\[
M(1,-12k)\cong S^3\setminus J_k\qquad(k\ge0).
\]

**Proof.** Removing the unknot `C_0` gives a solid torus with a meridional disk obtained by truncating its spanning disk. A disk twist of order `q` sends the boundary basis by `mu -> mu+q lambda`, `lambda -> lambda`. Thus it takes `mu-q lambda` to the meridian slope and extends over that filling. Localize this motion to the augmentation slab. It twists exactly the three disk-piercing strands and extends to the ambient filled `S^3`, preserving a tubular neighborhood of the old knot and its actual meridian.

To determine the crossing sign, the filling relation is `c w^{-q}=1`, so `c=w^q`. The displayed slab action consequently conjugates each of `a,b,d` by `w^{-q}`. The positive three-strand full twist `(sigma_2 sigma_3)^3` has exactly the conjugation action by `w^{-1}`. Thus the localized disk motion is the `q`-th positive full twist in the stated convention. This identifies the geometric disk surgery; the homeomorphism is not inferred solely from equality of group presentations. Putting `q=12k` changes the exponent from four to `4+36k`. ∎

The surgery slopes in this proof are geometrically marked by the disk. The infinite conclusion does not require recognition of sampled fillings, numerical isometries or a slope guess from cusp shapes.

## 5. Infinitely many prime members and infinitely many knot types

Thurston's hyperbolic Dehn filling theorem applies to the fixed complete finite-volume parent `M`. The slopes `(1,-12k)` tend to infinity, while the knot cusp stays unfilled. Consequently all sufficiently large `k` give hyperbolic knot exteriors. The exact source is *The Geometry and Topology of Three-Manifolds*, electronic version 1.1 (March 2002), Theorem 5.8.2, printed p.104; its neighboring proof and source identity are retained in the [source excerpt](sources/thurston-gt3m-2002-dehn-filling-volume-excerpts.pdf). Hyperbolic knots are prime, by the centralizer argument recorded in the audit.

It remains to exclude infinitely many repetitions of a finite collection of knots. Fix any embedded horocusp at the circle cusp. Its flat torus gives nonzero Euclidean vectors for `mu,lambda`. If `ell_k` is the length of `mu-12k lambda`, then

\[
\ell_k\ge12k\lVert\lambda\rVert-\lVert\mu\rVert\longrightarrow\infty.
\]

For sufficiently large `k`, Theorem 1.1 of Futer–Kalfagianni–Purcell, *Dehn filling, volume, and the Jones polynomial*, retained [18 December 2006 author edition](sources/futer-kalfagianni-purcell-dehn-volume-2006-author.pdf), printed p.2, applies to this one filled cusp. It gives

\[
\left(1-\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ell_k}\right)^2\right)^{3/2}
\operatorname{vol}(M)
\ \le\ \operatorname{vol}(M(1,-12k)).
\]

Its geometrization hypothesis is satisfied because these sufficiently long fillings are already hyperbolic by Thurston's theorem. Its hypotheses allow filling a subset of the cusps. Thurston's strict volume-decrease theorem, Theorem 6.5.6, printed p.138, gives

\[
\operatorname{vol}(M(1,-12k))<\operatorname{vol}(M).
\]

The manifolds differ because the parent has two cusps and the filling one. The two inequalities show that the filled volumes converge to the parent volume from below.

If only finitely many homeomorphism types occurred in this hyperbolic tail, Mostow–Prasad rigidity would give only finitely many volumes, each strictly below the parent volume. Their finite maximum would be below it, contradicting convergence. Thus infinitely many exterior homeomorphism types occur, hence infinitely many knot types. Rigidity is sourced in Aschenbrenner–Friedl–Wilton, *3-Manifold Groups*, Theorem 1.7.1, in the retained [author copy](sources/afw-3manifold-groups-final-author-2015.pdf). This completes the theorem. ∎

This argument does not prove that the volumes are monotone in the displayed parameter. It proves there is an infinite subsequence of distinct volumes and prime knots.
