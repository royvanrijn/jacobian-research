# The exact free-meridian stabilizer

Date: 19 November 2026.

This classical mapping-class deduction identifies every braid tail that
fixes the protected free meridians exactly. Its unrestricted topological
proof is separate from the finite free-word controls below. No historical
priority, external review, or formal verification is asserted.

## Statement and conventions

Use the usual Artin action on the free meridian basis `x_1,...,x_n`, with
the repository Hurwitz convention `T(a,b)=(b,b^-1 a b)`. Let `P` be an
arbitrary subset of protected strand positions, and let
`m_1<...<m_s` list its complement. Define

\[
g_{i,j}=\sigma_i\cdots\sigma_{j-2}\sigma_{j-1}
             \sigma_{j-2}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_i^{-1}
\quad(i<j).
\]

**Theorem FreeMeridianStabilizer.** The pointwise stabilizer

\[
\{\beta\in B_n:\beta(x_i)=x_i\text{ in }F_n\text{ for every }i\in P\}
\]

is exactly the subgroup generated by
`g_(m_1,m_2),...,g_(m_(s-1),m_s)`. It is isomorphic to `B_s` (trivial
for `s=0,1`). Thus the canonical protected mobile-band subgroup is the
full stabilizer under the stated exact-word condition.

The distinction between a fixed word and a fixed conjugacy class is
essential. Fixing a peripheral conjugacy class merely fixes its puncture
and permits point pushing. For example a nontrivial two-strand pure
full twist fixes both peripheral conjugacy classes but does not fix the
two free generators individually.

## 1. A based meridian determines its ending arc

Let `D` be a disk with a finite set of interior marked points. Fix a
boundary point `q`, an interior marked point `p`, and a simple proper arc
`a` from `q` to `p`, with its interior avoiding all marked points.
Take a small terminal disk around `p`. Following `a`, circling `p`
positively once, and returning along `a` defines a based element `x_a`
in the punctured disk's fundamental group.

**Arc lemma.** If `x_a=x_b` for two such arcs with the same endpoints,
then `a,b` are properly homotopic relative to their endpoints, hence
isotopic relative to endpoints and the other marked points.

To prove the first conclusion, truncate both arcs at a common small
circle `c` about `p`, and join their terminal points along `c`. Their
path difference is a loop `w` based on `c`. Equality of the two based
meridians says that `w` centralizes the primitive peripheral generator
`c`. Its centralizer is exactly `<c>`: after changing the free basis to
make this peripheral loop one generator, write a reduced word as
`c^a v c^b`, where a nonempty `v` starts and ends with letters different
from `c^±1`. The reduced conjugate `w c w^-1` cannot equal `c` unless
`v` is empty. Thus the two terminal paths differ only by winding about
the ending puncture. That winding is absorbed in the terminal segment
of a proper arc ending at the marked point. Equivalently, its endpoint
is allowed to move on `c` before the terminal circle is collapsed to
the marked point. This proves the proper homotopy assertion. The
homotopy-to-isotopy assertion is the classical arc version of the
bigon criterion.

Now let a boundary-fixing orientation-preserving homeomorphism `f`
induce an Artin automorphism fixing `x_a`. Abelianization first ensures
that it fixes `p`; the arc lemma then says that `f(a)` is isotopic to
`a` relative to its endpoints.

## 2. Simultaneous arc fixing and cutting

For the standard Artin meridian basis choose the standard disjoint
boundary-to-puncture stems below the ordered punctures. Their boundary
starting points may be chosen DISTINCT, joined to the common group
basepoint by fixed boundary paths. Since every braid homeomorphism fixes
the boundary pointwise, exact fixing at the common basepoint is equivalent
to exact fixing at those separate boundary points.

If all protected free meridians are fixed, Section 1 fixes the isotopy
class of every protected stem. The stems are pairwise disjoint, with
distinct endpoints. Simultaneous ambient isotopy therefore gives a
representative preserving all protected stems. Adjust its parametrization
on each interval, by an ambient isotopy in a small neighborhood, so that
it fixes every stem pointwise. Orientation preservation fixes both sides
of each stem, as well as the outer boundary.

Cut the disk along all protected stems, treating their terminal marked
points as boundary points after cutting. The result is again a disk,
with exactly the `s` mobile marked points in its interior. The adjusted
homeomorphism induces a boundary-fixing homeomorphism of this cut disk.
Conversely every such cut-disk homeomorphism extends across the cuts and
fixes every protected meridian. This gives precisely the embedded mobile
braid group. A twist about a SINGLE protected marked point adds no
extra framing generator: the once-marked disk has trivial mapping class
group, and the cutting has already fixed the stem pointwise.

This argument needs only surjectivity onto the stabilizer to prove the
displayed subgroup equality. Injectivity also has a direct left inverse.
Delete every protected strand from the embedded mobile braid. Each canonical band between
consecutive mobile positions becomes the corresponding adjacent Artin
generator of `B_s`, with the same sign. Thus deletion composed with
the displayed embedding is the identity on `B_s`. In particular no
extra relation or hidden framing kernel is introduced.

## 3. Identification with the literal canonical bands

In the slit disk join each pair of consecutive mobile punctures by an
arc running above the intervening protected punctures. These arcs form
an embedded chain, and their half twists generate its entire mapping
class group: a regular neighborhood of the chain is a disk containing
all mobile punctures, and the puncture-free region between its boundary
and the outer slit boundary contributes only a boundary twist, already
the full twist on that chain. Equivalently straighten the chain to the
usual ordered configuration in a disk.

Here is an explicit identification with the literal band word, rather
than only an abstract isomorphism with a mobile braid group. Birman--Ko--Lee
define their embedded band on positions `s<t` by

\[
a_{t,s}=(\sigma_{t-1}\cdots\sigma_{s+1})\sigma_s
             (\sigma_{s+1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{t-1}^{-1}).
\]

Their equation (4) and Figure 1(a), printed p.325, identify this with the
single embedded half-twist band that exchanges the endpoint strands and
passes on one fixed side of all intervening strands. Apply the index
reversal `iota(sigma_l)=sigma_(n-l)` to their band with
`t=n-i+1,s=n-j+1`. Term by term this gives

\[
\iota(a_{n-i+1,n-j+1})
=\sigma_i\cdots\sigma_{j-2}\sigma_{j-1}
                    \sigma_{j-2}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_i^{-1}
=g_{i,j}.
\]

Index reversal is geometrically a half-turn of the ordered punctured
disk, so the resulting bands are the same simple interval-connecting
bands on the other fixed side of the puncture line. Choose the protected
stems on the opposite side. This also agrees with the repository's
Artin free-word convention: the direct word calculation below fixes
each intervening protected meridian, whereas the opposite band generally
does not. For consecutive mobile positions there are only protected
punctures between the endpoints, so these interval bands avoid all
protected stems. Bands for successive mobile intervals have disjoint
interiors and form exactly the chain in the preceding paragraph.

Its exact free-word action on
`(a,c_1,...,c_r,e)`, `W=c_1...c_r`, is

\[
(WeW^{-1},c_1,...,c_r,e^{-1}W^{-1}aWe),
\]

which independently verifies that every protected intermediate meridian
is fixed. Thus these are the canonical bands already used in the repo.

## Primary statements inspected

Farb--Margalit, *A Primer on Mapping Class Groups*, Princeton
Mathematical Series 49 (2012), original published book PDF:

<https://euclid.nmu.edu/~joshthom/teaching/ma589/farbmarg.pdf>

- Section 1.2.7, printed pp.35--36: proper arcs with marked endpoints,
  relative endpoint conventions, and the arc homotopy/isotopy statement.
- Lemma 2.9, printed pp.61--62: simultaneous isotopy of a collection of
  individually isotopic curves/proper arcs under the disjointness
  hypotheses, which the distinct protected stems satisfy.
- Section 9.1.3--4, printed pp.243--245, including Theorem 9.1:
  the identification `B_s = Mod(D_s)` and half-twist generators.

Birman--Ko--Lee, *A New Approach to the Word and Conjugacy Problems in
the Braid Groups*, Advances in Mathematics 139 (1998), 322--353:

<https://www.math.columbia.edu/~jb/bkl-newpres.pdf>

- Equation (4), Figure 1(a), and its adjacent geometric description,
  printed p.325: the explicit Artin word and embedded-band realization.
- Applying the elementary index-reversal automorphism gives exactly the
  canonical bands used here, as calculated above.

The author book page
<https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~farb/books.html> links the official

## Finite verification and limits

The standard-library [checker](verify_free_meridian_stabilizers.py)
checks the exact band free-word formula, fixes every protected free
meridian for every tested protected subset, compares the framed action
with ordinary Hurwitz moves, verifies the band braid relations, and
deletes protected strands to recover the corresponding adjacent
generators and selected mobile braid words. Altered band routing and
replacement of fixed words by fixed conjugacy classes are rejected by
explicit controls.

The [receipt](free_meridian_stabilizers_verification.json) pins the proof,
checker, source ledger and the two primary PDFs. These bounded word
calculations support the signs, framing and embedding. They do not
prove the arc lemma, simultaneous-isotopy input or the unrestricted
full-stabilizer theorem; those are established in the written proof.

```bash
python3 research/meridional-rank/representations/verify_free_meridian_stabilizers.py \
  --write-receipt research/meridional-rank/representations/free_meridian_stabilizers_verification.json
```
