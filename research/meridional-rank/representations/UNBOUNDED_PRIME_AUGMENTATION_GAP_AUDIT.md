# Protected braid tails and a prime five-bridge augmentation gap

Date: 18 November 2026. This is the current proof note for the first augmentation-gap moonshot. The all-bridge prime-family goal remains open. The results here are a uniform paired-span obstruction, a complete obstruction to using many powers of the original five-strand braid, and one certified prime five-bridge example.

Let `mr` be meridional rank, `br` bridge number, and `ar_C` ordinary complex augmentation rank in the conventions of [PRIME_AUGMENTATION_GAP_FAMILY.tex](PRIME_AUGMENTATION_GAP_FAMILY.tex). Write

\[
\begin{split}
U={}&\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3^{-2}\sigma_2\sigma_3^{-2}
       \sigma_2\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^3\sigma_3^{-1},\\
\beta={}&U\sigma_2^3,\qquad
W_b=\beta\sigma_4^3\cdots\sigma_{b-1}^3.
\end{split}
\]

The strand convention throughout is

\[
T(a,b)=(b,b^{-1}ab),\qquad T^{-1}(a,b)=(aba^{-1},a).
\]

## 1. A prime five-bridge gap

**Theorem 1 (one prime knot).** Put

\[
g=\sigma_3\sigma_4\sigma_3^{-1},\qquad
B=U(\sigma_2g)^4\in B_5,\qquad J=\widehat B.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{ar}_{\mathbb C}(J)\le4<
\operatorname{mr}(J)=\operatorname{br}(J)=5.
\]

The knot is hyperbolic, hence prime. Its presented braid diagram has 30 crossings; minimal crossing number and exact augmentation rank are not asserted.

The literal braid is

```text
[1,2,-3,-3,2,-3,-3,2,1,1,2,2,2,-3,
 2,3,4,-3,2,3,4,-3,2,3,4,-3,2,3,4,-3].
```

**Proof.** Place the following double transpositions on the five top strands, composing permutations from right to left:

\[
(01)(23),\quad(14)(23),\quad(05)(16),\quad(12)(67),\quad(08)(79).
\]

Direct application of the displayed Hurwitz rules returns this literal tuple after all 30 letters. The first four generate the old `A_8` (order 20160). Adjoining `(08)(79)` generates `A_10`: it is the two-new-symbol extension lemma proved in [ALTERNATING_TARGET_PRIME_FAMILIES.tex](ALTERNATING_TARGET_PRIME_FAMILIES.tex), Theorem `thm:targets`, with old symbols `0,7` and new symbols `8,9`. In brief, conjugating `(a u)(c v)` by old even permutations fixing `c` and moving `a` gives three-cycles on `u` and two old symbols; these generate `A_(m+1)`. Repeating while fixing `a,u` introduces `v`. All generators are even.

Four double transpositions supply at most eight edges on ten symbols, so cannot generate a transitive subgroup. The full alternating quotient therefore forces `mr(J)>=5`. The strand permutation of `B` is a five-cycle, giving a knot and the upper bound `mr(J)<=br(J)<=5`. The protected-tail theorem below applies to `(sigma_2 g)^4` and gives `ar_C(J)<=4`.

For primeness, the literal braid is bound, crossing by crossing and including all five closure identifications, to the retained spherical one-component PD code. Spherogram constructs its exterior. A fresh rigorous SnapPy Krawczyk interval verification at 100 bits succeeds on that exterior and on the separately reloaded retained complete triangulation; logarithmic gluing equations and positive tetrahedron orientations are also checked. There is one cusp and 30 tetrahedra. The decorated triangulation signature is

```text
ELvLLLzAvzwwAAMLQPQQQbfggiknmltsvprwxwuuxABCBzzDACDDmtpbvumtgtwmfgapmegjbvgsbfefgth_BbrrRq
```

Thus the knot exterior has a complete finite-volume hyperbolic metric. A composite knot cannot have such an exterior. One group-theoretic proof is that its amalgamated-product presentation over the common meridian makes the two nontrivial summand longitudes generate a nonabelian subgroup of the meridian centralizer, whereas every nonidentity-element centralizer of a torsion-free hyperbolic 3-manifold group is abelian. The latter follows immediately from the discrete faithful representation into `PSL(2,C)`; see Aschenbrenner–Friedl–Wilton, *3-Manifold Groups*, proof of Theorem 2.5.1, printed p.36, in the retained [author copy](sources/afw-3manifold-groups-final-author-2015.pdf). The longitudes are nontrivial and outside the meridian subgroup by peripheral injectivity of nontrivial knot exteriors, and reduced amalgam normal forms give their noncommutation. This proves primeness. ∎

The hyperbolicity computation uses pinned software imports, with the same trust scope as the existing exact knot geometry work. It is an internal rigorous interval replay, not a formal verification or an independent review.

## 2. A protected-tail theorem in every bridge number

For `b>=5`, let `T_b` be the subgroup of `B_b` generated by

\[
\sigma_2,\quad g=\sigma_3\sigma_4\sigma_3^{-1},
\quad\sigma_5,\ldots,\sigma_{b-1}.
\]

For `b=5` the last list is empty. These braids fix the meridian words at positions one and four exactly. On a tuple whose fourth entry is `C`, introduce the framed middle tuple

\[
(M_2,M_3,CM_5C^{-1},\ldots,CM_bC^{-1}).
\]

**Lemma 2 (protected product).** The generators of `T_b` act on this framed tuple by the ordinary adjacent Hurwitz moves. Consequently its ordered product is invariant, and the first and fourth entries of the full tuple are unchanged.

**Proof.** The assertion for `sigma_2` is immediate. On three consecutive entries `(a,C,e)` at positions three through five, direct substitution gives

\[
g:(a,C,e)\longmapsto
(CeC^{-1},\ C,\ e^{-1}C^{-1}aCe).
\]

Hence on `(a,CeC^{-1})` this is precisely `T`. Every `sigma_j`, `j>=5`, acts on two framed entries, because conjugation by the fixed `C` commutes with the Hurwitz formulas. The inverse generators satisfy the inverse identities. ∎

For a representation whose meridians have nonzero displacement rank one, write each meridian as `M=I-u phi`, with common nonzero meridian eigenvalue `mu`. Let `r` be the total root-span dimension and `q` the total covector-span dimension. This notation permits `mu=1` and does not require a preserved bilinear form or an irreducible representation.

**Theorem 3 (uniform paired-span obstruction).** If `tau` belongs to `T_b` and the closure of `U tau` is a knot, then over every field every such representation satisfies

\[
r\le b,\quad q\le b,\quad\min(r,q)\le b-1,
\quad r+q\le2b-1.
\]

In particular

\[
\operatorname{ar}_{\mathbb C}(\widehat{U\tau})\le b-1.
\]

**Proof.** The `b` top meridians generate the knot group. Their root and covector spans are invariant under the corresponding matrices and their inverses, so they contain those of all conjugate meridians; hence `r,q<=b`.

Suppose `r=q=b`. Then the `b` top roots are independent and the `b` top covectors are independent separately. In particular the old four pairs are separately independent. Use the labels and first fourteen crossing equations in the proof of Theorem `thm:paired` in [PRIME_AUGMENTATION_GAP_FAMILY.tex](PRIME_AUGMENTATION_GAP_FAMILY.tex). These are initial labels `(1,0,8,13)` and final labels `(1,6,16,13)` on the first four strands. The extra strands do not meet this prefix. By Lemma 2 the two outer labels are identified exactly by closure, just as in that proof.

For clarity, the local argument depends only on the following four-pair situation, which remains true in ambient dimension greater than four. Put

\[
A=M_2,\quad B=M_8,\quad C=M_{13},\quad D=M_0,
\]

and let `H,F` be the three-dimensional root and covector spans of `A,B,C`. Replacing the initial `M_1` by `A=D^{-1}M_1D` changes the four root and covector families triangularly with unit diagonal. Thus if `D=(v,delta)`, then `v` is outside `H` and `delta` outside `F`. The other pairs appearing in the local fold calculation lie in `H,F`.

The first fold in the cited proof compares

\[
DAD^{-1}=X^{-1}DX,\qquad X=M_{10},
\]

outside `H,F`. Its displayed rank-one formulas give `X=A` and `M_11=D`, without dividing by `1-mu`. The next three prefix crossings send `(D,P)` through `(P,N),(N,Q),(Q,C)`. Writing the pair of `P` as `(p,pi)`, the components outside `H,F` force

\[
1+\pi(v)\delta(p)/\mu=0,
\]

and the same displayed formulas give `Q=D` and `P=C`. This part uses no assertion that the ambient space has dimension four. Therefore the prefix middle pair is `(D,Y)`, where `Y=M_16`.

Let `E_5,...,E_b` be the unchanged new-strand matrices. Closure, fixed outer entries, and Lemma 2 now give

\[
DY\,(CE_5C^{-1})\cdots(CE_bC^{-1})
=DB\,(CE_5C^{-1})\cdots(CE_bC^{-1}).
\]

All matrices are invertible, so right cancellation yields `Y=B`. This is the only place where the old proof used the tail. The remaining eight relations are exactly its equation `(eq:core)`, the reduced `13a_392` system. They contain the separately independent triples `A,B,C` and all their root/covector spans lie in `H,F`. The paired-span obstruction in [GENERIC_KCH_EXTENSIONS.tex](GENERIC_KCH_EXTENSIONS.tex), Lemma `lem:cores`, case `392`, excludes simultaneous root and covector dimension three over every field and every nonzero `mu`, including `mu=1`. This contradiction proves `min(r,q)<=b-1`, and the sum bound follows.

For an irreducible nontrivial KCH representation the root span is the whole representation and the common fixed space is zero, so `r=q=dim(V)`. Cornwell's Theorem 1.2 and Definition 1.8 identify the admitted augmentation ranks with those irreducible dimensions. This gives the asserted complex augmentation upper bound. ∎

This argument extends the local obstruction through a carefully specified subgroup of tails. An arbitrary extra-strand braid, including the original `sigma_4^3` tail, need not protect the fourth meridian and is not covered.

## 3. The elementary protected chain is composite

There is an immediate full-alternating construction in every `b>=5`:

\[
\tau_b=\sigma_2^3g^3\sigma_5^3\cdots\sigma_{b-1}^3.
\]

Use the old four alternating generators and, for `4<=j<=b-1`, use

\[
x_j=(0\,2j)(2\,2j+1).
\]

These generate `A_(2b)` by the two-new-symbol lemma. The first band acts on the framed pair involving `x_2` and `C x_4 C^{-1}`; its product has order three. Each subsequent adjacent pair likewise has product order three. Since a third Hurwitz power fixes a pair of involutions whose product has order three, the full tuple returns. Each new odd twist splices in the next strand of the closure permutation, so the closure is a knot. Thus Theorem 3 and the alternating displacement bound give `ar_C<=b-1<mr=br=b`.

This construction does not supply new prime examples. Indeed,

\[
\begin{split}
U\tau_b
&=\beta\,\sigma_3\sigma_4^3\sigma_3^{-1}
             \sigma_5^3\cdots\sigma_{b-1}^3\\
&\sim(\sigma_3^{-1}\beta\sigma_3)
             \sigma_4^3\sigma_5^3\cdots\sigma_{b-1}^3.
\end{split}
\]

Here `~` is braid conjugacy and `sigma_3` commutes with every displayed generator of index at least five. This is the usual closed-braid connected sum with one positive trefoil per new strand. Its closure is `K_0 # T_(2,3) # ... # T_(2,3)` with `b-4` trefoils, where `K_0` is the original four-bridge prime knot. Only this elementary chain is identified as composite; Theorem 1 shows that other protected tails can be prime.

## 4. A complete failure test for the original five-strand powers

**Theorem 4 (exact symmetric reflection obstruction).** The group of `closure(W_5^n)` has a quotient onto `S_6` taking meridians to transpositions if and only if

\[
2\mid n\quad\text{or}\quad147\mid n.
\]

In particular, for every `n>=2`, `gcd(n,5)=1`, satisfying this condition, the already-proved prime knot `K_(5,n)` has

\[
\operatorname{ar}_{\mathbb C}(K_{5,n})=5.
\]

Thus those infinitely many prime members cannot be augmentation-gap examples.

**Proof of the complete reduction.** Five transpositions generating `S_6` have a connected five-edge support graph on six symbols, hence a tree; repeated edges are impossible. Their ordered product is a six-cycle: as each tree edge is inserted it joins distinct components of the preceding forest and therefore merges distinct cycles of the current permutation. Conjugate the product to `(012345)`.

There are 15 possible transpositions. For every ordered first four factors, the product-one equation with the fixed six-cycle uniquely forces the fifth; retain the tuple exactly when that factor is a transposition. This exhausts the domain, with 1296 tuples, all connected. The Hurwitz action of `W_5` preserves it. Its complete literal orbit census is

| Period | Number of cycles |
|---:|---:|
| 2 | 15 |
| 6 | 2 |
| 10 | 3 |
| 24 | 1 |
| 66 | 3 |
| 72 | 1 |
| 147 | 2 |
| 180 | 1 |
| 228 | 2 |

Every even period here is divisible by two and period two occurs; the sole odd period is 147. Thus a tuple is fixed by `W_5^n` exactly for the stated divisibility condition. Since the normalization did not discard any simultaneous-conjugacy class, the condition is necessary and sufficient for every full `S_6` transposition quotient.

The standard five-dimensional complex representation of `S_6` is irreducible and each transposition has displacement rank one and meridian eigenvalue `-1`. Such a quotient therefore gives an irreducible five-dimensional KCH representation and, by Cornwell's correspondence, augmentation rank at least five. The bridge upper bound is five, so equality follows. This argument uses a rank-one representation of the symmetric quotient, not an unsupported inference from the double-transposition alternating quotient. ∎

For example, the existing period-two witness is

```text
[(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(2,4),(0,5)].
```

The exhaustive count gives 30 normalized tuples fixed by `W_5^2`, agreeing with the prior five conjugacy classes times the six-element centralizer of the six-cycle. A disconnected would-be witness is explicitly rejected.

Among the original prime powers, the first case not eliminated by this necessary test is `K_(5,3)`, of crossing number 60 and genus 28. Absence of this particular finite reflection quotient does not prove its augmentation rank is at most four; other rank-five KCH representations remain unexcluded.
