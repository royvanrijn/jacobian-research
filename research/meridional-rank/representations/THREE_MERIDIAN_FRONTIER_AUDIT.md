# The three-meridian / four-bridge obstruction

Audit: 18 November 2026. This is a target audit and falsification record,
not a new MRC theorem or a novelty claim.

## Precise target and known boundary

For every classical knot K, let G be its knot group and mr(K) the least
number of meridians generating G. The full conjecture is mr(K)=b(K).
The first unresolved rank frontier is

    for every K, mr(K)<=3 implies b(K)<=3.

One K with mr(K)=3 and b(K)=4 disproves MRC. Boileau--Jang--Weidmann
prove this implication when the double branched cover is a graph
manifold (Pacific J. Math. 292 (2018), Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3):
https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.2018.292.61
https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.00816

The two-meridian characterization is known with orbifold geometrization.
The 2026 K3 problem list still poses full MRC as Problem 1.18(a), and
the closed hyperbolic rank-two/genus-two question as Problem 3.35(a):
https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/websites.umass.edu/dist/b/22144/files/2026/04/K3-problem-list-watermarked.pdf

There is no single strongest family theorem. Recent published positive
families include fishnets of arbitrary bridge number and high bridge
distance (Blair--Kjuchukova--Pfaff, 2026):
https://doi.org/10.1017/S030500412610214X

The November 11, 2026 Gao--Gu--Li--Wang preprint proves MRC for every
finite iterated untwisted Whitehead double of every nontrivial two-bridge
knot. It also proves ar(Wh(square knot))<=5<6=mr=b. This external
augmentation-gap result is already cited in the repository:
https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15673v1
It is a preprint, not a refereed published theorem in this audit.

## Exact available transfer and missing step

Assume G is generated by three meridians m1,m2,m3. In the order-two
orbifold quotient Gamma=G/<<m1^2>>, put t=bar(m1), a=bar(m2)t,
b=bar(m3)t. Each bar(mi) is an involution, so

    Gamma=<t,a,b>, t^2=1, tat=a^-1, tbt=b^-1.

Its parity kernel Pi=pi_1(Sigma_2(K)) is generated by a,b: move every t
to the right and use parity to remove the final t. Conjugation by t is
the actual deck involution and inverts this specific generating pair.
A small ordinary generating set of Pi alone does not give such a pair.
Three torsion generators of Gamma do not by themselves show that three
meridians generate the original group G.

The geometric missing step is whether this specific inversion is induced
by the hyperelliptic involution of a genus-two Heegaard splitting of the
actual cover. Such a splitting descends to two balls containing three
trivial arcs each, giving b(K)<=3. Conversely, a three-bridge presentation
lifts to such an equivariant splitting.

Boileau--Weidmann explicitly distinguish an inversion from one realized
by a genus-two hyperelliptic involution:
https://www.math.uni-kiel.de/algebra/de/weidmann/research/material-research/inversions-pdf
Jorgensen's matrix argument already supplies an inversion for every
two-generated hyperbolic lattice. Geometric realization, rather than
another matrix inversion, is the unresolved gate. The broad claim that
every inversion is hyperelliptic would also settle the hyperbolic
rank-two/genus-two question. No such claim is proved here.

Terminology matters: some papers call every involution with quotient S^3
hyperelliptic. That weaker definition does not supply a genus-two
splitting and does not close this gate.

## Falsification checks

1. Dropping the deck-inversion marking fails. The retained
   GENERAL_BRANCHED_COVER_RANK_BOUNDS.tex proves that the double-cover
   group of T(4,7) has ordinary rank two but mr(T(4,7))=b(T(4,7))=4.
   Thus cover rank<=2 does not imply bridge<=3 without more hypotheses.
   This Seifert example does not disprove the hyperbolic rank/genus
   question.

2. An inversion only on H_1 is too weak. In the retained prime hyperbolic
   family K_n, the deck action on cyclic H_1=Z/(117+366n) is -1, yet the
   cover group has rank three and mr(K_n)=b(K_n)=4. A homology-only
   inversion criterion would be false. This uses the marked-family and
   symmetry theorems as premises; their software imports are not rerun.

## Repository result and significance decision

The prime twist family satisfies ar_C(K_n)=3<mr(K_n)=b(K_n)=4 and
rank(pi_1(Sigma_2(K_n)))=3. It is therefore already excluded from the
three-meridian premise by the elementary kernel calculation above.
Further rank/Fox calculations on this family do not approach the first
unresolved frontier. The unbounded protected augmentation-gap families
also satisfy MRC and do not resolve this transfer.

A significant result would prove the geometric realization theorem for
every deck-inverted generating pair arising from three original
meridians, or produce a knot with a certified triple generating its
original group and an independent bridge-four lower bound. The former
settles the first universal frontier; the latter disproves MRC. Neither
is established in this audit. Additional sharp-four examples or
augmentation gaps fail this target test.
