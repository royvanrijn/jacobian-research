# The three-meridian / four-bridge obstruction

A direct trace certificate proves meridional rank four for \(T(4,5;2,2)\).
For the second input, \(T(5,19;2,1)\), a five-dimensional finite-field
representation proves meridional rank and bridge number five. The new representation also gives the sharp bound in the order-two
orbifold group. The original seven-sheet certificate gives an independent
obstruction to three generators and the deck-action result.

## Precise target and known boundary

For every classical knot K, let G be its knot group and mr(K) the least
number of meridians generating G. The full conjecture is mr(K)=b(K).
The first unresolved rank frontier is

    for every K, mr(K)<=3 implies b(K)<=3.

One K with mr(K)=3 and b(K)=4 disproves MRC. Boileau--Jang--Weidmann
prove this implication when the double branched cover is a graph
manifold (Pacific J. Math. 292 (2018), Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3):
https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.2018.292.61
https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.00816

The two-meridian characterization is known with orbifold geometrization.
The 2026 K3 problem list still poses full MRC as Problem 1.18(a), and
the closed hyperbolic rank-two/genus-two question as Problem 3.35(a):
https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/websites.umass.edu/dist/b/22144/files/2026/04/K3-problem-list-watermarked.pdf

There is no single strongest family theorem. Recent published positive
families include fishnets of arbitrary bridge number and high bridge
distance (Blair--Kjuchukova--Pfaff, 2026):
https://doi.org/10.1017/S030500412610214X

The November 11, 2026 Gao--Gu--Li--Wang preprint proves MRC for every
finite iterated untwisted Whitehead double of every nontrivial two-bridge
knot. It also proves ar(Wh(square knot))<=5<6=mr=b. This external
augmentation-gap result is already cited in the repository:
https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15673v1
It is a preprint, not a refereed published theorem in this audit.

## Exact available transfer and missing step

Assume G is generated by three meridians m1,m2,m3. In the order-two
orbifold quotient Gamma=G/<<m1^2>>, put t=bar(m1), a=bar(m2)t,
b=bar(m3)t. Each bar(mi) is an involution, so

    Gamma=<t,a,b>, t^2=1, tat=a^-1, tbt=b^-1.

Its parity kernel Pi=pi_1(Sigma_2(K)) is generated by a,b: move every t
to the right and use parity to remove the final t. Conjugation by t is
the actual deck involution and inverts this specific generating pair.
A small ordinary generating set of Pi alone does not give such a pair.
Three torsion generators of Gamma do not by themselves show that three
meridians generate the original group G.

The geometric missing step is whether this specific inversion is induced
by the hyperelliptic involution of a genus-two Heegaard splitting of the
actual cover. Such a splitting descends to two balls containing three
trivial arcs each, giving b(K)<=3. Conversely, a three-bridge presentation
lifts to such an equivariant splitting.

Boileau--Weidmann explicitly distinguish an inversion from one realized
by a genus-two hyperelliptic involution:
https://www.math.uni-kiel.de/algebra/de/weidmann/research/material-research/inversions-pdf
Jorgensen's matrix argument already supplies an inversion for every
two-generated hyperbolic lattice. Geometric realization, rather than
another matrix inversion, is the unresolved gate. The broad claim that
every inversion is hyperelliptic would also settle the hyperbolic
rank-two/genus-two question. No such claim is proved here.

Terminology matters: some papers call every involution with quotient S^3
hyperelliptic. That weaker definition does not supply a genus-two
splitting and does not close this gate.

## Exact orbifold generation and the deck marking

Fix a meridian image t in the order-two orbifold quotient Gamma_2.
Parity splits through the involution t, so Gamma_2=Pi semidirect <t>.
Write tau for conjugation by t. For a fixed tuple of meridians whose
first image is t, put u_i=bar(m_i)*t for the other entries. Moving each
t to the right shows that the tuple generates Gamma_2 exactly when
the u_i generate Pi. Each u_i is inverted by tau.

There is also an exact existence criterion in Gamma_2. Its meridians
are the conjugates of t. Conjugating by an element w of Pi gives

    (w*t*w^-1)*t=w*tau(w)^-1.

Conjugation by an arbitrary element of Gamma_2 gives the same set, since
that element has the form w*t^e. Thus Gamma_2 is generated by at most
three meridians exactly when Pi has a generating pair of elements of the
form w*tau(w)^-1. The expression certifies conjugacy to t of the resulting
involutions. A pair characterized solely by tau(u)=u^-1 has not thereby
been supplied with that conjugacy witness.

Applying the parity argument to all the generators of a meridional
presentation shows that tau acts by minus the identity on Pi's
abelianization. In particular, if Pi is cyclic or trivial, tau is
inversion and every SL_2 trace is invariant.

## Repository result and significance decision

The prime twist family satisfies ar_C(K_n)=3<mr(K_n)=b(K_n)=4 and
rank(pi_1(Sigma_2(K_n)))=3. It is therefore already excluded from the
three-meridian premise by the elementary kernel calculation above.
Further rank/Fox calculations on this family do not approach the first
unresolved frontier. The unbounded protected augmentation-gap families
also satisfy MRC and do not resolve this transfer.

A significant result would prove the geometric realization theorem for
every deck-inverted generating pair arising from three original
meridians, or produce a knot with a certified triple generating its
original group and an independent bridge-four lower bound. The former
settles the first universal frontier; the latter disproves MRC. Neither
is established in this audit. Additional sharp-four examples or
augmentation gaps fail this target test.

## Generation certificates and a fixed-tuple lifting failure

A three-seed diagram-coloring search cannot produce a counterexample with
bridge number four. Blair--Kjuchukova--Velazquez--Villanueva,
[Theorem 1.3](https://doi.org/10.4310/CAG.2020.v28.n2.a2), prove that the
minimum Wirtinger number over diagrams equals bridge number. A successful
three-seed propagation already proves bridge at most three. General group
relations, rather than only that propagation rule, must certify a putative
counterexample's generating triple.

Orbifold generation of a SPECIFIED triple is insufficient even for the
trefoil. In its meridional presentation G=<a,b | aba=bab>, put

```text
n=a^2 b a^-2 b^-1,  b'=n b n^-1,  c=a b' a^-1.
```

The meridians a,b',c map to the three distinct transpositions in the
order-two orbifold quotient S3. But the homomorphism
a -> (123), b -> (134) maps G onto A4, sends n to (13)(24), and sends
ALL THREE proposed generators to (123). Their subgroup is therefore proper.
Both finite permutation computations were checked exactly. The trefoil
has other generating lifts; this is not mr>m2 or a counterexample to MRC.

A naive relative kernel abelianization cannot repair this issue. Put
N=<<m1^2>>_G, H=<the proposed meridians>, K=H intersect N. Orbifold
surjectivity gives G=HN. Modulo [N,N], every conjugate of m1^2 by an element
of G equals one conjugated by an element of H, and that element belongs
to K. Hence N=K[N,N], regardless of whether H=G. If N=K gamma_j(N),
commutator collection gives [N,N] contained in [K,K] gamma_(j+1)(N),
so induction gives G=H gamma_j(N) for every finite lower-central stage.
The trefoil example still has H proper.

A genuine positive certificate can instead give, for EVERY original
presentation generator x_j, a word V_j in the proposed meridians and an
explicit free-group identity

```text
x_j V_j(m1,m2,m3)^-1 = product_l u_l r_(k_l)^(epsilon_l) u_l^-1,
epsilon_l in {1,-1}.
```

Here the r_k are ONLY original knot-group relators. Free reduction verifies
the certificate. Such finite certificates exist exactly when the triple
generates. Adding meridian-square relators would change the assertion.

## Other cone orders and finite-index subgroups

Two further elementary checks constrain a proposed geometric reduction.
They are route controls, with no claim of novelty or of settling MRC.

First, a subgroup H of a classical knot group generated by ambient
meridians cannot have proper finite index. Suppose X_H is its finite
exterior cover. On each boundary torus T let e_T be the least positive
meridian exponent lifting to that torus. Every stated generator of H
is conjugate in H to a lifted meridian on a torus with e_T=1. That
slope is primitive, since the ambient meridian is primitive in the
peripheral Z^2. Fill ALL such tori along that slope. Every generator
dies, so the resulting compact orientable manifold W has pi_1(W)=1.
If any unfilled torus remains, chi(W)=chi(boundary W)/2=0, whereas
b_1(W)=b_3(W)=0 gives chi(W)=1+b_2(W)>0. Thus every e_T=1.
The meridian fixes every coset (its cycle length is constant on each
peripheral orbit). Since it normally generates the knot group, the
transitive coset action is trivial and [G:H]=1. This does not show
that a proper infinite-index meridional subgroup is free.

Second, subgroup separability gives an ALL-ORDERS test for a FIXED tuple:
for finitely generated H in a subgroup-separable G,

```text
H=G iff image(H)=G/<<mu^n>> for every n>=1,
    iff this holds for every sufficiently large n,
    iff this holds for every sufficiently large factorial order n!.
```

Indeed, if H is proper, a finite quotient Q separates it from some
element of G. Put d=ord_Q(mu). The separating quotient factors through
G/<<mu^n>> whenever d divides n, including all sufficiently large
factorials. Hyperbolic knot groups satisfy the separability premise;
see [Aschenbrenner--Friedl--Wilton, Corollary 4.2.3(1)](sources/afw-3manifold-groups-final-author-2015.pdf#page=70).
The same proof works for any set of cone orders containing a multiple
of every positive integer. In fact a proper fixed tuple subgroup admits
a finite quotient in which it has proper image, and that quotient witnesses
nongeneration for every cone order divisible by the order of mu in it.
Thus for a finitely presented subgroup-separable knot group, the fixed
tuple question has finite certificates on both sides. A positive certificate
uses only original relators, and a negative certificate gives a finite
quotient with proper image. Enumerating both types in parallel terminates
for the fixed tuple. This does not interchange the tuple with the cone
order or decide whether some tuple of a given length generates.

An arbitrary unbounded sequence of orders is insufficient, even with
the SAME tuple. In the trefoil put

```text
G=<a,b | aba=bab>, v=b^-1 a, q=v b v^-1, H=<a,q>.
```

In the S3 quotient a=(12), b=(23), one has q=a, so H is proper.
Nevertheless its image generates G/<<a^n>> for EVERY ODD n. Here is
an all-order proof, rather than extrapolation from finite examples.

Let Delta=aba=bab and z=(ab)^3=Delta^2. Braid rewrites give
Delta a=b Delta and Delta b=a Delta, so z is central. In fact
G=<Delta,y | Delta^2=y^3>, y=ab, a=y^-1 Delta,
b=Delta^-1 y^2; hence G/<z>=C2*C3=PSL2(Z). Its matrix model gives

```text
a=T=[[1,1],[0,1]], b=[[1,0],[-1,1]],
q=[[-1,1],[-4,3]], q T=-V4, V4=[[1,0],[4,1]].
```

The image P=<T,V4> is Gamma0(4) in PSL2(Z). To verify generation,
write a determinant-one matrix as [[A,B],[4R,D]], with A odd.
For R nonzero, left multiplication by a power of T makes
|A'|<=2|R|, strictly less because A' is odd. A power of V4 then makes
|R'|<=|A'|/2<|R|. Repeating this integer descent reaches R=0, when
the matrix is plus or minus a power of T.

The action on primitive projective columns modulo four has six points.
Its full image Q has order 24, the stabilizer P has image order four,
and T has order four. Thus P contains the kernel of this action.
For odd n, T^n generates <T> in Q; T normally generates PSL2(Z),
so the normal closure of T^n maps onto Q. Consequently
PSL2(Z)=P normal_closure(T^n). In G_n=G/<<a^n>> this says
G_n=H_n <z_n>. Centrality makes H_n normal, and it contains a_n,
which normally generates G_n. Therefore H_n=G_n for every odd n.

In particular every sufficiently large prime order passes for this
proper fixed subgroup. The example is Seifert, so it does not refute
a specifically HYPERBOLIC long-filling theorem. Such a theorem would
need its own geometric proof. The finite projective action and braid
identities are included in the retained replay.

## A homological positive certificate in the hyperbolic case

There is a useful alternative to writing every original generator in the
proposed meridians. It is a consequence of classical three-manifold
theorems, not a claim of a new major theorem.

Let G be the group of an orientable finite-volume hyperbolic
three-manifold. If a finitely generated noncyclic subgroup H satisfies
b_1(H;Q)=1, then [G:H] is finite. To prove this, apply the
[Tameness Theorem, AFW Theorem 4.1.1](sources/afw-3manifold-groups-final-author-2015.pdf#page=68)
to N=H^3/H, writing N=int(C). The compact orientable C is irreducible.
A spherical boundary component would, by irreducibility of N, force
N to be R^3 and H trivial, so none occurs. Half-lives/half-dies gives
sum of genera of boundary components <=b_1(C)=1. Therefore C is
closed or has exactly one torus boundary component. The closed case
gives finite volume. If that torus compresses, irreducibility makes C
a solid torus, contradicting noncyclicity. Otherwise its injected Z^2
is parabolic and gives an embedded finite-volume rank-two cusp U with
compact frontier. Since N has just one end, N minus int(U) is compact:
otherwise the compact frontier separates two noncompact sides. Thus
N has finite volume. In either case its covering degree is finite.

Now suppose G is a HYPERBOLIC CLASSICAL KNOT group and H is generated
by ambient meridians. The preceding finite-index argument strengthens this to

```text
H noncyclic and b_1(H;Q)=1  =>  H=G.
```

For a proposed triple (m1,m2,m3), it therefore suffices to give TWO
identity words R1,R2 in these elements, each certified as a product
of conjugates of ORIGINAL group relators, whose exponent-sum vectors
in Z^3 are linearly independent. These relations force b_1(H;Q)<=1,
while the ambient meridional homomorphism H onto Z forces equality.
A finite quotient in which the triple has noncommuting images certifies
noncyclicity. These data would certify generation in the original group
without first producing words for every original generator.

For a fixed triple of positive meridians in a hyperbolic knot group,
this certificate is also necessary if that triple generates G. The
exponent sums of all identity words form a subgroup of Z^3 whose quotient
is H_1(G;Z)=Z. Its rank is two, so two independent vectors have identity
words as preimages. The original finite presentation supplies finite
relator witnesses for those words. The tuple generates a nonabelian group,
and residual finiteness supplies a finite quotient detecting a
noncommuting pair.

This criterion requires the justified hyperbolic ambient manifold and
identities in the original group. Relations only in a finite quotient or
after meridian-power filling do not suffice. The trace obstruction below now proves that no such pair of independent
original-group relations can certify a meridian triple for this input.
The criterion remains a sufficient test for other hyperbolic inputs.

### A deck trace obstruction in arbitrary characteristic

If an automorphism alpha of a group H inverts a generating pair a,b,
then for EVERY field k and EVERY rho:H->SL_2(k),

    tr rho(alpha(w))=tr rho(w), EVERY w in H.             (T1)

Here is a direct proof including positive characteristic. For determinant-one
matrices A,B write x=tr A, y=tr B, z=tr AB. Cayley--Hamilton gives

    A^-1=xI-A, B^-1=yI-B,
    BA=(z-xy)I+yA+xB-AB.                                (T2)

These identities reduce every word matrix to a linear combination of
I,A,B,AB with coefficients in Z[x,y,z]. For example this span is stable
under right multiplication by A and B, using (T2), A^2=xA-I and
B^2=yB-I. Thus its trace is an integer polynomial in x,y,z.
Replacing A,B by A^-1,B^-1 preserves all three trace coordinates:
tr(A^-1 B^-1)=tr((BA)^-1)=tr(BA)=z. This proves (T1), with no
irreducibility, characteristic-zero or lifting hypothesis.

The parity-kernel argument above therefore has the following consequence.
For ANY classical knot, if a representation of its double-cover group
over ANY field has a trace changed by the actual deck outer action,
then mr(K)>=4. Indeed, three meridians would give an inverted generating
pair. Changing the chosen meridian changes the deck representative by an
inner automorphism of the parity kernel, which cannot change traces.
Explicitly two odd lifts are h*t and t with h in the kernel, and their
conjugation actions differ by Inn(h).

### A direct certificate on the literal braid

All the following matrices have determinant one over F_13:

    K0=(12 8; 7 8),  K1=(10 0; 7 4),
    K2=(1 9; 4 11),  K3=I_2.

For A in SL_2(F_13), put

    J(A)=(0 A; A^-1 0),                                 (T3)

in two-by-two block notation. Then J(A)^2=I_4 and

    J(A) J(B) J(A)^-1 = J(A B^-1 A).                    (T4)

Use the positive Artin convention in which a left-over-right crossing
changes the top meridian images (X,Y) to (XYX^-1,X).
Thus the corresponding colour pair changes by

    (A,B) -> (A B^-1 A,A).                              (T5)

Applying (T5) successively along the literal word
sigma1^4 (sigma1 sigma2 sigma3)^5 returns (K0,K1,K2,K3) EXACTLY.
The checker verifies all nineteen steps and the four closure equations;
it also independently multiplies the actual four-by-four matrices at each
crossing. This is a complete finite certificate of the closed-braid
Wirtinger relations. The strand permutation is a four-cycle, so the
closure is a knot and the four x_i are actual meridians.

Consequently x_i -> J(K_i) gives a representation of the actual knot
group which kills meridian squares. Every even word maps to a block
diagonal matrix with determinant-one blocks. Its first block defines a
representation rho of the parity kernel. For the even word
w=x3*x0*x1*x2, exact multiplication gives

    image(w)=diag(U,V),
    U=(2 12; 1 0), V=(0 2; 6 4).                       (T6)

Conjugating by x3, whose image is J(I), swaps these blocks. Therefore

    tr rho(w)=2 != 4=tr rho(tau(w)) in F_13.             (T7)

By (T1) no representative of this deck outer action inverts a generating
pair. Three meridians cannot generate the original knot group. Together
with mr(K)<=b(K)<=4 from the literal four-braid, this proves

    mr(K)=b(K)=4.                                       (T8)

This proof does NOT use SnapPy, the simplified presentation, hyperbolicity,
a character-variety Groebner computation, or a lift from F_13 to C.
It is an internal mathematical proof with an exact finite replay, not an
external-review or priority assertion. It closes this candidate only;
the universal three-meridian implication remains open.

The parity criterion shows that (T7) excludes three meridians already
in Gamma_2. For every even n the quotient map Gamma_n -> Gamma_2 carries
meridians to meridians. Four braid meridians generate Gamma_n, so its
least number of meridional generators is exactly four for every even n.

The same trace witness excludes cyclic Pi by the action on abelianization.
The (1,1) presentation above bounds ordinary rank and Heegaard genus by
two, so both are exactly two. The least genus of an actual deck-invariant
splitting with two trivial-tangle quotients is three. The four-bridge
sphere gives genus three and a smaller genus of this type would give a
bridge presentation with at most three bridges.

This is not a novelty claim for the general trace method. Cornwell's
related complex-character obstruction is already discussed in the
[reflective augmentation audit](REFLECTIVE_AUGMENTATION_RANK_GAP.tex),
including his Theorem 5.5. The direct arbitrary-field proof above is
self-contained and the F_13 certificate requires no complex lift.

from a failed finite representation search or a coset limit is made.
The direct trace obstruction now supplies nongeneration for EVERY triple.
No finite monodromy-orbit certificate can generate the whole F16 from two
seeds of the stated form, since it would produce an excluded triple.
Further conjugator or Stallings enumeration on this knot is unnecessary.


## An exact rank-five certificate for T(5,19;2,1)

The closure \(K\) of the five-braid \(\delta^{19}\sigma_1^2\), with
\(\delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_3\sigma_4\), satisfies
\(\operatorname{mr}(K)=b(K)=5\). The images of meridians in every
even-order meridian quotient also require five meridional generators.

Work over \(\mathbb F_{37}\) and set
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}
36&0&0&0&0\\
0&17&29&0&0\\
0&4&33&0&0\\
0&0&0&33&13\\
0&0&0&12&7
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u=(1,1,0,1,0)^{\mathsf T},\qquad v=(5,13,26,17,34).
\]
Define
\[
A=I+uv,\qquad Q=P^4,\qquad Z=A^{-1}Q,\qquad E=Q^{-1}AQ,\qquad
B=ZAZ^{-1},\qquad C=ZBZ^{-1},\qquad D=ZCZ^{-1}.
\]
Here \(P\) is invertible and \(vu=35=-2\), so \(A\) is a rank-one
reflection with \(A^2=I\). The five matrices \(A,B,C,D,E\) are
conjugate and all square to \(I\).

Give the five top strand meridians the colours \((A,B,C,D,E)\).
Apply \((U,V)\mapsto(UVU^{-1},U)\) at each of the 78 crossings in
the displayed order. Exact multiplication returns the starting
tuple, so the five closure relations give a representation
\(\rho\colon\pi_1(S^3\setminus K)\to\mathrm{GL}_5(\mathbb F_{37})\).
The [standard-library checker](verify_t51921_meridional_rank.py)
replays the updates and computes the determinant of the first rows of
the five displacement matrices \(A-I,\ldots,E-I\). These rows, in order, are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
5&13&26&17&34\\
5&21&32&11&1\\
8&12&21&11&21\\
5&15&31&19&26\\
5&21&15&11&6
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This matrix has determinant \(31\) in \(\mathbb F_{37}\).
Hence the image has zero common fixed space.

Every meridian of a knot is conjugate to a strand meridian or its
inverse, so its image fixes a hyperplane. Four hyperplanes in
dimension five have a nonzero common intersection. A vector fixed
by those four images would be fixed by the subgroup they generate.
Therefore four meridians cannot generate the knot group. Since
\(\operatorname{mr}(K)\le b(K)\le5\) from the five-braid, equality
holds. The proof uses the literal braid and the matrix representation.

Let \(m\) be a meridian and \(j\ge1\). Since \(\rho(m)^2=I\),
\(\rho\) factors through \(G/\langle\!\langle m^{2j}\rangle\!\rangle\).
The same argument rules out four images of meridians generating
that quotient, and the five strand meridians still give a generating
set. In particular, the order-two orbifold group has meridional
involution generating number five.

**Same-colour corollary.** The checker also verifies that
\(\delta^{19}\) returns the top tuple and that \(A\) and \(B\) commute.
On a commuting pair the Artin update swaps the entries, so
\(\sigma_1^2\) also fixes the tuple. Both braid actions are invertible.
Consequently the same certificate gives
\(\operatorname{mr}=b=5\) for the closures of
\(\delta^{19r}\sigma_1^{2s}\) for integers \(r,s\) with \(\gcd(r,5)=1\).
The condition on \(r\) makes the braid permutation a five-cycle, so
each closure is a knot.

Replay from the repository root with

```sh
python3 research/meridional-rank/representations/verify_t51921_meridional_rank.py
```

## A trace-invariant input excluded by a seven-sheet cover

The next input is fixed, rather than a family-expansion claim:

    beta = sigma1*sigma2*sigma3*sigma4^(-1) in B5,
    K = closure(beta*Delta5^(-8)),
    Delta5^2 = (sigma1*sigma2*sigma3*sigma4)^5.

Up to mirror, this is T(5,19;2,1). The seven-sheet argument proves a
lower bound of four for its knot group and rules out every
meridional-involution triple in its order-two orbifold group. The
orbifold obstruction also excludes every inverted generating pair in
the actual deck outer class. The matrix certificate above determines
the original group's meridional rank exactly.

**Exact axis identification.** Hilden--Lozano--Montesinos-Amilibia,
Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 129 (2000), 477--490, Section 2,
Corollary 2.2 and Remark 2.4, give a marked unknot-axis normal form for
two-bridge links. The ordinary continued fraction

    [2,1,-2,1,2,1,-2,-1,2] = 22/3

has their sign vector (1,1,1,-1). Since all four labels have absolute
value one, their diagram is precisely the closed braid beta with its
axis. Lee--Seo, Bull. Aust. Math. Soc. 79 (2009), 273--284, printed
pp.273--275, Figures 1--3, reproduce this axis diagram; printed p.274
was rendered and visually inspected. Reversing the strand numbering
conjugates by Delta5, and changing the cyclic cut conjugates the braid.
A global mirror changes all crossing and surgery signs together.
[HLM](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100004679),
[Lee--Seo](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972708001251).

**Cover and framing.** The two components of B(22,3) are interchangeable
unknots. The double cover branched on either component lifts the other
to the two-bridge knot B(11,3), namely 6_2 up to mirror. One exact model
takes the lens-space double cover L(22,3) and its index-two cover
L(11,3); the two reflection classes downstairs give the two components.
The residual period on B(11,3) has this B(22,3) quotient link.

On preferred peripheral coordinates for that period, the map is
mu_up -> mu_down, lambda_up -> 2*lambda_down: the knot maps with degree
two, and the homologically zero longitude has no meridian term.
Thus +1/2 surgery upstairs descends to +1/4 surgery on the quotient
unknot. In the convention that +1/n surgery removes an axis by n
negative full twists, this gives beta*Delta5^(-8). Its odd numerator
makes the extension on the filled solid torus free, so no extra branch
component appears. Mirror conventions may reverse the displayed surgery
sign; the two signs are kept together.

Consequently Sigma_2(K) is a numerator-one, denominator-two surgery on
6_2, up to these sign conventions. Its H1 is zero. The upper tunnel
splitting gives Heegaard genus and group rank at most two.
Brittenham--Wu, Theorem 1.1 (printed p.98) and Remark 6.3 (p.112),
show that every nonintegral surgery on a hyperbolic two-bridge knot is
hyperbolic. Thus BOTH signs here are hyperbolic; the group is noncyclic,
and its rank and the cover's Heegaard genus are exactly two.
[Brittenham--Wu, published paper](https://doi.org/10.4310/CAG.2001.v9.n1.a4).

Optional interval checks with SageMath 10.9 and SnapPy 3.3.2 also
succeeded for both literal census fillings Manifold("6_2") with slopes
(1,2) and (1,-2), using verify_hyperbolicity(bits_prec=160) on the
original filled triangulation. These supplementary checks do not certify
a surgery-sign convention and are not needed for the published
hyperbolicity implication.

**Independent bridge lower bound.** Put delta=sigma1*sigma2*sigma3*sigma4.
Mirror beta*Delta5^(-8), then conjugate by Delta5, which reverses the
generator indices. The braid becomes

    (sigma4^-1*sigma3^-1*sigma2^-1*sigma1)*Delta5^8
      = delta^-1*sigma1^2*delta^20
      = delta^19*sigma1^2.

This is the positive twisted torus braid T(5,19;2,1). Vafaee's
published Theorem 1 (restated as Theorem 3.1) makes it an L-space knot:
in the notation K(p,kp-1;s,r), take p=5, k=4, s=2 and r=1. The braid
contains a positive full twist, so its braid index is five; Krishna--
Morton's published Theorem 1.3 then gives bridge index five as well.
Their proof uses the first Alexander-polynomial gap and Floer torsion
for L-space knots. Thus b(K)=5 uses published premises and needs no
general twist-positive bridge theorem. Sage's exact braid arithmetic
also checked the displayed identities; neither numerical recognition
nor a computed knot table supplies the lower bound.
[Vafaee, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1311.3711v2),
[Krishna--Morton, Theorem 1.3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00029-024-00996-6).

**Why the trace obstruction cannot decide it.** Reid--Walsh, Section 2.4,
proof of Lemma 2.8, mark the period by a basepoint on its axis and give
tau(a)=b, tau(b)=a for the original upper meridians. The same swap
descends over the filling. Since a,b remain conjugate and generate the
filled group, every SL2 representation over every field has
tr(a)=tr(b). Swapping them fixes all three trace coordinates
tr(a), tr(b), tr(ab), hence fixes every word trace by (T1)--(T2).
Thus the direct trace method that eliminated T(4,5;2,2) cannot eliminate
this input.
[Reid--Walsh, Section 2.4](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/areid/walsh_reid_final.pdf).

**Direct nonabelian cover certificate.** Use the positive mirror
T(5,19;2,1), with the literal braid delta^19*sigma1^2. Write m1,...,m5
for its strand meridians. On sheets numbered 0,...,6 take

    m1 -> (0 1)(2 3),
    m2 -> (1 2)(4 5),
    m3 -> (1 6)(3 4),
    m4 -> (0 6)(2 4),
    m5 -> (2 5)(3 6).

The checker derives all five Artin closure relators by the positive
Hurwitz update (U,V)->(U V U^-1,U) through all 78 crossings. The five
permutations satisfy every relation and act transitively. Each has five
cycles and permutation index two.

Here is the complete small certificate for the fundamental group of
the connected seven-sheet branched completion Y. In the right Schreier
graph use base sheet zero and scan letters +m1,-m1,...,+m5,-m5. Its
tree edges (start sheet, generator index) are

    (0,1), (0,4), (1,2), (2,4), (2,5), (6,5).

The tree transversals are

    r0=1, r1=m1, r2=m1*m2, r3=m4*m5,
    r4=m1*m2*m4, r5=m1*m2*m5, r6=m4.

Order all 29 remaining positive edges lexicographically by
(start sheet, generator index), and let yj be the corresponding
Schreier generator r_v*m_i*r_(v.m_i)^-1. Over F13 send all yj to I
except

    y5  -> (0,12;1,3),     y8  -> (1,1;1,2),
    y11 -> (2,12;12,1),    y13 -> (4,9;4,6),
    y17 -> (9,10;1,7),     y18 -> (6,4;9,4),
    y22 -> (7,3;12,9),     y28 -> (3,1;12,0).

All matrices have determinant one. The checker reconstructs and
verifies all 35 lifts of the five braid relations and all 25 branch
fillings: one lift of m_i^length for EVERY cycle of EVERY mi, including
fixed points. Every one of these 60 raw relations maps to I, while
y5=m1*m3*m4^-1 maps to (0,12;1,3), not I. Thus pi1(Y) is nontrivial.
No Tietze simplification, software-derived knot group, surgery framing,
lens-space identification or numerical geometry enters this certificate.

**Why every triple is excluded.** If any three meridians generated
the knot group, their seven-sheet presentation graph would have seven
vertices. Each generator's two 2-cycles and three 1-cycles are killed by
branched filling; deleting one edge from each cycle leaves two edges
per generator. Connectivity is preserved. The resulting graph has
seven vertices and six edges, hence is a tree. The branched-cover group
would be trivial, contradicting the matrix certificate. Equivalently,
the general branched-cover rank bound gives

    mr(K) >= ceil((7-1+rank(pi1(Y)))/2) >= 4.

The same graph argument applies to a generating triple of conjugates
of the meridional involution in Gamma=G/<<m1^2>>. The 2-cycle reverse
edges are removed by the involution relations and all fixed-sheet
loops die in the underlying branched completion. It again forces
pi1(Y)=1. Therefore Gamma has no such triple either.
The [general rank-bound proof](GENERAL_BRANCHED_COVER_RANK_BOUNDS.tex)
supplies the argument for arbitrary meridian cycle types; the proof
above spells out this special case.

**Why every deck inversion pair is excluded.** Let Pi=pi1(Sigma_2(K))
and fix the actual meridional involution t, so Gamma=Pi semidirect <t>.
The preceding surgery description and Brittenham--Wu give a
contractible universal cover of the three-manifold Sigma_2(K) and Z(Pi)=1.

Every order-two element s of Gamma is Pi-conjugate to t. To see this,
Gamma acts by lifts of the branched-cover involution on that
contractible universal cover. If s acted freely, its quotient would be
a three-dimensional K(C2,1), impossible because
H^n(C2;F2)=F2 for every n>=0 by the periodic resolution. Thus s fixes a
point. Its projection lies on the one connected branch circle. Lift a
path along that circle to the chosen branch basepoint. Uniqueness of
path lifting makes s fix this lifted path. A Pi deck transformation
takes its endpoint to the chosen point fixed by t; the conjugated s
and t are then lifts of the same involution fixing the same point,
hence equal.

Suppose alpha=Inn(g)*tau inverts a generating pair (u,v) of Pi.
Then alpha^2=id, so g*tau(g) is central and therefore is 1.
Consequently s=g*t satisfies s^2=1 and

    Gamma=<s,u*s,v*s>.

These are three nontrivial involutions, all conjugate to t, contrary
to the seven-sheet obstruction. Thus NO representative of the actual
deck outer class inverts ANY generating pair.

In particular, ordinary cover rank two and invariance of EVERY
two-dimensional word trace are not sufficient for a deck inversion.
This does not refute Boileau--Weidmann's inversion-realization question:
the required inversion does not exist. Their Lemma 6 and Corollary 8
also preserve the outer inversion class under Nielsen changes and
generation-preserving root replacements between invertible pairs.
[Boileau--Weidmann, pp.107--108](https://msp.org/gtm/2008/14/gtm-2008-14-007p.pdf).

Together with the rank-five certificate, this example has
\(\operatorname{mr}(K)=b(K)=5\), while its double branched cover has
ordinary group rank two. The seven-sheet argument independently
gives a lower bound of four for meridional-involution generation.
