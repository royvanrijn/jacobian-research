# Classical principal minors applied to root feedback

Reviewed 15 November 2026. [Proof](PROOF.md), [pins](sources.json). No priority claim.

[Joost Berson, Linearized polynomial maps over finite fields, arXiv1201.1137v2, Lemma5.7 and Remark5.8](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.1137v2) states the domain matrix criterion: vanishing of every nonempty principal minor gives permutation similarity to a strictly triangular matrix. The remark explicitly attributes that version to van den Essen, Corollary6.3.9. The shortest-cycle proof included here is a proof of that classical lemma, not a new matrix theorem. Berson's polynomial-map applications concern linearized/no-mixed-term maps; we do not identify those maps with our compiler maps.

Our deduction is the exact independent-diagonal formula for whole-source compiler maps with arbitrary corrections in k[u,sigma,X], followed by the differential identity identifying the matrix pattern with actual root dependencies. The full correction normal form and resulting polynomial block/orbit stages use that formula. This relationship is broader than native higher-profile coefficients and does not follow from ordinary nilpotence alone.

The retained [UAQTILT1 proof](../universal-keller-structure/PROOF.md) applies the published Cohen quadratic-tilt theorem to arbitrary h with h'(0)!=0. It proves each normalized stage atomic even when a root-dependent correction changes the cubic coefficient. This requires the stated polynomial landing and independent target coefficients; no generic-monodromy inference is made from finite controls.

The [higher-return audit](../universal-keller-higher-return/LITERATURE_AND_GAPS.md) reviews the recent Wang-Yan nonlinear nilpotent family and its explicit time inverse. A nilpotent matrix with nonzero diagonal entries can survive a common scalar perturbation but fail our identity with independent r_i. Neither the recent classification claims nor a hypothetical JC(r) inverse theorem is needed here.
