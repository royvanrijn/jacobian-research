## 2. A finite degree3/4 cover controls the central normalization

Take the reduced components over C in N that meet the source, normalize
them, and factor their maps through C^nu=A1. This is a finite cover of
degree d0. Its smooth projective completion Z is a possibly disconnected
cover of P1. Its components are exactly the projective normalizations
of the irreducible factors of f0. Denote their number by c, total genus
by G, and number of points above the infinity of C^nu by n0.

There are exactly24 finite branch values from the two normalization
preimages of each C node, each with one transposition, and b additional
branch values, each with one e-cycle, from C intersect B. There are no
other finite branch values. These are branch values on C^nu, not the
parameter values of the nonlinear h-pencil.

To see the node assertion, use the disjoint cyclic local factors of
PF2OMIT6--7. At a target C node, the two C meridians are disjoint
transpositions. The finite normal surface consists locally of two
degree2 charts and two degree1 charts. In a degree2 chart the map is
(s,t)->(s^2,t), with the other C branch pulling back to t0. Restricting
to that other branch gives a degree2 ramification point, lying on the
omitted R. The other degree2 chart gives the corresponding point over
the other normalization preimage. Each residual cover therefore has
one transposition at each of the24 preimages.

For H=C the generic omitted D sheet is excluded from d0. At the node
its two distinct normalization points occupy the two degree1 charts.
In the residual cover over each C preimage, the D point belonging to
the other branch is an additional, unramified omitted point. This does
not change the transposition. In particular a trivial-inertia omission
must not be erased from the puncture count.

At C intersect B, the disjoint C2 and Be cyclic factors leave exactly
the stated residual e-cycle. If H=B, its additional omitted point is
one of the fixed sheets. If H=C, its whole vertical sheet has already
been excluded. Elsewhere the finite cover is etale. Passing to a
smooth point of B's normalization avoids all B singularities by hypothesis.

Riemann--Hurwitz, summed over all components, now gives

    2G-2c = 24-d0+(e-1)b-n0.                       (1)

No connectedness of this central residue cover is assumed.

## 5. The entire infinity bifurcation set

The chain rule and the invertible Keller differential identify every
affine critical germ of f with the corresponding critical germ of h.
Since h has finitely many critical points and F is quasi-finite, f has
isolated affine critical points. The Suzuki--Gavrilov identity therefore
applies:

    chi(f_c)-chi(f_gen)=mu_c(f)+lambda_c(f).        (6)

The named primary source is Gwozdziewicz--Ploski, Section4, printed
page124; its definition of lambda is the sum of Milnor jumps at infinity.
This geometric identity is an import, not a symbolic checker theorem.
Using(3)--(5) gives the exact forced infinity contribution

    lambda_0(f)=23v = 46 or69.                     (7)

There is also a complete description away from zero. Let h_B be h
on B's A1 normalization and put

    rho_c=sum_(h_B(t)=c)(local_degree_t(h_B)-1),
    m_c=w*sum_(y in Crit(h), h(y)=c) n_B(y)*mu_y(h),

where n_B(y) is the number of B-normalization branches at y. For c!=0,
the vertical C and any H=C primes do not occur. Euler integration and
the fact that h has no infinity bifurcations give

    chi(f_c)-chi(f_gen)=6*mu_c(h)+w*rho_c,
    mu_c(f)=6*mu_c(h)-m_c,
    lambda_c(f)=w*rho_c+m_c.                       (8)

The correction m_c is nonnegative. If it is nonzero, a point of B maps
to a critical point of h, so h_B has zero derivative there. Thus rho_c
is already positive. Conversely rho_c>0 makes lambda_c(f)>0. All zeros
of h_B are simple, by transverse incidence, so rho_0=0. We obtain

    InfinityBif(f) = {0} union CriticalValues(h_B), (9)
    sum_(c!=0)lambda_c(f)=w*(b-1)+sum_(c!=0)m_c.   (10)

In particular no such actual source pencil can have only one infinity
bifurcation value. Neither(7) nor(9) is an exclusion of a Keller map
without an independent incompatible source-boundary constraint.
