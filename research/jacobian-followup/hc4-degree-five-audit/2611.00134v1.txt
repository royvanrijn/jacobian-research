     The four-dimensional Hessian conjecture for quintic potentials
                                            with a ternary cone top

                                                 David Niedbala Giraudin∗

                                                        5 November 2026




                                                              Abstract
            The Hessian conjecture HCn  asserts that iff∈C[x              1, . . . , xn] has det Hess(f)∈C             ×, then
        ∇fis a polynomial automorphism.  It is known forn≤3, false forn≥5, and open forn= 4,
        where  it  implies  the  plane  Jacobian  conjecture.   Z.  Ni  proved  HC4  for  potentials  of  degree
        at most four.  We prove HC4  for every potential of degree five whose top homogeneous form
        depends on exactly three linear forms.  The proof combines a weighted analysis of the Hessian
        determinant with exact computer-algebra certificates (Gr¨obner bases and Rabinowitsch tests
        overQ); one branch reduces to plane Jacobian pairs of degrees (4,3), which are excluded by
        Magnus’  theorem.   A  self-contained  verifier  is  supplied  as  an  ancillary  file.   The  binary  and
        unary cone tops in degree five remain open.

6    Certificates

The  ancillary  fileverify            hc4    ternary.py(Python  withpython-flint, sympy,  and  Singular
through Sage/passagemath) performs 29 checks, labelled S1a–S8e as cited above, and printsALL
CHECKS  PASSED; it runs in a few minutes.  Identities are verified with fully symbolic coefficients
(or with undetermined functions where stated).  Exclusions use the Rabinowitsch criterion overQ;
since a Gr¨obner basis equal to{1}overQis also{1}overC, the conclusions hold overC.  The
classification of S4 (III.1) is certified twice,  by minimal associated primes and by Rabinowitsch
tests (S8a).

Scope. Theorem 1 does not treat quintic potentials whose top form depends on two linear forms
or on one; these cases, and hence HC4 in degree five, remain open.
