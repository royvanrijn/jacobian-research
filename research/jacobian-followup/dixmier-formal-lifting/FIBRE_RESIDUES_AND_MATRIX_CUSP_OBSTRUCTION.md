# Fibre residues and the cusp obstruction for matrix quantum lifts

15 November 2026. **QFR1.** A reducing scalar block in one classical
matrix fiber gives a degree-independent dual test for the first
quantum relation. Applied to QRA1's monic elliptic matrices in the
physical Poisson plane, it gives a nonzero residue at u=v=0.

No polynomial-coefficient ordinary formal Weyl pair can deform those
matrices, even after changing the exact primitive relation. The
obstruction module generated by this defect is exactly k[u,v]/(u,v).
All polynomial first corrections also have zero quadratic residue,
which excludes quantum parameters of order two. Finite copies and
identity spectators preserve both exclusions.

For arbitrary parameter ramification, the residue still excludes
models whose reducing fiber block persists through the sub-quantum
orders. Persistence is a separate condition, not automatic at this
resonant cusp. These results do not obstruct the ordinary rank-one
forward Long graph, whose unrestricted formal quantization exists.

## A reducing fiber block tests all first corrections

Let S be a polynomial symplectic algebra over a characteristic-zero
field. In a chosen PBW ordering write the matrix product as
\[
 A\star B=AB+h\mu_1(A,B)+O(h^2).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Suppose F_i^0 in Mat_r(S) commute. A lift
F_i=F_i^0+hA_i+... satisfying [F_i,F_j]_star=hJ_ij I must solve
\[
 d_1(A)_{ij}=[A_i,F_j^0]+[F_i^0,A_j]
       =C_{ij},\qquad
 C_{ij}=J_{ij}I-\mu_1(F_i^0,F_j^0)+\mu_1(F_j^0,F_i^0).
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
Every A_i is an arbitrary polynomial matrix. No degree or differential
order bound has been imposed.

At a point z suppose an idempotent P is a common reducing block, with
P F_i^0(z)=F_i^0(z)P=lambda_i P for the relevant generators. Then
\[
          \Lambda_{P,z}(H)=\operatorname{tr}(P H(z)P)
          \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
          \Lambda_{P,z}(d_1(A)_{ij})=0.                    \tag{3}
\]
Compression kills the two commutators in (2) because their zeroth
operators act scalarly on that block. Thus the necessary condition is
\[
                         \Lambda_{P,z}(C_{ij})=0.          \tag{4}
\]
This is an exact obstruction class, rather than a test of a selected
correction box. The matrix trace can be replaced by the compressed
matrix itself when that is useful.

## The physical elliptic pair has a cusp residue

Use the monic elliptic model of
[QRA1](FINITE_MATRIX_ACTIONS_AND_PRIMITIVE_ADMISSION.md), but now
quantize its physical plane: the target has [v,u]=27h and the desired
source relation is [Y,X]=hI. This is not the surface cotangent action
where u,v, and x,y, are commuting position pairs.

Its prescribed special matrices are
\[
 X_0=\begin{pmatrix}
 -u/3&-v/3&0\\0&2u/3&-v/3\\4/3&0&2u/3
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Y_0=\begin{pmatrix}
 -v/3&0&-uv/6\\2u/3&-v/3&u^2/2\\0&2u/3&-v/3
 \end{pmatrix}.                                          \tag{5}
\]
They commute and describe x,y on the finite monic normalization with
w=y/x. Write E_ij for matrix units indexed by 0,1,2, and put
L(A,B)=[B,X_0]+[Y_0,A]. The first-order defect is
D=I-27((Y_0)_v(X_0)_u-(X_0)_v(Y_0)_u). At the cusp,
\[
 X_0(0)=\tfrac43E_{20},\qquad Y_0(0)=0,\qquad
                        D(0)=\operatorname{diag}(-8,1,7).
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
The middle block P=E_11 is a reducing scalar block. Hence
\[
 \Lambda(H)=H_{11}(0,0),\qquad
                  \Lambda(L(A,B))=0,\quad \Lambda(D)=1.    \tag{7}
\]
All polynomial first corrections are excluded. Consequently no full
ordinary formal physical Weyl pair with reduction (5) exists.
This excludes a finite bounded realization as well, but assumes no
common spatial bound and no exact relation Y=XW.

## The generated obstruction module has length one

There are exact polynomial identities
\[
 \begin{split}
 uD&=L(A_u,0),&
 A_u&=12E_{01}+\tfrac{21}{2}E_{12},                       \tag{8}\\
 vD&=L(A_v,B_v),&
 A_v&=-\tfrac92E_{00},&
 B_v&=-\tfrac{21v}{4}E_{02}-3E_{10}.                     \tag{9}
 \end{split}
\]
These identities hold in Mat_3(k[u,v]), not only at the cusp.
Let [D] denote the defect in coker(L). If f[D]=0, (7) gives f(0)=0.
Conversely (8)--(9) kill every element of (u,v). Therefore
\[
       \operatorname{Ann}_{k[u,v]}[D]=(u,v),\qquad
                   k[u,v][D]\simeq k[u,v]/(u,v).         \tag{10}
\]
The defect disappears after inverting u or v; the identities provide
explicit first-order corrections there. Inverting b=(u^3-v^2)/27
also kills it. These are localized first-order solutions, not
all-orders or globally finite quantum actions.

This distinguishes a punctual admission obstruction from a generic
field obstruction. A test over k(u,v), or on smooth generic energy
fibers, necessarily misses this class.

Changing from normal order to symmetric order changes the defect by
L(-(27/2)*(X_0)_(uv),-(27/2)*(Y_0)_(uv)). The two defects therefore
represent the same class and have the same period. The cusp obstruction
is not an artifact of the chosen PBW ordering.

## The residue is strong against every quadratic lower correction

Let the common quantum parameter be a(t)=t^2 c(t), with c(0) nonzero.
Allow arbitrary polynomial matrices in
X=X_0+tA_1+t^2A_2+..., Y=Y_0+tB_1+t^2B_2+....
The first two equations are
\[
 L(A_1,B_1)=0,\qquad
 L(A_2,B_2)+[B_1,A_1]=c(0)D.                              \tag{11}
\]
The second equation has no solution because every polynomial cocycle
in the first equation satisfies Lambda([B_1,A_1])=0. Here is a
complete weight proof of that universal assertion.

Assign weights u=2,v=3 and use basis weights 0,1,2. A matrix monomial
u^a v^b E_ij has operator weight 2a+3b+i-j. Then
\[
 \deg_wX_0=2,\quad\deg_wY_0=3,\qquad
 (A,B)\text{ of deformation weight }d:
              \deg_w A=2+d,\quad\deg_w B=3+d.              \tag{12}
\]
The cocycle equation is homogeneous. A has no weights below d=-4;
B has none below d=-5. Lambda of a quadratic term pairs only
d_1+d_2=-5. Positive d would need a nonexistent partner below -5.
The only relevant finite coefficient systems have
\[
 \begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 d&-5&-4&-3&-2\\ \hline
 \text{number of coefficients}&1&3&6&9\\
 \text{rank of cocycle equations}&1&3&4&5\\
 \text{kernel dimension}&0&0&2&4 .
 \end{array}                                             \tag{13}
\]
These are exhaustive homogeneous systems, not degree cutoffs:
the equation 2a+3b=degree+j-i lists every possible monomial.

For d=-3 a full kernel basis is
(2(E_01+E_12),uE_02) and (0,I).
For d=-2 it is
(I,0),
(2E_00+2E_11+(3u/2)E_02,uE_01),
(-2E_00,uE_12),
((3/2)E_00,-(v/4)E_02+E_10+E_21).
Coefficient comparison gives these bases and the ranks in (13);
the exact checker also records nonzero minors of the original systems.

At the cusp, every d=-3 cocycle has A proportional to E_01+E_12
and B scalar. Every d=-2 cocycle has diagonal A and B proportional
to E_10+E_21. Their symmetrized quadratic residue is zero: the
two contributions to the middle diagonal entry cancel. All other
weight pairings have a zero kernel or zero residue. Hence
\[
              L(A,B)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                               \Lambda([B,A])=0.         \tag{14}
\]
Applying Lambda to (11) gives 0=c(0), a contradiction. Quantum
parameters of order one or two are therefore excluded, with
arbitrary scalar units and all polynomial correction degrees.

The same argument permits spatial formal power series regular at the
cusp: every relevant negative-weight component is still finite, and
no positive component can contribute to the residue. Poles which
remove the cusp change this coefficient category.

The argument also works for k finite copies of the monic module.
The kernel systems then have Mat_k coefficients. Taking trace in
that factor makes the coefficient products cyclic, so the same
symmetrized cancellation proves a zero quadratic period. The
right-hand period is k*c(0), nonzero in characteristic zero.
Adding identity spectator Weyl variables gives coefficient rings
over which the same finite rational systems and cancellation hold.

## A persistent block obstructs every ramification order

For a general a(t)=t^e c(t), e>=1, evaluate coefficient matrices at
the target cusp using ordinary matrix multiplication; denote them
by Xhat(t),Yhat(t). Below order e the target is commutative.
Suppose the sub-quantum fiber representation has an idempotent
\[
 P(t)^2=P(t),\quad P(0)=E_{11},\qquad
 [P(t),Xhat(t)]=[P(t),Yhat(t)]=0\pmod {t^e}.               \tag{15}
\]
This is a condition on the whole lower jet, not just on P(0).

Lift the idempotent and a basis of its image to K[[t]].
An ordinary scalar-parameter matrix G(t), with G(0)=I, makes it
constant. Conjugation by G has no spatial derivative terms.
The off-block entries of both evaluated matrices then vanish
modulo t^e. Trace of the compressed ordinary commutator is zero
through order e: the internal block commutator has trace zero,
and the off-block products are divisible by t^(2e). Thus
\[
 [t^e]\operatorname{tr}
        \big(P(0)[Yhat(t),Xhat(t)]P(0)\big)=0.             \tag{16}
\]
The first star correction at this order uses only X_0,Y_0.
The desired Weyl equation instead makes (16) equal to
c(0)*Lambda(D)=c(0), a contradiction. Higher star terms start
at order 2e. Finite copies replace this period by k*c(0).

So every ramified model with a persistent middle block is excluded.
For e>=3 the block can in principle mix with the other nilpotent
piece before quantum order. It has the same source character there;
ordinary idempotent lifting does not make it commute with the
deformed pair. No automatic persistence or all-ramification exclusion
is asserted. The e=2 result above does not assume persistence.

This identifies the structural remaining escape. It does not propose
a succession of larger finite-jet or degree searches.

## Canonical transport and module models

The ordinary obstruction persists after adjoining arbitrary classical
direct summands: the old middle block still compresses every first
correction to zero and retains period one. The order-two result is
proved above for finite copies of the same monic module, not for an
arbitrary added summand.

Both exclusions survive polynomial matrix changes of special frame.
Such a frame has an inverse modulo the parameter and lifts to an
invertible matrix in the formal star completion. A lift in the
changed frame would pull back to one excluded above. The same
argument applies to actual invertible quantum source and target
canonical changes, including ones mixing identity spectators.
Polynomial symplectic automorphisms have formal quantum
automorphism lifts by QFL1, so no spatial bound on a transport
representative is required for this formal nonlifting statement.

The argument also excludes an arbitrary parameter-flat algebraic
module with this identified special module, rather than just a
chosen free matrix model. Lifts of a special free left basis give
compatible free frames in every finite parameter quotient, as in
QMC1. Its full right action would therefore give the forbidden
formal matrices. A different special module remains a different
admission problem.

The positive QRA1 surface and cotangent actions use commuting u,v with
separate conjugates. They have a different target Poisson structure
and remain valid; they are not physical-plane lifts of (5).

These statements do not obstruct the ordinary forward Long graph:
for a scalar special symplectic frame psi_i I, (2) has zero
compressed Poisson defect, consistently with QFL1. The monic
inverse-isogeny module in (5) is not that graph.

## Relation to recent quantization results

The normalization map to the classical physical correspondence is
(x,w) -> (u=4w^2-3x,v=8w^3-9xw,x,y=xw). Its differential has rank
one at x=w=0, instead of the generic rank two. The smooth
Lagrangian hypothesis in
[Baranovsky--Ginzburg--Kaledin--Pecharich](https://arxiv.org/html/1403.3493v2)
therefore does not justify quantizing this normalization module.

[Lauritzen--Thomsen](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16636v1) supplies
related first-order Hochschild obstruction methodology; no
identification with their particular endomorphism class is assumed.
[Baranovsky--Huey](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12910v1) treats
extensions of quantized vector bundles and of module morphisms.
The free left bundle here already quantizes. Lifting its endomorphisms
individually does not impose the simultaneous Weyl relation, which
is the obstructed condition in (7).
