# Stable obstruction to completing the uncorrected cubic pair

14 November 2026. **QCC1.** Long's two cubic polynomials
\[
 R=x(2-3xq),\qquad
 D_0=\frac{1+3xq}{2}p-3q^2z
\]
satisfy {D_0,R}=1, but cannot be part of a full polynomial symplectic
endomorphism, in any polynomial degree. In fact D_0 alone cannot
occur as a component of such a map. Both obstructions persist after
adding arbitrarily many spectator canonical pairs.

Their joint polynomial Poisson centralizer is exactly the toric
surface K[U,V,W]/(UW-V^3), with a Poisson character at its singular
point. A normal-ordered quantum version has an independent stable
noncompletion proof from a finite-dimensional polynomial eigenspace.

The natural polynomial shear removing Long's Hamiltonian correction
is canonical only when its shift is a polynomial in R. Long's actual
correction is outside that class. These results exclude this specific
compression route; they do not exclude other cubic representatives or
a bounded quantization of the corrected Long map.

## The cubic pair and its polynomial coordinates

Use {p,x}={z,q}=1 over a characteristic-zero field K and set
\[
 a=1-3xq,\qquad B=3x^2p+2az,\qquad
 c=(1+3xq)/2.
\]
The formulas are the uncorrected fourth coordinate and first component
in [Long, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23777v1).
Direct calculation gives
\[
                \{D_0,R\}=1,\qquad \{R,x\}=\{R,q\}=\{R,B\}=0.
 \tag{1}
\]
The matrix taking (p,z) to (D_0,B) has determinant
\[
                 2ac+9x^2q^2=1.
\]
Its polynomial inverse is
\[
                     p=2aD_0+3q^2B,\qquad
                     z=-3x^2D_0+cB.                    \tag{2}
\]
Thus (x,q,D_0,B) is a polynomial coordinate system. Since {R,D_0}=-1,
\[
                  \operatorname{Cent}_{\{,\}}(R)=K[x,q,B].
 \tag{3}
\]

On this ring delta={D_0,-} satisfies
\[
 \delta x=c,\quad \delta q=-3q^2,\quad
 \delta a=-\frac32qa,\quad \delta B=\frac92qB.           \tag{4}
\]

## The joint centralizer is an explicit singular surface

Localizing at q gives K[a,q,q^{-1},B], where x=(1-a)/(3q).
Equation (4) becomes
\[
       \delta=\frac{3q}{2}
                  (-a\partial_a-2q\partial_q+3B\partial_B).
\]
Hence its invariant Laurent monomials are a^i q^j B^k with
\[
                     i,k\ge0,\quad j\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
                                  i+2j=3k.             \tag{5}
\]
A finite invariant Laurent sum that is polynomial in (x,q,B) cannot
contain a negative q exponent. To see this, take its smallest such
exponent j. After substituting a=1-3xq, the coefficient of q^j at
q=0 is a nonzero polynomial in B: for a fixed (j,k), (5) determines
i uniquely. Terms with larger j cannot cancel it. Thus j>=0.

The nonnegative solutions of (5) are generated by
\[
                 (3,0,1),\quad(1,1,1),\quad(0,3,2).
\]
Indeed for j<=k use powers of the first two vectors; for j>k use
the second and third, with exponents 3k-2j and j-k.
Consequently the entire joint centralizer is
\[
 \begin{split}
 C&=K[U,V,W]\subset K[x,q,p,z],\\
 U&=a^3B,\qquad V=aqB,\qquad W=q^3B^2,\qquad UW=V^3.
 \end{split}                                           \tag{6}
\]
The exponent vectors span a rank-two lattice, and UW-V^3 is
irreducible, so it generates the relation ideal. There are no
additional generators or relations in higher degree.

The induced brackets are
\[
                    \{U,V\}=2U,\qquad
                    \{V,W\}=2W,\qquad
                    \{U,W\}=6V^2.                     \tag{7}
\]
All brackets vanish under the augmentation
\[
                         \chi:C\to K,\quad U,V,W\mapsto0.
\]
Thus chi is a Poisson character. In particular no elements S,T in
C can satisfy {S,T}=1, without any degree restriction.

With n-2 spectator canonical pairs, the joint centralizer is
C tensor the polynomial Poisson algebra on those spectators.
Applying chi gives a Poisson map to the spectator algebra, whose
Poisson tensor has rank 2(n-2). A completion of (R,D_0) would require
n-1 canonical pairs in this centralizer. Their images under chi
would have bracket matrix of rank 2(n-1), impossible in rank
2(n-2). This proves stable polynomial noncompletion.

The statement is global and polynomial. Allowing arbitrary spatial
formal series or rational functions changes the centralizer and is
outside this obstruction.

## The uncorrected component alone is excluded

The argument has a stronger form that does not fix R. Let
\[
                 \mathcal V=c\partial_x-3q^2\partial_q.
\]
Its polynomial invariant ring on K[x,q] is K. Indeed the localized
weighted-Euler calculation above, with no B variable, leaves
i+2j=0; polynomiality forces i=j=0.

Since D_0 is linear in momenta, its Hamiltonian derivation preserves
momentum degree. For every polynomial P with {D_0,P}=0, its
zero-momentum term P(x,q,0,0) is therefore a polynomial invariant
of the base vector field and hence a scalar. Zero-momentum evaluation defines
a character of the entire Poisson centralizer of D_0. It is Poisson:
the zero-momentum term of {P,Q} uses spatial derivatives of those
scalar terms, so it is zero.

After adjoining spectators this character again maps the centralizer
to the spectator Poisson algebra. The same rank argument excludes
n-1 canonical pairs in it. Hence D_0 cannot be any one component
of a full n-pair polynomial symplectic map, for any n>=2.

More generally, a cotangent Hamiltonian linear in k momenta whose
base vector field has only scalar polynomial invariants has such a
Poisson character. With n-k spectators its centralizer cannot contain
more than n-k canonical pairs. This gives the same component
obstruction whenever k>=2.

## Which polynomial fiber shears are actually canonical

For any H in K[x,q,B], the derivation H{R,-} is locally nilpotent:
{R,-}=-partial_(D_0) in the coordinates (2), and H is invariant.
Its exponential is the polynomial automorphism
\[
 \sigma_H(x)=x,\quad \sigma_H(q)=q,\quad
 \sigma_H(p)=p-2aH,\quad
 \sigma_H(z)=z+3x^2H.                                  \tag{8}
\]
It fixes R and B and sends D_0 to D_0-H. Its inverse is sigma_(-H).

This polynomial automorphism is Poisson if and only if
\[
                                  H\in K[R].           \tag{9}
\]
First, preservation of {p,x}=1 in (8) forces
\[
                          6ax^2 H_B=0,
\]
so H is independent of B. The remaining momentum-shift bracket
then requires
\[
                       3x^2H_x+2aH_q=0.               \tag{10}
\]
Over K(x,q), use coordinates (R,x). The derivation in (10)
annihilates R and sends x to 3x^2, so its rational constants are
K(R). A polynomial H in K(x,q) belonging to K(R) lies in K[R]:
restricting q=0 gives H(x,0)=F(2x), so a reduced denominator
of F could not remain. This proves necessity. Conversely H=F(R)
makes H{R,-} a Hamiltonian derivation with a polynomial primitive
of F, proving sufficiency.

Long's actual D is D_0+H_Long with H_Long in K[x,q,B].
The source formula has, at x=0,
\[
       H_{\rm Long}=-\frac13B^2+\frac92q^2B-\frac{29}{4}q^4.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus its B derivative is nonzero. The ordinary polynomial
automorphism sigma_(H_Long) removes the correction from D,
but is not Poisson.

More generally no polynomial symplectic automorphism can send
the corrected Long D to D_0, even when R may change: the full
corrected map would be transported to a map with the excluded
component D_0. The same conclusion holds after stabilization.
Other cubic representatives must change D genuinely rather than
remove its correction by such a canonical normalization.

## A finite-eigenspace obstruction for quantum centralizers

Here is a general criterion independent of this example. Let P be
in A_k(K), and suppose its action on the polynomial representation
has a nonzero finite-dimensional eigenspace E_lambda of dimension d.
Its centralizer acts on E_lambda, giving a unital homomorphism
\[
                     \operatorname{Cent}_{A_k}(P)
                                  \longrightarrow\operatorname{Mat}_d(K).
 \tag{12}
\]
After adjoining n-k spectator pairs, expand in their PBW basis.
Every coefficient of an element commuting with P still commutes
with P. Thus (12) gives
\[
      \operatorname{Cent}_{A_n}(P)
                       \longrightarrow
                         \operatorname{Mat}_d(A_{n-k}(K)).
 \tag{13}
\]
This centralizer cannot contain a Weyl algebra A_r for r>n-k.
A unital map from A_r is injective by simplicity. But images of
its finitely many generators have some finite spatial bound D,
so degree-m source monomials map into degree-Dm target matrices.
Their dimensions grow as m^(2r) and m^(2(n-k)), respectively,
which precludes injectivity. This is the elementary GK-dimension
comparison; no uniform bound on D is required.

## Normal-ordered cubic pairs that never admit a full Weyl completion

In the parameter Weyl algebra with [p,x]=[z,q]=h, h nonzero, use
the displayed normal PBW ordering and define, for each integer m>=0,
\[
                 D_m=cp-3q^2z+\frac{3m}{2}h q.
 \tag{14}
\]
Then [D_m,R]=h exactly. On K[x,q], after p=h partial_x and
z=h partial_q, D_m acts as h(V+3mq/2), where
\[
                            V=c\partial_x-3q^2\partial_q.
\]
Its zero eigenspace is exactly
\[
 E_m=\operatorname{span}_K
       \{a^{m-2j}q^j:0\le j\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\},
 \qquad \dim E_m=\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1.                  \tag{15}
\]
Indeed localization at q makes V the two-variable weighted Euler
operator in (4). The equation selects weights i+2j=m, and the
same smallest-q-exponent argument excludes negative exponents.
This proves both exhaustion and finiteness for every m.

For n>=2, (13) with k=2 excludes n-1 Weyl pairs in the
centralizer of D_m, even allowing arbitrary dependence on the
spectators. Therefore D_m cannot occur as any one component of a full
n-pair Weyl endomorphism in any degree. In particular the exact
cubic pair (R,D_m) cannot be completed.
The m=0 instance is the uncorrected normal-ordered cubic pair.

The ordering restriction is explicit. The symmetric Weyl
quantization of the classical D_0 equals
\[
                           cp-3q^2z-\frac94h q,
\]
because div(V)=-9q/2. It is not one of (14). The finite-eigenspace
argument does not assert the same quantum centralizer conclusion
for every scalar correction or ordering. The classical
noncompletion theorem still concerns the fixed classical pair.

## Consequence for the bounded-lift program

for any full cubic symplectic tuple. Equations (6)--(15) show that
the obvious cubic pair inside Long's construction cannot be
extended to such a tuple, even with arbitrarily high degrees or
extra spectator pairs. Deleting the high-degree Hamiltonian
correction is an ordinary polynomial coordinate simplification
that fails to preserve the canonical relations.

the corrected component; changing R alone cannot remove this obstruction. Alternatively seek a full
bounded scalar-anomaly tuple, or the compatible algebraic model
