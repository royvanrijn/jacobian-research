# Centralizer index factorization and rational polarization

15 November 2026. **QCI1.** For any actual full bounded Long lift, adjoining
all rational commutants of its fixed position images still leaves a
division extension of completed degree three. Thus the ambient division
ring cannot be generated by that coefficient field and the conjugate
momenta. No ambient rational division automorphism, even with parameter
poles, can make those position images purely positional.

The generic indices factor as centralizer-field degree times a residual
division index. For Long the residual indices are at least three; a
nontrivial position-centralizer extension forces the whole indices to
be at least nine. These statements apply in rank-one projective orders
and after identity stabilization, without a scalar free frame.

An independent finite-lattice test works in every finite module rank:
bounded powers of a rational matrix and a full finite invariant Weyl
lattice force a scalar polynomial relation. Genuine rational residual
extensions of every degree exist, but a displayed family fails this test
in every positive rank, even after rational matrix frame changes.

No bounded Long lift is constructed or excluded in all categories.
The rational-polarization statement fixes the source position field.
An arbitrary mixed change of source positions and momenta is outside it.

## The division tower

Let L=K((h)), A=A_n(L) with [p_i,x_j]=h*delta_ij, and D=Frac A.
Let E be A or a rank-one finite projective Weyl endomorphism order
as in QPD1. Suppose an actual full tuple in E satisfies
\[
 [F_i,F_j]=[T_i,T_j]=0,\qquad [F_j,T_i]=h\delta_{ij}.       \tag{1}
\]
Write
\[
 C=L(F_1,\ldots,F_n),\quad W=L(F_1,\ldots,F_n,T_1,\ldots,T_n),
 \quad H=\operatorname{Cent}_D(F_1,\ldots,F_n).             \tag{2}
\]
W denotes the generated division ring, isomorphic to the source Weyl
division ring. Assume H is commutative. QPD1 identifies it with Frac B,
B=Cent_E(F), and makes H/C algebraic. Put
\[
                     U=D(H,T_1,\ldots,T_n)\subset D.       \tag{3}
\]
The generic left/right indices N_l=[D:W]_l and N_r=[D:W]_r are finite
for actual scalar bounded tuples by QDI1, and for the finite-left
projective models by QMC1's growth argument.

The division ring U depends only on the fixed positions and the existence
of individual rational conjugates. Any other such conjugates T_i'
differ from T_i by elements of H, so they generate the same U with H.
No flatness assumption on the alternative choice is needed for this
invariance. We use the given flat tuple for the index calculation.

## A finite coefficient extension contributes its field degree

For every finite intermediate field C subset J subset H, all derivations
nabla_i=-ad(T_i)/h preserve J. Differentiate a minimal polynomial for
alpha in J: separability expresses nabla_i(alpha) rationally in alpha
with coefficients in C. The derivations commute and lift partial_(Fi).

The generated ring J[T_1,...,T_n] is the corresponding differential
operator ring. Its PBW relations are faithful inside D: a putative
finite relation can be commuted with the F_i at a maximal multiorder
to isolate a nonzero coefficient times a factorial and a power of h.
There is therefore no unexpected operator relation.

The retained differential-field degree lemma in
[QDI1](QUANTUM_DIVISION_AND_COMPLETION.md) gives
\[
                  [D(J,T):W]_l=[D(J,T):W]_r=[J:C].        \tag{4}
\]
For completeness, a field basis of J/C is independent over W:
clear a common operator denominator and compare derivative PBW
coefficients. Its right W-span is finite-dimensional and preserved by
J and the T_i. Nonzero operators act injectively, hence surjectively,
so their inverses preserve the span as well. It is the generated
division ring. The left argument is identical.

Every finite [J:C] divides N_l and N_r and is bounded by them.
Choose such J with maximum degree. Adjoining any further element
of H cannot increase that maximum, so H=J. Thus H/C is finite without
assuming finite generation of B. Set d=[H:C]. Division-tower dimensions
now give
\[
        N_l=dM_l,\qquad N_r=dM_r,\qquad
                       M_l=[D:U]_l,\quad M_r=[D:U]_r.     \tag{5}
\]
Both residual indices are finite integers. No equality of left and
right indices is assumed or imported.

Since U and d are independent of the choice of full completion of
the fixed positions, so are N_l and N_r for every flat completion
inside the same ambient D. This does not compare different position
pairs or arbitrary source polarizations.

## Centralizer coefficients lie in the completed image

Now assume the actual integral algebra has a faithful formal rank-one
frame as in QSC1, the F_i are integral in that frame, and their reductions
have a prescribed full polynomial symplectic completion psi of classical
function-field degree delta. The independent position reductions f_i satisfy
\[
                 \operatorname{Cent}_{\{\,,\,\}}(f_1,\ldots,f_n)
                                          =K[f_1,\ldots,f_n]. \tag{6}
\]
Actual generic h poles in the momenta can first be removed by QET1
while preserving flatness and attaining the prescribed classical tuple.
That operation subtracts polynomials in F, so it changes neither W nor U.
Use the standard h-valued completion Dhat and let What be the closure
of W. QDI1, or the corresponding QMC1 formal rank-one frame, gives
\[
                         [\widehat D:\widehat W]_l
                          =[\widehat D:\widehat W]_r=\delta. \tag{7}
\]
QSC1's formal centralizer induction identifies every actual centralizer
element with a Laurent h-series of polynomials in F. QPD1 clears every
rational commutant into that actual centralizer. Consequently
\[
                  H\subset\overline{C}\subset\widehat W,
                                      \qquad\overline U=\widehat W. \tag{8}
\]
Here closure is h-adic, and overline C is the completed position field.
There is no claim that H equals C before completion. Fractions of the
formal centralizer expansions lie in K(t1,...,tn)((h)).
For a projective model, perform the calculation in its faithful formal
rank-one frame; simultaneous division conjugation transports (8) back.
An algebraic scalar free frame is not required.

Apply the generic-versus-completed dimension inequality of QDI1 to
D/U. By (8), completion of U is still What. Hence
\[
                       M_l,M_r\ge\delta,\qquad
                              N_l,N_r\ge\delta d.          \tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, for either side,
\[
                    N-\delta=\delta(d-1)+d(M-\delta).    \tag{10}
\]
This is an index identity and a dimension accounting, not a claimed
canonical splitting of vector spaces. The centralizer-field extension
is lost at the selected completion, while the residual extension still
has completed degree delta.

## Consequences for Long and its identity stabilizations

For Long's standard position images, QSC1 proves (6) and QDI1 proves
delta=3. This includes arbitrary commuting quantum deformations of both
positions, not only exact-R lifts. QPD1 supplies commutative rational
centralizer saturation and the projective field hypotheses.

QTB1 forbids nontrivial Galois factors. Thus
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 d=1 &: H=C,\quad B=L[F],\quad M_l,M_r\ge3,\\
 d>1 &: d\ge3,\quad H/C\text{ non-Galois},\quad N_l,N_r\ge9.
 \end{array}                                             \tag{11}
\]
In particular, either generic whole index below nine forces a
birational position-centralizer field. This is a restriction on an
already full lift; it does not construct one by prescribing its index.

After adding any number of identity pairs, the classical joint
centralizer is K[R,S,extra_positions]: commuting with those positions
removes their momenta, and flat scalar extension preserves QSC1's
remaining kernel. The full classical degree remains three.
The same argument therefore applies to an actual bounded lift of
the stabilized map, even with h-small mixed corrections.

These are centralizer-field and residual-division indices of one
specific tower. Their distinction remains essential; (5) supplies a
relation, not an identification of the indices.

## A coefficient-field model cannot account for the lift

If delta>1, (9) implies U is a proper sub-division-ring of D. Therefore
\[
                     D\ne D(H,T_1,\ldots,T_n).            \tag{12}
\]
An ordinary differential-field construction in which the ambient
division ring is generated by the position-centralizer coefficients
and their translating derivatives would have M_l=M_r=1.
Such a model cannot be an actual Long lift satisfying (6)--(7),
in any degrees or finite coefficient-field extension.

This excludes a whole construction mechanism. It does not identify
a curve-isogeny field with H merely because that field appears in a
candidate. QIM1 is a curve differential module, not an actual Weyl
realization of this forbidden form. Noncentral-energy bridges and
genuinely residual division extensions remain separate possibilities.

## No rational target polarization of the fixed positions

Suppose an L-algebra division automorphism sigma of D made all sigma(F_i)
belong to the standard rational position field L(x1,...,xn).
Their Jacobian with respect to x is generically invertible, because
the functions are algebraically independent in characteristic zero.

The full rational centralizer of these functions is exactly L(x).
To see this, use total differential order in D, with rational x
coefficients and rational homogeneous momentum symbols. Commutation
with all sigma(F_i) makes the vector of momentum derivatives of the
principal symbol zero, since the position Jacobian is invertible.
Thus that principal symbol is a rational x coefficient and the order
is zero. Subtract that coefficient. A remaining nonzero element has
negative order and the same commutation property, which would force
order zero again. Hence there is no remainder. This is the usual
scalar coordinate-field maximality mechanism; compare
[Bavula, Corollary3.10](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0401263).

Let g_i=sigma(F_i), J=(partial_(x_j)g_i), and B0=J^-1. The commuting
first-order inverse-Jacobian mates are
\[
                       T_i^0=-\sum_j (B0)_{ji}(x)p_j.     \tag{13}
\]
Each sigma(T_i)-T_i^0 centralizes all g_j, so is in L(x).
Conversely the invertible matrix B0 recovers every p_j from these
mates and coefficient functions. Thus
\[
                          \sigma(U)=D,\qquad U=D,        \tag{14}
\]
contradicting (12). Therefore no such rational target polarization
exists for Long or its identity stabilizations.

No h-regularity, spatial degree or pole bound is imposed on sigma:
the contradiction is algebraic in D after the original completed-index
argument. An arbitrary formal transformation need not act on D and
is outside the statement. A mixed source transformation that changes
the chosen position field is also outside it. Birational
reparametrization of the same position field does not change the
coefficient saturation mechanism.

## Controls that prevent overextension

The rescaled cotangent examples of QNI1 satisfy every full relation
and have identity classical reduction. Their indices are
\[
                            \delta=1,\quad d=3,\quad
                            M_l=M_r=1,\quad N_l=N_r=3.    \tag{15}
\]
They obey (9) with equality, and have a genuine positional model.
This proves that delta>1 cannot be omitted.

The unscaled cotangent of the retained Keller map instead has classical
degree three and is also a coefficient-field model. It does not satisfy
(6): the classical centralizer of its position images f_i(x) is
K[x], strictly larger than K[f]. Its coefficient field is not inside
the completed image. It is not a counterexample to (8)--(9).
Thus classical joint-centralizer rigidity is a real hypothesis, not
a consequence of noninvertibility or the full Weyl relations.

A commutative valued-field tower checks the index accounting:
\[
             u=z^3+z,\qquad t=u+h u^3,\qquad
               L(t)\subset L(u)\subset L(z).             \tag{16}
\]
Its generic degrees are 3,3 and9. The selected h-adic completion of
L(u)/L(t) has degree one, since t=u+h*u^3 has a unique formal inverse
near u=t. The residual completion has degree three. This gives
d=3,M=3,delta=3,N=9 and loss6. It is a valued-field control, not
a Weyl tuple or a Long construction.

For the positional recovery calculation, use g1=x^2+y,g2=y.
The rational mates -p_x/(2x), p_x/(2x)-p_y commute, and recover
p_x=-2x*T1 and p_y=-T1-T2. Its classical positional centralizer

## A finite carrier cannot support a transcendental matrix with bounded powers

There is an elementary finite-lattice test that does not require rank one,
projectivity, a formal frame or a scalar centralizer. Let A=A_n(L), let
D be its division ring, and give Mat_r(D) the maximum of the Bernstein
degrees of its entries, with deg(0)=-infinity. A full left A-lattice
M in D^(1 times r) means a finitely generated left A-submodule whose
left D-span is the whole row space. For every Q in Mat_r(D),
\[
 \begin{split}
 Q\text{ is algebraic over }L
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad&
 \sup_{k\ge0}\deg(Q^k)<\infty\\
 &\text{and some full finite left A-lattice satisfies }MQ\subset M .
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{17}
\]
Algebraic means one nonzero polynomial in L[t] vanishes on the matrix;
it does not mean that every entry is algebraic.

Here is a proof in every finite rank. Clear common right denominators
in each column of finitely many generators of M. For an invertible
diagonal G over D, this gives M'=MG subset A^(1 times r), still finite
and full, with Q'=G^-1 Q G preserving M'. Bounded powers are invariant
under every invertible rational matrix frame, since
\[
 \deg(G^{-1}Q^kG)\le\deg(G^{-1})+\deg(Q^k)+\deg(G),               \tag{18}
\]
and the converse follows with G^-1. For a finite generating row m_j
of M', all m_j*(Q')^k therefore lie in one finite-dimensional
L-vector space of polynomial rows. Their linear dependence supplies
a nonzero f_j in L[t] with m_j*f_j(Q')=0. The product of these
polynomials kills every generating row: the factors are central
polynomials in the same matrix, so their order can be chosen
separately for each row. It consequently kills the full left
D-span, giving f(Q')=0 and hence f(Q)=0.

Conversely, if a monic polynomial of degree d kills Q, the module
sum_(0<=j<d) A^(1 times r)*Q^j is finite, contains the standard
polynomial row module, and is stable under Q. Its powers also lie
in the finite L-span of 1,Q,...,Q^(d-1), proving both properties
on the right of (17).

An algebraic matrix Q cannot have any rational matrix mate T with
[Q,T]=h*I_r. For its monic minimal scalar polynomial f,
[f(Q),T]=h*f'(Q)=0 contradicts minimality in characteristic zero.
Thus a component of a full Weyl right action on any finite
torsion-free left module must have powers of unbounded Bernstein
degree in every rational matrix frame. This is a test for a specified
rational action, not an assertion that every matrix of nonpositive
degree is forbidden. A nilpotent rational matrix can be algebraic
and can preserve a finite lattice.

For example Q_0=((0,x^-1),(0,0)) has degree minus one, Q_0^2=0,
and preserves the free lattice generated by (x,0),(0,1).
It has no Weyl mate. This exception is essential in higher rank.
The proof uses finite-dimensional Bernstein pieces, so it does not
apply merely from bounded differential order with arbitrary rational
coefficient poles. The rank-one symbol constraints in
[QHI1](HAMILTONIAN_IDEALIZER_CRITERION.md) remain stronger in their
description of the entire graded idealizer.

## Genuine residual division extensions can fail every finite lattice test

For every integer m>=2, consider the actual rational Weyl pair
\[
 F_m={x^{1-m}p\over m},\qquad T_m=x^m,\qquad
 [F_m,T_m]=h,\qquad \deg F_m=2-m,\quad [p,x]=h.                 \tag{19}
\]
These are finite rational operators, not formal series. Write
W_m=D(F_m,T_m) subset D_1(L). With T_m as the position, the
coefficient extension L(x)/L(x^m) has degree m; the translating
derivative is F_m, and p=m*x^(m-1)*F_m. The differential-field
basis theorem (4) therefore gives both whole division indices m.

With F_m as the position, its full rational centralizer is L(F_m).
Indeed, in L(x)[p], leading-order commutation forces a commuting
order-k operator to have leading coefficient c*(x^(1-m)/m)^k,
with c in L. Subtract c*F_m^k and induct on order. The finite
operator centralizer is L[F_m]. Clearing a rational commutant
by a polynomial in F_m using QPD1's curve case, or
[Bavula, Lemma3.7](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0401263),
then gives the asserted rational centralizer. Consequently
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 \text{chosen position and mate}&d&M_l=M_r&N_l=N_r\\ \hline
 (F_m,T_m)&1&m&m\\
 (T_m,-F_m)&m&1&m .
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{20}
\]
For the second row, the rational centralizer of x^m is L(x), and
adjoining it and F_m recovers D_1. The same full source Weyl
algebra occurs in both rows. Thus centralizer degree versus
residual degree depends on the position field, while the whole
index does not. Mixed source changes really can redistribute the
two factors; the fixed-position qualification above is substantive.

The classical rational field extension of this pair also has
degree m: adjoining x with x^m=T_m recovers p=m*x^(m-1)*F_m.
The basis 1,x,...,x^(m-1) is independent over the image residue
field. It remains independent over the completed quantum image,
while the generic index m is an upper bound on the completed
index by QDI1's residue-basis argument. Hence the completed index
is exactly m as well. In particular m=3 genuinely realizes a
residual degree-three division extension; such extensions are
not algebraically impossible.

Nevertheless no full finite left A_n(L)-lattice of any finite
positive rank can carry the right action obtained by diagonal
repetition of this pair, even after a rational matrix frame
change. Powers of F_m*I_r have degree k*(2-m), uniformly bounded
above for all k>=0. Equation (17) would make this matrix algebraic,
contradicting its rational mate T_m*I_r. This already excludes
stability under the one operator F_m*I_r. For m=2 the obstruction
uses transcendence despite degree zero; for m>=3 it uses the
negative degree. No bound on lattice generators or mate orders
is imposed.

The exclusion persists after any number of identity pairs,
since the same Bernstein power bound holds in the larger Weyl
division ring. It also persists under arbitrary polynomial source
Weyl automorphisms: a stable lattice for the transformed full
generators would be stable under their whole generated algebra,
which contains F_m. Polynomial target Weyl automorphisms preserve
the exclusion by pulling a proposed finite lattice back
entrywise. Both automorphisms and their inverses must act on
the actual Weyl algebra. Arbitrary rational target changes do
not meet this last requirement.

These rational models are not Long lifts, and QIM1's elliptic
curve action is not identified with their diagonal action.
A different rational embedding or genuinely matrix-valued action
can have unbounded powers and is not excluded by (17).
The family separates the existence of the required residual
division degree from the existence of a finite carrier, even
allowing arbitrary finite module rank and algebraic frame changes.

the exact valued-field tower and formal inverse prefix, field-degree

its full first-order relations and a positive delta1 shear. It also

the genuine rational Weyl relations and source role reversal, and a
negative-degree nilpotent matrix with an actual finite free lattice.
The finite controls do not construct a Long lift or prove universal
degree exhaustion. Index finiteness, differential PBW independence,
the finite centralizer field, tower factorization, completed
containment, residual lower bound, fixed-position rational polarization
and all identity stabilizations use written proofs and the retained
formal/index theorems. The arbitrary-rank finite-lattice test (17),
its frame covariance and the all-m rational-family exclusion are written
proofs; sampled powers do not prove these universal assertions.
No new external equality of generic left/right indices is used.

A new actual carrier must retain a residual division extension of
degree at least three after all rational position commutants have
been adjoined. Such residual extensions do exist rationally, as (19)
shows; their finite-lattice admission is a further substantive gate.
Coefficient covers and rational target charts cannot supply that missing
extension for the fixed Long positions. Other quantum position fields,
mixed source polarizations and higher-rank matrix models remain
open. No actual finite Long tuple, scalar or matrix counterexample,
all-representative nonlifting, DC2/JC2 resolution, independent theorem
replay, formal verification, external review or novelty priority
is asserted.
