# Nilradical trace restriction and cyclic quotient descent

PF2RULED117, 16 November 2026. The local surface-to-curve trace restriction
left open in PF116 is resolved, including the actual cyclic quotient source.
The argument retains the nonreduced pullback and every branch gluing
condition. It is an automatic identity for these finite covers, so it does
not exclude the sharp PF116 examples or close the whole-plane finite-algebra
problem. The full research goal remains ACTIVE.

## 1. The smooth-source theorem

Let R=C{u,v}, S=C{x,y}, and let g define a finite analytic map R->S.
Let f be reduced and contain ALL branch values. Write

    F=f(g)=a b,    b=F_red,    a=F/b,
    A=R/(f),      T=S/(F),    B=S/(b),    I=(b)/(F).

The letters A and B here denote curve RINGS. They need not be normal or
irreducible. All their fraction rings below are total fraction rings.
For every component B_j over a component of A, let r_j be the generic
TRANSVERSE ramification index of the surface map. Let D_r be multiplication
by the tuple (r_j) in Q(B). This differs from the generic degree of the
map on the reduced curve component.

There is a canonical inclusion, induced by T->B,

    Hom_A(B,A) -> Hom_A(T,A).

Its image is exactly the nilradical annihilator. More precisely,

    Hom_A(T,A)=T c,
    Hom_A(B,A)=Ann_I(T c)=a T c=B Tr_T.                 (1)

Here c is a free dual-module generator chosen so that Tr_T=J c, where
J=Jac(g) and J/a is a unit. The last expression uses the ordinary finite
free algebra trace of the NONREDUCED algebra T/A, factored through B.
The map B->Hom_A(B,A) sending z to z Tr_T is an isomorphism.

Using the ordinary, unweighted, generic trace of Q(B)/Q(A) to embed the
dual as a fractional ideal gives

    B^vee_A := {z in Q(B): Tr_(Q(B)/Q(A))(z B) subset A}
             = D_r B.                                 (2)

In particular, the trace in (1) is the RAMIFICATION-WEIGHTED trace

    Tr_r(z)=sum_j r_j Tr_(K(B_j)/K(A_i))(z_j),         (3)

with the sum taken over components above A_i. Equation (2) concerns the
actual glued ring B, not a product of normalization rings.

Proof. S is finite free over R: the target parameters are a system of
parameters in the regular Cohen--Macaulay source, so miracle flatness
applies. Finite complete-intersection duality gives

    Hom_R(S,R)=S c,       Tr_(S/R)=J c.               (4)

Equivalently, BEFORE quotienting, this dual is J^(-1)S under the generic
trace pairing. The algebraic inputs are Stacks Lemmas49.12.4 and49.12.6
(tag0BWB). For analytic germs the same calculation uses the regular
sequence P(x,y)-u,Q(x,y)-v and its Jacobian in the finite analytic
complete intersection. Alternatively complete both local rings: the
Koszul duality and determinant proof works for power series, finite
modules commute with completion, and faithful flatness descends the
identities. No claim that a power-series algebra is a finite-presentation
polynomial algebra is needed.

At each generic preimage divisor the characteristic-zero different has
order r_j-1, while F has order r_j. Because f contains ALL branch values,
there is no other Jacobian divisor. Unique factorization in S gives
J=epsilon a for an analytic unit epsilon. This step would fail if a
branch-value component were omitted from f.

Base change of the dual finite free R-module gives

    Hom_R(S,R) tensor_R A = Hom_A(T,A).

This is arbitrary base change (Stacks Lemma49.2.10, tag0BVF); A need not
be smooth or reduced for this base-change step, and T remains nonreduced.
Ordinary matrix trace also commutes with base change. The generator c in
the quotient is a FORMAL MODULE GENERATOR. One must NOT regard 1/J as a
fraction of T: J is generally a zero divisor there.

Left exactness of Hom identifies Hom_A(B,A) with Ann_I(Hom_A(T,A)).
The colon calculation in the DOMAIN S is

    (ab):(b)=(a),     (ab):(a)=(b).

Thus the annihilator is a T c, is free over B, and is generated by
J c=Tr_T since epsilon is a unit. No Ext1 vanishing and no replacement
of T by B in base change have been assumed.

Both A and B are reduced, and B is finite torsion-free over A. To see
the last assertion, every component of B maps dominantly onto a target
curve component, so a nonzerodivisor of A remains a nonzerodivisor on
every component of B. Generically the finite algebra T over K(A_i) is
a product of Artin local algebras, with residue fields K(B_j) and
lengths r_j. A composition series shows that the trace of multiplication
by z is r_j times its residue-field trace in each factor. This proves
(3). The generic separable trace pairing is nondegenerate, so every
A-linear functional on B has a unique trace representative in Q(B).
Equations (1) and (3) then give exactly (2), including at branch
intersections. This proves the theorem in every degree.

## 2. Descent through the actual singular normalization

Suppose G is a finite group of automorphisms of S fixing g, and S->N=S^G
is unramified in codimension one. This includes the small cyclic
uniformizations in PF105--116; no Gorenstein assumption on N is made.
Put

    T0=N/(f),     B0=(T0)_red=B^G.

Then the SAME conclusions hold downstairs:

    Hom_A(B0,A)=B0 Tr_(T0/A),
    (B0)^vee_A=D_r B0.                               (5)

The r_j in this formula are now indexed by downstairs curve components.
They are unchanged by the quotient because it is unramified at their
generic points. N is finite free over R: Reynolds averaging makes it
an R-module direct summand of the finite free module S, and finite
projective modules over the local ring R are free.

Here is the descent, including its actual maps. Exactness of invariants
in characteristic zero gives T0=T^G and B0=B^G; the latter equality also
identifies the radical quotient. Restrict an invariant functional to
the invariant subring. Its inverse extends a functional by composing
with the Reynolds projection. Consequently

    Hom_A(B0,A) = Hom_A(B,A)^G,
    Hom_A(T0,A) = Hom_A(T,A)^G.                        (6)

These equalities mean canonical isomorphisms via restriction and
averaging, not extension of coefficients to separate curve branches.
They commute with the inclusions defined by T->B and T0->B0. For
example, the Reynolds projection takes ker(T->B) into ker(T0->B0),
which proves compatibility with their annihilators directly.

The trace functional Tr_T is fixed by G. The isomorphism B->B Tr_T
in (1) is therefore G-equivariant and its invariants are B0 Tr_T.
On Q(B0), the generic weighted trace upstairs restricts to |G| times
the generic weighted trace downstairs. Indeed, on a branch orbit the
number of branches times the degree of the stabilizer field extension
is |G|; transverse indices are constant on that orbit. Characteristic
zero and generic freeness give this equality even when G permutes
branches. Equivalently, ambient field-trace transitivity gives
Tr_(S/R)|N=|G| Tr_(N/R), then base change gives the same formula.
The nonzero constant |G| is invertible. This proves (5) and identifies
it with the nilradical annihilator INSIDE THE ACTUAL ambient dual

    Hom_R(N,R) tensor_R A = Hom_A(T0,A).

We have not claimed that Hom_R(N,R) is free as an N-module. It need not
be: PF116 has non-Gorenstein N for every m>=2. Its indicated
nilradical annihilator nevertheless IS free over the reduced curve B0.
In fact B0 is Gorenstein: A is a one-dimensional hypersurface, B0 is
finite Cohen--Macaulay over A, and its canonical module is Hom_A(B0,A),
which (5) makes free of rank one. This consequence uses finite duality
for canonical modules; it does not assert that B0 is a plane curve or
that its Kahler Jacobian ideal has the plane-curve duality of Pol.

## 3. The PF116 residue bridge and its limit

UPSTAIRS, let R_C and R_B denote logarithmic-residue fractional ideals.
PF116 established the actual equality

    R_B = D_r (B span g_C*(R_C)).

Together with (2) this becomes the desired embedded comparison

    R_B = B^vee_A (B span g_C*(R_C)).                 (7)

Products here are products of fractional B-ideals in Q(B); the trace
dual is not simply identified with R_C or with R_B. Both the diagonal
D_r and the source coefficient module remain visible. Downstairs,
define R0^quot to be the invariant upstairs residue module, viewed in
Q(B0). The invariant logarithmic pullback basis in PF116 and Reynolds
averaging of its coefficients give

    R0^quot = D_r (B0 span h_C*(R_C))
            = (B0)^vee_A (B0 span h_C*(R_C)).         (8)

This defines a quotient residue module; it does NOT identify intrinsic
Kahler logarithmic forms on singular N or import plane-curve Jacobian
duality for the generally non-plane curve B0.

The whole nonreduced trace restriction, its gluing, and the cyclic
quotient descent are now closed LOCAL gates. These automatic identities
also hold for PF116's sharp finite covers with marked open C* times C.
They supply no additional local contradiction and no stronger local
five-eta constant. They do not show regularity of global trace moments
on C[P,Q], preserve the polynomial source lattice across infinity, or
prove that the actual finite normalization equals C[x,y]. Those remain
the global finite-algebra/whole-plane finish line. No Bernstein-root,
connection, Brieskorn-lattice or new-preprint transport is inferred.

## 4. Exact controls and necessity of the hypotheses

For g=(x,y^3+xy), set f=4u^3+27v^2. Then

    f(g)=(x+3y^2)^2(4x+3y^2),  J=x+3y^2.

The nonreduced algebra is rank3 over A. Its ordinary trace has values
(3,0,-2u) on the basis (1,y,y^2); it equals J times the dual generator
that extracts the coefficient of y^2 in the monic cubic presentation.
On the reduced source branches, use the common target normalization
(u,v)=(-3t^2,-2t^3). The source coordinate y is t on the critical branch
and -2t on the ordinary branch. Therefore

    ordinary reduced-curve trace(y)=-t NOT in C{t^2,t^3},
    weighted trace(y)=2t-2t=0 in C{t^2,t^3}.

The two smooth source branches have contact2. In the common target
parameter their actual gluing imposes equality of constants and
2*(linear coefficient on branch1)+(linear coefficient on branch2)=0.
This makes the weighted trace regular. A constant tuple (c1,c2) belongs
to the codifferent exactly when c1=2c2, whereas a constant tuple belongs
to the source ring exactly when c1=c2. Thus D_r=(2,1) has zero valuation
on both branches but cannot be discarded from the embedded ideal.

For g=(x^p,y^q), f=uv, the reduced source is xy=0. On the x-axis the
transverse index is q and the field degree is p; on the y-axis they are
p and q respectively. Hence D_r=(q,p), in that order. For a constant
tuple the codifferent gluing condition is p*c_x=q*c_y, so (q,p) satisfies
it. This checks a reducible target and prevents interchanging transverse
ramification with reduced-curve degree. Omit the branch value u=0 in
g=(x^2,y), taking f=v instead: J=2x but F/b=1, so the theorem's crucial
unit-Jacobian cancellation fails. The smooth reduced curve extension
C{u}->C{x}, u=x^2, has codifferent (1/(2x))C{x}, not just C{x}.

Finally PF116's actual m=2 cover has small group C5 of weights(3,1),
non-Gorenstein ambient N of type1/5(1,2), and reduced equation b=X H.
Every monomial of b has character4, the character of dX wedge dY;
thus its curve dualizing generator is invariant, in agreement with
(5). The actual transverse weights are (8,1,1), not all ones. The
checker verifies this actual polynomial character and reduced equation;
it does not replace the all-group descent proof by finite samples.
