## 1. A winding-one stage preserves BOTH preferred generators

Let J be an oriented knot with actual meridian M and preferred longitude
L. They commute. Use the ordinary cable convention of PF80/82: r is
the winding, k the meridional parameter, and ru-kv=1. The root-amalgam
and peripheral formulas are

    F=M^k L^r=z^r,
    mu=M^u L^v z^(-v),       lambda=mu^(-rk) F.       (1)

For r=1, eliminate z=M^k L. Every Bezout solution has u=1+kv.
Substitution, using only the peripheral commutation, gives

    mu=M^(1+kv) L^v (M^k L)^(-v)=M,
    lambda=M^(-k) M^k L=L.                          (2)

This works for EVERY integer k and every Bezout choice. The remaining
presentation is precisely the companion presentation. In particular,
the isomorphism is the identity on the companion group and on the
preferred peripheral pair, rather than an unmarked group isomorphism.
No meridian shear or correction of a later ordinary cable parameter
occurs. Repeated removals commute with all later root-amalgam steps.

There is also a direct geometric proof, including the r=1 case without
appealing to a genuine-cable formula outside its stated range. In a
companion solid torus S1 times D2 the curve

    t |-> (exp(it), epsilon*exp(ikt))

is isotopic to its core by shrinking epsilon to zero. Every intermediate
curve is embedded because its first coordinate is injective on S1.
Isotopy extension gives an ambient isotopy supported in the companion
neighborhood. Knot orientations and the preferred zero-linking longitude
are preserved. Hence the exterior homeomorphism carries every slope
a*mu+b*lambda to a*M+b*L, with its sign and integral coefficients.

For ANY finite permutation representation the added generator is uniquely
forced to be rho(M)^k rho(L). Restriction and extension are inverse;
the image group and peripheral monodromies agree. The ambient isotopy
lifts to the corresponding covers and extends across matched fillings.
Consequently all marked filled groups and defined oriented linking
invariants agree. This is degree-free and does not require trivial
longitude monodromy or an integral-null-core hypothesis. Those extra
conditions are required only by the particular PF92 linking routine.

## 2. Initial unknot stages and the essential parameter distinction

An initial T(1,q) or T(p,1) is an unknot, with group generated by its
meridian M and preferred longitude L=1. For a cable on this unknot,
(1) becomes z^r=M^k. For k=1 eliminate M=z^r; the Bezout equation
gives mu=z and lambda=1. For k=-1 eliminate M=z^(-r); it gives
mu=z^(-1) and lambda=1. Thus any leading sequence of winding-one
stages and (r,+/-1) cables on the STILL UNKNOT companion can be
removed, keeping the preferred meridian and longitude.

If the next stage has r,k>1, its group and preferred pair are exactly
those of the INITIAL torus knot T(k,r): generators M,z, relation
M^k=z^r, meridian M^u z^(-v), longitude mu^(-rk)z^r. Subsequent
cables use this pair unchanged. This resets the initial torus stage;
it does not truncate a nontrivial companion.

For a NONTRIVIAL companion, k=1 is not the reduction in Section1.
The equation z^r=M L^r does not provide that elimination of z.
A concrete colored-cover control distinguishes the two operations:
the retained three-sheet trefoil coloring has mutual linking2, while
its winding3/parameter1 cable with z=rho(M) has mutual linking18.
The full unfilled peripheral routine computes both integrally.
Erasing such a stage would lose actual colored linking data.
Negative parameters are not replaced by their absolute values.

If all stages leave an unknot, its exterior group is Z. Every finite
image is cyclic. It cannot surject onto S4; in particular a transposition
image generates only C2 and cannot act transitively on four sheets.
This excludes the unknot alternative under the full-final-S4 hypothesis
without any source-curve classification or coefficient calculation.

## 3. What the primary splice theorem covers

The pinned [Neumann--Norbury primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0110286v1),
Section4, describes the knot at infinity and its actual successive
cablings, then states necessary and sufficient conditions (1)(i)--(iii).
For a nontrivial reduced diagram its entries satisfy

    p_i>1, q_i>1, gcd(p_i,q_i)=1,
    Delta_i=p_i-p_(i-1)*q_(i-1)*q_i<0 (i>=2),
    p_1<q_1,
    p_(i+1) in <q_1...q_i, p_1*q_2...q_i,
                  p_2*q_3...q_i, ..., p_i>.         (3)

The displayed Delta_1 in the HTML has an inconsistent subscript on its
right-hand side; the initial condition is p_1<q_1. No recurrence for
Delta_1 is used here. Our ordinary cable convention is

    (r_i,k_i)=(q_i,p_i), i>=2.                       (4)

Thus the later ordinary winding and parameter are both at least2,
and k_i<r_i*p_(i-1)*q_(i-1), exactly the strict infinity inequality
used by PF80--96. The first torus pair can be exchanged, as an
oriented torus knot; this does not exchange the later r_i,k_i.
The virtual-component linking formula in the same section fixes the
ordinary preferred framing; raw Puiseux exponents are not substituted
for k_i. Section5.1, Theorem5.1 and its full proof explain why
lower-weight corrected polynomial keys retain the actual splice.
No literal-binomial assumption is introduced by this coverage step.

All generators of the semigroup in (3), following a genuine positive
prefix, are integers at least2. Hence its positive elements cannot
include1. This excludes a k_i=1 continuation in such a reduced
polynomial infinity diagram. It does NOT say that a general (r,1)
satellite of a nontrivial knot is the companion. The sign/semigroup
conditions come from the classification; peripheral algebra alone
does not turn an arbitrary satellite into an algebraic knot at infinity.

The empty reduced diagram is handled by the unknot argument above.
The source's statement about nontrivial diagrams is not applied to
claim that an unknot must have p_i,q_i>1. Rooted resolution data and
coordinate-dependent normal-degree numbers are not transported by
deleting arbitrary resolution vertices: (2) removes actual winding-one
cables; (3) invokes an actual reduced target splice. No equality between
different compactifications' canonical coefficients is needed here.
