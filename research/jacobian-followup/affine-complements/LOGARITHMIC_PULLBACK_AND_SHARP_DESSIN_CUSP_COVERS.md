## 1. Exact logarithmic pullback in every finite plane degree

Let g:(C2,0)->(C2,0) be a finite complex analytic germ. Let C={f=0} be
a reduced target divisor containing ALL branch values of g, and let
B={b=0} be its REDUCED inverse image. C and B may be reducible. Then
the actual pullback induces an isomorphism of O_source-modules

    O_source tensor_(O_target) Omega1(log C)
                    -> Omega1(log B).                    (1)

It is equivariant under any finite group of source automorphisms preserving
g. In the actual cyclic-axis normalization setting use g=h composed with
the smooth uniformization; (1) is an UPSTAIRS statement. It is not an
identification with Kahler differentials of the singular surface N.

Proof. At a general point of each preimage component E_j choose smooth
target divisor coordinates u,v. Locally g has transverse ramification
index r_j and has the form u=x^(r_j),v=y after analytic coordinate changes.
Pullback sends du/u to r_j dx/x and dv to dy. Thus pulled-back logarithmic
1-forms and their exterior differentials have at most SIMPLE divisorial
poles on B. Multiplication by b makes their coefficients holomorphic in
codimension one, hence everywhere on the smooth source. This gives the
inclusion in (1), including over the isolated singular points of C and B.

Every reduced plane curve is a free divisor. Its logarithmic 1-forms form
a free rank2 module, with determinant line generated by dU wedge dV/f
(Saito criterion). Choose a target basis omega_1,omega_2. In codimension
one, the characteristic-zero different formula is

    div Jac(g)=sum_j(r_j-1)E_j,
    div(f composed with g)=sum_j r_j E_j.

There is no omitted critical divisor because C contains ALL branch values.
Consequently

    g*omega_1 wedge g*omega_2
           =unit * dx wedge dy/b.

Relative to any source logarithmic basis, the two pulled-back forms have
a holomorphic transition matrix with UNIT determinant. It is invertible,
proving (1). This proof is independent of field degree, Puiseux pairs,
quadratic jets, branch count and the scalar-Morse condition.

For PF115's chosen branch and sole critical axis A={x=0}, one has
f(g)=x^e H, Jac(g)=epsilon*x^(e-1), b=xH. The determinant cancellation
is exactly x^(e-1)/(x^e H)=1/(xH), not a replacement of the nonreduced
pullback x^e H by xH in the finite algebra.
