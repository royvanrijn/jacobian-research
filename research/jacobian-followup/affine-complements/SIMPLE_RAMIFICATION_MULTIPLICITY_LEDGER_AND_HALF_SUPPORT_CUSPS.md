# Simple ramification multiplicity ledger and half-support cusps

PF2RULED119, 16 November 2026. For an ACTUAL plane Keller normalization
whose ramified omitted primes all have index2, the complete local fibre
lengths and global excess are determined by the multiplicities of the
image normalization branches. In PF118's involutory half-support case,
the only possibilities are an immersed image or ONE ordinary cusp.
This closes all higher-cusp and multiple-cusp alternatives in that
case. It does not exclude its entire binary affine family or close
the global finite-algebra problem. The full goal remains ACTIVE.

## 1. Simple ramification gives an exact local degree

Retain the original finite N->A2, the whole marked open U=A2, and ALL
omitted primes. Suppose all ramified omitted primes have e=2. PF105
gives f=1; PF106 gives no cyclic quotient point on an index2 prime.
Unramified omitted primes are etale at every finite point. Hence N
is SMOOTH everywhere. Each omitted prime is a smooth A1, and its map
to the normalization of its image is an isomorphism.

At a point p on a ramified prime D, let m be the multiplicity of the
chosen normalization branch of C=pi(D) at pi(p). Then

    ell_p=m+1,    eta_p=ell_p-2=m-1.                 (1)

For m>=2, that image branch has one Puiseux pair (m,m+1). The local
monodromy of this connected factor is the natural S_(m+1) action.
For m=1 it is the ordinary degree2 smooth-branch factor.

Here is the calculation, already contained in the ALL-INDEX corank-one
argument of PF105, specialized without its higher-index hypotheses.
The source is smooth and its Jacobian has a simple zero along the smooth
curve D. The differential cannot have rank zero: that would make its
Jacobian have order at least2. Choose coordinates so pi=(u,G(u,z)).
If D is transverse to the kernel, the branch is smooth and ell=2.
Otherwise D={u=phi(z)}, with ord(phi)=m>=2. Finiteness excludes phi=0
identically. After subtracting the target function G(u,0),

    G_z=V(u,z)(u-phi(z)),     V(0,0)!=0.

Writing phi(z)=c z^m+higher terms gives, on D,

    ord(u)=m,    ord(G)=m+1,
    leading(G)=V(0,0)c*m/(m+1) z^(m+1).

The two consecutive orders are coprime, hence give the stated single
Puiseux pair and local multiplicity m. Setting u=0 gives ord_z G(0,z)=m+1;
the fibre ideal (u,G) therefore has length m+1. Units and all higher
analytic terms are retained; no universal analytic normal form for
every (m,m+1) branch is claimed.

The connected local factor is unramified off that chosen image branch.
Its general meridian is ONE transposition: there is one critical germ
and its normalized map has degree1. A plane curve local complement is
normally generated by its geometric meridians; for an irreducible
branch these are conjugate. Thus the local group is generated by
transpositions. Connectivity makes its transposition graph connected,
so the image is S_(m+1). This is a LOCAL symmetric group, not an
assertion that the global degree-d monodromy is S_d.

## 2. An exact global multiplicity ledger

For every distinct ramified image C_j, write r_j for the number of
ramified primes with that image, and nu_j:A1->C_j for its normalization.
At each parameter a let m_j(a) be the multiplicity of the chosen image
branch, and define the finite nonimmersion defect

    M_j=sum_(a in A1)(m_j(a)-1).

Every ramified prime over C_j sees exactly the SAME parameters and
multiplicities, since its normalized map is an isomorphism. Therefore

    eta_(each D over C_j)=M_j,
    d-1=sum_j r_j(2+M_j)+t0,                         (2)

where t0 is the number of unramified omitted primes. This is an EXACT
specialization of PF2OMIT6, including every ramified and unramified
prime, not an extra term inserted into an unchanged old ledger.

There is also an exact pointwise formula. For every finite y,

    #U_y=d-sum_j r_j sum_(a in nu_j^-1(y))(m_j(a)+1)
           -sum_(D unramified) b_D(y),                (3)

where b_D(y) is the number of image normalization parameters of that
unramified prime over y. Unramified primes are counted separately even
if their images coincide. They have local length1, whereas (1) gives
every local ramified length. Distinct prime curves are disjoint in N.
Thus a negative right side is forbidden and zero is allowed. Arbitrary
tangencies, self-intersections and extra unramified images are retained.

## 3. The entire involutory half-support case

Assume PF118's equality case, with generic degree d=4r and minimum
nontrivial support d/2, and suppose every minimum-support branch
inertia is an involution. This includes every surviving natural
binary affine action. PF118 gives one FULL branch curve C, r ramified
primes, all e=2,f=1, smooth N, and no unramified omitted prime with
image C. The curve is not contractible, has normalization A1, and has
double values with precisely two smooth branches (arbitrary contact).
Every such double value has EMPTY open source fibre.

Let M=sum_a(m(a)-1) for C. Equations (2) and d=4r give

    t0+r*M=2r-1.

Since M is a nonnegative INTEGER, M can only be0 or1. The complete
remaining alternatives are

| Case | Image normalization | Total excess Eta | Unramified omitted primes t0 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| I | Immersive at every finite parameter | 0 | 2r-1 |
| II | Exactly one nonimmersive parameter, an ordinary cusp (2,3) | r | r-1 |

Indeed M=1 means one parameter with m=2. Equation (1) gives Puiseux
pair (2,3), the ordinary cusp, and a length3 local factor on EVERY
ramified curve. No branch of multiplicity>=3 and no two ordinary
cusps can occur: either would give M>=2 and t0<=-1. The ordinary cusp
cannot share its value with another normalization parameter, because
PF118 forces both branches at any double value to be smooth. Thus
the affine image singularities are only two smooth branches with
arbitrary finite contact, and optionally ONE ordinary cusp.

In Case II the finite fibre over the cusp consists of r ramified
points of length3 and r etale points of length1. The local group is
S3 with orbit profile 3^r 1^r: cusp meridians satisfy aba=bab, while
a^2=b^2=1, so their nontrivial image is S3, diagonal across the r
three-sheet factors. The remaining r sheets are fixed. Some fixed
points can still be removed by unramified omitted primes with OTHER
images. No exact open-source cusp fibre count or source surjectivity
is asserted without those branch counts in (3).

For the degree32 AGL_5(2) candidate, r=8. Case I has t0=15 and Eta=0;
Case II has t0=7 and Eta=8. These are necessary profiles, not actual
plane realizations. The degree4 case is already excluded by published
Domrina2000/HC4MYKR0 and is not reopened here. The binary affine
family in general and other surviving monodromy actions remain OPEN.

## 4. Literature and the boundary of this reduction

The local calculation is credited to the retained PF105 corank-one
proof; its new use here is the GLOBAL repetition across all primes
with one image. Recent local generic-cover literature is relevant
but does not supply a global completion theorem for this profile.
Kulikov, arXiv2503.07099v1, Introduction0.1(i)--(iii), requires the
TOTAL ramification curve to map birationally onto the branch curve.
For r>1 our total ramification curve maps with degree r, so that
hypothesis fails. One cannot promote each individual normalized
isomorphism to a global generic-cover or Chisini hypothesis.

Orevkov, arXiv2512.07986v1, Section1(A2), assumes bijectivity on EVERY
irreducible component of the reduced inverse image, not merely the
distinguished ramified component. Its broader local classification
is not imported. The primary HTML hypotheses were inspected and
pinned. The full theorems of either paper are not claimed proved here.

The shared Bergman result HC4MYLW0/1 identifies the finite trace dual
with polynomial L2 vectors, but its missing L4/multiplication-domain
density assertion remains OPEN. The new multiplicity ledger does
not supply that analytic improvement. Nor do immersed finite images
make their complements cyclic automatically: behaviour at infinity
has not been controlled. No affine fundamental-group recognition,
source action extension, finite Keller realization, or global finite
normalization equality is inferred.
