# Torus knots at infinity are excluded on the marked Wright surface

14 November 2026. PF2RULED34 excludes an entire global boundary type in
every possible finite field degree and at every pole height.

Suppose the actual marked Wright surface admits a finite Keller target
map. Its entire boundary image cannot have a torus knot at affine
infinity. In particular, its two polynomial normalization degrees cannot
be coprime in any polynomial target-coordinate presentation.

The obstruction uses the homology class of the omitted line at infinity.
The end homology group alone is insufficient. Iterated cable knots with
more than one essential cabling stage remain open.

## Actual hypotheses and the marked end

Keep [PF2WRIGHT1](WRIGHT_MARKED_MODEL.md) and the finite-map hypotheses
of [PF2RULED32](GLOBAL_BOUNDARY_OBSTRUCTION.md):

    A=C[Y,XY,X^2Y,X^3Y+X] inside C[X,Y],
    N=Spec A, U=A2_(X,Y)=N minus R,
    pi=(P,Q):N -> A2 finite, [C(N):C(P,Q)]=d,
    det D_(X,Y)(P,Q)=c != 0.

The full branch curve is C=pi(R), with R=A1, index two and residue
degree one. Its normalization gamma(t)=(p(t),q(t)) is finite birational.
The all-degree monodromy is S_d. The boundary budget gives d>=3.

The specified completion, already identified by PF2WRIGHT1, is

    S=F1, B=C0+2f, E=C0+3f,
    N=S minus B, R=E minus B,
    B^2=3, E.B=4, and B intersect E is one point.

A tubular boundary M_B of the smooth rational B is the oriented circle
bundle with Euler number of absolute value three. Thus

    H1(M_B,Z)=Z/3, generated by a normal meridian mu of B.

The end knot K_R of R represents

    [K_R]=(E.B)*mu=4*mu != 0.                         (1)

To compute this class, use a local parameter on E at B. A defining
function for B has order four there; its argument winds four times on
the normal circle. Base-circle contributions vanish in the total
circle-bundle homology. This retains the actual source marking.
No arbitrary reidentification of the omitted curve is allowed.

Take a sufficiently large sphere in the affine target. Its preimage
M under pi is smooth. Indeed pi is etale outside R; along R the
target curve is smooth and transverse to that sphere at sufficiently
large radius. The proper radial function |pi|^2 is a submersion there,
also on R. It supplies a product end for the pair (N,R). Comparing
cofinal product neighborhoods with the tubular end gives

    H1(M,Z)=Z/3, and [M intersect R] != 0.             (2)

Only this homology statement is needed; no classification of all
Seifert fibrations on a lens space is used.

The restricted map M->S3 is a connected degree-d simple branched
cover of the knot K=C intersect S3. All finite curve singularities
are inside the sphere. The branch knot has one end because C has
normalization A1. There is exactly one ramified knot upstairs,
with index two and degree one over K.

The monodromy of this boundary cover is the whole S_d, rather than
just a transitive subgroup. The epimorphism

    pi1(S3 minus K) -> pi1(A2 minus C)

is the argument in the proof of Leidy--Maxim Theorem4.7, pinned in
[sources](sources/torus-infinity/manifest.json). That epimorphism does
not require the projective curve to meet the line at infinity
transversely. Compose it with the actual global S_d representation.

## A general topological lemma

Let f:M->S3 be a connected simple d-sheeted branched cover of a
nontrivial torus knot T(p,q), with gcd(p,q)=1 and d>=3. Suppose its
monodromy is S_d and H1(M,Z) is finite.

Then the unique ramification knot of f is zero in H1(M,Z).

This lemma does not assume that M is the Wright end, that its first
homology is cyclic, or that the branch curve has prescribed cusps.

### Lift the circle fibration, with its filling slopes

Give S3 its Seifert fibration over S2 with exceptional orders p,q;
the branch torus knot is a regular fiber. A regular fiber h is
central in the torus-knot complement group. Since the monodromy is
S_d and its center is trivial for d>=3, h maps to the identity.

Consequently every generic fiber lifts with fiber degree one. The
lifted fibration extends over the filled tori:

* over an exceptional order p, a monodromy cycle of length l gives
  a source exceptional order p/l; similarly for q;
* over the branch regular fiber, the one transposition is locally
  (z,w)->(z^2,w) in a fibered solid torus; its ramification core is
  a regular fiber upstairs. The fixed sheets give ordinary product
  solid tori.

These statements use the fiber framing, not an unadjusted preferred
longitude. This matters when the normal framing twists around the
branch knot. Trivial monodromy of h is exactly what gives the product
description in the fiber framing. In particular the ramification
knot represents the regular-fiber class h_M.

The lifted base is an oriented closed surface. Finite H1(M,Z)
forces its genus to be zero, since first homology surjects onto
that of the base.

### Riemann--Hurwitz leaves only one cycle split

Let alpha,beta,tau be base monodromies around the two exceptional
values and the branch value, with alpha*beta*tau=1. Write c(sigma)
for a permutation's cycle count, including fixed points. The base
cover has degree d and target and source both have genus zero.
Therefore

    c(alpha)+c(beta)+c(tau)=d+2.
    c(tau)=d-1, hence c(alpha)+c(beta)=3.              (3)

After exchanging p,q, alpha is a d-cycle, and beta has two cycles
of lengths r,s, with r+s=d. Multiplying a full cycle by a
transposition cuts it into precisely those two cycles.

The group generated by alpha,beta is generated by alpha,tau.
The graph of alpha-conjugates of tau has gcd(d,r) components,
with vertices numbered cyclically. Because that group is S_d,

    gcd(r,s)=gcd(d,r)=1.                              (4)

The exceptional-value relations give

    p=d*A, q=r*s*k, A>=1, k>=1.                       (5)

Here q is a multiple of lcm(r,s)=r*s. Coprimality of p,q also
gives gcd(A,q)=1. The three source Seifert orders, retaining order
one entries, are consequently

    A, s*k, r*k.                                     (6)

### The regular fiber is zero in homology

Use e for the rational Euler number of an oriented Seifert
fibration, with either consistent overall sign convention.
For the displayed fibration of S3, |e|=1/(p*q).

In the lifted fibration

    |e(M)|=d/(p*q).                                  (7)

For clarity, (7) also holds despite ordinary branching in the base.
One can prove it by counting a section obstruction. If a target
exceptional pair is (a,b), each cycle of length l gives the pair
(a/l,b), so its rational contribution is multiplied by l. The
sum of these l is d. Integer section-obstruction terms also pull
back with degree d. The extra ordinary branch point has product
local model and adds no exceptional contribution. This proves (7).
Scott Theorem3.6 states naturality for unbranched Seifert covers;
we do not cite it as already covering this branched situation.

The usual abelianized Seifert presentation now gives

    |H1(M,Z)|=A*(s*k)*(r*k)*|e(M)|
             =q/(r*s)=k.                            (8)

This formula is a determinant calculation: for cone orders a_i,
relations a_i*x_i+b_i*h=0 and sum x_i+b*h=0 have determinant
of absolute value product(a_i)*|e|. It remains valid with order-one
entries and for bad base orbifolds; no geometric uniformization
assumption is used.

On killing the regular fiber, the abelianized orbifold group has
presentation

    A*x=0, (s*k)*y=0, (r*k)*z=0, x+y+z=0.

Its order is the gcd of its maximal minors:

    gcd(A*s*k, A*r*k, r*s*k^2)=k.                    (9)

Indeed gcd(r,s)=1 first gives gcd(A*s*k,A*r*k)=A*k,
then gcd(A,q)=1 gives gcd(A*k,q*k)=k. This is an
integral gcd argument, without parameter denominators.

The quotient H1(M,Z)/<h_M> therefore has the same finite order k
as H1(M,Z). Hence h_M=0. The ramification knot is a regular fiber,
so its homology class is zero. This proves the lemma in all d>=3.

## Exclusion on the marked Wright surface

If K at infinity were a nontrivial torus knot, the lemma would
give [M intersect R]=0, contradicting (1)--(2).

If K were the unknot, its complement group would be cyclic and
could not surject onto S_d for d>=3. Thus it is excluded too.

Therefore the target boundary has neither the unknot nor any torus
knot as its link at infinity, in every possible finite degree and
at every pole height. The local cusp count, collision count and
intersection multiplicities do not enter this exclusion.

If deg p and deg q are coprime, their polynomial parametrization
has torus knot T(deg p,deg q) at affine infinity. See the proof
of Borodzik Corollary5.19; its statement and source are pinned.
Degree one gives the unknot. Applying the theorem after any
polynomial target automorphism gives the invariant necessary gate:

    gcd(deg p,deg q)>1 in every target-coordinate presentation. (10)

This is a condition on boundary normalization degrees. It is not a
new claim about the already studied poles of P,Q along B or their
ordinary degrees on the original source.

More strongly, every target-coordinate normalization polynomial has
composite degree, and therefore degree at least four. Suppose one such
polynomial p had prime degree l. Choose a second target coordinate q
and repeatedly subtract a multiple of p^j when its leading degree is
divisible by l. These are invertible target shears and they strictly
decrease deg q. The terminal q is nonconstant by PF2RULED32's
coordinate-projection gate. Its degree is not divisible by l, hence
is coprime to l, contradicting (10). Degree one was already excluded.
This excludes every prime boundary-coordinate degree uniformly,
including degree three. It does not classify composite boundary degrees.

## Controls and routes that did not give an exclusion

The end group Z/3 by itself does not prove the theorem.
For example degree4 monodromies for T(4,9) give orders (1,9,3),
Euler number of absolute value1/9 and first homology Z/3.
A regular fiber, including the ramification knot, is zero there.
Such an unmarked topological cover is consistent. Equation(1)
is the extra Wright-specific information that excludes it.

The first approach tested in this pass was the weighted Euler number
of the [PF2RULED33 sign cover](RESIDUAL_DOUBLE_COVER.md). For cusp A2
and collision orders m_i, quotient-group weights give

    chi_orb(W)=1/3+sum_i 1/(2*m_i),
    chi_orb(Z)=4/3+sum_i 2/m_i=4*chi_orb(W).

Thus weighted Euler multiplicativity balances for all collision
orders. It supplies no contradiction. This failure is retained
instead of being used to justify deeper coefficient calculations.

The S4/V4 quotient also forces a cyclic triple cover of W,
quasi-etale and ramified only at the cusp. In divisor language
its eigensheaf has reflexive cube O_W, has nonzero local class in
Cl(A2)=Z/3 at that cusp, and trivial local class at every collision.
This is a further class-group gate, not a proof that the class
cannot exist. The torus-end argument above supplies an actual
exclusion without assuming that gate is impossible.

The three [primary sources](sources/torus-infinity/manifest.json)

and coprime-degree link description. Their full proofs are imported,
not independently formalized. The section-obstruction extension
to the present branched base map is written explicitly above.

The remaining link can have genuine iterated cabling. A satellite
torus decomposition is not a single Seifert fibration, and the lemma
does not apply to that case automatically. The original residual
embedding, all mixed-weight finite Keller pairs on the marked ring,
and compatible completion of an arbitrary normalization remain open.
No full quartic packet or research goal is closed; no formal
verification, independent full reproof, external review or novelty
priority is claimed.
