# Global prime counts and the immersed r7 ledger

14 November 2026. PF2OMIT2 extends the [pointwise ledger](PROOF.md) using
the full plane boundary theorem PF2BATT1. The finite normalization is
N -> A2, its source open is U=A2, and its degree is d. Its omitted primes
are pairwise disjoint, each is unibranch, and each has normalization A1.
Every unramified omitted prime is etale at all its finite points, has
residue degree one and an immersed polynomial image. These are the same
explicit external inputs as in [the boundary audit](../jc2-boundary-attachment/PROOF.md).
No statement transports Borisov's projective common point through a target
blowup without a separate comparison of the two Stein models.

## 1. An Euler identity indexed by omitted points

For an omitted prime D, let e_D be its generic normal index and let
ell_q be the ambient local fibre length at q in D. Set

\[
 E_D=\sum_{q\in D}(\ell_q-e_D).                            \tag{11}
\]

Only finitely many summands are nonzero, and every summand is nonnegative.
Here is a useful justification of the latter point. Base-change N along
the normalization A1 -> D -> A2. The resulting finite flat algebra has a
section corresponding to D. Over its generic parameter the local factor
at that section has length e_D. Take the scheme-theoretic closure of this
generic factor, removing parameter torsion. Its algebra is finite and
torsion free of rank e_D over C[t], hence flat. Its support is the closed
section. At every closed parameter it is a quotient of the ambient fibre
algebra supported at that section, of length e_D. Consequently ell_q>=e_D.
This argument also shows why special fibre length can increase.

The finite-flat pointwise identity gives

\[
 \#N_y=d-\sum_{q\in(N\setminus U)_y}(\ell_q-1).
\]

Integrate this constructible equality with compactly supported Euler
characteristic. Since each omitted prime is homeomorphic to A1,

\[
 \chi(N)=d-\sum_D[(e_D-1)+E_D].
\]

On the other hand, chi(N)=1+#\{omitted primes\}. Thus the exact global
length ledger is

\[
 \boxed{d-1=\sum_{D\text{ omitted}}e_D+\sum_D E_D.}        \tag{12}
\]

All omitted e=1 primes occur in the first sum, although they have E_D=0
and contribute zero to the different. Equation (12) is a prime-indexed
Euler identity. Residue degrees remain independent fields: if a residue
map has degree f>1, its special local degrees are reflected in E_D; one
must not replace this sum by sum(e*f) while dropping that change of terms.
The existing residue-normalized Orevkov ledger is compatible with this
identity, but is not needed for its proof.

If R_1,...,R_s are the ramified omitted primes and t is the number of
unramified omitted primes, (12) becomes

\[
 \boxed{t=d-1-\sum_{i=1}^s e_i-\sum_{i=1}^s E_i.}         \tag{13}
\]

The boundary theorem's existence of a ramified prime implies t<=d-3 for a
nonautomorphic plane Keller map. This is not a bound on the number of
target images: several primes can have the same image.

Two local finite controls distinguish the exceptional term from generic
ramification. The cubic (u,w) -> (u,z=w^3+u*w) has e=2 along its critical
curve; the ordinary cusp value has local fibre length3, adding one to E.
The double cover h^2=u^3−v^2 has the same e=2 and cusp branch image, but
its fibre over the cusp has length2, adding zero. These are local finite
algebras, not global plane Keller-source realizations.

## 2. Immersed ramification images remove all exceptional excess

Assume the entire reduced ramification divisor is R_1 union ... union R_s,
each has residue degree one, and the normalization of each image C_i is
immersive in A2. At q in R_i there is just one ramification germ in N,
because omitted primes are disjoint and unibranch. Its target germ is one
smooth branch of C_i. The other local target branches, if present, have
trivial inertia on the connected local factor at q: otherwise they would
give another ramification germ there.

Purity and the tame local cover of a smooth branch therefore identify
this factor with the cyclic cover

\[
 C[[u,v]]\longrightarrow C[[s,v]],\qquad u=s^{e_i}.
\]

Equivalently, its local monodromy is generated by the one inertia cycle
on a connected orbit. Thus N and R_i are smooth at q and ell_q=e_i.
All E_i vanish, so

\[
 t=d-1-\sum_i e_i.                                       \tag{14}
\]

Let b_i(y) be the number of normalization branches of C_i over y and
b_D(y) the analogous count for each unramified omitted prime D. Then

\[
 \#U_y=d-\sum_i e_i b_i(y)-\sum_{D:e_D=1}b_D(y).           \tag{15}
\]

Different primes are counted separately, including when they share an
image. The local lengths, not merely their generic values, are now proved
to be e_i and 1 at every finite point under these immersion hypotheses.

## 3. The entire immersed normal-r7 family

Retain the explicit hypothesis that the entire affine ramification
divisor consists of one R with (e,f)=(2,1), and d=6. Suppose its image C
is an immersed birational degree-(6,10) polynomial curve with odd infinity
gap21. Its complete affine delta is12 by PF2D6O1 and PF2R7CUSP1's infinity
calculation. This is the immersed part left outside the earlier two-class
nonimmersion table.

Equation (14) gives exactly t=3 additional unramified omitted primes. For
every finite y,

\[
 \boxed{\#U_y=6-2b_C(y)-B(y),\qquad B(y)=\sum_{j=1}^3b_j(y).} \tag{16}
\]

The full pointwise ledger is:

| Number b_C(y) | Ramified omitted points | Length of each | Maximum B(y) | B(y) for a singleton source fibre |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 | none | 6 | 5 |
| 1 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 3 |
| 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 3 | 3 | 2 | 0 | impossible |

Four branches at one finite value are impossible for such a cover. The
three-branch row may have an empty source fibre; this is not itself a
contradiction.

Let q be the number of the three unramified primes whose image equals C.
The positive affine delta guarantees a value with at least two branches:
an immersed birational normalization cannot produce a singular unibranch
plane germ. At a value with b branches the contributions of R and these
q primes alone have total length (2+q)*b. Hence

\[
 q\le\lfloor6/b\rfloor-2.
\]

In particular q<=1. At least two of the three unramified primes have
images different from C. If C has a three-branch value, q=0 and none of
the extra omitted primes can lie over that value. Their images may still
coincide with each other.

Together with PF2R7BND1, this forces an additional nonproper target
component for every gap21 image in the single-ramified-row setting:
both classified nonimmersed cases and the entire immersed family.
It does not identify that component's equations or infinity attachments,
and does not exclude any whole normal-r7 family. The single entire
ramification-row hypothesis is preserved, not derived from a terminal
passport alone.

## 4. A general singleton-zero obstruction off the branch locus

Let the source fibre at zero be one reduced point, and assume zero lies
outside the finite normalization's branch locus. Every omitted point above
zero is then on an e=1 omitted prime, with length one. Thus

\[
 \sum_{D:e_D=1}b_D(0)=d-1.                                \tag{17}
\]

Equation (13) is global and still includes ramified primes over other
values. Put a=#\{unramified omitted primes whose image avoids zero\}.
Separating zero and positive branch counts in (17) yields

\[
 \boxed{\sum_{D:e_D=1}\max(b_D(0)-1,0)
    =\sum_{i=1}^s(e_i+E_i)+a\ge2+a.}                     \tag{18}
\]

Consequently a singleton affine fibre outside the branch locus cannot be
explained by unramified omitted image germs that are all smooth there.
At least two branch-surplus units are required, counted per prime. One
ordinary node on one omitted prime is insufficient if all other omitted
image germs there are smooth and no other prime shares that node image.
Two different primes can share a nodal image; their contributions must
then be counted twice.

For immersed r7, t=3 and sum b_j(0)=5. The unordered complete integer
possibilities are (0,0,5), (0,1,4), (0,2,3), (1,1,3), (1,2,2).
For either classified cusp image the excess E_R is epsilon, so t=3−epsilon.
When epsilon=1 the possible pairs are (0,5), (1,4), (2,3). These are
necessary branch-count patterns, not realized polynomial image curves.
At a zero lying on a ramified image, use the actual ramified lengths in
the original ledger; (18)'s off-branch hypothesis is essential.

## 5. Why the old even-row excess budget needs a completeness audit

For the retained r=10 pair, the ramification images are two disjoint
four-node quintics. Under the stated two entire ramified rows, e1=e2=2
and f1=f2=1, both normalizations are immersive. Equation (14) therefore
requires exactly one further unramified omitted prime, rather than
producing a contradiction. It may map onto one of these immersed curves
or onto another immersed curve. A node on a shared image would then have
omitted lengths 2+2+1+1=6 and no source point, which is numerically allowed.

This does not construct a cover or prove that r=10 occurs. It identifies
the unsupported omission-completeness step in the historical exclusion.
The [scope audit](STEIN_SCOPE_AUDIT.md) preserves the exact implicitizations,
local models and restricted braid checks while retracting the global
whole-even-row conclusion until a full omitted ledger is supplied.
