# The finite normalization must carry a free boundary class lattice

A missing affine-space condition can be expressed directly on the
finite normalization of the target. For a nontrivial Keller map, that
normalization has a free divisor class group with the omitted prime
divisors as basis, and its canonical class is a nonzero positive
ramification vector in that basis.

This excludes torsion-canonical normalizations, including normal
global affine complete intersections and normal globally monogenic
models. It gives class-group rank bounds for the exceptional PSL2(11)
action. It does not by itself exclude that action.

Work over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field k. Let
F:X=A^d -> Y=A^d be polynomial with nonzero constant Jacobian, let
K=k(Y), L=k(X), and let nu:Z -> Y be the finite normalization of Y in L.
The finite normalization is fixed by the target polynomial ring,
rather than by a primitive-element equation chosen for L/K.

## 1. The boundary is a basis, and the canonical vector is effective

**KMFI87.** Regard X as an open subset of Z. Let D_1,...,D_r be all
codimension-one irreducible components of Z-X; omitted subsets of
higher codimension do not enter this list. Then

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Cl}(Z)
           =\bigoplus_{j=1}^r\mathbf Z[D_j].}
 \tag{1}
\]

For the canonical Weil class, and the ramification indices e_j of
nu along those divisors,

\[
 \boxed{[K_Z]=\sum_{j=1}^r(e_j-1)[D_j].}
 \tag{2}
\]

If [L:K]>1, some e_j>1. In particular r>=1 and [K_Z] has infinite
order. Its exact divisibility in this free class group is
gcd{e_j-1 : e_j>1}. This is divisibility of a Weil class, **not**
the Cartier or Q-Gorenstein index.

Here are the complete arguments.

The normalization is finite since k[Y] is excellent and L/K finite.
Every element integral over k[Y] belongs to k[X]: it is also integral
over this normal polynomial ring and lies in its fraction field.
The canonical map X -> Z is an open immersion by the
[algebraic Zariski main theorem](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03GS),
since F is separated and quasi-finite. Thus Z and X have the same
function field, and all regular functions of Z are polynomials on X.

Every Weil class of Z is generated by the boundary classes. Indeed
restrict a divisor to X; since Cl(X)=0 its restriction is principal,
say div_X(h). Subtract div_Z(h); only the boundary divisors remain.
There are no relations among them. A relation
div_Z(h)=sum a_j D_j makes both h and h^-1 regular on X, using
normality and the height-one valuations. The only units on X are k*,
so h is constant and all a_j are zero. This proves (1), not merely
a rank bound or generation statement.

For (2), use the rational top form
eta=nu^*(dy_1 wedge ... wedge dy_d). At every codimension-one point
of normal Z the source is regular. A tame extension of the relevant
discrete valuation rings has local target parameter t=u*s^e.
A separating residue transcendence basis supplies the other d-1
differentials. Differentiating gives top-form order e-1: the leading
coefficient e*u is a unit in characteristic zero. The residue
extensions are separable. Thus

\[
                         \operatorname{div}_Z(\eta)
                                  =\sum_E(e_E-1)E.
 \tag{3}
\]

This is the usual local ramification calculation behind
[Riemann-Hurwitz](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1B), applied
at the generic point of each divisor; the differential argument
above supplies the dimension-independent step.
On X, eta=(det DF) dx_1 wedge ... wedge dx_d is nowhere zero.
Consequently every term with e_E>1 is a boundary divisor, giving (2).

If all e_E were one, branch purity over the regular target would make
the finite map nu etale everywhere. Affine space over k has no
nontrivial connected finite etale covers, so [L:K]=1. These purity
and simple-connectedness inputs are the same as in KMFI1.
For degree>1 the vector (2) is nonzero, and the free basis (1)
proves every remaining assertion.

Unramified omitted divisors are allowed and contribute independent
basis vectors with canonical coefficient zero. The theorem assumes
neither that every omitted divisor is ramified nor that all unramified
normalization primes are present.

The [strict-local image refinement](LOCAL_IMAGE_AND_MARKS.md), KMFI94,
now shows that this boundary has pure codimension1: there are no
additional omitted components away from the listed divisors.
If the class rank equals the number of ramified normalization primes,
the affine source is exactly the full etale locus and every fiber
cardinality equals the common-fixed-sheet mark of its strict local group.

## 2. A normal complete-intersection model cannot be the normalization

**KMFI88.** For a fixed ordinary-affine target Y and finite field
extension L/K of degree>1, there is no ordinary-affine Keller source
model if its finite normalization Z has torsion canonical Weil class.
In particular this excludes:

- Cl(Z) torsion, including Cl(Z)=0;
- Z a normal global complete intersection in affine space, including
  a normal affine hypersurface;
- a globally monogenic normal integral closure
  \(k[Y][\theta]=k[Y,T]/(G)\), with G monic irreducible of degree>1.

These exclusions persist under independent affine stabilization with
the target ring fixed up to polynomial coordinates. They do not
cover an arbitrary birational replacement of the target ring.

The first assertion follows from KMFI87. For a normal global affine
complete intersection, at its regular codimension-one points the
conormal sequence has trivial determinant: both the ambient top
forms and the global regular-sequence conormal determinant are free
with specified generators. Hence its canonical Weil class is zero.
A normal monogenic model is such a hypersurface. For stabilization,
Z times A^s is the finite normalization for L(u)/K(u); normality
and the relevant torsion-canonical or complete-intersection property
persist. Apply the same theorem in dimension d+s.

The hypothesis concerns the **actual normal integral closure**.
A monic primitive-element equation whose ring is not normal is not
excluded. Smoothness and being locally a complete intersection do
not imply torsion canonical class. The next exact control shows
why both distinctions matter.

### An actual cubic map: its hypersurface is not normal, its normalization is smooth

Take the retained degree-three S3 map (KMFI11), with
u=1+xy and g=1-(3/2)xy+x^2 z:

\[
 A=(u+u^2-2u^3g)/x^2,\quad
 B=(1+2u-3u^2g)/x,\quad C=xg.
 \tag{4}
\]

The quotients are polynomials and the whole Jacobian is1. Set W=ug.
Then

\[
 W^3-W^2+BCW-AC^2=0.
 \tag{5}
\]

This monic hypersurface is singular along C=W=0, a codimension-one
subset; it is not normal. Let

\[
                  H=W(W-1)/C=-(B+y)/3.
\]

Its normal integral closure is the finite free rank3 algebra over
k[A,B,C] with basis 1,W,H and multiplication

\[
 W^2=W+CH,\qquad WH=AC-BW,\qquad H^2=AW-BH-A.
 \tag{6}
\]

It is the coordinate ring of the rank-one matrix locus

\[
 \operatorname{rank}
 \begin{pmatrix} H+B&1-W&C\\ A&-H&W\end{pmatrix}=1.
 \tag{7}
\]

The sum of the matrix's second and sixth entries is1. Thus the zero
matrix is absent; the rank-one determinantal variety is smooth
there, and this hyperplane cuts it transversely (the radial tangent
has nonzero derivative of that sum). Hence (7) is a smooth integral
threefold. It is the normalization: (6) spans it freely, independence
follows from H=(W^2-W)/C over K, and its fraction field is the source
field by the retained inverse formulas.

More explicitly, project the rank-one matrix to its column line
[a:b] in P1. It has two affine3 charts. With coordinates (r,c,q)
on a!=0, and (s,a,h) on b!=0, they are

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 &A&B&C&W&H\\ \hline
a\ne0&r q&q+r-r^2c&c&rc&-r+r^2c\\
b\ne0&a&s a-h&s(1+sh)&1+sh&h .
\end{array}
 \tag{8}
\]

On their overlap,
\[
 s=1/r,\qquad a=r q,\qquad h=-r+r^2c,
 \quad \det\frac{\partial(s,a,h)}{\partial(r,c,q)}=r.
 \tag{9}
\]

The overlap is G_m times A2. Each chart has trivial Picard group,
and its only units are k*. Overlap units are k* times r^Z. Line
bundle gluing therefore gives Pic(Z)=Cl(Z)=Z. Equation (9) gives
a primitive canonical class.

Put y=-3H-B on Z. The divisor where the column line is [1:y] is
an irreducible affine plane D. On the first chart its reduced
equation is
\[
               d=r-y=q-r+2r^2c=0.
\]
The finite projection to (A,B,C) has Jacobian -d on this chart.
On the other chart its equation is d2=1-sy=0; the transition
d=r*d2 shows [D]=[K_Z], the same primitive generator. This is
the only ramification divisor, of index2.

For completeness the complement Z-D is exactly the original A3.
The column line there normalizes uniquely to [x:1+xy].
The inverse on the first chart is
\[
 x=1/d,\quad y=-3H-B,\quad
 z=c d^3-d^2+(3/2)y d.
\]
On the second chart it is
\[
 x=s/d2,\quad y=-2h-sa,\quad
 z=\frac{d2}{2}
   (4y^2-a-sy^3+2say-s^2ay^2).
\]
These formulas agree on overlaps and are regular away from D.
They invert (4). The source patches x!=0 and u!=0 cover A3,
because u-xy=1. Thus this known actual map realizes precisely
the rank-one, primitive-canonical boundary case. Its normalization
is smooth and locally a complete intersection, but is **not a
global affine complete intersection**. No additional monodromy
occurrence family is inferred.

## 3. The boundary rank required by exceptional PSL2(11)

**KMFI89.** Suppose a Keller map has either exceptional degree11
PSL2(11) action. Then its finite normalization has a free class
group of rank at least2. More precisely, each nontrivial affine
target inertia divisor forces the following distinct ramified
boundary basis elements, using the complete local table KMFI82:

| Inertia | Decomposition | Mandatory boundary primes | Their canonical coefficients |
|---|---|---:|---|
| C2 | C2 | 4 | 1,1,1,1 |
| C2 | V4 | 3 | 1,1,1 |
| C2 | C6 | 2 | 1,1 |
| C2 | D12 | 2 | 1,1 |
| C3 | C3 | 3 | 2,2,2 |
| C3 | C6 | 2 | 2,2 |
| C5 | C5 | 2 | 4,4 |

The lower bound is the sum of the mandatory counts over all such
target divisors, plus any unretained unramified primes. Additional
unramified primes may also be omitted over target divisors with
trivial inertia, so the table is not a complete boundary census.
The canonical divisibility is exactly1 if any affine involution
inertia occurs, exactly2 if there is no involution inertia but
some inertia3, and exactly4 if all nontrivial affine inertia is5.
There is at least one nontrivial affine inertia divisor by KMFI1.

Consequently a rank-one normal completion, including the cubic
threefold (7), cannot support this action in any dimension.
A rank2 or higher normalization is a requirement, not a construction
or a sufficient condition.

There is a stronger bound for the surviving independent-curve
case of KMFI86. Let a and b count its affine target divisors with
nontrivial inertia2 and3 respectively. Then

\[
 a+b>0,\qquad
 \boxed{\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Cl}(Z)\ge4a+3b\ge3.}
 \tag{10}
\]

If a>0 the canonical vector is primitive; if a=0 it has exact
divisibility2.

To justify the sharper statement, complete the Galois curve cover
at a branch point over k. Its local extension is cyclic, of order
e in {2,3,11}, with equation z^e=t-c (and 1/t at infinity).
After independent parameter extension and completion at any
affine target divisor, every resulting factor is still a cyclic
Kummer extension of degree dividing e. If the target valuation
restricts trivially to k(t), the curve discriminant is a unit and
the induced inertia is trivial. At a finite nonbranch point the
local cover is split and again unramified.

Since each e is prime, nontrivial inertia must have order e and
its decomposition is exactly the same cyclic group C_e.
Inertia11 is forbidden by KMFI1, as it has no fixed sheet.
Thus only the rows (C2,C2) and (C3,C3) can occur with nontrivial
affine inertia; in particular C6 decomposition and inertia5
are absent in this independent-curve class. Apply the first and
fifth rows of the boundary table and sum. The argument does not
assume that every unramified tail is retained.

The general rank2 bound and torsion-canonical obstruction are
absolute necessary conditions on any proposed affine realization.
The sharper rank3 bound uses the curve presentation. None excludes
all possible exceptional degree11 maps, nor decides arbitrary
other actions. The full action spectrum remains open.

## Reproduction and scope of assurance

Run

    .venv/bin/python research/jacobian-followup/monodromy-theory/verify_boundary_class.py

The checker verifies the exact cubic map, normalizing algebra,
two smooth affine charts and both inverse charts, class-transition
determinant, ramification divisor and discriminant; it also derives
the boundary rows afresh in both exceptional actions and checks the
canonical divisibilities. The all-dimensional class-group argument
and normal-model obstruction have the written proofs above.
Primary-source hashes and scope controls are retained. These are
hybrid proofs, without formal verification, independent whole-proof
replay, or external review.
