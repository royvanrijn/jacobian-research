# Complete two-meridian tests at A_k branch singularities

The complete subgroup marks KMFI95 do not say which subgroups can
actually be images of a specified local branch complement. Here that
gap is closed for every A_k plane singularity, at the level of local
monodromy representations. In particular an ordinary cusp need not
have S3 image: A4 and either A5 class can also occur as finite images.
Conversely a tacnode already admits full PSL2(11) local image.

These are necessary conditions on a hypothetical Keller map, and
exact compatibility statements for local representations. No ordinary
affine-space realization or absolute exceptional-action exclusion follows.

## 1. Local presentation and the unbounded finite reduction

Work first over C. Let F:A^d->A^d be a polynomial Keller map with
either exceptional eleven-sheet PSL2(11) action, d>=2.
Assume that the **whole reduced branch divisor of the generic normal
closure**, near a target point y, is analytically
\[
             v^2-u^m=0,\qquad m>=2,                         \tag{1}
\]
times a smooth (d-2)-dimensional factor. This is A_(m-1).
There are no extra branch components in this hypothesis.

**KMFI98.** Its local monodromy image J is generated by meridians
a,b of orders in {2,3,5}, with
\[
       \operatorname{alt}_m(a,b)=\operatorname{alt}_m(b,a),   \tag{2}
\]
where alt_m is the alternating word of length m, starting with its
first argument. All possible representations into PSL2(11), all
image subgroup classes and both common-fixed-sheet marks are
exhausted by the retained295 simultaneous-conjugacy orbits of
184041 ordered pairs. The spectrum for every m is periodic modulo660.

Here is the geometric input and its scope.
Projection to u has two punctures in a transverse v-disk, exchanged
by a half-twist m times around u=0. The two meridians freely generate
the fiber complement. Filling the u-disk imposes equality with their
images under that braid; these two relations are equivalent to (2).
The local calculation for v^p-u^q and its node and cusp cases is
[Oka, Section2.2, printedpp3--4](https://indico.ictp.it/event/a04204/contribution/86/material/0/0.pdf).
The extra smooth factor contributes no fundamental group.
For example m2 gives ab=ba and m3 gives aba=bab.

Restrict the finite analytic normalization of the actual generic
normal closure to a sufficiently small ball with (1) deleted.
Its monodromy supplies such a quotient. A meridian around an actual
branch component is nontrivial. KMFI1 limits its order to2,3 or5.
For odd m the two meridians are conjugate, as they encircle the same
irreducible branch. This also follows directly from (2).
For even m there are two branches, and their orders may differ.

Every actual source point gives a local inverse section by the
analytic inverse-function theorem, hence a common fixed sheet of J.
Different source points give different sheets. Thus
\[
                    \#F^{-1}(y)\le |\Omega^J|.             \tag{3}
\]
This is also the analytic form of KMFI94. If there are no omitted
unramified normalization divisors, the full etale locus is present
and (3) is an equality. A point with mark0 must be omitted.
None of this requires F to be finite.

The tests also apply over algebraically closed characteristic-zero
fields when the branch germ has the indicated formal A_k type:
the map and the finite jet determining this finitely determined
singularity descend to a finitely generated characteristic-zero
field, which embeds into C. Geometric inertia, group images and
fiber cardinalities are unchanged after algebraically closed
constant extension. Alternatively the local tame braid presentation
is the profinite version of (2).

The reduction is finite for a mathematical reason, not a cutoff.
For c=ab,d=ba and m=2r or2r+1, respectively, (2) becomes
\[
              c^r=d^r,\qquad c^r a=d^r b.                 \tag{4}
\]
If o=ord(ab), both equations are periodic in m with period2o.
The element orders of G are1,2,3,5,6,11, so every2o divides660.
It therefore suffices to test one complete660-residue period.

There are429 nonidentity elements of allowed orders. Simultaneous
conjugation partitions all429^2 pairs into295 disjoint orbits.
The checker constructs every orbit, verifies disjointness and exact
coverage, computes each generated subgroup and both marks, and
tests (4) against the literal alternating word. Thus its660 rows
classify every m>=2, with no bound on k=m-1.
Each retained orbit representative also specifies an actual group
homomorphism from the presentation (2). This is not a polynomial
Keller map, nor an assertion that every such quotient extends to
a global Keller normal closure.

In particular neither C6,F55 nor G is an ordinary-cusp image under
the allowed inertia condition, although these subgroups occur in
the full abstract mark table. The two A5 possibilities matter:
an inertia5 cusp may force omission in one exceptional action and
permit one source point in the other. An inertia3 cusp has at most
one point when its image is A4, and at most two when it is cyclic.
The existing conditional S3 cusp statement KMFI96 is now one row
of a complete classification.

Here is a small explanatory check on the exhaustion. In the braid
group the central element is (ab)^3. A noncyclic subgroup of G that
is generated by a braid pair has, in this census, trivial center.
Quotienting the braid center gives the (2,3,e) triangle relations,
with e the meridian order. For e2,3,5 these give S3,A4,A5 as the
possible noncyclic images; the exact pair census distinguishes
the two S3 and two A5 embeddings. The census itself does not
assume a triangle-group classification.

## 3. Sharp limits of this obstruction

**KMFI99.** A full-G two-meridian local representation exists
exactly for the following m:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
m\ {\rm odd}:& 5\mid m\ \text{or}\ 11\mid m,\\
m\ {\rm even}:&4\mid m\ \text{or}\ 6\mid m\
                  \text{or}\ 10\mid m\ \text{or}\ 22\mid m .
\end{array}                                                \tag{5}
\]
If both meridian orders are restricted to {2,3}, the exact criterion is
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
m\ {\rm odd}:&11\mid m,\\
m\ {\rm even}:&10\mid m\ \text{or}\ 12\mid m\
                                      \text{or}\ 22\mid m .
\end{array}                                                \tag{6}
\]
The statements concern representations of the local group with
allowed meridians, not occurrence of G as a Keller action.
They follow by evaluating all295 pair orbits and (4); the checker
verifies the displayed divisibility criteria on the complete
period, hence in every degree.

In particular a **tacnode A3**, m4, is the first A_k type admitting
full G. Both meridians then have order5 and their product has
order2; there are2640 ordered pairs, in four conjugacy orbits.
For canonical matrices in SL2(F11)/{+I,-I}, one pair is
\[
 a=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\10&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 b=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\10&8\end{pmatrix}.                    \tag{7}
\]
Both determinants are1, their projective orders are5,
ord(ab)=2, and they generate all660 elements.
Therefore abab=baba=1. This explicitly gives a surjective local
monodromy representation despite the very mild branch singularity.

For irreducible A_k germs, m is odd. The first full-G image is
the ramphoid cusp A4, m5, again with inertia5. One exact pair is
\[
 a=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\10&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 b=\begin{pmatrix}1&7\\4&7\end{pmatrix}.                     \tag{8}
\]
It has orders5,5,5 for a,b,ab and satisfies the length-five relation.

Without inertia5 the first full-G type is A9, m10: the two
meridians have order3 and product order5. Among irreducible germs
the first is A10, m11, with meridian orders3 and product order11.
There are2640 and1320 ordered pairs respectively.

Since G has no common fixed sheet in either action, every point
with one of these full local images must be outside the affine
Keller image. Omission of codimension2 strata is allowed, as the
actual cubic control KMFI96 shows. Thus neither the existence of
these representations nor their forced omission proves or disproves
exceptional11 occurrence. In particular ordinary-cusp restrictions
cannot be extrapolated to tacnodes or to all branch singularities.
