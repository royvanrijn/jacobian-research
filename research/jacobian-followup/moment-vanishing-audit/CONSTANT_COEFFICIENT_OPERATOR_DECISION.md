# Exact constant-coefficient vanishing decisions from a Gaussian--Laurent encoding

15 November 2026. Internal all-order, all-degree constructive theorem.
The general solver is not implemented or executed. Classical algebraic
holonomic localization and integration are explicit inputs.
No novelty, independent replay, external review or formal verification
is claimed. The [checker](verify_operator_gaussian_laurent.py) tests
the contraction, shifted-amplitude identities and formal total derivatives,
including controls for missing logarithmic measure and invalid specialization.

## 1. Scope: every fixed operator order, without a positive vanishing conjecture

Let K be a specified effective finitely generated characteristic-zero field.
Let P,Q in K[x_1,...,x_n] and let

    Lambda=lambda(partial_x),       lambda in K[z_1,...,z_n]

have constant coefficients. No homogeneity or order-two assumption is made.

**Theorem.** There are terminating exact constructions deciding:

* whether Lambda^m(P^m)=0 as a polynomial for every m>=1;
* whether Lambda^m(QP^m)=0 eventually, for a specified Q;
* whether the eventual statement holds for every polynomial Q.

In the third case, failure has a computable bound on multiplier degree,
and a least failure-multiplier degree is computable. This decides whether
a specified pair (Lambda,P) is a GVC counterexample.
After an embedding K->C, the same finite tests cover arbitrary complex
multiplier coefficients, since each multiplier is a finite monomial sum.

For any polynomial parameter family over Q, a terminating construction also
computes the ordinary coefficient ideal generated by all coefficients in x
of Lambda^m(P^m), m>=1, a sufficient finite m-prefix, and the least such
prefix. Universal fixed n, symbol-order bound r and polynomial-degree bound
D are included, as are nonreduced constrained parameter families.
Finite lists of specified marked tails are included too.

These are computability results, not universal positive GVC, coefficient
classification, efficient complexity bounds or executed large calculations.
The operator remains lambda(partial_x) in n variables, with its actual
order. Auxiliary variables in the encoding are not a dimension- or
order-preserving positive-theorem transfer. In particular nothing here
settles the remaining Delta^k,k<=5, witness classification or quadratic
compression problem.

## 2. Contraction and one Laurent coefficient

Introduce n circular Gaussian pairs with

    E(Z_i W_j)=delta_ij,

and all other pairings zero. Regard the original x as independent
indeterminates, not as a point where the output is evaluated. Polynomial
Wick contraction gives the exact identity

    E(lambda(Z)^k Q(x+W) P(x+W)^m)
       =Lambda^k(QP^m)(x).                                  (1)

For a monomial Z^alpha this is the elementary Taylor identity
E(Z^alpha f(x+W))=partial_x^alpha f(x); linearity proves (1).

Define the normalized diagonal series

    H_Q(u,x)=sum_(m>=0) Lambda^m(QP^m)(x)*u^m/(m!)^2.

Then

    H_Q=CT_v E[Q(x+W)
                  exp(v lambda(Z)+(u/v)P(x+W))].             (2)

At fixed u-degree m, the v constant term selects exactly the m-th power
of lambda and the m-th power of P. Both factorials are retained.

More generally every amplitude A in K(x)[u,Z,W,v,v^-1] has a well-defined
series under the functional

    T(A)=CT_v E[A exp(v lambda(Z)+(u/v)P(x+W))].               (3)

For a term u^h v^r Z^a W^b, coefficient u^m has exactly
l=m-h and k=l-r in the two exponentials. If either is negative the term
is zero; otherwise it is one finite polynomial contraction divided by
l!k!. Thus every coefficient is a finite sum, even when the resulting
series is divergent analytically. No exchange of infinite analytic
integrals is invoked.

## 3. The correct localized connection, including the dv/v term

Put phi=v lambda(Z)+(u/v)P(x+W)-sum_i Z_i W_i. With the logarithmic
measure dv/v, the formal total derivatives acting on amplitudes are

    nabla_Zi=partial_Zi+v lambda_i(Z)-W_i,
    nabla_Wi=partial_Wi+(u/v)P_i(x+W)-Z_i,
    nabla_v=partial_v+lambda(Z)-(u/v^2)P(x+W)-1/v,
    nabla_u=partial_u+P(x+W)/v.                              (4)

The -1/v in nabla_v is essential. Equivalently the ordinary-volume
connection has generator exp(phi)/v.
Coefficientwise Gaussian IBP kills the Z,W total derivatives. For v,
the identity

    CT_v(partial_v B-B/v)=0

is just the vanishing residue of an exact Laurent derivative; it proves
T(nabla_v A)=0. Also T(nabla_u A)=partial_u T(A).
Every coefficient calculation is finite by section 2.

The connection is finite rank on the open set v!=0. Its localization,
as a module over the full polynomial Weyl algebra, is holonomic and
effective; then algebraic integration in Z,W,v is holonomic over the
u-line. These are classical inputs. For localization see
[Oaku, arXiv:1609.04676v1, section 2, theorems 2.11--2.14 and the following
algorithmic passage](https://arxiv.org/html/1609.04676v1).
For integration use the retained
[Oaku construction](https://www.lab.twcu.ac.jp/oaku/Oaku2013.pdf).

Concretely clear denominators in the first-order equations of (4), then
localize that presentation at v. On v!=0 normal ordering gives exactly
the rank-one connection. Clearing denominators alone can introduce
extra modules supported at v=0; do not use that unlocalized presentation
as the desired module.

Let H be its top integration module over D_u=K(x)[u]<partial_u>.
The functional T descends to a D_u-linear map H->K(x)[[u]].
Every seed, including Q(x+W), therefore has a computable nonzero scalar
u-differential annihilator with polynomial u coefficients.
An algebraic telescoping identity consists of that operator applied to
the seed and a finite sum of the total derivatives (4), with Laurent
polynomial amplitudes. Formal identities (3)--(4) prove annihilation,
without a contour assumption.

The [origin-indicial zero test](GENERIC_POINT_FINITE_MOMENT_ALGORITHM.md)
decides H_1=1, and the
[two-endpoint polynomiality test](FINITE_MULTIPLIER_MIXED_DECISION.md)
decides whether H_Q is polynomial in u. Its coefficients are polynomials
in x, so zero over K(x) is exactly polynomial zero in x. It is invalid
to substitute x=0: for Lambda=partial_x,P=x^2, every positive pure output
vanishes there, although Lambda^m(P^m) is nonzero for every m.

## 4. Why a finite module test covers every original multiplier

Full localization and integration compute finitely many D_u-generators,
represented by Laurent polynomial amplitudes. Testing only a seed
annihilator, or a basis after rational localization in u, is insufficient.

We must check that all-Q operator eventuality controls every such amplitude.
For the atom A=v^r Z^a W^b, its u^m coefficient, when m-r>=0, is

    1/(m!*(m-r)!) *
    sum_(j<=b) binom(b,j) (-x)^(b-j)
      partial_x^a [Lambda^(m-r)(U^j P(U)^m)]_(U=x).           (5)

The derivatives act before evaluating U=x; the displayed exterior
(-x) factors are not differentiated. This follows from (1) and
W^b=(U-x)^b. Polynomial u factors merely shift a finite number of indices,
and rational x coefficients do not alter eventual vanishing.

If r>=0, put n=m-r and rewrite the inside of (5) as

    Lambda^n[(U^j P(U)^r) P(U)^n].

If r<0, it is Lambda^(-r) applied to Lambda^m(U^j P(U)^m).
Thus all-Q GVC eventuality makes every amplitude series polynomial:
positive Laurent shifts are absorbed into a fixed multiplier P^r;
negative shifts add a fixed number of constant-coefficient derivatives.
Derivatives of a zero output polynomial remain zero. Initial exceptional
indices are finite.

Conversely if all amplitude images are polynomial, the particular
amplitudes Q(x+W) give the original eventuality property. Therefore

    all original Q eventually vanish
      iff T(H) is contained in K(x)[u]
      iff finitely many full generator images are polynomial. (6)

The image is nonzero because T(1) has constant coefficient one; if it lies
in the polynomial module, it equals that module, by its D_u-simplicity.

There is an explicit finite list of original candidate multipliers.
Expand each generator into atoms u^h v^r Z^a W^b. For each atom retain

    Q(U)=U^j P(U)^max(r,0),       j<=b.                      (7)

If all those finitely many original marked series are polynomial, (5)
makes every generator image polynomial, so (6) proves the all-Q property.
The converse is immediate. Thus the finite candidate tests themselves
decide the pointwise GVC conclusion.

A sufficient failure-multiplier degree bound is the largest
|b|+max(r,0)deg P among those atoms. If failure occurs, some candidate in
(7) fails. Finite monomial testing up to this bound then finds the least
witness degree, since a nonpolynomial finite linear combination requires
a nonpolynomial monomial image. No explicit new bound is computed here.

Constant coefficients are used both in (1) and in commuting fixed extra
derivatives with Lambda. Variable-coefficient operators are excluded.
For example (x partial_x)^2(x^2)=4x^2, whereas freezing the symbol and
using x^2 partial_x^2 gives 2x^2.

## 5. Ordinary operator-coefficient ideals are computable

For a polynomial parameter family over R=Q[c], let I_infinity be the ideal
generated by every x coefficient of Lambda^m(P^m),m>=1.
Given a proposed ordinary ideal J, take its full primary decomposition,
normalize each primary radical and inject its primary quotient into the
finite algebra A over the normalization fraction field, exactly as in
the [ordinary Gaussian ideal proof](PRIMARY_THICKENING_MOMENT_IDEAL_ALGORITHM.md).
Retain embedded components and nilpotents.

Now extend that scalar field by independent x variables.
A[x]->A tensor_F F(x) is injective, because A is a finite-dimensional
F-vector space. The localized connection (4) is a finite-rank connection
over this field with A-valued matrices. Holonomic localization/integration
and scalar annihilator enumeration give the same finite-coordinate
indicial zero oracle, with all coefficient-algebra nilpotents retained.

Testing T(1)-1=0 in every such algebra decides whether every output
coefficient belongs to J. If it fails, a finite u coefficient returns
an output polynomial Lambda^m(P^m) outside J[x]. Ordinary Groebner
membership finds one of its finitely many x coefficients outside J.
Adjoin that coefficient to J and repeat. Strict ideal ascent and ACC
force termination, with the full ordinary I_infinity as output.

All added coefficients come from finitely many m. Their largest index
gives a sufficient prefix; exact monotone Groebner tests against the
computed target give the least prefix. The same argument applies to a
fixed finite list of marked sequences or tails, by using their seeds
and subtracting the specified finite initial polynomials.

In particular n,r,D determine a computable finite pure-prefix function.
This is not a computed number, a scheme classification, or a least
Delta^k witness theorem.

For the tiny family Lambda=partial_x,P=a*x+b, one has
Lambda^m(P^m)=m!*a^m. The full ordinary coefficient ideal is (a),
with least prefix one. Modulo (a^2), T(1)-1=a*u is nonzero; replacing
the quotient by its radical would falsely lose the first obstruction.

## 6. Replay controls and source boundaries

The checker verifies contraction in one and two variables, including mixed
operator orders, Laurent shifts of both signs, total derivatives (4) and
the time connection. Omitting -1/v is detected already at Lambda=P=0,
A=v. It also detects invalid origin specialization, frozen
variable-coefficient symbols, and loss of a nilpotent coefficient.

The retained [Dvorsky--Long example](../../archive/non-elliptic/extended-geometry/DVORSKY_GVC5_COUNTEREXAMPLE.md)
is used only as an order-three regression: P=(T+C)(AD+BT),
Lambda=partial_T(partial_A partial_D-partial_B partial_C),Q=-C.
Its previously proved nonzero tail survives the (m!)^2 normalization.
The first few contractions check the new encoding; no new witness,
lower-order operator, auxiliary-variable compression or claim that a
bounded replay proves its tail is made.

[Belitskii--Boix--Kerner, arXiv:2607.27279v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27279v1)
was read in the introduction, selected results and section 2.3 definitions.
It concerns exponentiating x-adically nilpotent derivations and preservation
of power-series rings. General differential operators here need not be
derivations. We do not infer holonomicity from an unrestricted
exponential-closure rule: the explicit rational-phase connection proves it.
The abstract page lists 29 July 2026, while the HTML body prints
24 August 2026; both are retained in the source audit.

[Zhao, arXiv:0704.1691v2](https://arxiv.org/html/0704.1691v2), latest
listed revision 28 October 2007, was reread for nilpotence and the
order-two/Hessian distinctions in the introduction. Its historical open
statements are not the current status; existing local counterexamples
remain authoritative. No new positive vanishing theorem is imported.

The new external foundation is effective algebraic localization of a

Independent review of the diagonal/shift and ideal-oracle synthesis is
