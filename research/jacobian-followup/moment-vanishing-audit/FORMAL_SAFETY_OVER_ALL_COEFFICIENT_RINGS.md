## 1. Full statement

Let $A$ be **any** commutative $\mathbb Q$-algebra, $P\in A[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$
of degree at most $D\geq1$, and $T=\lambda(D_x)$ for an arbitrary formal
constant-coefficient symbol $\lambda\in A[[\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n]]$.
The symbol may have a nonzero constant term. All actions are defined by finite
jets on each polynomial. No Noetherian, reduced, finite-type or bounded-nilradical
hypothesis is placed on $A$.

The following are equivalent:

1. For every $Q\in A[x]$, $T^m(QP^m)=0$ for all sufficiently large $m$.
2. There are integers $C\geq1,E\geq0$ such that
   $T^m(QP^m)=0$ whenever $m>C\deg Q+E$.
3. The image pair over $A_{\rm red}$ has property 1.
4. The image pair over every prime residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ has property 1.

Thus qualitative safety is invariant under reduction and is detected by **all
prime residue fields**, even for arbitrary formal symbols. The bounds are for
the specified input, including its coefficient ring. If in addition the
original pair satisfies **every** positive pure identity $T^mP^m=0$, then
$C+E$ is a linear multiplier-degree cutoff with no intercept. In particular,
the complete formal-symbol eventuality-to-linearity implication holds over
**every commutative $\mathbb Q$-algebra**, with nilpotents retained.

There is also a finite certificate formulation: property 1 is equivalent to
the existence of integers $e\geq1,r\geq0,C_1\geq1$ such that

$$
 ((\lambda^e)_{\leq r}(D),P^e)\text{ has linear cutoff }C_1,
 \qquad r+1>eD(C_1eD+1).                                      \tag{1}
$$

For an all-order pure original pair, $e=1$ suffices. This is an existence
certificate, not an implemented general solver or a test for the least slope.

## 2. Affine bounds survive high-order tails

This extends the [retained tail argument](HIGH_ORDER_OPERATOR_TAILS_AND_FINITE_SAFETY_CERTIFICATES.md)
by carrying an intercept through the same inequalities.
Suppose over any $\mathbb Q$-algebra that $B,P$ have the affine cutoff

$$ B^j(MP^j)=0\quad\text{if }j>C\deg M+E,\quad C\geq1,E\geq0. $$

Let $R$ have order at least $s$ and set $a=CD+1$. If $s>Da$, then $B+R,P$
have the affine cutoff

$$
 m>\frac{sC+a}{s-Da}\deg Q+\frac{sE}{s-Da}.                    \tag{2}
$$

**Proof.** In the finite binomial expansion, the term with $R^\ell$ is

$$ {m\choose\ell}R^\ell B^{m-\ell}
       \big[(QP^\ell)P^{m-\ell}\big]. $$

It vanishes if $m>C\deg Q+E+a\ell$, or by derivative order if
$s\ell>\deg Q+Dm$. A surviving term therefore satisfies

$$
 m\leq C\deg Q+E+a\ell
 \leq C\deg Q+E+a(\deg Q+Dm)/s,
$$

which gives (2). The argument uses no division in the coefficient algebra.
Formal tails act locally finitely on every term. If $s\geq2Da$, the sufficient
integer coefficients $2C+1,2E$ work in (2). The negative tail can be used to
remove high-order terms as well. No pure assumption is needed here. ∎

## 3. Affine bounds lift through arbitrary nilradicals

Suppose the reduction of a specified formal pair over $A$ has an affine
cutoff $C_0,E_0$. Then the original pair has some affine cutoff, without
assuming pure identities over $A$.

Put

$$
 C_1=2C_0+1,\quad E_1=2E_0,\quad a_1=C_1D+1,\quad
 r=Da_1,\quad s=r+1.
$$

Removing the symbol tail above $r$ over $A_{\rm red}$ is legitimate, since
$s\geq2D(C_0D+1)$. Section 2 shows that the finite core $B=\lambda_{\leq r}(D)$
has reduced affine cutoff $C_1,E_1$.

Take the finite polynomial ring $S=\mathbb Q[c_1,\ldots,c_q]$ of all
phase coefficients through degree $D$ and symbol coefficients through order
$r$, including the constant symbol coefficient. Let $I_{C_1,E_1}$ be the
ordinary ideal generated by all output coefficients

$$
 B^m(x^\alpha P^m),\qquad m>C_1|\alpha|+E_1,\quad m\geq1,
$$

and put $J=\sqrt{I_{C_1,E_1}}$. These are ideals in a **finite** polynomial
ring; $J$ has finitely many generators. The actual coefficient map $S\to A$
sends $I_{C_1,E_1}$, and hence $J$, into $\operatorname{Nil}(A)$. The finitely
generated nil ideal $JA$ is therefore nilpotent: choose $L\geq1$ with
$(JA)^L=0$. No power of the full nilradical is assumed zero.

The coefficient argument in **MVPOWERALIGN20261115** extends to this affine
cutoff without change apart from retaining $E_1$. Explicitly, $u$ symbol
derivatives and $v$ phase derivatives, with $k=\max(u,v)$, give

$$
 (m)_u(m)_v D_\alpha B^{k-u}
 \{B^{m-k}[Q M_\beta P^{k-v}P^{m-k}]\}.                       \tag{3}
$$

The marker in braces has degree at most $\deg Q+Dk$. At every complex point
of $V(J)$ it is zero if
$m>C_1\deg Q+E_1+(C_1D+1)k$. Consequently all coefficient derivatives of
orders less than $K$ vanish there once
$m>C_1\deg Q+E_1+a_1(K-1)$.

Apply the **same retained radical differential-power / symbolic-power /
ordinary-power comparison** in the regular parameter ring $S_\mathbb C$,
and contract ordinary powers faithfully to $S$. Its hypotheses are unchanged:
$J$ is radical, the ambient ring is regular of dimension $q$, and the selected
field points satisfy the specified affine identities by definition of $I$.
This gives

$$
 m>C_1\deg Q+E_1+a_1(qL-1)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{each core output coefficient lies in }J^L.             \tag{4}
$$

This is an extension of the written power-alignment argument, not an
unqualified application of its previously stated pure linear-cutoff scope.
The derivative identity (3) and its degree estimate explain why the intercept
can be carried throughout. The retained Zariski–Nagata and ELS inputs are
used only in this finite regular parameter ring.

After evaluation in $A$, (4) supplies a core affine cutoff $C_1,E_2$, where

$$ E_2=E_1+a_1(qL-1). $$

Add back the original, arbitrary formal tail. Since $s=Da_1+1$, (2) gives
the explicit sufficient affine cutoff

$$
 C_A=sC_1+a_1,\qquad E_A=sE_2.                               \tag{5}
$$

These integers may depend on the finite core and its actual nilpotence
exponent $L$. All higher tail coefficients are unrestricted. The lifting
uses no section $A_{\rm red}\to A$ and no nilpotence bound on all coefficients.
The zero algebra is immediate; $J=0$ is handled by order-zero vanishing,
as in the retained comparison. ∎

## 4. Constructible compactness makes field bounds uniform for one input

Assume every prime residue-field pair of a fixed $A,P,\lambda$ is
qualitatively safe. We first obtain one affine bound over $A_{\rm red}$.

For each triple $(e,r,C)$ satisfying

$$ e\geq1,\quad C\geq1,\quad r+1>eD(CeD+1), $$

consider the full ordinary linear cutoff ideal for the finite pair
$((\lambda^e)_{\leq r}(D),P^e)$ with cutoff $C$. The coefficient ring of
that universal finite pair is a finite polynomial ring, so the ideal has
finitely many generators. Substitute the coefficients of $P^e$ and of
$(\lambda^e)_{\leq r}$. They are polynomial expressions in the coefficients
of $P$ and the finite original symbol jet through $r$. The result in $A$
is a finitely generated ideal $J_{e,r,C}A$.

Every

$$ V(J_{e,r,C}A)\subset\operatorname{Spec}A $$

is open and closed in the **constructible** topology: its complement is a
finite union of principal Zariski opens. This is precisely the topology
defined in [Stacks 08YF](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/08YF);
the finite-generated-ideal description is [Stacks 00F5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00F5).
For any ring, its spectrum with this topology is quasi-compact
([Stacks 0901](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0901)).

These sets cover the entire prime spectrum. To see this, fix a prime and
work over its residue field $K$. Eventuality for $Q=1$ gives an integer
$e\geq1$ such that $T^{em}P^{em}=0$ for all $m\geq1$. The pair
$(T^e,P^e)$ is pure and qualitatively safe, since all its marked sequences
are subsequences of original ones. If $P\ne0$ and the symbol constant
term is nonzero in $K$, $T$ is invertible on $K[x]$ (scalar plus a
degree-lowering operator), contradicting eventuality for $Q=1$.
Thus either $P=0$, which is trivial, or the symbol has zero constant term.
The [full field theorem](GENERIC_BERNSTEIN_EQUATIONS_AND_FORMAL_GAP_CLOSURE.md)
then gives a linear cutoff for the paired pair. Its
[finite-jet certificate](HIGH_ORDER_OPERATOR_TAILS_AND_FINITE_SAFETY_CERTIFICATES.md)
supplies some $r,C$ satisfying the displayed inequality. The chosen prime
therefore belongs to $V(J_{e,r,C}A)$.

Constructible quasi-compactness now gives a **finite subcover**, without
Noetherianity of $A$ or of the infinite symbol coefficient space. For a
triple in that subcover put $D_e=eD$, $a_e=CD_e+1$, $s=r+1$ and

$$ N=\left\lceil\frac{sC+a_e}{s-D_ea_e}\right\rceil. $$

At every prime in its subset, the retained tail theorem gives cutoff $N$
for the full paired pair. Write an original index as $m=ek+j$, $0\leq j<e$.
Then

$$
 T^m(QP^m)=T^j(T^e)^k[(QP^j)(P^e)^k],
$$

which is zero if $k>N(\deg Q+Dj)$. Thus this subset has the common
original affine bound

$$ m>eN\deg Q+(eND+1)(e-1).                                 \tag{6} $$

Take maxima of the two integer coefficients in (6) across the finite
subcover. Every original output above that affine bound is zero in every
prime residue field. Each of its finitely many output coefficients is
therefore in $\operatorname{Nil}(A)$, and the reduced pair has that one
affine bound.

This argument uses coefficientwise specialization of formal symbols only.
It does not identify tensor products of full formal power-series rings
with their coefficientwise base changes. Each tested output and each
certificate involves only finitely many coefficients. ∎

## 5. Completion of the theorem and certificates

Property 1 descends to every prime residue field: test monomial markers
first, then take a maximum of the finitely many thresholds for an arbitrary
field polynomial marker. Sections 4 and 3 prove property 4 implies property 2.
Property 2 implies property 1 immediately. Descent to reduction and the same
prime-field argument give the equivalence with property 3.

If all positive pure identities hold in $A$, a positive-degree marker with
degree $\delta\geq1$ satisfies
$C\delta+E\leq(C+E)\delta$. Constant markers are already zero at every
positive index by purity. This proves the full all-ring linear conclusion.

For the certificate statement, property 1 makes $T^mP^m=0$ for all
$m\geq e$ for one finite $e$. Then $(T^e,P^e)$ is pure and safe over $A$.
The all-ring conclusion just proved gives its linear cutoff, and the
retained all-ring finite-jet theorem gives (1). Conversely (1) gives a
paired linear cutoff by the high-tail theorem; (6) gives an original affine
cutoff. If the original pair is pure, use $e=1$.

No circularity occurs: Section 4 uses the **field** theorem and its already
retained finite-jet certificates; Section 3 is a direct finite-core argument;
only after proving the all-ring result do we extract the all-ring certificate.
No general Artinian-matrix twist, primary decomposition or coefficient-field
choice is needed. Those preliminary possible routes were not adopted.

## 6. Boundaries and preserved failures

The cutoff depends on the specified input. There is no bound independent of
nilpotent thickness in known negative sizes. For the retained DVG scaling
line, $P=\epsilon P_0$ over $K[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^L)$ is pure and safe,
but the fixed linear marker survives for every $1\leq m<L$; its least
integer cutoff is $L-1$ for $L\geq2$. Reduction gives the zero phase.
This is consistent with safety invariance and excludes transferring an
unchanged sharp field coefficient through arbitrary nilpotents.

Every prime is essential. Coordinate projections of an infinite product
of coefficient rings do not list all its primes; unbounded component
cutoffs alone do not give a counterexample to this theorem. Ordinary
Zariski quasi-compactness also does not suffice for a cover by closed
certificate sets. Their finite presentation makes them **open in the
constructible topology**, which is the compactness actually used.

The result does not prove pure-to-safe GVC, close either complete exceptional
Haar MZ case, compute a universal numerical cutoff, implement a general
ideal/holonomic producer, or cover variable-coefficient operators or formal
nonpolynomial phases/multipliers. All retained unsafe field seeds, raw-source
rigidity, reversed-time ODE and actual Rees/product countercontrols remain valid.
