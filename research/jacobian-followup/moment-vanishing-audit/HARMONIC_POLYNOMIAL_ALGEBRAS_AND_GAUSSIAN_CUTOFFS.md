# Harmonic polynomial algebras, finite translated gates and sharp Gaussian cutoffs

15 November 2026. Internal written deductions. Independent replay, external review and formal verification remain pending. Small exact controls check transcription only. No novelty claim or complete classification of Gaussian-safe phases is made.

The connection is to Oskar Riedler, *Polynomial harmonic morphisms and eigenfamilies on spheres*, Mathematische Zeitschrift 311,56 (2025), [published text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00209-025-03849-w), [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19565v2). Sections2.1 and3.1 supply the geometric terminology and explicit examples. The Gaussian cutoff and ordinary translated-gate arguments below are proved directly and do not rely on the deeper field-only implication in its Corollary2.5.

## 1. Heat pairing and the harmonic-power criterion over every Q-algebra

Let A be any commutative Q-algebra, with no reducedness or Noetherian hypothesis. Put R=A[X_1,...,X_n], Delta=sum_i partial_i^2, Gamma(F,G)=sum_i partial_i F partial_i G, and H=exp(Delta/2). The heat series is finite on each polynomial. Define the standard Gaussian functional by L(F)=(HF)(0).

For P in R the following are equivalent:

* Delta P=0 and Gamma(P,P)=0.
* Every polynomial in P is harmonic.
* Both P and P^2 are harmonic.
* H(P^m)=P^m as polynomials in the independent translation coordinates for every m>=1.
* H(P)=P and H(P^2)=P^2.

The chain rule gives

    Delta f(P)=f'(P) Delta P+f''(P) Gamma(P,P).

This proves the first three equivalences (use 2 invertible). On bounded-degree polynomials T=H-1 is nilpotent. Since Delta=2 log(1+T), ker(H-1)=ker Delta over A, proving the remaining equivalences. No positivity or analytic integration is needed for these statements.

Suppose also P(0)=0 and all monomials of P have degree at least r>=1. Then, for every polynomial Q of degree delta and every m>=1,

    r*m>delta  implies  L(Q P^m)=0.                            (1)

This includes all pure moments. To prove (1), the polynomial Gaussian source identity is

    L(exp(s dot X) F(X))=exp((s dot s)/2) (HF)(s).

It is an identity coefficient by coefficient in formal s; every requested coefficient is a finite polynomial calculation. It follows either by completing the square formally in the heat operator or from the monomial Wick formula. For F=P^m, harmonicity gives HF=F, whose order at the origin is at least rm. Multiplication by exp(s dot s/2) cannot lower that order. Extract the derivatives corresponding to Q. Thus no convergence, real contour change, coefficient-field hypothesis or large uniform nilpotence exponent enters (1).

Over C the coefficient 1/r is sharp for each nonzero P with actual origin order r. Write P_r for its lowest homogeneous part. The lowest part of P^m is P_r^m, of degree rm and nonzero. Choose alpha of that degree with nonzero coefficient. Extracting the s^alpha coefficient in the source identity gives

    L(X^alpha P^m)=alpha! [X^alpha] P_r^m !=0.

Hence no smaller real linear coefficient works for all Q. Equivalently Q_m=conjugate(P_r)^m gives the positive Fischer sum sum_|alpha|=rm alpha! |[X^alpha] P_r^m|^2. This is a family of markers depending on m, proving sharpness of a cutoff; it is NOT one fixed bad marker or a failure of safety. The least integer slope is still1 when r>=1; the reciprocal statement records the sharper rational degree bound. Over nonreduced algebras use only the lower-order bound (1), since products can have extra vanishing.

## 2. Exactly two translated polynomial tests generate the full ordinary gate

Fix n,D and let P be the universal degree-at-most-D phase over Q[c_alpha]. All identities below mean equality of coefficient ideals in this ordinary polynomial coefficient ring, with translations left as independent polynomial variables y.

Let J be generated by the coefficients of Delta P and Gamma(P,P). Then J is exactly the ideal generated by the coefficients of

    H(P)-P,       H(P^2)-P^2.                                (2)

It is also the ideal generated by H(P^m)-P^m for all m>=1. For any F of bounded degree, H-1 is Delta times an invertible finite triangular operator with rational coefficients. Conversely Delta=2 log(H). Thus the coefficient ideals of (H-1)F and Delta F agree. Apply this first to F=P and then P^2, and use Delta(P^2)=2P Delta P+2 Gamma(P,P). This proves the ordinary equality for (2), including every nilpotent coefficient. Modulo J, the chain rule proves every later identity; the first two are among them, giving equality with the full ideal.

An equivalent formulation uses centered translates:

    L_X((P(y+X)-P(y))^m)=0 as a polynomial in y.              (3)

The first centered test is HP-P. The second is HP^2-P^2-2P(HP-P), so their coefficient ideals again generate J. Modulo J, H acts as evaluation on the whole algebra A[P], and binomial expansion makes (3) zero at every m. Thus the whole translation-centered pure property has the literal ordinary prefix2 in these polynomial-output tests. For an ordinarily centered phase additionally impose P(0)=0.

This gate is stronger than ordinary pure nullity at y=0. It does NOT say the ordinary Gaussian pure prefix is2, does not improve cubic94, and does not impose purity for uncentered translates of a nonconstant phase. For example Z+Z^2 in a circular variable satisfies the criterion but has nonzero P(y) at general y; its centered translate is the object in (3).

For fixed r, add coefficients of P in degrees<r to the gate. Then all mixed moment coefficients with rm>deg Q lie in that same ordinary gate ideal, by Section1 over its universal quotient. This is an actual ordinary containment with no radical replacement. In particular the full mixed integer1-cutoff ideal and ordinary pure ideal are contained in the centered harmonic gate. Equality with the ordinary pure ideal in the unrestricted phase space is NOT asserted.

## 3. Whole polynomial algebras and composition

For any finite tuple F=(F_1,...,F_k) over A,

    Delta F_i=0,       Gamma(F_i,F_j)=0 for all i,j            (4)

is equivalent to every element of A[F_1,...,F_k] being harmonic. Necessity follows by testing F_i and F_i F_j; sufficiency follows from

    Delta f(F)=sum_i f_i(F) Delta F_i
               +sum_ij f_ij(F) Gamma(F_i,F_j).

No algebraic independence or injective polynomial parametrization is needed. These are finite polynomial coefficient identities in every degree and dimension. Equivalently H fixes the generators and their pairwise products; those tests generate exactly the full ordinary heat-fixing coefficient ideal. The same triangular argument as Section2 and Delta(F_iF_j)=F_i Delta F_j+F_j Delta F_i+2 Gamma(F_i,F_j) prove this over every Q-algebra.

Every centered P=f(F)-f(F(0)) consequently satisfies Section1 and the cutoff given by its actual origin order. Polynomial compositions of any degree remain in this class. Gaussian polynomial automorphisms from the [shear transfer theorem](GAUSSIAN_SHEAR_NORMAL_FORM_AND_TRANSFERS.md) carry these safe phases to further safe phases with the transported degree loss; the shear need not preserve harmonic powers.

## 4. Geometric scope, source examples and coefficient-ring boundary

Over C, (4) is precisely a Euclidean polynomial (0,0)-eigenfamily in the terminology of Riedler's Definition2.4; a single P is a polynomial harmonic morphism. This names a familiar sufficient structure, not a newly discovered harmonic-morphism theorem. Riedler's Corollary2.5 states a field-level shortcut: pointwise gradient isotropy for polynomials already implies harmonicity. Its underlying horizontally-conformal-polynomial theorem is credited there to Ababou--Baird--Brossard. We record this source statement as context only; neither our ring theorem nor the implemented classifier relies on dropping the harmonic equations.

The ring distinction is essential. In A=Q[e]/(e^2), P=e X^2 has Gamma(P,P)=0 and P(0)=0, yet Delta P=2e and L(P)=e!=0. Thus a field gradient-only shortcut cannot be used on arbitrary coefficient rings. The equations Delta P=0 must be retained for the all-ring certificate and the ordinary gate equality.

Harmonic algebras need not come from one fixed isotropic linear coordinate space. For a concrete source example, use three complex real-coordinate pairs z=x1+i x2, u=x3+i x4, w=x5+i x6 and a real coordinate t=x7. Example3.3(iv) at gamma=1 is

    P=z^2 w+2zu t-u^2 conjugate(w).                            (5)

Direct differentiation gives Delta P=0 and Gamma(P,P)=0: the w/conjugate(w) contribution is -4z^2u^2 and the t contribution is +4z^2u^2. It has order3 and hence the sharp Gaussian coefficient1/3. Every polynomial p(P) with p(0)=0 is safe; if p has orderk, its sharp coefficient is1/(3k), in unbounded degree.

At the real point z=1,u=w=t=0 its real-coordinate gradient in the w pair is (1,i); at u=1,z=w=t=0 it is (-1,i). Their complex bilinear pairing is -2. Gradients at different points therefore do not span an isotropic subspace, excluding a representation by a fixed isotropic linear coordinate space. This is the necessary direction of Riedler's Theorem3, also immediate by the chain rule for a polynomial in fixed mutually isotropic linear forms. The coordinate test does not assert that (5) lacks all torus-weight or nonlinear-shear descriptions.

The two quadratic generators in Example3.3(i), F1=zv+uw and F2=z conjugate(w)-u conjugate(v), form a tuple satisfying (4) in eight real coordinates. The generated polynomial algebra is harmonic even though their gradients across different points are not uniformly isotropic. Direct symbolic differentiation checks all equations without invoking a classification theorem.

This is a proper subclass of safe phases. In the normalized circular coordinates E(ZW)=1 and E(T^2)=1, the retained safe shear S=W-ZT-Z^3/2 has Delta S=0 but Gamma(S,S)=-2(T+Z^2)!=0. Its powers cease to be harmonic already at2; safety is retained by Gaussian conjugacy, not by (4). Harmonicity alone is also insufficient: the real linear T is harmonic, while L(T^2)=1. Neither these certificates nor the [real-image exclusion](GAUSSIAN_REAL_CURVES_AND_SMALL_BAD_MARKERS.md) classify every rank-two phase or settle the full general vanishing problem.

## 5. A direct ordinary-Laplacian GVC core and formal higher-order tails

The same differential hypotheses also give a direct vanishing result, stronger in a different sense than Gaussian integration. Centering P at0 is unnecessary here. If Delta P=Gamma(P,P)=0, then for every m>=1 and multiplier Q,

    Delta^m(Q P^m)=0 whenever m>deg Q,                        (5)

as a polynomial identity over every Q-algebra. More generally Delta^k(QF)=0 for k>deg Q whenever Delta F=0. Introduce independent x,y and apply Delta_x+2 sum_i partial_(x_i)partial_(y_i)+Delta_y to Q(x)F(y) before restricting to y=x. The three summands commute and Delta_y F=0. Every remaining factor differentiates Q at least once. More than deg Q factors therefore give zero. This proves (5) without any claimed transfer from scalar Gaussian moments to differential outputs.

The coefficient1 in (5) is sharp as a universal GVC coefficient even for fixed harmonic phase degree r>=1. In a circular pair with Delta=2 partial_Z partial_W, take P=Z^r and Q=W^m. At the boundary,

    Delta^m(W^m Z^(rm))=2^m m! (rm)_m Z^((r-1)m) !=0.

For r>1 this distinguishes the sharp GVC coefficient1 from the sharp Gaussian coefficient1/r; scalar expectation and polynomial differential outputs must not be identified.

Apply [the high-operator-tail theorem](HIGH_ORDER_OPERATOR_TAILS_AND_FINITE_SAFETY_CERTIFICATES.md) to this certified base B=Delta with cutoffC=1. For any phase degree boundD>=1 and any formal constant-coefficient tail R whose derivative orders are at least

    s>D(D+1),

the operator Delta+R has all positive pure outputs zero on P and the sufficient mixed cutoff

    m>ceil((s+D+1)/(s-D(D+1))) deg Q.                         (6)

This holds over every Q-algebra and for arbitrary finite or formal tails. It supplies all-degree positive operator cores without requiring the phase degree to balance the order2 Laplacian. For D>2 this is outside that simple equal-degree balance. The tail bound is sufficient, not asserted sharp; phase deformations and variable-coefficient tails are excluded. The existing high-tail bound calculator is implemented; this note supplies the new finite harmonic-core certificate feeding it. No general formal-symbol eventuality-to-linearity conclusion follows, and OP-GVC-FORMAL-LINEAR stays open.
