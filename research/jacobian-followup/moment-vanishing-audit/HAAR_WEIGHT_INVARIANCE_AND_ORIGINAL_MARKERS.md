# Haar weight invariance with all original markers

17 November 2026. Internal deduction checked by the argument below; no novelty
or priority claim. Independent replay, formal verification and external review
remain false. This is a written proof, with no computational checker claimed.

The [Gaussian weight theorem](GAUSSIAN_AMPLITUDE_IDEALS_AND_WEIGHT_INVARIANCE.md)
suggests an elementary Haar analogue for both exceptional models SL_2/2O and
SL_2/2I, and indeed SL_2/Gamma for every finite subgroup Gamma. The
[weighted Bezout identities](REGULAR_BEZOUT_MAPS_AND_WEIGHTED_HAAR_TAILS.md)
retain their Jacobian weights. The theorem removes a weight from qualitative
safety only when ALL original markers have been controlled. It supplies no
unweighted purity or exceptional MZ decision.

## 1. Statement and quantifiers

Let X=SL_2(C)/Gamma, A=C[X], and h be normalized Haar on the compact core
SU(2)/Gamma after conjugating Gamma into SU(2). For P in A and nonzero rho in A,

    [for every Q in A, h(rho Q P^m)=0 for all sufficiently large m]
       iff
    [for every Q in A, h(Q P^m)=0 for all sufficiently large m].       (1)

Each threshold may depend on Q, P and rho. No common threshold for the
entire algebra, nonzero h(rho), positivity, purity or degree bound is assumed.
With h_rho(a)=h(rho a) and sr denoting all-marker eventual vanishing,

    sr(ker h_rho)=sr(ker h).                                          (2)

The weight must be regular in the ORIGINAL algebra. An unverified rational
or ambient expression does not meet this premise.

## 2. Proof by the ideal of eventual-annihilating weights

Fix P and put

    J_P={a in A: for every Q in A, h(a Q P^m)=0 for m sufficiently large}.

This is an ideal: addition takes the maximum of two thresholds for fixed Q;
multiplication by b in A replaces Q by the fixed original marker bQ.

Let D in sl_2(C) act on A through the LEFT action. Left and right actions
commute, so D preserves right-Gamma-invariant regular functions. Differentiating
compact Haar invariance and complexifying gives h(Df)=0. Therefore, for m>=1,

    h((Da) Q P^m)=-h(a (DQ) P^m)-m h(a Q (DP) P^(m-1)).               (3)

For a in J_P the markers DQ and Q(DP) are fixed original regular functions.
If their thresholds are N_1,N_2, (3) vanishes for
m>=max(1,N_1,N_2+1). The factor m is harmless. Hence D(J_P) is contained
in J_P for every D. No invariance of P is required: DP stays in the marker.

The rational SL_2-action on A is locally finite. For every finite-dimensional
SL_2-submodule V, J_P intersect V is Lie-stable and hence group-stable,
because SL_2 is connected in characteristic zero. Thus J_P is group-invariant.
The classical finite-dimensional implication is
[Milne, section 2.8](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/2007a.pdf#page=8).
Equivalently for SL_2, exponentiate the two locally nilpotent root derivations;
their root subgroups generate the group.

A proper ideal of the finitely generated C-algebra A has a common zero.
The zero set of a group-invariant ideal is invariant; by transitivity a
nonempty such set is all of X. Since A is reduced, its ideal must then be
zero. Thus the only invariant ideals are 0 and A. No assumption that J_P
itself is radical was used. If rho!=0 belongs to J_P, then J_P=A and
1 belongs to J_P, proving the forward implication. The reverse implication
uses the marker rho Q. This proves (1)-(2).

The same proof works on any connected affine reductive homogeneous space
with invariant Haar: local finiteness, connectedness, transitivity, reducedness
and h(Df)=0 are the properties used. No arbitrary coefficient-ring extension
is asserted.

## 3. Finiteness behind the eventual quantifier

The ideal generated by all iterated Lie derivatives of nonzero rho is
nonzero and Lie-stable, so the preceding argument makes it A. Consequently
there is a FINITE unit identity

    1=sum_i a_i D_(i,1)...D_(i,r_i)(rho), a_i in A.                    (4)

Multiply by Q P^m and integrate. Repeated IBP moves each finite derivative
word onto a_i Q P^m. For m>=max r_i, the expansion uses finitely many fixed
rho-weighted original markers with powers P^(m-j), 0<=j<=max r_i, and
polynomial factors in m. Their finitely many thresholds have a maximum.
No infinite collection of marker-dependent bounds is interchanged.
This does not compute (4) for arbitrary rho or assert equal numerical
cutoffs, effective marker-degree bounds or equality of integration sources.

## 4. What the weighted transfer must cover

Suppose a regular Phi:X->Y has the identity

    h_X(rho Phi^*Q (Phi^*P)^m)=c h_Y(Q P^m), Q in C[Y].               (5)

Even if P is safe on Y, this initially controls only B=Phi^*C[Y] inside
A=C[X]. The set of weights annihilating these B-marked tails need not be an
A-ideal: multiplication by an arbitrary original function leaves B. Also
D(B) and B D(Phi^*P) need not lie in B. The preceding proof therefore does
not apply to the restricted-marker condition.

Sufficient additional input is a valid reduction of EVERY original marker.
For example, Phi^* may be surjective, or each R in A may have a finite
identity expressing h_X(rho R (Phi^*P)^m) through safe target marked moments
with fixed shifts. This must be proved in the original algebra. Equality
of fraction fields, a list of target markers or a Jacobian identity alone
is not a full-marker reduction.

For the regular exceptional Bezout maps the retained formula is

    h_Gamma(W_b (Q o Phi_b) (P o Phi_b)^m)=h_SL2(Q P^m).              (6)

The stronger R-model identities likewise test sigma_b^*R. Neither statement
alone controls ALL original markers of A_Gamma. Once that additional gate
is closed for a safe target phase, (1) removes nonzero W_b from safety.
It does not remove W_b from (6) term by term.

For the retained Hopf phase target safety is already false: the original
marker M_b=W_b(Q_H o Phi_b) has its positive infinite tail for every b.
This theorem leaves that tail and its missing unweighted all-positive purity
intact. The canonical first moments remain nonzero on both exceptional
models. Both complete exceptional MZ questions remain OPEN.

## 5. Purity can change on the exceptional models

Choose a nonzero right-Gamma-invariant homogeneous binary form p(z,w) of
positive degree, as a function of one row of SL_2. Such forms exist for
every finite Gamma: take a product of the orbit of a linear form.
It has one nonzero left-torus weight lambda. Hence h(p^m)=0 for every
m>=1. Each fixed Q has finite torus support, so h(Q p^m)=0 eventually:
p is safe.

Let p^dagger restrict to the complex conjugate of p on SU(2)/Gamma.
It is regular because conjugation of SU(2) matrix entries is polynomial
in opposite-row entries; right-Gamma invariance extends from the compact
core. Its torus weight is -lambda. Put rho=1+p^dagger. Then rho!=0,
h(rho)=1, and

    h(rho p)=h(p^dagger p)=integral |p|^2>0,
    h(rho p^m)=0 for every m>=2.                                    (7)

Thus p is unweighted pure and safe, while the rho-weighted functional is
safe but not all-positive pure. This is an actual original exceptional
algebra example. It proves that (1) cannot be upgraded to equality of
all-positive purity loci. It is not an exceptional unweighted MZ failure.
