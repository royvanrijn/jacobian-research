# A complete positively graded cubic chart and a global dimension theorem

17 November 2026. Claim `SIC2B33GLOBALGRADED20261117`. Work over a
characteristic-zero field, extending scalars when necessary to split
\(q=2zw+t^2\). The first moment removes the constant harmonic. Write
\(F=H_1+H_2+H_3\), with all fifteen coefficients unrestricted, and
\(\mu_n=h(F^n)\). The [checker](verify_highest_cubic_global_chart.py)
and [receipt](highest_cubic_global_chart_verification.json) support the
following result.

**Theorem.** The zero set of the first fourteen moments in the full
sixteen-dimensional phase space has dimension seven. The all-order pure
locus also has dimension seven. These statements include every quadratic
Jordan type and every point of \(D=0\).

More concretely, seven fixed rational rotation charts cover \(H_3\ne0\).
In each chart, removing amplitude and one unipotent parameter leaves an
explicit twelve-variable positively graded moment algebra. Its first-fourteen
quotient is finite over a five-variable polynomial algebra, generated by
at most 15,638 monomials of weight at most60. For the first eighteen
moments the bounds improve to 15,528 and56. Every fibre of these finite
maps has length at most the respective bound in characteristic zero.
These numbers are upper bounds, not asserted rational lengths or ranks.

This is a global dimension and finite-module theorem. It does not identify
the remaining pure components, prove a safe cover or establish full Image.
The finite-field Groebner step has an explicit Singular dependency;
there is no msolve dependency.

## 1. Seven fixed rotations give complete coverage

Use the circular harmonic bases in [the adapter](conformal_euler_cubic.py).
Let

\[
 M=w\partial_t-t\partial_z,\qquad
 \exp(sM):(z,t,w)\longmapsto
 (z-st-s^2w/2,\ t+sw,\ w).
\]

This preserves \(q\), orientation and \(h\). For each cubic basis vector
of charge \(j=-3,\ldots,3\), its transformed coefficient of \(w^3\) is
a nonzero constant times \(s^{j+3}\). The resulting map from harmonic
cubics to polynomials of degree at most six is an isomorphism. Evaluations
at \(s=0,1,\ldots,6\) have nonzero determinant, checked exactly.
Consequently, for every nonzero \(H_3\), at least one of these **seven
fixed rational rotations** makes its \(w^3\) coefficient nonzero.
The oriented swap \((z,t,w)\mapsto(w,-t,z)\) then makes its
\(z^3\) coefficient nonzero. No coefficient support is discarded.

On the open set \(\gamma_3\ne0\), divide the phase by \(\gamma_3\).
The same lowering transformation fixes \(\gamma_3\) and changes
\(\gamma_2\) to \(\gamma_2-3s\gamma_3\). Thus there is a unique
lowering parameter setting

\[
 \gamma_3=1,\qquad \gamma_2=0.
\]

Each such open set of the moment zero locus is therefore isomorphic to
\(\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^1\) times the normalized moment chart.
This explicit product, rather than an unrestricted group-orbit dimension
estimate, justifies adding exactly two dimensions later.

The normalized phase has coefficients

\[
 (l_w,l_t,l_z,b_{-2},b_{-1},b_0,b_1,b_2,
 c_{-3},c_{-2},c_{-1},c_0,c_1)
\]

and fixed cubic terms \(z^3+0z^2t\). The
[complete coefficient adapter](highest_cubic_chart.py) retains all linear,
quadratic and remaining cubic coefficients.

## 2. A positive grading and one global elimination

For a coefficient of circular charge \(k\), assign weight \(3-k\).
The thirteen weights are

\[
 (4,3,2,5,4,3,2,1,6,5,4,3,2).
\]

They are positive: the weight-zero coefficient of \(z^3\) has been fixed,
and the weight-one cubic coefficient has been set to zero. The action is
\(F\mapsto a^3F(a^{-1}z,aw,t)\). It preserves the normalized chart,
and \(\mu_n\) is homogeneous of weight \(3n\). Every normalized
moment zero set contracts to \(z^3\).

The second moment has constant pivot \(4/35\) on \(c_{-3}\). Its exact
elimination is

\[
 c_{-3}=-\frac{35}{4}\left(
 \frac23l_zl_w+\frac13l_t^2+\frac4{15}b_2b_{-2}
 +\frac2{15}b_1b_{-1}+\frac15b_0^2
 +\frac1{21}c_1c_{-1}+\frac17c_0^2\right).
\]

This is a polynomial substitution with no localization or omitted case.
The resulting ring has twelve variables, positive weights at most five,
and residual moments \(R_n\) of weight \(3n\), for \(n\ge3\).

At the origin of this chart, all order-\(r\) moment equations are thus
determined by

\[
 n\le\lfloor5r/3\rfloor.
\]

Indeed a monomial of ordinary degree at most \(r\) has weight at most
\(5r\). This finite-jet statement is not an infinite-tail safety theorem.
The bound is sharp for every \(r\ge2\): use the marker directions
\((w^2)^{3s}\) for \(r=3s\),
\((w^2)^{3s}(t^2-zw)\) for \(r=3s+1\), and
\((w^2)^{3s+1}w\) for \(r=3s+2\), at the indicated boundary moments.
Their spherical contractions with the remaining power of \(z^3\) are
nonzero. For the middle case the contraction is
\(-6s(6s)!/(12s+3)!!\), with \(s\ge1\). The eliminated quadratic
coefficient contributes no term to these particular derivatives.

Here is the characteristic-zero lifting argument. In each weight \(d\),
the degree-\(d\) ideal subspace is the image of a finite integer Macaulay
matrix: multiply every primitive equation by monomials of the complementary
weight. Matrix rank over \(\mathbb Q\) is at least its rank modulo the
prime. Therefore

\[
 \dim_{\mathbb Q}(R/I)_d
 \le\dim_{\mathbb F_{65521}}(R/I)_d.
\]

More specifically, adjoining the modular standard monomials to that
matrix has full row rank modulo the prime, hence over \(\mathbb Q\).
Those same monomials span the rational fibre, possibly redundantly.
All higher-weight pieces are zero. This argument relies on positive
homogeneity; it does not infer a characteristic-zero affine exclusion
from an inhomogeneous modular unit calculation.

Finally lift these monomials to the **full** twelve-variable quotient.
Induct on weight. Reducing a homogeneous element in the central fibre
expresses it in the listed monomials, plus a sum of base variables times
strictly lower-weight elements. The induction expresses it over \(A\)
in the same finite list. Thus the entire first-fourteen (respectively
first-eighteen) chart algebra is finite over \(A\), with the stated
generator bound. Tensoring this presentation with any base residue field
gives the uniform fibre-length bound. No freeness is asserted.

On the characteristic-zero **central fibre**, every moment with
\(n\ge19\) has weight at least57 and therefore is already zero in the
prefix18 algebra. Thus its full moment ideal equals its prefix18 ideal.
Its support is exactly the origin, and its ordinary maximal ideal has
nineteenth power zero. Neither this tail bound nor this zero-dimensional
statement applies to the full five-parameter-base family.

## 4. The dimension is exactly five on the chart, seven globally

For the lower bound, the entire one-sided nullcone appears explicitly in
the normalized chart. Start with a positive-charge phase and apply
\(\exp(sM)\). In final coordinates its image has

\[
\begin{aligned}
 c_0&=-s^3-c_1s,& l_t&=-l_zs,& l_w&=-l_zs^2/2,\\
 b_0&=-sb_1-s^2b_2,&
 b_{-1}&=-3s^2b_1/2-2s^3b_2,&
 b_{-2}&=-s^3b_1/2-3s^4b_2/4,\\
 c_{-1}&=-3c_1s^2-9s^4/2,&
 c_{-2}&=-5c_1s^3/2-9s^5/2,&
 c_{-3}&=-5c_1s^4/8-5s^6/4.
\end{aligned}
\]

The adapter checks this entire polynomial identity. The incidence
parameter satisfies the monic cubic \(s^3+c_1s+c_0=0\), so this image
is finite over \(A\). It projects onto every geometric base point and
has dimension five; its parametrization has Jacobian rank five at a
retained rational point. All its phases are pure and full-Image safe by
the existing one-sided theorem. Thus the chart has dimension at least
five for every prefix and for the full pure ideal. Finiteness over the
five-dimensional base supplies the reverse bound.

Each of the seven cubic opens is a product with two normalization
parameters, so its first-fourteen and all-order loci have dimension
seven. The complement \(H_3=0\) has only eight coefficients after
\(\mu_1\); its nonzero quadratic polynomial \(\mu_2\) bounds its
moment zero locus by dimension seven as well. This proves both global
dimension assertions. The degree-at-most-two Image result already
handles safety on that complement.

This also gives a quantitative bound for the original cyclic target:
\(\dim A_N\le3\) for every \(N\ge14\), with no assertion of
finiteness. Indeed the original prefix cone has dimension at most seven.
On every irreducible component meeting \(D\ne0\), amplitude scaling
makes the homogeneous degree-six invariant \(D\) nonconstant; imposing

trivial oriented stabilizer and its quotient removes exactly three
rotation dimensions. Thus \(7-1-3=3\). Zero-dimensionality and length51
remain open; a bound of three does not imply either.

## 5. A global finish can be studied at this one singular phase

Every irreducible all-order pure component meeting \(H_3\ne0\) contains
\(z^3\). The connected rotation and amplitude groups preserve each
component. The seven fixed charts put a point of that component in the
normalized chart, and the positive grading contracts it to \(z^3\).

In fact the full normalized moment algebra embeds in its localization
at the graded origin and then in its completion. If a polynomial with
nonzero constant term killed a nonzero graded element, the lowest-weight
term of their product would be nonzero. The localization map is therefore
injective. Krull intersection gives injectivity into the local completion.
The same statements apply to the reduced moment algebra.

Consequently, proving all-original-marker tail identities in the completed
**reduced** normalized pure germ would prove those identities globally
on this chart. Equivalently one may establish generic safety on every
actual pure component through this germ and use the retained polynomial
descent theorem. The seven rotations and two normalization parameters
then cover every cubic phase. This requires actual component or marked-tail
information; the dimension theorem and the finite-jet bound alone do not
supply it. Original markers and all output coefficients must be retained.

For a homogeneous target of weight \(d\), ordinary ideal membership in
the full moment ideal uses only moments \(n\le\lfloor d/3\rfloor\)
and multiplier monomials of weight \(d-3n\). For a radical target
\(f^e\), replace \(d\) by \(e\deg f\). Thus a fixed power gives an
explicit finite rational linear-algebra problem. The needed radical
exponents and the component decomposition are still unknown.
