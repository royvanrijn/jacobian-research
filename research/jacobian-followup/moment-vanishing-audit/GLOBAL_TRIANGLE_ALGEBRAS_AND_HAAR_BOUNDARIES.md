The global period lost by branch completion has an explicit description
on the **entire original left-torus-invariant algebra**. Both quotients
become polynomial/Laurent algebras with three exact conditions: at the
two radial endpoints and at one branch value. Haar is an interval
integral followed by a Laurent constant coefficient. This controls all
markers for a torus-invariant phase. It also gives the exact domain
for every auxiliary height in the equivariant Hopf construction.

This is not a reduction of every possible exceptional phase to a
torus-invariant one. The full exceptional MZ problems and the complex

## 1. Coordinates and the full theorem

Write A_Gamma=C[SL_2/Gamma] and let T be the original left diagonal
torus. Use the [exact quadratic model](QUADRATIC_TRACE_MODELS_AND_HAAR_RESIDUES.md)
with XG-YF=1, eta^2=K,delta=F partial_X+G partial_Y, and inherited
weights(m,n,-n,-m,-r_0). Define

    r=XG,
    2O: j=F^3/(108G^2), (p,q)=(4,3),
    2I: j=-1728F^5/G^3, (p,q)=(3,5).                        (1)

Every element of A_Gamma^T has a unique expression

    P(r,j)=sum_(k in Z) A_k(r) j^k,
    A_k in C[r], finite sum.                               (2)

Precisely, the image is the following algebra B_(p,q):

    k>0: r^(pk) divides A_k(r),
    k<0: (r-1)^(-qk) divides A_k(r),                         (3)

and, writing P=sum_l a_l(j)r^l,

    (j-1)^ceil(l/2) divides a_l(j) in C[j,j^(-1)]
                                      for every l>=1.       (4)

These conditions are necessary and sufficient in ALL degrees. They
are linear conditions on the coefficients for any supplied finite
expression. No condition is imposed on the constant coefficient
a_0(j) except what is already forced by (3).

For every such P, normalized Haar is exactly

    h_Gamma(P)=integral_0^1 A_0(r)dr
              =CT_j integral_0^1 P(r,j)dr.                 (5)

The endpoint powers4/3 and3/5 retain the respective exceptional
boundary conditions. The order-two branch produces the ceiling in
(4). Omitting any of these conditions gives a different algebra.

## 2. Proof of the complete global regularity criterion

The exact image S in the quadratic model consists of a_0+eta a_1
whose jth delta derivatives have the specified branch divisibility.
All weights of the ordinary target A_*=C[SL_2] are multiples of4;
the eta weight is2 modulo4. Consequently a weight-zero element of S
has no odd eta part. It is an element of A_* of inherited weight0.

On FG!=0, the target ring is C[r,F^(+-1),G^(+-1)]. Its weight-zero
monomial lattice is generated by F^3/G^2 on2O and F^5/G^3 on2I.
Both exponent pairs are primitive. This proves the unique form (2).

First impose ordinary target regularity. The exact endpoint rule
from the quadratic model says that a Laurent coefficient of F^aG^b
must be divisible by r^max(0,-b)(r-1)^max(0,-a). Thus initially

    2O: r^(2k) for k>0, (r-1)^(-3k) for k<0,
    2I: r^(3k) for k>0, (r-1)^(-5k) for k<0.               (6)

Next impose the original quotient's even-image test

    delta^d P divisible by K^ceil(d/2) in A_* for all d>=1. (7)

On the branch j=1, both F and G are units, and

    delta^d P=(FG)^d partial_r^d P.

Since K is a unit times(j-1) there, (7) is exactly
partial_r^d P divisible by(j-1)^ceil(d/2). Taking its constant
coefficient in r at d=l proves the necessity of (4). Conversely,
(4) proves every one of those derivative divisibilities, since
ceil(l/2)>=ceil(d/2) whenever l>=d.

On2I, K=G^3(1-j)/144 has no other divisor on the target SL_2:
its zero locus has F,G both nonzero. Thus (6),(4) suffice globally.

On2O, K=-3G^3(1-j) also has the component G=0, missed by that
chart. At its generic point F is a unit and r=XG. The individual
term r^l j^k has G-order l-2k. Its dth delta derivative, when
nonzero, has the SAME G-order:

    delta^d(r^l j^k)=const*(l)_d
                     X^(l-d) F^(3k+d) G^(l-2k).

Different (l,k) cannot cancel: their X and G exponents determine
l,k uniquely. The strongest condition for this term is at d=l,
namely

    l-2k>=ceil(l/2), equivalently l>=4k.

For k>0 this changes r^(2k) in (6) to r^(4k); for k<=0 it adds
no condition. K has these two reduced components and no others.
The target is normal, so divisibility by K^N is checked along its
prime divisors. This proves necessity and sufficiency of (3)-(4).

Finally the quadratic trace theorem gives h_Gamma(P)=h_*(P).
The target interval/constant-term formula takes CT_(F,G) after
integrating in r. Only the j^0 term can have zero F,G exponents,
so it becomes (5), with no additional normalizing factor.

## 3. All markers, global heights and the exact scope

If a phase P is T-invariant and Q is ANY original regular marker,
torus projection gives

    h_Gamma(Q P^m)=h_Gamma(Q_0 P^m), Q_0 in A_Gamma^T.       (8)

Every Q_0 in the invariant algebra is itself an allowed marker.
Therefore eventual marked safety for each T-invariant phase is
equivalent in the full quotient and in B_(p,q) with functional (5).
This is an exact all-marker reduction for this class of phases.
It does not assert that arbitrary original phases are T-invariant
after an automorphism or another permitted change of variables.

Every equivariant Hopf auxiliary height tau_b lies in this complete
algebra. Its remaining condition is the exact global identity

    CT_j integral_0^1 1/(1-t*tau_b(r,j)^2) dr
                         =artanh(sqrt(t))/sqrt(t).          (9)

It is a formal-series equality in t, with (3)-(4) retained. Thus
both endpoint divisors and the branch at j=1 are present in the
height problem. Branch completion cannot replace this formula by
its value on j=1 or omit the endpoint constraints.

## 4. A universal Newton bound and an exact finite linear model

Let P be nonconstant, with least and greatest Laurent exponents
-a,b. Then a,b are positive integers. Writing W=a+b, we have

    deg_r P<=2W,
    p*b<=2(a+b), q*a<=2(a+b).                               (10)

Proof. After multiplication by a Laurent unit, each nonzero
coefficient a_l(j) in (4) is a polynomial of degree<=W. It cannot
vanish at j=1 to order greater than W. Hence l<=2W. The largest
positive exponent's nonzero coefficient is divisible by r^(pb),
and the least negative exponent's coefficient by(r-1)^(qa).
Their radial degrees give the other two bounds. If there were only
nonnegative exponents and a positive one b, these same bounds would
give p*b<=2b, impossible since p>2. The nonpositive case is similar;
support only at0 forces constants directly by (4).

In particular the allowed endpoint ratios are

    2O: 1<=a/b<=2,
    2I: 1/2<=a/b<=2/3.                                    (11)

For general p,q>2 the same algebraic argument would require
(p-2)(q-2)<=4 for a nonconstant element. Here the strict inequality
matches the spherical signatures(2,3,4) and(2,3,5). This is an
elementary consistency consequence of the conditions, not an
exhaustion claim about other groups or hyperbolic orbifolds.

For any prescribed window[-a,b], (10) bounds radial degree by2W.
All conditions (3)-(4) are therefore a finite EXACT linear system.
One may first multiply monomials by their endpoint factors, then
impose the finite j=1 derivatives. This supplies a complete coefficient
space for that window, rather than a guessed degree truncation.
No finite universal Laurent-width bound or purity criterion follows.

## 5. Three generators from the full degree filtration

There is also a finite global generating description. For a binary
invariant q(z,w) of even degree2d define its middle polarization

    M_q=[t^d] q(x+t z,y+t w).

Its original left weight is0 and its unipotent depth is d. The
filtration by radial degree in (2) equals that depth filtration.
The leading map D^d/d! identifies its associated graded algebra with
C[z,w]^Gamma, with bottom degrees divided by2. This follows from
the SL_2 ladder decomposition: a weight-zero vector from V_(2d)
has depth d, and every bottom invariant of degree2d is reached.
Leibniz gives multiplicativity.

Since the full Klein base algebra is generated by f,g,c, finite
descending degree induction gives

    A_Gamma^T=C[M_f,M_g,M_c].                               (12)

Its associated-graded Hilbert series is

    2O: (1+t^9)/((1-t^4)(1-t^6)),
    2I: (1+t^15)/((1-t^6)(1-t^10)).                         (13)

The one quadratic Klein relation lifts to a relation with the same
leading weighted part plus lower weighted terms. Standard finite
leading-term reduction proves that it generates all relations, so
this is a filtered hypersurface deformation of that Klein base.
The lower relation is not expanded in the retained checker.

This is finite degree induction for a supplied polynomial, not a
continuous passage to a spatial formal completion. It supplies no
MZ ascent from the associated graded; the earlier non-transfer
example remains in force.
