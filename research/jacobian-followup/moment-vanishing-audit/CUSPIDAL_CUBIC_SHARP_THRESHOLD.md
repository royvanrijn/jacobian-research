# Homogeneous cuspidal ternary cubics: sharp pure threshold ten

Claim `G3CUSP10_20261114`, over C in characteristic zero.

Every homogeneous cubic in three independent standard real Gaussian
coordinates whose complex projective curve is irreducible and cuspidal
has a nonzero positive pure moment by order10. This bound is sharp.
Consequently this entire homogeneous cuspidal class is excluded from
the all-order pure-vanishing hypothesis in `OP-GMC3-CUBIC`.

The explicit sharp witness is

    Z=(X+iY)/sqrt(2), W=(X-iY)/sqrt(2),
    P=Z^2*T+W^3,

where X,Y,T are independent standard real Gaussians. Its first nine
positive pure moments vanish and

    E(P^10)=1508855040000.

The exact all-order formula is

    E(P^n)=0                         if 10 does not divide n,
    E(P^(10r))=binomial(10r,6r)*(12r)!*(6r-1)!!  for r>=1.       (1)

Thus the least initial pure-moment prefix that detects all-order
vanishing for **arbitrary ternary cubics** is at least10. No global upper
bound is established here. The earlier sharp8 result for centered
three-factor products and lower6 example on the smooth-leading locus
retain their narrower scopes.

The subsequent [full affine cuspidal theorem](CUSPIDAL_AFFINE_EXCLUSION.md)
closes the entire cuspidal-leading GMC class, including all lower terms.
It proves pure-nonvanishing for nonisotropic cusps and F2(cusp)!=0;
the isotropic remaining chart uses the W-linear GMC theorem. The sharp10
bound here retains its homogeneous scope.

## 1. Elementary cuspidal normal form

Put a cuspidal singular point at [0:0:1]. Homogeneity and singularity
write the cubic as Z*Q2(X,Y)+C3(X,Y). Its nonzero repeated tangent cone
has rank1, so a linear change puts Q2=X^2. Write

    F=X^2*Z+u*X^3+v*X^2*Y+w*X*Y^2+h*Y^3.

Irreducibility forces h!=0; if h=0 the polynomial has the factor X.
Put s=-w/(3h), and substitute

    Y -> Y+s*X,
    Z -> Z+z0*X+z1*Y,
    z1=-v-2w*s-3h*s^2,
    z0=-u-v*s-w*s^2-h*s^3.

The transformation has determinant1, and direct expansion leaves
X^2*Z+h*Y^3. Rescaling Y gives the normal form

    F=X^2*Z+Y^3.                                             (2)

Conversely (2) is irreducible and its only projective singular point
is [0:0:1], with a double tangent. Hence this reduction covers the
entire stated class, rather than a selected coefficient family.

After the invertible linear change of three Gaussian coordinates the
covariance is an arbitrary invertible complex symmetric matrix

    G=[[a,d,e],[d,b,f],[e,f,c]],   Delta=det(G)!=0.             (3)

There is no positive-semidefinite restriction: the coefficients are
complex. Invertibility follows from G=B*B^T with B invertible. It is
essential to the conclusion.

## 2. Five exact covariance charts

Let M_n(G)=E((X^2*Z+Y^3)^n). Odd n vanish by total-degree parity. The
first five even moments are computed by Wick pairings. In particular

    M_2/3=a^2*c+2a*b*f+4a*e^2+5b^3+4b*d*e+4d^2*f.           (4)

The transformations

    (X,Y,Z) -> (lambda*X,mu*Y,mu^3*lambda^(-2)*Z)

multiply F by mu^3. They preserve all the required moment-zero equations.
Their covariance weights are

    a:(2,0), b:(0,2), c:(-4,6),
    d:(1,1), e:(-1,3), f:(-2,4).

Every monomial in M_n has weight(0,3n), checked from the full symbolic
formulas. Nonzero complex lambda and mu give the following exhaustive
normalizations. Equation (4) then eliminates one variable with a nonzero
constant coefficient.

| Original branch | Normalization | Eliminated variable |
|---|---|---|
| a*b!=0 | a=b=1 | c |
| a=0, b*d!=0 | b=d=1 | f |
| b=0, a*d!=0 | a=d=1 | c |
| a!=0, b=d=0 | a=1 | c |
| a=b=0, d!=0 | d=1 | f |

Two remaining branches need no calculation. If a=d=0 and b!=0, (4)
is5b^3 and is nonzero. If a=b=d=0, the Gram determinant is zero, excluded
by (3). The normalizations exist over C: normalize a or b using a square
root where nonzero, then normalize d with the second torus parameter.

In each chart let J be generated by M4,M6,M8,M10 after this exact M2
elimination, and let delta be the resulting determinant, up to a nonzero
rational scalar. All generator denominators are cleared before passing
integer polynomials to Singular. Exact rational calculations give

    a=b=1 chart:       delta^4 in J;
    each other chart: delta^12 in J.                         (5)

The four two-variable boundary computations also give J:delta^infinity=(1).
They are checked a second time by exact symbolic polynomial division.
For the generic three-variable chart a completed52-generator rational
standard basis is retained. Its determinant-power normal forms have
11,113,113,0 terms for powers1,2,3,4 respectively. Thus the fourth-power
membership in (5) is an exact rational result; it is not inferred from
a finite-field unit computation.

If M2,M4,M6,M8,M10 all vanished with Delta!=0, one of the five charts
would apply and contradict (5), or one of the two direct branches would
already contradict (4) or (3). This proves the upper bound10 on the
entire irreducible homogeneous cuspidal locus.

## 3. The sharp witness and its all-order formula

The Gaussian covariance in Z,W,T coordinates is

    [[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,1]],

which has determinant-1 and comes from the displayed invertible change
of three real Gaussian coordinates. The polynomial Z^2*T+W^3 is exactly
the normal form (2), so its projective curve is irreducible and cuspidal.
Its cusp is [Z:W:T]=[0:0:1], a nonisotropic point.

Expand P^n, taking j copies of Z^2*T and n-j copies of W^3. The Wick
expectation can be nonzero only if

    2j=3(n-j),  and j is even.

These conditions force n=10r and j=6r. There is exactly one surviving
term, and E(Z^(12r)*W^(12r))=(12r)! while E(T^(6r))=(6r-1)!!.
This proves (1) for every order, and proves sharpness10 without a finite
extrapolation. In particular, the example has nonzero moments at every
positive multiple of10; it is a finite-prefix witness.
