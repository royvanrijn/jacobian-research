# Nilpotent lifting and the formal geometry of mixed safety

15 November 2026. Internal written deductions from the
[Gaussian field theorem](FIBERWISE_POLYNOMIAL_HOM_AND_GAUSSIAN_FIELD_UNIFORMITY.md)
and the [all-model transfers](POLYNOMIAL_GAUSSIAN_REDUCTION_AND_ALL_MODEL_FIELD_UNIFORMITY.md).
These foundations and the deductions below await independent specialist review.
Classical differential/symbolic-power comparison and uniform symbolic containment
are imported explicitly. No novelty or formal verification is claimed. The
[small checker](verify_nilpotent_safe_completion.py) tests identities and failure
controls; it does not prove the imported theorems or implement a general solver.

## 1. Coefficient space and the pure equations

Fix Gaussian dimension n and phase degree d. Set

    R=Q[c_beta: |beta|<=d],  q=binomial(n+d,d),
    P=sum_beta c_beta X^beta,
    I_pure=(E(P^m):m>=1).

Let I_N be the ordinary ideal generated by all E(X^alpha P^m) with
m>N|alpha|, m>=1, for an integer N>=0. In particular I_pure is contained
in every I_N. All ideals are coefficient ideals, with their nilpotents retained.
The existing algorithms compute each I_N and I_pure theoretically; they have
not been executed for the universal family here.

Choose an integer C>=1 supplied by the field theorem. Thus the pure-null safe
field locus is V(J), where

    J=sqrt(I_C),   sqrt(I_N)=J for every N>=C.

J is a radical ideal over Q. Its scalar extension to C is radical, since Q is
perfect and R/J is a reduced finite-type algebra. The safe locus is nonempty
(it contains the zero phase), so J is proper. The case J=0 is allowed below.

The word safe means eventual vanishing for every polynomial multiplier.
Pure-null means that all positive pure moments vanish, including over rings.
These are separate requirements throughout.

## 2. Coefficient derivatives measure thickness

For a monomial marker X^alpha of degree delta, write

    mu_(alpha,m)=E(X^alpha P^m).

For a coefficient multi-index gamma with |gamma|=k<=m, direct differentiation
in R gives the identity

    partial_c^gamma mu_(alpha,m)
      =(m)_k E[X^(alpha+sum_beta gamma_beta beta) P^(m-k)].       (1)

The new marker degree is at most delta+dk. At each complex point of V(J),
the common field cutoff therefore makes (1) zero whenever

    m>C delta+(Cd+1)k.                                         (2)

The order-zero derivative is included. Derivatives with k>m vanish anyway.
Consequently, for K>=1,

    m>C delta+(Cd+1)(K-1)
       implies mu_(alpha,m) in J_C^{<K>}.                       (3)

Here J_C=J C[c] and the differential power consists of polynomials whose
coefficient partial derivatives of orders less than K all lie in J_C.
Vanishing at all complex points implies membership because J_C is radical.

Two classical inputs now do the essential work:

* Zariski--Nagata for radical ideals in a characteristic-zero polynomial
  ring identifies J_C^{<K>} with the symbolic power J_C^{(K)}. The exact
  radical-ideal statement and its reduction to primes are in Sidman--Sullivant,
  Corollary 2.9, printed page937 (PDF page8),
  [published source](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-2009-047-5).
* Ein--Lazarsfeld--Smith, Theorem A / Theorem2.2, proves
  J_C^{(qL)} contained in J_C^L for every L>=1. The ambient dimension q
  bounds the codimension of every component. See printed pages2,7--8 of
  [math/0005098v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0005098v3), revised4October2000.
  The theorem concerns a smooth ambient space and a radical ideal; it is
  applied in R_C, never in the potentially singular pure quotient.

Faithfully flat contraction of J_C^L back to R yields the ordinary containment

    m>C delta+(Cd+1)(qL-1)
       implies mu_(alpha,m) in J^L.                            (4)

If J=0, the order-zero vanishing already makes the relevant moments zero,
so (4) remains valid. When J is nonzero, q may be replaced by the maximum
codimension of the complex irreducible components of V(J), which is at least1.
No symbolic-power base-change assertion is needed: apply both imported
theorems over C, then contract the ordinary power.

## 3. A bound for every nilpotent thickness

Let A be any commutative Q-algebra, with a coefficient map R->A. Write JA
for the ideal generated by the images of J. If (JA)^L=0, (4) gives

    E(QP^m)=0 whenever
    m>C deg Q+(Cd+1)(qL-1).                                   (5)

This holds for all Q in A[X], even without a pure-null hypothesis: expand Q
in monomials. It is an affine degree bound, including constant markers.

If P is also pure-null, the constant-marker moments are already zero.
For every positive marker degree, (5) is implied by the linear bound

    m>B_L deg Q,  B_L=C+(Cd+1)(qL-1).                         (6)

Thus a fixed bound on the nilpotence exponent gives a common linear cutoff.
No splitting A_red->A, primary decomposition of A, finite-type hypothesis on
A, or finite total length is required. The bound is sufficient, not sharp.
It depends on a theoretically computable field constant C; no numerical
universal C or general bound producer has been computed or implemented here.

## 4. Nilpotent invariance on the pure-null scheme

**Theorem.** For a pure-null P over an arbitrary commutative Q-algebra A,
the following are equivalent:

1. P is safe over A.
2. Its image in A_red=A/Nil(A) is safe.
3. Its phase over every prime residue field k(p) is safe.
4. Every element of the image of J is nilpotent.
5. (JA)^L=0 for some finite L.

Safety descends to quotients; monomial multipliers also give descent to
prime fraction fields. The field theorem then puts the image of J in every
prime ideal of A, proving nilpotence. Conversely a finitely generated nil
ideal is nilpotent: if its finite generators have exponents e_i, the power
1+sum_i(e_i-1) is zero by the pigeonhole principle. J is finitely generated
even when A is not Noetherian. Equation(6) finishes the converse. The
equivalence with reduced safety also follows from the retained reduced-ring
theorem, or from applying these implications to A_red.

This is a theorem about pure-null lifts. An arbitrary lift need not satisfy
the pure equations; equation(5) alone does not make its first moments zero.
If Nil(A)^L=0, every pure-null phase whose reduction is safe has the common
bound(6). Removing dependence on L is impossible in the retained failing
sizes, by the existing counterexample scaling lines.

## 5. The cutoff filtration is the safe-ideal topology

Put B=R/I_pure, bar J=J/I_pure, and bar I_N=I_N/I_pure. For each L>=1,
(4) for positive marker degrees, with the pure moments killed separately,
proves

    N>=B_L implies bar I_N contained in (bar J)^L.              (7)

In the other direction, for each N>=C, radical equality and Noetherianity
give some finite s(N) with

    (bar J)^s(N) contained in bar I_N.                         (8)

For example choose powers of a finite generating set of bar J lying in
bar I_N, then use the same pigeonhole argument. This exponent is
theoretically computable from the full ordinary ideal I_N.

Hence these two decreasing ideal filtrations are cofinal, and canonically

    lim_N B/bar I_N = lim_L B/(bar J)^L.                       (9)

The union of prescribed-cutoff schemes has, on arbitrary discrete
Q-algebras, exactly the functor of the formal completion of the pure-null
scheme along its reduced safe closed subscheme: maps B->A for which the
image of bar J is nilpotent. Here formal-completion maps are continuous
maps to A with the discrete topology. No claim about arbitrary
noncontinuous maps from the completed ring is made.

Both qualifications are necessary. The ideals need not stabilize, and
(7) holds after quotienting by I_pure. For the constant phase P=e, every
I_N=(e) and J=(e), so I_N is not contained in J^2 in Q[e]. The pure
quotient removes this obstruction. Differential powers also cannot be
replaced by ordinary powers before applying uniform containment: for
J=(ab,ac,bc), abc belongs to J^{<2>} but not J^2.

## 6. Full SIC and arbitrary constant-coefficient GVC

The [polynomial Gaussian transfer](POLYNOMIAL_GAUSSIAN_REDUCTION_AND_ALL_MODEL_FIELD_UNIFORMITY.md)
preserves pure equations and every ordinary coefficient cutoff ideal over
Q-algebras. For GVC use R_x=lambda(Z)P(x+W), retaining output x independent;
for full SIC use f(Z,x+W). Let J_orig be the radical closed pure-safe ideal
in the original finite coefficient polynomial ring, and I_pure,orig its
ordinary pure ideal.

Every output coefficient of every pulled-back Gaussian safe-ideal generator
vanishes at every complex original safe point. It therefore belongs to
J_orig after the fixed scalar extension identifying split and standard
Gaussians. Radical membership here is in the original regular coefficient
space. Thus (J_orig A)^L=0 makes the Gaussian safe ideal nilpotent of
exponent at most L in A'[x], where A' is that fixed faithfully flat scalar
extension. Apply(5)--(6) in A'[x] and transfer back; the cutoff comparison
controls all original multiplier degrees in both directions.

In particular the same nilpotent-invariance theorem holds for pure-null
GVC phases, allowing the constant-coefficient operator to vary too, and
for arbitrary nonseparable pure-null SIC phases. Sufficient bounds are

    GVC: B_L using Gaussian (n',d')=(2n,r+D),
    SIC: B_L using Gaussian (n',d')=(2n,D),
    q'=binomial(n'+d',d'), C=C_G(n',d').                       (10)

For SIC of bidegree(r,s) one may take D=r+s. Nil(A)^L=0 suffices in both
models. No direct coefficient differentiation of varying operator powers
is assumed. Coefficientwise Gaussian transfer and the radical membership
argument above handle this issue. The original cutoff ideals, modulo their
own pure ideal, satisfy(7)--(9) with(10); the converse cofinality follows
from original radical stabilization. Thus all three models have the same
formal-completion description, separately in each fixed coefficient space.

## 7. Scope and next gates

This closes qualitative lifting through arbitrary nilpotent coefficient
rings, bounded-thickness uniformity, and the cofinal topology of ordinary
mixed cutoffs. It does not identify the individual ideals with powers of J,
compute sharp general thickness exponents or numerical field constants,
classify the reduced safe locus by normal forms, or prove the positive
moment conjectures. The known scaling counterexamples and all protected
ternary, operator-order, phase-degree and SIC frontiers are unchanged.
Independent review of the field foundation and this new use of symbolic
containment remains necessary. General ideal and completion producers
remain theoretical. Exact individual cutoff geometry and structural
classification are the remaining questions, rather than qualitative
nilpotent lifting itself.
