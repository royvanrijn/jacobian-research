## Statements

Use circular Gaussian coordinates E(ZW)=E(T^2)=1. Let

    P=W*(a+bZ+cT+fT^2)+G(Z,T),    f!=0, deg G<=3.  (1)

**Rank-one theorem.** Every positive pure Gaussian moment of P is zero
if and only if b=0 and G=0. Hence the pure-null members of (1) are exactly

    P=W*A(T),       A(T)=a+cT+fT^2.

For these members, E(Q*P^m)=0 whenever m>deg Q. A sufficient initial
pure-moment gate for the classification is 425.

**Full W-linear cubic theorem.** The complete sixteen-coefficient family

    P=W*(a+bZ+cT+dZ^2+eZT+fT^2)+G(Z,T), deg G<=3, (2)

satisfies GMC. Its first 888 zero pure moments imply all-order pure
vanishing. Every pure-null member satisfies

    E(Q*P^m)=0 whenever m>3*deg Q.                 (3)

The coefficient three is sharp in this family, by the previously registered
triangular-shear examples. None of these statements asserts that an arbitrary
ternary cubic admits a W-linear Gaussian frame. Higher W powers remain
the global cubic gate.

## Exact generating function and period bridge

Put q=1-bt and A(T)=a+cT+fT^2. Conditional GL1 gives

    g=t*A(T)/q,
    I(t)=E(exp(tP))=(1/q)*E_T exp(t*G(g,T)).       (4)

Thus the rational polynomial phase and normalized periods are

    Phi(t,T)=t*G(t*A(T)/q,T)-T^2/2,
    I_Gamma(t)=(1/q)*integral_Gamma exp(Phi) dT.   (5)

This is a formal identity for I, not a convergence assertion. The finite
polynomial twisted quotient has normalized connection

    nabla[u]=[u_t+(Phi_t+b/q)*u].                  (6)

The scalar b/q is the logarithmic derivative of 1/q. Gaussian integration
by parts makes (4) a formal horizontal functional. If I=1, its restriction
to the submodule generated by [1],nabla[1],... is rational with values
1,0,... . The existing [seed-submodule argument](MIXED_PERIOD_AT_INFINITY.md)
and [Hien--Roucairol period comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/0704.1739v1)
then produce an actual constant normalized period, without assuming the
seed cyclic or the formal series convergent. We will exclude such a period
unless b=0 and G=0.

## A shrinking-period observation

Suppose a polynomial phase in T has, at t=infinity,

    Phi=t^p*(V(T)+O(1/t)),   p>=1,

coefficientwise, where V is nonconstant and has the same T-degree d as
Phi. Coefficients may be rational in t and holomorphic in 1/t after this
rescaling. Then every zero-exponential scalar period of exp(Phi)dT tends
to zero on a small generic sector, including degenerate critical points.

Here is a contour justification that does not assume a nonzero Hessian.
Use the inverse-phase cuts and finite-degree real-algebraic length bound
from the [logarithmic-period proof](QUADRATIC_W_LOG_PERIODS.md), with ordinary
Euclidean length in place of logarithmic length. All roots of Phi=w remain
bounded because the leading T coefficient of t^(-p)*Phi tends to that of V.
For |w|<=K*log|t| they lie in disks about the zeros of V of radius

    O((log|t|/|t|)^(1/d)).                        (7)

Indeed t^(-p)*Phi=V+O(1/t) on a fixed disk, and the polynomial V has a
uniform lower bound by a constant times distance-to-its-zero-set to power d.
The small additional w/t^p term satisfies the same bound up to log|t|.

The lifted bounded-action cut pieces in these disks are arcs of real
algebraic curves of bounded degree. The projection/coarea length estimate
bounds their total Euclidean length by a constant times (7). Multiplicity
and coincident critical values are retained as separate local inverse
branch differences before grouping their common action. Their integrands
have bounded absolute value. The remaining descending pieces can be
chosen in Re(w)<=-K*log|t|; their length in a polynomially large T disk is
polynomially bounded, so choosing K large makes their contribution tend
to zero. The far tails are uniformly controlled by the leading monomial.
Thus the zero-action periods shrink. Nonzero positive-power Puiseux actions
are separate exponential blocks and cannot supply a nonzero constant.

As in the preceding note, this uses the standard one-variable cut
decomposition, keeping all critical multiplicities. It is a written
analytic argument, not an inference from generic Morse examples.

We also need the elementary escape model

    t^p*v*T^n+t^r*k*T^m,  p>r,  1<=n<m, v*k!=0.

Set beta=(p-r)/(m-n), T=t^beta*zeta. The nonzero escaping critical points
satisfy

    zeta^(m-n)=-n*v/(m*k),
    leading action=(1-n/m)*v*zeta^n !=0,
    leading Hessian=n*(n-m)*v*zeta^(n-2) !=0.      (8)

Lower powers cannot cancel this highest action. The m-n escape saddles
and n-1 bounded critical dimensions total m-1. This covers all escape
cases below; no saddle is silently omitted when the leading degree drops.

## The b=0 chart

Write

    G=G0(T)+Z*G1(T)+Z^2*G2(T)+Z^3*G3,
    deg Gj<=3-j.

Then

    Phi=-T^2/2+t*G0+t^2*A*G1+t^3*A^2*G2+t^4*A^3*G3. (9)

If j>=1 is the largest index with Gj!=0, the leading t coefficient is
A^j*Gj, of degree 2j+deg Gj. For j=3 it has degree six, larger than every
lower term. For j=2 its degree is four or five, while lower terms have
degree at most four. For j=1 its degree is two, three or four. Except for
the next case, it has the same degree as Phi. The shrinking-period
observation excludes a constant period.

The exception is j=1, G1 a nonzero constant and G0 cubic. The leading
terms at escape are t^2*f*G1*T^2+t*[T^3]G0*T^3. Model (8) has p=2,r=1,
n=2,m=3: the escape action has degree four and is nonzero. The one bounded
critical dimension shrinks. Thus no constant period appears here either.

If G is independent of Z, Phi=t*G0(T)-T^2/2. A nonconstant G0 of degree
at least two satisfies the shrinking observation. A linear G0 has the
Gaussian escape model p=1,r=0,n=1,m=2, with nonzero action of degree two.
A nonzero constant G0 gives exp(t*G0) times the fixed Gaussian period.
It too cannot give a constant. This exhausts G!=0.

If G=0, (4) is I=1. More directly P=W*A(T) has strictly negative circular
weight. For a monomial Z^i*W^j*T^k, a mixed moment can be nonzero only when
m=i-j. Hence m>deg Q suffices for every Q. This proves the b=0 classification
and its GMC assertion.

## The b!=0 chart

Use polynomial division in T by S=bZ+A(T). Uniquely write

    G=R0(Z)+T*R1(Z)+S*L(Z,T),
    deg R0<=3, deg R1<=2,
    L=alpha*Z+beta*T+gamma.                       (10)

Restriction of R=R0+T*R1 to Z=-A(T)/b is triangular in the monomial basis

    1,T,Z,ZT,Z^2,Z^2T,Z^3,

whose respective leading T-degrees are 0,1,2,3,4,5,6, with nonzero leading
coefficients. Let V(T)=R(-A(T)/b,T), of degree n when nonconstant. Its
vanishing high coefficients force the corresponding high R monomials to
be absent, so R(s*A(T),T) has degree at most n for every scalar s. A
constant V forces R to be that same constant.

Since S(g,T)=g/t, the exact phase is

    Phi=t*R(g,T)+alpha*g^2+beta*g*T+gamma*g-T^2/2. (11)

Also g=-A/b+O(1/t). The first term is t*V+O(1), and its bounded correction
has degree at most n.

If alpha!=0 the remaining limiting phase has quartic coefficient
alpha*f^2/b^2. If alpha=0,beta!=0 it has cubic coefficient -beta*f/b.
If alpha=beta=0, its quadratic coefficient is

    K0=-gamma*f/b-1/2.

When the relevant nonzero limiting degree m is four, three or two, a
nonconstant V of degree n>=m satisfies the shrinking observation.
For 1<=n<m, model (8), with p=1,r=0, gives all m-n escape saddles with
nonzero actions, while the n-1 bounded dimensions shrink. If V is
constant, the phase minus t*V tends to a fixed polynomial of degree m
with nonzero leading coefficient. Its unnormalized periods are bounded
holomorphic functions of 1/t on fixed decaying contours. The prefactor
1/q=O(1/t) makes every zero-action normalized period tend to zero.

### The flattened quadratic case

It remains to handle

    alpha=beta=0,  gamma=-b/(2f),                 (12)

for which K0=0. Its exact quadratic coefficient, before the R term, is
-1/(2q), vanishing only to first order in 1/t. If n>=2 the leading t*V
still has the full phase degree, so the shrinking observation applies.

If n=1, R=r+u*T with u!=0. The phase is quadratic, with quadratic
coefficient -1/(2q) and linear coefficient t*u+O(1). Its Gaussian saddle
escapes like t^2, with a nonzero action of degree three. Its width is
O(t^(1/2)), and the factor 1/q gives O(t^(-1/2)); no constant block exists.
This is also model (8) with p=1,r=-1,n=1,m=2.

If V=r is constant, R=r exactly and direct Gaussian integration gives

    I_Gamma=constant*q^(-1/2)*exp(H(t)),
    H=t*r-b*a*t/(2f*q)+b^2*c^2*t^2/(8f^2*q).     (13)

No nonzero constant is possible. Its logarithmic derivative would require
H'=-b/(2q), but a rational derivative has zero residue at every finite
pole while -b/(2q) has residue 1/2 at t=1/b. Equivalently even a cancelled
positive action leaves the strictly decaying factor t^(-1/2) at infinity.
The check of this exceptional cancellation is essential; simply claiming
a stable limiting Gaussian here would be incorrect.

Thus every b!=0 case lacks a constant normalized period. This finishes
the rank-one theorem.

## A sufficient 425-moment rank-one gate

The rational phase has actual T-degree d between two and six. Every
coefficient of q^3*Phi is polynomial in t of degree at most four. The
normalized source Phi_t+b/q has denominator q^4 and numerator degree at
most four. Write the leading coefficient of Phi_T as A(t)/q^3, A!=0.

In the basis 1,T,...,T^(d-2), leading-term reduction of a connection column
needs at most d cancellations. The common denominator and numerator bounds
are therefore

    H=q^4*A^d,
    deg H<=B=4d+4,       deg N_ij<=B.

For b=0, q=1 and the same conservative bound holds. At minimal seed rank
s<=d-1, polynomial cofactors give a scalar equation with coefficients of
degree at most B*s*(s+1)/2. Outside b=0,G=0 its zeroth coefficient is
nonzero by the no-constant-period proof. The initial-moment order argument
from the preceding notes then gives the worst prefix

    (4*6+4)*5*6/2+5=425.

Thus 425 initial zero moments force b=0,G=0. This is a uniform degree
argument, not a numerical expansion through moment425 or an exact ideal
certificate, and it does not assert optimality.

## Completing all W-linear cubics

For (2), a nondegenerate quadratic coefficient is excluded by the
[logarithmic conic theorem](QUADRATIC_W_LOG_PERIODS.md), with sufficient
prefix888. On f=0 the entire fifteen-coefficient family is already closed,
with sufficient gate447. If f!=0 and e^2-4df=0, the Gaussian null rotation
lambda=-e/(2f) reduces the quadratic coefficient to f*T^2 and preserves
W-linearity and degree. The rank-one result now supplies the sufficient
gate425 and GMC safety on that last chart. The largest bound is888.

For the mixed cutoff, the old completed f=0 families have either strictly
one-sided support, a pure-null quadratic, or the polynomial triangular
shear from the affine-W classification. Their cutoffs are deg Q, deg Q and
3*deg Q respectively. The new rank-one family has cutoff deg Q, preserved
by the linear Gaussian rotation. Hence (3) holds throughout (2). The
[sharp cubic shear examples](CUBIC_AFFINE_W_CLASSIFICATION.md) prove the
coefficient three cannot be lowered for the whole W-linear family.

## Exact criterion for a W-linear Gaussian frame

Write an arbitrary cubic as P=F3+F2+F1+F0 in homogeneous pieces, and let
B be the Gaussian bilinear form. There is a Gaussian circular frame in
which P is W-linear if and only if a nonzero vector v satisfies

    B(v,v)=0,    grad F3(v)=0,    F2(v)=0.         (14)

Indeed

    D_v^2 P(x)=2*grad F3(v) dot x+2*F2(v).

Thus the last two conditions are exactly D_v^2P=0. Complete the isotropic
v to a circular orthogonal basis: choose a null partner w with B(v,w)=1,
then a unit vector in their orthogonal complement. In that frame v is
the W direction, and D_W^2P=0 is precisely the absence of W^2,W^3,W^2Z,W^2T.
The converse follows by taking the W direction of such a frame.

Consequently any global cubic GMC failure must have an empty projective
intersection in (14). The current global question is whether pure moment
vanishing can occur with that intersection empty and fail GMC. Condition
(14) is a testable criterion, not a proof that every pure-null cubic admits
the frame. A smooth leading plane cubic has no projective singular point
and is outside this frame theorem; it needs a separate argument.
