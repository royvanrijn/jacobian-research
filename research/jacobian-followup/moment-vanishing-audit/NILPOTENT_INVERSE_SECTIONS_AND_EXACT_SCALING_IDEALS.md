# Nilpotent inverse sections and exact Gaussian scaling ideals

16 November 2026. Internal written deductions; independent replay, external review and formal verification remain pending. Classical Newton lifting is used with a displayed proof, without a novelty claim. No large inverse-branch calculation is run.

The [weighted cotangent inverse theorem](WEIGHTED_COTANGENT_INVERSE_AND_FAILED_SAFETY_PULLBACK.md) gave exact field directions and only sufficient ordinary equations over nonreduced rings. This note completes that boundary constructively: full safety over an arbitrary coefficient algebra is equivalent to nilpotence of the finite inverse-section residual ideal. It also computes every ordinary mixed-cutoff ideal on the explicit counterexample scaling line. This is a direct algebraic argument; it does not depend on the general holonomic Gaussian field-uniformity theorem, symbolic-power containment, or an unimplemented general ideal oracle.

## 1. Ordinary inverse-tail ideals have finite composition generators

Fix a characteristic-zero field K and a normalized Keller map F in K[x_1,...,x_n]^n, so F(0)=0, JF(0)=I and det JF=1. Let D>=1 bound its degree and let g be its formal inverse. Let v=(v_1,...,v_n) be independent direction parameters and R=K[v]. Write

    b(s)=g(sv), b_N(s)=sum_(1<=k<=N) [s^k]b(s)*s^k,
    J_N=(all coefficients of F(b_N(s))-sv) in R, N>=1.

Every b_N is a polynomial with coefficients in R, obtainable recursively from the identity linear part. J_N has finitely many displayed generators: the residual has degree at most DN in s. Let T_N be the ordinary ideal generated by all coefficients [s^k]b_i(s), k>N, for all i. Then, literally as ordinary ideals,

    J_N=T_N.                                                (1)

Modulo T_N the formal inverse is b_N, so its polynomial composition residual is zero; hence J_N is contained in T_N. Modulo J_N, b_N solves F(b_N)=sv and has zero constant term. Formal uniqueness over every commutative K-algebra identifies it with g(sv), giving T_N contained in J_N. This proof uses quotients, retaining all nilpotents; it is not a radical equality or a numerical prefix assertion.

In particular J_(N+1) is contained in J_N. For any K-algebra A and direction v in A^n,

    J_N A=0 iff g(sv) is polynomial of degree at most N.      (2)

These finite ordinary ideals exactly encode all n position-marker tails in the weighted Gaussian application below. A cutoff on all other markers can be larger.

## 2. Nilpotent Newton correction supplies an explicit degree bound

Put B=D^(n-1). The affine-line degree bound in GMCINVERSELINE20261115 says that every polynomial inverse restriction over a field has degree at most B. It used only the classical isolated affine Bezout product upper bound. The argument did not need Euler weights.

Let A be any commutative K-algebra, with no finite-type or Noetherian hypothesis, and specialize the direction. Put I=J_B A. The following are equivalent:

1. g(sv) belongs to A[s]^n.
2. Its image at every prime residue field is polynomial.
3. Every element of I is nilpotent.
4. I^L=0 for some finite integer L>=1.

The implication1 to2 is immediate. For2 to3, the field bound and (2) put the finitely many residual coefficients in every prime of A. A finitely generated nil ideal is nilpotent: generator exponents e_i give L=1+sum_i(e_i-1). Thus3 implies4 even if the full nilradical of A is not nilpotent.

Here is a constructive proof of4 to1, including a bound. Start c_0=b_B. Let A_F=JF and use its polynomial inverse adj(A_F), since det A_F=1. Iterate

    r_j=F(c_j)-sv,
    c_(j+1)=c_j-A_F(c_j)^(-1)r_j.                           (3)

Polynomial Taylor expansion gives r_(j+1) in the square of the coefficient ideal of r_j. Thus r_j belongs to I^(2^j)[s]. All c_j have zero constant term. If 2^k>=L, then c_k is an exact polynomial solution, and formal uniqueness makes it g(sv). No analytic convergence or Henselian completeness is used; this is a finite correction in a nilpotent ideal.

Let H=(n-1)(D-1) bound the polynomial inverse Jacobian degree and M=H+D. If deg_s c_j<=e_j, then deg_s r_j<=D e_j and (3) gives e_(j+1)<=M e_j. Hence

    E_L=B*M^ceil(log_2 L)                                  (4)

bounds the inverse-line degree whenever I^L=0; for L=1 the exponent is0. Zero directions are harmless. These bounds are sufficient, not asserted sharp.

Applying the same construction over R/J_B^L and then using (1) proves an explicit ordinary-ideal containment

    J_(E_L) contained in J_B^L.                             (5)

The functor union_N V(J_N) on arbitrary discrete K-algebras is exactly maps R->A with nilpotent image of J_B: the discrete-point functor of the formal completion along V(J_B). The equations J_B=0 alone give the exact FIELD locus; nilpotence, rather than literal vanishing, is the complete all-ring criterion. This constructive special-family completion agrees with the broader retained [nilpotent-safety theorem](NILPOTENT_LIFTING_AND_FORMAL_SAFE_COMPLETION.md), but does not use its analytic or symbolic-power foundations.

## 3. Full Gaussian safety over arbitrary coefficient algebras

Assume now JF(x) W x=W F(x), for a diagonal matrix of nonzero K-weights W. Use split Wick covariance W^(-1), and put

    P_a=a^T JF(x)^(-T)p, v=W^(-1)a.

The prior formal Gaussian-Lagrange proof and determinant cancellation work over every K-algebra A. Every formal inverse coefficient and Wick coefficient is a finite polynomial expression with rational denominators; the moving-branch Jacobian determinant is identically1. Therefore

    L[Q(x) exp(sP_a)]=Q(g(sW^(-1)a))                        (6)

holds over A as well. In particular P_a is pure-null for every direction, including nilpotent ones. It is safe against every polynomial marker in A[x,p] if and only if b(s)=g(sW^(-1)a) is polynomial.

Necessity uses the n coordinate markers in (6), taking the maximum of their finite tail endpoints. For sufficiency introduce momentum source rho after rescaling p to Wp. The prior completed formal identity is

    L[Q(x) exp(sP_a+rho^T Wp)]=Q(b(s,rho))/j_s(b(s,rho)),
    b=rho+s h_a(b), h_a=W^(-1)JF^(-1)a,
    j_s(x)=det(I-s Jh_a(x)).

At rho=0, b=b(s) and j_s(b)=1. Implicit derivatives use polynomial adjugates, and higher finite rho derivatives use only polynomial operations. Thus polynomial b gives polynomial exponential moments for every momentum marker over A, with no division by a nilpotent or field-only argument.

More explicitly, if deg b<=e, put

    C_n,H(e)=max(e,(2n-1)*(1+e*max(H-1,0))).                 (7)

The numerator/adjugate degree estimate in the preceding proof is coefficientwise and remains valid over A. It gives L[Q P_a^m]=0 whenever m>C_n,H(e)*deg Q. Combined with (4), the nilpotent residual certificate I^L=0 supplies the explicit common cutoff C_n,H(E_L). It includes all source markers, not just markers pulled from the target. A supplied nilpotence certificate is sufficient input for this constructive bound; deciding arbitrary ideals or nilpotence in arbitrary coefficient rings is not an implemented algorithm.

Let I_C denote the full ordinary coefficient ideal generated by all L[x^alpha p^beta P_a^m] with m>C*(|alpha|+|beta|), m>=1. Pull J_N back by v=W^(-1)a. Formula (6), factorial units and (1) give

    J_N contained in I_N,
    I_(C_n,H(N)) contained in J_N.                          (8)

The second containment applies the coefficientwise all-marker proof in R/J_N. Thus the explicitly computable position-tail ideals and the full mixed-cutoff ideals define cofinal filtrations. This does not identify their individual levels with one another. Equation (5) and (8) give constructive sufficient containments in every prescribed power J_B^L.

For an arbitrary normalized Keller map without weights, take its earlier scaling suspension and phase

    F_t=t^(-1)F(tx), A_t=J_xF_t,
    R_a=z+(a+partial_t F_t)^T A_t^(-T)y.

Over every K-algebra A the same argument shows full safety iff g(sa) is polynomial, equivalently iff the residual ideal J_B(a) has nilpotent image. The position branch is (s^(-1)g(as^2),s). If deg g(sa)<=E_L, its degree is at most e'=max(1,2E_L-1). The suspended position dimension is n+1, its degree is at most2D-1, and H'=n*(2D-2) bounds its inverse Jacobian degree. Therefore C_(n+1),H'(e') is an explicit sufficient all-marker cutoff. The parameter functor is again the same formal completion. This gives an all-degree arbitrary-ring phase criterion without assuming a general formal-symbol holonomic source.

## 4. Every cutoff ideal on the known Gaussian failure line

Use the degree12 phase P and normalized weighted six-dimensional map from the preceding note. On the direction v=(1,1,0), write

    U(s)=(1-2s^2)^(-1/2),
    x(s)=(U(s)-1)/s,
    z(s)=-s^2*(1-2s^2)-3s^2*sqrt(1-2s^2).

The all-degree inverse identities give

    L[x P^(2k-1)]=(2k-1)!*binomial(2k,k)/2^k !=0,
    L[z P^(2k)]=(2k)!*c_k !=0,                             (9)

where c_1=-4, c_2=5 and

    c_k=-3*binomial(1/2,k-1)*(-2)^(k-1), k>=3.

For j>=1, binomial(1/2,j)*(-2)^j is negative, so these even coefficients are nonzero. Thus a LINEAR source marker, chosen as x at odd indices and z at even indices, detects every positive moment index. Each marker is fixed on its parity subsequence. The proof is the exact binomial identity, not a finite search.

Over K[e], put P_e=eP. Let I_N^line be the full ordinary mixed-cutoff ideal for integer N>=0. Pure moments are identically zero. Any nonconstant marker has degree d>=1, and m>Nd implies m>=N+1; its moment is divisible by e^(N+1). Conversely at m=N+1, (9) gives a nonzero K-unit times e^(N+1), using a degree-one marker. Consequently

    I_N^line=(e^(N+1)) for EVERY integer N>=0.               (10)

This is an exact individual ordinary ideal calculation with all nilpotents retained. Fixed complex linear covariance conjugation preserves marker degrees and their spans, so (10) also holds for the ordinary independent Gaussian realization over Q(i).

For every coefficient algebra A and element eta in A, eta P is safe iff eta is nilpotent. If eta has exact nilpotence order L>=1, the least nonnegative integer linear mixed-cutoff coefficient is exactly L-1: all powers with m>=L vanish, and the linear marker at m=L-1 prevents any smaller coefficient when L>=2. For eta=0, L=1 and the least coefficient is0. No nilpotence-independent slope or finite fixed ordinary gate can describe this line. Its cutoff completion is K[[e]], with continuous maps to discrete A, and its reduced safe scheme is the single point e=0.

The foundational degree is7, so the field inverse-line bound is B=49. Taking eta=e in K[e]/(e^51) gives a safe phase of sharp coefficient50 whose inverse branch has a nonzero degree50 term in z. Thus the field equation J_49=0 fails on this safe nonreduced point. This is an explicit demonstration of the prior field/ring distinction; no degree49 branch expansion is needed.
