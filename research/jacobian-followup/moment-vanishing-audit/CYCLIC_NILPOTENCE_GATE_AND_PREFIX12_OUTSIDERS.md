## 4. Square-system lifting needs no nonsingularity

The following elementary lemma strengthens the negative test available
here.

**Lemma.** Let \(A=\mathbb Z_{(P)}\), and let \(f_1,\ldots,f_n\)
be polynomials in \(n\) variables over \(A\). If their special fibre
has an isolated closed point, the generic fibre is nonempty. An open
condition holding at that point may be imposed throughout.

**Proof.** Localize \(A[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) at the closed point in
question, also inverting any specified open-condition function. This
is a regular local ring \(T\) of dimension \(n+1\).
Since the special point is isolated,
\(T/(f_1,\ldots,f_n,P)\) is nonzero and zero-dimensional.
The ordered list \(f_1,\ldots,f_n,P\) is therefore a system of
parameters in the Cohen-Macaulay ring \(T\), hence a regular sequence.
It follows that \(P\) is a nonzerodivisor on
\(T/(f_1,\ldots,f_n)\). Inverting \(P\) leaves a nonzero ring,
so the characteristic-zero fibre is nonempty. Equivalently the
one-dimensional local complete intersection is flat over the DVR.
See [Stacks02JN](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02JN) and
[Stacks00R4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00R4). \(\square\)

The lift may be ramified or nonreduced. No rational point modulo \(P\),
invertible Jacobian or explicit algebraic coordinates are required.
This does not make equality of modular and proposed global lengths an
exhaustion certificate: other generic affine components can avoid
the special fibre entirely.
