# Capacity amplification under polynomial composition

16 November 2026. Written all-degree deduction from retained capacity,
invariant-degree and primitive-base theorems. No new degree campaign or
CAS construction. Independent replay, formal verification and external
review are not claimed. Both FULL exceptional MZ cases remain **OPEN**;
external review is deferred. The conclusion constrains genuine pure
failures and their primitive factors, not just individual test phases.

## 1. Audited low Peter-Weyl side restriction

We adopt ONLY the Mathieu capacity section of the coordinator's
[inward coupling packet](../cross-programme-connections/INWARD_COUPLED_RESTRICTIONS_2026_11_16.tex).
Its surface/Keller and Factorial conclusions are not imported into this
proof. Here is a direct audit from the already registered inputs.

For a nonzero charged component f_i of minimum matrix degree 2D_i,
let M_i be the maximum root multiplicity of its leading right binary
form. The retained
[logarithmic capacity theorem](LOGARITHMIC_JETS_AND_SPARSE_ALL_RING_SAFETY.md)
uses

    cap(f_i)=D_i if M_i<=1; otherwise D_i-M_i.             (1)

If a mixed phase has k distinct weights, and ONE positive component
and ONE negative component both have capacity at least k, then it
has a proper model with its ORIGINAL holomorphic volume. In particular
EVERY nonconstant polynomial recomposition is NOT eventually pure.
This is stronger than a test of the phase's first moment.

The retained [degree data](NONSPHERICAL_PAIRS_AND_EXCEPTIONAL_SL2.md)
give binary invariant generator degrees 8,12,18 for 2O and 12,20,30
for 2I. The minimum projective orbit lengths are 6 and12, so

    M_i <= floor(D_i/3) on 2O,
    M_i <= floor(D_i/6) on 2I.                           (2)

For 2O a component outside filtered PW degrees8 and12 has leading
matrix degree at least16. If M_i<=1 its capacity is at least8;
otherwise it is at least D_i-floor(D_i/3)>=6. For 2I a component
outside PW degree12 has leading degree at least20. In degrees20
through22 its maximum root multiplicity is at most1, giving capacity
at least10. From degree24 onward D_i-floor(D_i/6)>=10.

Therefore an eventually pure mixed phase with at most SIX weights
on 2O, or at most TEN on 2I, must have ONE ENTIRE charged side in
filtered PW degrees8/12, or PW degree12, respectively. A high-degree
component on each side instead gives the original proper-volume model
and excludes EVERY nonconstant recomposition in all degrees.

The low side need not be zero. This is not unrestricted six/ten-weight
closure or a highest-degree bound. For coefficient rings, the inherited
nilpotence classification applies to the fixed linear restricted classes
excluding the indicated low PW projections, as in the packet and the
retained all-ring proof. It must not be promoted to the unrestricted
six/ten-weight locus, or to a family whose fibres can acquire a low-only
charged side at specialization. The new deductions below are over C.

## 2. Matrix degree and leading root multiplicity under powers

The matrix filtration on C[SL2] has associated graded algebra

    C[x,y,z,w]/(xw-yz),

which is a domain. Indeed the determinant polynomial is irreducible;
for the principal relation det-1, the leading ideal is generated by
det. Hence the minimum degrees of two nonzero functions add under
multiplication. This remains true for the invariant subalgebra and
for semi-invariant components viewed in C[SL2]. No cancellation of a
nonzero leading product is possible in this associated graded domain.

For a charged component v of degree 2D with leading binary form F,
the leading form of v^j is F^j. Its degree is 2jD and its maximum
root multiplicity is jM. For j>=2, F has a root and jM>=2, so

    cap(v^j)=j(D-M).                                    (3)

In particular the simple-root bonus cap(v)=D from (1) is NOT retained
when taking a square: a simple root becomes multiple. This distinction
is essential for the constants below.

## 3. A bound for every polynomial composition, in every degree

Let r be ANY original invariant phase with both positive and negative
weights. Let i_+>0 and i_-<0 be its largest and smallest weights, with
components r_+,r_- of minimum degrees 2D_+,2D_- and leading maximum
root multiplicities M_+,M_-. Let H be a polynomial of degree d>=2,
with nonzero leading coefficient a_d. Put f=H(r) and let k be the
ACTUAL number of distinct weights of f, including zero if present.

The two extreme components of f are EXACTLY

    f_(d i_+) = a_d r_+^d,
    f_(d i_-) = a_d r_-^d.                              (4)

The top power r^d has unique contributions at those two extremes.
Every lower power in H has strictly smaller positive extreme and
strictly larger negative extreme, so cannot cancel (4). Equations
(3) and (4) imply:

    if k <= d min(D_+-M_+,D_--M_-),                     (5)
    then f has a proper ORIGINAL-volume phase model,
    and EVERY nonconstant K(f) is NOT eventually pure.

This uses the actual possibly sparse support of H(r), not an assumed
generic support or a bound obtained by expanding without cancellations.
It also retains all lower terms and arbitrary coefficient relations.

On 2O, D_+,D_->=4 and (2) give D_+-M_+,D_--M_->=3.
On 2I, they are at least6 and give D_+-M_+,D_--M_->=5.
Consequently EVERY eventually pure such f satisfies

    k > 3d on 2O,    k > 5d on 2I.                     (6)

The contrapositive gives a simultaneous all-recomposition nonpurity
result through (5); no reflection of purity from H(r) to r is used.
The conclusion holds afresh after any allowed automorphism, since
phi(H(r))=H(phi(r)), using the transformed phase's OWN support and
minimum matrix degrees. There is no bound on the total degree of r.

## 4. Consequence for the canonical primitive factor

The retained
[primitive-base theorem](PRIMITIVE_BASE_CHARACTERS_AND_FIBRE_EULER_CONSTRAINTS.md)
writes EVERY nonconstant original phase as f=G(r), with r primitive
on the ORIGINAL quotient. If f is mixed, r is mixed: a polynomial of
a one-sided or neutral phase cannot acquire weights of the other sign.
Therefore (6) applies whenever deg G>=2.

It follows that EVERY eventually pure MIXED phase with

    k<=6 on 2O,    k<=10 on 2I

is primitive on the original quotient (G has degree1). This concerns
all such pure phases, including safe ones; it does not classify them
as safe. In the remaining near-minimal window, k=5 or6 on 2O and
k=7,...,10 on 2I, a hypothetical genuine failure must simultaneously:

* be primitive on the original quotient;
* keep one ENTIRE charged side in the stated low PW space;
* satisfy the retained strict-leading, image-interior, cyclic-stabilizer,
  and automorphism constraints.

These assertions are not a finite collection of degree tests. They
remove nonlinear primitive-base recompositions throughout those windows
while retaining phases of arbitrarily large total matrix degree.

## 5. The quadratic upstairs branch on 2O must be retained

The primitive-base theorem gives r=q0^e with q0 primitive upstairs.
For 2I, e=1, so the preceding near-minimal pure phases pull back with
geometrically connected generic fibre on SL2. For 2O, e=1 or2. In the
second branch q0 is sign-semi-invariant and r=q0^2. A primitive quotient
phase need NOT be primitive upstairs; the quadratic possibility survives.

There is a stronger compositional bound in that branch. Suppose

    f=G(q0^2),    d=deg G>=1,

and f is mixed and eventually pure. The extreme components of q0 are
sign-semi-invariant. They are fixed by 2T, so their minimum binary
degree is at least6 by the retained 2T character series. Also -I is in
2T, so their matrix degrees are even, written 2D_+,2D_- with D_+,D_->=3.
Although their binary forms are semi-invariant rather than invariant,
their ZERO DIVISORS are invariant under the FULL projective O group.
Thus M_+,M_-<=floor(D_+/3),floor(D_-/3), and

    D_+-M_+,D_--M_->=2.

The extreme components of f are the 2d-th powers, so their capacities
are at least4d by (3). The capacity theorem now gives

    k > 4d on the 2O sign-root branch.                  (7)

This holds for d=1 too. In particular a degree-two recomposition of an
original primitive sign-square phase requires at least NINE weights
if eventually pure; the general 2O bound would give only seven.
For d=1, (7) still permits k=5 or6 and does not remove the sign branch.

For a genuine failure, the coarse support bounds can be displayed as:

| Original primitive factor | deg G=1 | deg G=d>=2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2O, trivial upstairs character | k>=5 | k>=3d+1 |
| 2O, sign upstairs character | k>=5 | k>=4d+1 |
| 2I | k>=7 | k>=5d+1 |

The first column uses the retained general four/six-weight theorem;
the second uses (6)--(7). Exact component data can strengthen every
row via (5). This table is a necessary condition, not an existence
claim or a safe classification of the surviving rows.
