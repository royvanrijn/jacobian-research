# Abstract

Let \(P\in\mathbf C[x]\) have degree at least two, let \(W_P^{\mathrm{red}}\)
be the reduced constant span of the inverse roots of \(P(X)-z\), and let
\(q(P,dx)\) be the cyclic rank of the fixed
class \([dx]\) in

\[
\mathcal H_P=
\frac{\mathbf C(s)[x]\,dx}
     {\{(\partial_x f+sP'f)\,dx:f\in\mathbf C(s)[x]\}}.
\]

The parent note, relative to standard formal stationary phase, identifies this
rank with the finite inertia conductor on \(W_P^{\mathrm{red}}\). We prove that
this conductor
always collapses to the representation dimension:

\[
\boxed{q(P,dx)=\dim_{\mathbf C}W_P^{\mathrm{red}}.}
\]

The proof uses only the polynomial Riemann--Hurwitz identity, the fixed-free
action of the full cycle at infinity on the augmentation module, rank
subadditivity, and characteristic-zero semisimplicity. It turns the
inverse-character problem into a necessary-and-sufficient constant root-span
criterion.

As an arbitrary-degree consequence, the indecomposable phases with \(q\le3\)
are exactly powers (\(q=1\)), Chebyshev phases (\(q=2\)), and indecomposable
quartics (\(q=3\)), up to affine changes. For arbitrary raw
\(P,A\in\mathbf Q[x]\), an exact finite Krylov reducer computes the cyclic rank
over \(\mathbf Q(s)\), returning a nonzero independence minor and, when
applicable, the first exact dependence relation. Independent quotient oracles,
mutation tests, ordinary/\(-O\) replay, and post-freeze held-out inputs support
the implementation.

The decomposable affine classification is not complete. We prove right-factor
heredity, then exhibit the aligned rank-three phase

\[
P(x)=(x^4+x)^3=x^3(x^3+1)^3,
\]

which defeats a natural provisional composition atlas. The exact remaining
problem is equivariant fusion of root-block spans under Ritt moves. All
statements remain conditional on the parent fixed-seed stationary-phase bridge;
the reviews supplied here are internal AI mathematical reviews, not external
specialist or peer review.

## 1. Setup and imported bridge

After an affine source translation, write the roots of \(P(X)-z\) as
\(x_1,\ldots,x_d\) with \(\sum_i x_i=0\). Let \(G\) be the geometric monodromy
group, let

\[
M=\mathbf C[\Omega]=\mathbf C\mathbf1\oplus M_0,
\]

and define the coordinate evaluation map

\[
A_P:M\longrightarrow L,
\qquad e_i\longmapsto x_i.
\]

The trace line is killed, and the constant root-span representation is

\[
W_P^{\mathrm{red}}=A_P(M_0)=A_P(M).
\]

For each finite branch value \(v\), choose positive inertia
\(\sigma_v\). The parent note proves, relative to the standard formal
stationary-phase theorem and after fixing the Fourier sign, trace removal,
seed, Kummer labels, and absence of a source-at-infinity block, that

\[
q(P,dx)=\sum_v\operatorname{rank}(\sigma_v-I\mid W_P^{\mathrm{red}}).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Two separate fixed-text reviews found no critical or major mathematical error
in the stationary-phase translation or the polynomial constituent argument.
Those are internal reviews and do not replace an external specialist reading.

Affine changes preserve the quantities in question. Source pullback changes
\(dx\) only by a nonzero scalar. A target scaling is absorbed by
\(s\mapsto\alpha s\), while target translation changes the Krylov powers by an
invertible triangular transformation. We therefore use depressed
normalization freely.

## 2. Conductor--span equality

Let the finite branch cycles be
\(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_r\), and let \(\tau\) be the \(d\)-cycle at infinity,
with

\[
\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_r\tau=1.
\]

For a \(G\)-module \(V\), put

\[
c(V)=\sum_{j=1}^r\operatorname{rank}(\sigma_j-I\mid V).
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (constituentwise conductor equality)

Every irreducible constituent \(V\) of \(M_0\) satisfies

\[
c(V)=\dim V.
\]

Consequently every subquotient \(W\) of \(M_0\), including
\(W_P^{\mathrm{red}}\), satisfies
\(c(W)=\dim W\). Under (1.1),

\[
\boxed{q(P,dx)=\dim_{\mathbf C}W_P^{\mathrm{red}}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

The full \(d\)-cycle \(\tau\) fixes only the trace line in \(M\), so
\(M_0^\tau=0\). Every irreducible constituent \(V\subset M_0\) is therefore
fixed-free under \(\tau\), and

\[
\dim V=\operatorname{rank}(\tau-I\mid V).
\]

Since \(\tau^{-1}=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_r\), repeated use of

\[
\operatorname{rank}(AB-I)
\le \operatorname{rank}(A-I)+\operatorname{rank}(B-I)
\]

gives \(\dim V\le c(V)\).

For the full permutation module,
\(\operatorname{rank}(\sigma_j-I)=d-\#\operatorname{cycles}(\sigma_j)\).
Finite Riemann--Hurwitz for a degree-\(d\) polynomial map gives

\[
c(M_0)=
\sum_j\bigl(d-\#\operatorname{cycles}(\sigma_j)\bigr)
=d-1
=\dim M_0.
\]

Maschke semisimplicity decomposes \(M_0\) into irreducibles. Both dimension
and \(c\) are additive on direct sums, so the sum of the nonnegative defects
\(c(V)-\dim V\), counted with multiplicity, is zero. Every defect is zero.
The action on an isotypic component is the irreducible action tensored with its
multiplicity space, so the equality passes to every submodule and quotient.
Applying (1.1) to \(W_P^{\mathrm{red}}\) proves (2.1). \(\square\)

The reduction is essential outside depressed coordinates. For
\(P=x^2+x\), the raw span of the two inverse roots contains their nonzero
constant sum and has dimension two, whereas \(q=1\); the reduced span generated
by their difference has dimension one. In the depressed convention used below,
the raw inverse roots already sum to zero, so we abbreviate
\(W_P^{\mathrm{red}}\) to \(W_P\).

Equation (2.1) is the promised necessary-and-sufficient inverse-character
rigidity criterion: \(q\le3\) exactly when the constant inverse-root span has
dimension at most three. It is not, by itself, an affine enumeration of all
polynomials with such a span.

## 3. Indecomposable phases of rank at most three

### Theorem 3.1 (conditional indecomposable classification)

Let \(P\) be indecomposable. Up to invertible affine source and target changes,
\(q(P,dx)\le3\) exactly in the following cases:

1. \(P=x^p\), for prime \(p\), with \(q=1\);
2. \(P=T_p\), for prime \(p\ge3\), with \(q=2\); or
3. \(P\) is an indecomposable quartic, with \(q=3\).

Every other indecomposable phase of degree \(d\) has \(q=d-1\ge4\).

### Proof

Indecomposability is equivalent to primitivity of polynomial monodromy. In the
primitive setting used by its proof, the König--Neftin formulation of the
classical polynomial-monodromy classification says that solvable monodromy
comes from a power, a Chebyshev polynomial, or an indecomposable quartic; the
remaining case is two-transitive and nonabelian almost simple. The word
*indecomposable* is missing from the printed sentence of their Theorem 2.1,
although it is required by the surrounding text and proof; Müller supplies an
unambiguous primitive-monodromy source.

If \(G\) is two-transitive, \(M_0\) is irreducible. Coordinate evaluation on
\(M_0\) is nonzero because it sends \(e_i-e_j\) to \(x_i-x_j\), so it is
injective. Thus \(W_P\simeq M_0\) and (2.1) gives \(q=d-1\).

Powers have a one-dimensional inverse span. Chebyshev inverse roots lie in the
two-dimensional Joukowski span generated by \(u,u^{-1}\). An indecomposable
quartic has primitive degree-four monodromy containing a 4-cycle at infinity;
this rules out \(A_4\), leaving the two-transitive group \(S_4\), and hence
\(q=3\). The decomposition identities \(x^{ab}=x^a\circ x^b\) and
\(T_{ab}=T_a\circ T_b\) force prime degree in the first two indecomposable
families. \(\square\)

The monodromy classification and augmentation-module argument are classical.
A scoped search did not locate the assembled fixed-seed cyclic-rank corollary;
that is a non-location result, not proof of novelty or priority.

## 4. A finite exact raw-polynomial test

For trusted JSON input \(P,A\in\mathbf Q[x]\), the supplied calculator works in

\[
\frac{\mathbf Q(s)[x]\,dx}
     {\{(\partial_x f+sP'f)\,dx:f\in\mathbf Q(s)[x]\}}.
\]

Write \(P'=\lambda x^{d-1}+L\), where
\(\lambda\ne0\) and \(\deg L\le d-2\). For \(j\ge0\),

\[
(\partial_x+sP')(x^j)
=jx^{j-1}+s x^jL+s\lambda x^{j+d-1}.
\]

This identity reduces every leading monomial uniquely to degree below \(d-1\).
Because \([\partial_s+P,\partial_x+sP']=0\) and \(P,A\) are
\(s\)-independent,

\[
\nabla_s^k[A\,dx]=[P^kA\,dx].
\]

The calculator forms the finite Krylov matrix with columns
\([A],[PA],\ldots,[P^{d-2}A]\). The first dependence determines the cyclic
rank. Its output contains:

- a nonzero rational-function minor proving independence of the first \(q\)
  iterates; and
- when \(q<d-1\), exact coefficients for the next iterate together with an
  identically zero quotient residual.

The implementation was checked in ordinary and optimized Python against two
independently assembled exact quotient oracles, 15 seeded dense rational cases,
four valid rank-zero amplitudes, five mutations, malformed-input controls, and
a four-case post-freeze held-out set including a nonconstant amplitude. This is
implementation evidence, not a computational proof of Theorems 2.1 or 3.1.
The parser is intended for trusted research input; no untrusted-input security
claim is made.

## 5. Right-factor heredity

### Proposition 5.1

If \(P=R\circ Q\), then

\[
\dim W_Q\le\dim W_P.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence every suffix in a complete polynomial decomposition inherits the same
upper bound.

### Proof

Depress \(Q\), absorbing the target translation into \(R\). Let \(u\) be
transcendental and let \(\xi_1(u),\ldots,\xi_m(u)\) be the roots of
\(Q(X)-u\). If \(y\) is one root of \(R(Y)-z\), then \(y\) is transcendental
and the substitution \(\mathbf C(u)\to\mathbf C(y)\), \(u\mapsto y\), is a
field isomorphism. It embeds a copy of the splitting field of \(Q(X)-u\).
Consequently no new constant-linear relation among the \(\xi_i\) appears after
substitution. The functions \(\xi_i(y)\) form one root block of
\(P(X)-z\), so their span, of dimension \(\dim W_Q\), lies inside \(W_P\).
\(\square\)

Together with Theorems 2.1 and 3.1, this forces every indecomposable rightmost
factor of a \(q\le3\) phase to be a power, Chebyshev phase, or indecomposable
quartic. It does not imply that the block dimensions add.

## 6. The degree-twelve block-fusion counterexample

Put

\[
Q(x)=x^4+x,
\qquad
P(x)=Q(x)^3.
\]

For every cube root of unity \(\zeta\),

\[
Q(\zeta x)=\zeta Q(x).
\tag{6.1}
\]

If \(y^3=z\), the roots of \(P(X)-z\) lie in the three blocks
\(Q(X)=y,\zeta y,\zeta^2y\). Equation (6.1) makes the last two blocks scalar
copies of the first, so all three block spans coincide. The quartic \(Q\) has
three simple critical points with distinct critical values; its monodromy is
\(S_4\), and \(\dim W_Q=3\). Heredity supplies the lower bound and scalar fusion
the upper bound, hence

\[
q(P,dx)=\dim W_P=3.
\]

The same identity is a Ritt monomial collision:

\[
(x^4+x)^3=H(x^3),
\qquad H(y)=y(y+1)^3.
\]

The phase is neither a power nor Chebyshev, is not an indecomposable quartic,
and is not affine-equivalent to the provisional family
\(h(x^m+b)\) with \(\deg h\le3\). Degree would force a cyclic affine deck
symmetry of order at least four, while the exponent set
\(\{12,9,6,3\}\) permits only order three. Exact ordinary/\(-O\) Krylov
receipts independently certify rank three, the equivariance identity, its
mutation failure, and the Ritt identity.

![Explanatory block-fusion diagram; not evidence](figures/block_fusion.png)

This counterexample prevents a premature arbitrary-degree atlas. The exact
remaining theorem is an equivariant block-fusion classification: for the
allowed rightmost factors \(Q\), classify outer maps \(R\) for which the
conjugate block spans fail to grow under composition, compatibly with Ritt
moves.

## 7. Prior-art boundary

The following components are established antecedents:

- formal Fourier--Laplace stationary phase and finite local decomposition;
- polynomial monodromy and the primitive full-cycle classification;
- permutation-module/root-relation and zero-dimensional Abelian-integral
  frameworks;
- Riemann--Hurwitz, Maschke semisimplicity, rank subadditivity, and Ritt
  decomposition theory; and
- finite exact reduction in zero-dimensional/twisted de Rham modules.

The candidate residue is narrower: the convention-complete fixed-seed bridge,
the application of the classical Scott--Riemann--Hurwitz--Maschke conductor
collapse to that bridge, the assembled indecomposable low-rank corollary, and
the explicit block-fusion counterexample and frontier. Scoped searches did
not locate these exact assembled statements. Non-location does not establish
novelty, priority, correctness, or significance.

## 8. Assurance and limitations

What is closed internally:

- two fixed-text reviews found no critical or major issue in the imported
  stationary-phase and polynomial-monodromy arguments;
- the corrected indecomposable theorem passed an adversarial audit;
- the exact reducer passed independent quotient-oracle, mutation, replay, and
  held-out checks; and
- the conductor equality, heredity lemma, and counterexample have separate
  proof and computational evidence tracks.

What remains open:

- signed external specialist review of the stationary-phase bridge and the new
  conductor equality;
- external independent reproduction;
- a complete decomposable affine/Hurwitz atlas for \(q\le3\);
- a dedicated classification of equivariant block fusion under Ritt moves;
- novelty/priority confirmation beyond the recorded scoped searches; and
- any claim of settled novelty, priority, or field-wide significance.

These are assurance dimensions and open research problems, not reasons to hide
an internally replayed candidate. The appropriate publication status is an
**unrefereed Evidence Press candidate** with the limitations stated explicitly.
Publication makes the exact package available for criticism, reproduction, or
refutation; it does not turn producer replay into independent verification.
