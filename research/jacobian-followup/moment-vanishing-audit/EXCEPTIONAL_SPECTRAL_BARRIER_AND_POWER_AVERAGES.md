# Exceptional orbit spans and spectral tests for power averages

15 November 2026. Internal written proof extending the
[nonspherical reduction](NONSPHERICAL_PAIRS_AND_EXCEPTIONAL_SL2.md).
The general exceptional moment problems remain open. This note classifies
an entire family generated by the existing normalizer witness and gives an
exact spectral method for testing higher-power averages.
The [checker](verify_exceptional_spectral.py) and
[source audit](exceptional_spectral_source_audit.json) distinguish finite
arithmetic certificates from the all-parameter proof. Independent specialist
review and formal verification remain outstanding.

The span of every averaged right translate of the normalizer witness
contains no counterexample. Its pure-moment ideal is a doubled hyperplane
for the binary tetrahedral and octahedral groups; for the binary
icosahedral group every member is unstable and pure-null. Higher powers
before averaging are a different family. The standard powers 2 through 6
on the two remaining models each have an explicitly nonzero second moment.

**Later result.** The [Haar-shear theorem](HAAR_SHEARS_AND_GENERAL_NULLCONE_CLASSIFICATION.md)
now closes moment-nullcone detection for all reductive homogeneous pairs,
without sphericity. Its witnesses are eventually marked-null. The two
exceptional global Mathieu--Zhao questions remain open.

## 1. Conventions and the family

Let G=SL_2(C), let h be normalized Reynolds on A=C[G], and write

    g=[[x,y],[z,w]],       xw-yz=1,
    u=-2xy,  v=2zw,  tau=xw+yz,       uv+tau^2=1,
    U=u^2,   V=v^2,   S=tau^2.

Use the regular right-normalizer-invariant circuit

    P_N=(1+U)(V-S(U+2)(2-S(U^2+2U+2))).

For a finite subgroup Gamma of G define

    E_Gamma f(g) = (1/|Gamma|) sum_(gamma in Gamma) f(g gamma),
    R_a f(g)=f(g a),
    W_Gamma=E_Gamma span_C{R_a P_N : a in G}.              (1)

Thus W_Gamma permits arbitrary finite complex linear combinations and
arbitrary complex right translates before averaging. It is a vector space
of functions on G/Gamma, not an algebra.

A substitution g -> diag(t,t^(-1))g gives exponents -4,0,4,8,12,16
in the exact expansion

    P_N = V + B(tau)
          + U(-6S+10S^2) + U^2(-2S+10S^2)
          + 5U^3 S^2 + U^4 S^2,
    B(tau)=1-6tau^2+5tau^4.                              (2)

These are substitution exponents. The action on functions uses the inverse
substitution, so positive exponents contract under the inverse cocharacter.
Right translations and E_Gamma commute with this left torus.

## 2. A graded moment lemma including the ordinary ideal

Let B be a finite-dimensional complex subspace of a graded G-algebra with
an invariant scalar functional h. Suppose every member of B has only
nonnegative substitution exponents, and the image of its exponent-zero
projection is the line C c. Assume

    h(c)=0,                 kappa=h(c^2) != 0.

Write the zero projection as lambda(f)c, with lambda a nonzero linear
functional on B. The subspace B itself need not be stable under the torus.

**Lemma.** For every f in B and m>=1,

    h(f^m)=h(c^m) lambda(f)^m.                            (3)

Consequently the following conditions are equivalent:

* every positive pure moment of f vanishes;
* h(f^2)=0;
* lambda(f)=0;
* f lies in the G-nullcone.

For the last equivalence, all positive exponents contract under the one
cocharacter, while an invariant second moment must vanish on the nullcone.
If the positive exponents are at least delta, any fixed marker Q whose
exponents lie between -e and e satisfies

    h(Q f^m)=0 whenever m delta>e

on the pure-null locus.

On the coefficient ring C[B], the ordinary ideal generated by all positive
pure moment polynomials is exactly

    I_infinity=(lambda^2),        radical(I_infinity)=(lambda). (4)

Indeed positive-exponent terms do not contribute to h(f^m), proving (3).
The first moment is identically zero; the second generates (lambda^2),
and every later moment is a multiple of this square. Thus the scheme is
a doubled hyperplane, not a reduced hyperplane. The least pure-only prefix
and least ordinary generating prefix are both 2.

If the zero projection is instead zero, all pure moment polynomials vanish
identically, every member contracts, and the ordinary moment ideal is zero.

## 3. Classification of the entire averaged right-orbit span

Write V_n=Sym^n(C^2). The retained exact finite-group character series give

    dim V_4^Gamma = 0                  for Gamma=2T,2O,2I,
    dim V_8^Gamma = 1,1,0              respectively.       (5)

**Theorem.** The spaces (1) have the following dimensions and moment schemes.

| Gamma | dim W_Gamma | Pure-null support | Ordinary pure-moment ideal |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 2T | 9 | A hyperplane of dimension 8 | Square of its linear equation |
| 2O | 6 | A hyperplane of dimension 5 | Square of its linear equation |
| 2I | 3 | All of W_Gamma | Zero |

For every member of these spaces, pure-nullity equals membership in the
G-nullcone and is decided by the second moment. Every pure-null member
satisfies the uniform marked bound

    h(Q f^m)=0 whenever 4m>deg_matrix_entries(Q).           (6)

In particular no choice of complex right translates or finite linear
combination in (1) supplies an exceptional counterexample. The conclusion
holds for any conjugate of Gamma in G.

### Right representation support and dimensions

On G/T use the adjoint sphere with coordinates
u=sqrt(1-tau^2) exp(i phi), v=conjugate(u) on its compact real form.
Right-normalizer invariance keeps only even spherical harmonic degrees l.
The right SL_2 types of P_N are precisely

    V_4, V_8, V_12, V_16, V_20, V_24,                    (7)

each with one cyclic copy in its right-orbit span.

Here is an algebraic verification of nonzero occurrence. In (2), the
components with angular exponents 2j, j=-1,0,...,4, have polynomial
amplitudes of largest harmonic degree 4+2j. For j=1,2,3,4 their highest
coefficients are nonzero, supplying l=6,8,10,12. The negative piece V has
degree l=2, and B has nonzero degree l=4. There is no l=0 term since h(P_N)=0.

For each l the right-T-fixed vector is one-dimensional. Thus the
Peter--Weyl coefficient tensor of P_N at this l has rank one, and its
right-orbit span is a single irreducible V_(2l). These irreducibles have
different highest weights. Complete reducibility separates their cyclic
summands, proving (7) as an assertion about the right-orbit span, not merely
a list of weights. Averaging maps each summand onto its Gamma-fixed space.

The already certified invariant dimensions give the complete count:

| Gamma | n=4 | n=8 | n=12 | n=16 | n=20 | n=24 | Sum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2T | 0 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 9 |
| 2O | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 6 |
| 2I | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 3 |

### Zero projection and its nondegenerate line

The only negative piece in (2) lies in the right type V_4, so (5) kills
the negative projection of every member of (1). The zero piece is

    B(tau)=(-8/7) P_2(tau)+(8/7) P_4(tau),               (8)

where P_l is the ordinary Legendre polynomial. Its right types are V_4
and V_8. Equation (5) therefore leaves exactly one possible zero line for
2T and 2O, and no zero part for 2I.

For 2T and 2O this line is actually attained: the V_8 term in (8) is
nonzero, its right translates span V_8, and averaging onto the nonzero
fixed line is surjective.

Its bilinear Haar square is nonzero even for complex, nonunitary
conjugates of Gamma. To see this without a positivity assumption, use
the nondegenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form on V_8. Finite
averaging E_Gamma is a self-adjoint idempotent for this form, since the
set of inverses of Gamma equals Gamma. Its fixed space therefore has
nondegenerate restricted form. When that space has dimension one, its
nonzero vectors have nonzero square.

The left weight-zero line is nondegenerate as well: invariance pairs
opposite weights, and a zero vector in the pairing on that one-dimensional
zero-weight space would be orthogonal to the whole irreducible module.
Schur orthogonality now makes the Haar square of the resulting nonzero
matrix coefficient nonzero. This proves the needed kappa != 0 for the
graded lemma.

The remaining exponents are among 4,8,12,16. Apply Section 2 with delta=4
to prove the theorem, including the full ordinary ideals and (6).

For the standard 2T and 2O embeddings, the zero line has the explicit
normalization C=(5/3)(x^4+y^4+z^4)-1 on the adjoint sphere, as in the
earlier octahedral computation. Both groups send the first axis onto the
six signed coordinate axes. Thus C(1)=2/3 and h(C^2)=16/189, giving

    lambda(f)=(3/2) [t^0] f(diag(t,t^(-1))),
    h(f^2)=(16/189) lambda(f)^2.                         (6a)

There is a concrete complex-parameter control. For a_s=[[1,s],[0,1]] and
f_s=E_Gamma R_(a_s) P_N, exact finite-group polynomial arithmetic gives

    lambda(f_s)=1+5s^4,
    h(f_s^2)=(16/189)(1+5s^4)^2                         (6b)

for Gamma=2T or 2O, for every complex s. The checker verifies polynomial
coefficients, not just samples of s. Hence this one-parameter family is
pure-null exactly at the four roots of 1+5s^4; each such member contracts.
Its full ordinary pure-moment ideal in C[s] is ((1+5s^4)^2), describing
four double points. On 2I the entire parameter family is pure-null and
contracts. These explicit complex translates therefore do not evade the
orbit-span theorem.

Adding a constant does not create a counterexample: the first moment
forces that constant to vanish. Applying one common left translate to a
member of W_Gamma also preserves its moment and nullcone conclusions.
The linear span of functions having different left translates is a larger
space; it is not classified here.

## 4. A spectral second-moment formula

Let f be any right-T-invariant regular function on G. Decompose it by
spherical harmonic degree as f=sum_l f_l, and put

    b_l=h(f_l^2),
    w_l(Gamma)=(1/|Gamma|) sum_(gamma in Gamma) P_l(tau(gamma)).

Then

    h((E_Gamma f)^2)=sum_l b_l w_l(Gamma).                (9)

For a finite Gamma contained in SU_2, w_l is the squared length of the
projection of the unit zonal vector onto its Gamma-fixed space. In
particular w_l>=0. This does not make (9) nonnegative: b_l is a bilinear
square of a complex function, not its Hermitian norm.

To prove (9), finite averaging is self-adjoint and idempotent for the
bilinear pairing h(fg), so h((Ef)^2)=h(f Ef). Distinct spherical harmonic
degrees are orthogonal. The right-T-fixed vector in degree l has
dimension one, so Schur orthogonality gives

    h(f_l(g) f_l(g gamma))=b_l P_l(tau(gamma)).

The zonal coefficient is P_l by the Legendre addition formula. Averaging
this equality proves (9). This argument also works for complex finite
subgroups, without the nonnegativity assertion about w_l.

The normalizations agree with NIST DLMF
[14.17.6](https://dlmf.nist.gov/14.17.E6) and
[14.18.2](https://dlmf.nist.gov/14.18.E2). Their selected TeX equations are
cached and hashed in the source audit. The new reduction (9) uses these
standard identities and the above Schur argument.

## 5. Coefficients for arbitrary positive powers

Set f=P_N^k, k>=1. Temporarily localizing at U, the regular circuit obeys

    P_N^k=U^(-k)(1+U)^k (1-(1+U)^2 tau^2)^(2k).

Thus its coefficient of U^j is the polynomial

    F_(k,j)(t)=sum_(r=0)^(2k)
       (-1)^r binom(2k,r) binom(k+2r,j+k) t^(2r),         (10)

with binomial coefficients outside their range equal to zero.
On the sphere U=(1-t^2) exp(2i phi), so its angular amplitude is

    A_(k,j)(t)=(1-t^2)^j F_(k,j)(t),        -k<=j<=4k.

For j<0, F_(k,j) is divisible by (1-t^2)^(-2j), as follows either from
regularity of the original circuit or direct polynomial division in (10).
Therefore all A_(k,j) are polynomial multiples of (1-t^2)^|j|.

Expand them in associated Legendre polynomials of even order:

    A_(k,j)(t)=sum_l a_(l,j) P_l^(2|j|)(t).

No square-root arithmetic is required because 2|j| is even. Triangular
polynomial expansion after removing (1-t^2)^|j| computes every coefficient.
Orthogonality gives the independent exact-integral formula for those
coefficients and yields

    b_l = ( a_(l,0)^2
            +2 sum_(j=1)^min(k,l/2)
               a_(l,j) a_(l,-j) (l+2j)!/(l-2j)! )/(2l+1). (11)

Only even l<=4k can contribute to (11). For positive j, its negative partner
has maximum harmonic degree 4k-2j; the zero amplitude has maximum degree
4k. The much higher, strictly positive angular pieces cannot contribute
to a bilinear square. This is why the second-moment calculation avoids a
large four-variable expansion.

Equations (9)--(11) are valid for every positive integer k. They are an
exact second-moment test, not an all-order pure-nullity decision whenever
the resulting second moment happens to be zero.

Every tested k>=2 therefore fails pure-nullity. This conclusion for each

or to linear combinations of different powers.

The checker uses rational polynomial arithmetic throughout. It verifies
342 associated-Legendre coefficients independently by polynomial
integration, checks the zonal recurrence against direct polynomial
evaluation, and recovers h(P_N^(2k))=0 for k=1,...,6 by summing the
unprojected contributions. The earlier octahedral value 16/189 is an
additional retained normalization control. A missing factor of 2 in the
paired angular terms is detected.

An initial prototype used Python integer division for an exactly zero
term and introduced floating-point zero. Its false failure of the
unprojected identity is preserved with the input and failure explanation.
The checker enforces Fraction coefficients and retains exact cancellation;
no mathematical conclusion is based on the failed prototype.

## 7. Remaining scope

The universal Mathieu--Zhao classification still depends on the two model
questions SL_2/2O and SL_2/2I. The later Haar-shear theorem closes the
moment-nullcone classification. This note eliminates the full space (1), including all
complex right translates and their linear combinations, and the particular
higher-power averages in the table. It does not exclude every averaged
higher power, their translated or combined families, or combinations with
different left orientations.

The next construction needs additional left-weight interactions or a
different all-order cancellation mechanism. A vanishing second moment is
only a necessary condition outside the classified orbit spans. Any proposed
exceptional counterexample still needs every positive pure moment zero
and either a nonzero invariant separating it from the nullcone or a fixed
marker with an infinite nonzero tail.
