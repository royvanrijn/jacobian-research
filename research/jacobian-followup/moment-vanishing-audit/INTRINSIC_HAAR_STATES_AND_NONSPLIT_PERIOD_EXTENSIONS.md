## 1. The full unlocalized source

First state the construction more generally. Let X be a smooth connected
complex affine variety of dimension n, with a nowhere-vanishing algebraic
top form Omega and dim H^n_dR(X)=1, generated by [Omega]. Let M be a
compact oriented real n-cycle with integral_M Omega!=0. Normalize its
period functional h(Q)=integral_M Q*Omega/integral_M Omega.

Both X=SL2/2O and X=SL2/2I satisfy these hypotheses. Polar decomposition
retracts the quotient onto its compact orientable three-manifold. Its
top cohomology is C; the invariant volume has nonzero normalized period.
The finite central/right covers and algebraic de Rham comparison retain
this conclusion. No spherical or isotypic assumption occurs here.

For ANY commutative C-algebra R, put A_R=C[X] tensor R and choose
P in A_R. All differential forms below are relative to R. The full
exponential top source is

    H_P=Omega^n(X)_R[t] /
          (d+t*dP wedge) Omega^(n-1)(X)_R[t].                (1)

It is a left D_R=R[t]<partial_t>-module. Multiplication by t acts
normally, and partial_t acts on representatives by partial_t+P.
These actions preserve the relations in(1), since the commutator of
partial_t+P with d+t*dP wedge is0. All top forms are Q*Omega, with
Q an ORIGINAL regular function; no amplitude subalgebra is substituted.

Reduction modulo t is an exact top-degree cokernel calculation:

    H_P/tH_P = Omega^n(X)_R/d Omega^(n-1)(X)_R
              = H^n_dR(X) tensor R = R[Omega].               (2)

It does NOT assume that H_P is finite over R[t], flat in P, cyclic
over D_R, or that specialization commutes with a lower cohomology group.

Stokes' theorem defines the normalized formal period map

    lambda_P([Q*Omega])=h(Q exp(tP))
      =sum_(m>=0) h(QP^m)t^m/m! in R[[t]].                  (3)

More generally polynomial t-dependent amplitudes are integrated
coefficientwise. The relations in(1) are integrals of
d(exp(tP)*beta), so they vanish. The map is D_R-linear.

## 2. A complete formal-state and polynomial-state theorem

For ANY left D_R-module H, a D_R-linear map to R[[t]] is uniquely
determined by its constant-term functional ell:H/tH->R. Conversely
every R-linear ell gives such a map by the EXACT formula

    phi(v)=sum_(m>=0) ell(partial_t^m v mod tH)*t^m/m!.      (4)

The derivative identity is immediate. To check t-linearity, use
partial_t^m(tv)=t*partial_t^m(v)+m*partial_t^(m-1)(v).
The first term is0 modulo tH, giving phi(tv)=t*phi(v).
No convergence or finite-generation assertion is used.

Applying(2), all formal states of H_P are exactly

    Hom_(D_R)(H_P,R[[t]])=R*lambda_P.                       (5)

The scalar a is phi([Omega])(0). Thus the normalized formal Haar state
is UNIQUE over every coefficient algebra, even nonreduced or non-Noetherian.
This is an intrinsic top-cohomology argument, independent of general
Gaussian field uniformity, stationary phase and inverse-map hypotheses.

Let

    J_P={a in R: a*h(Q exp(tP)) is polynomial in t
                   for EVERY original Q in A_R}.           (6)

Equation(5) identifies J_P EXACTLY with the image of

    Hom_(D_R)(H_P,R[t]) -> R, phi -> phi([Omega])(0).         (7)

It is an ordinary coefficient ideal. Therefore

## 3. Every failure is an indecomposable nonsplit extension

Return to R=C and the compact cycle. Every period in(3) is entire of
exponential type, by compactness. Let

    V_P=sum_(Q in C[X]) C[t]*h(Q exp(tP)) subset O(C).       (9)

This is the ACTUAL D-module image of H_P for that cycle. It is not
the abstract source or its generic connection. It is holonomic: the
Gauss--Manin direct-image construction and Fourier transformation give
a holonomic H_P, and its image is a holonomic quotient.

Since H_P/tH_P has dimension1, V_P/tV_P has dimension at most1.
Its unit-amplitude period has constant term1, so is not in tV_P.
Consequently

    dim(V_P/tV_P)=1.                                       (10)

Every nonzero C[t]-submodule L of C[[t]] has L/tL!=0. Otherwise
L=tL makes every element divisible by every t^m inside C[[t]], hence0.
Thus(10) proves that V_P is INDECOMPOSABLE as a D-module: a direct
sum of two nonzero submodules would have at least two dimensions
modulo t. This holds even before imposing purity.

Now suppose P is pure, so1 belongs to V_P, and write

    O=C[t], C_P=V_P/O,
    0 -> O -> V_P -> C_P -> 0.                             (11)

The constant1 spans(10), so C_P/tC_P=0: multiplication by t on C_P
is SURJECTIVE. It is injective as well. Indeed if a nonzero polynomial
r(t) times an entire F(t) is polynomial, F is rational and entire,
hence polynomial. Thus C_P is torsion-free over C[t], not only
t-torsion-free. In particular t acts BIJECTIVELY on C_P.

If C_P!=0, sequence(11) cannot split even as C[t]-modules. A section
would embed the t-surjective module C_P into V_P subset C[[t]], which
the preceding infinite-divisibility argument forbids. Hence it cannot
split as D-modules either. Conversely C_P=0 is exactly all-original-
marker safety. Therefore a pure phase fails MZ EXACTLY when its actual
module has a NONZERO quotient C_P and the resulting NONSPLIT sequence(11).

This sharpens generic-rank language: by entireness the quotient has
positive generic rank if nonzero, but it is the globally glued extension
with O that matters. Applying inverse Fourier transformation gives a
quotient of the ordinary top Gauss--Manin module in a nonsplit sequence

    0 -> delta_0 -> G_actual -> Fourier^-1(C_P) -> 0,       (12)

where partial_s is invertible on the last term. Here delta_0 is the

not an assertion that the entire Gauss--Manin module is that quotient.

**Theorem.** If that generic middle cohomology is0, every pure or
eventually pure P is safe for ALL original markers.

Here is a direct proof that also retains every exceptional fibre.

    G_P/uG_P = H^n_dR(X) = C.                              (14)

A holonomic point-supported D_s-module is a direct sum of copies of
delta_c. The following elementary Weyl argument supplies the exact

On a c-supported component let T=s-c; T is locally nilpotent and
[T,u]=-1. Define the finite Taylor projector

    Pi(v)=sum_(j>=0) u^j*T^j(v)/j!.

Then T*Pi(v)=0 by cancellation of adjacent terms, and

    v=sum_(j>=0) (-1)^j*u^j*Pi(T^j v)/j!.                  (15)

Applying powers of T proves uniqueness of these coefficients. Thus
the component is C[u] tensor ker(T), a sum of delta_c modules.
Finite holonomic length makes the total number of copies finite.
Its quotient by u has dimension equal to that number, and(14)
therefore forces

    G_P=delta_c for ONE scalar c, H_P=O*exp(c*t).           (16)

## 6. Why localized semisimplicity cannot close the theorem

A source check matters here. Sabbah's Theorem5.1.1, printed25/PDF31,
proves semisimplicity for a cohomologically tame function AFTER
localizing its Fourier--Laplace system at t. It does NOT assert
semisimplicity of G_P or H_P in Section4. Printed26/PDF32 explicitly
retains a map from a semisimple middle-extension system to G_P whose
kernel and cokernel are smooth modules; their Fourier transforms are
point terms at t=0. The localized theorem forgets those terms. Neither
tameness nor a generic semisimple connection is presumed to imply the
unlocalized hypothesis of Section4.

There is an exact countercontrol using ENTIRE functions. Put

    f(t)=(exp(t)-1)/t, f(0)=1,
    V=D_t*1 + D_t*f subset O(C).

One has t*f'=(t-1)f+1. Repeated derivatives give, modulo tV,
(j+1)f^(j)=j*f^(j-1), with f=1 at order0. Thus V/tV=C.
But f is transcendental entire, so V!=O. Its exact sequence is

    0 -> O -> V -> O[t^-1]*exp(t) -> 0.                   (19)

Indeed V consists of Laurent-polynomial combinations of1 and exp(t)
that are entire at0. Taking the coefficient of exp(t) gives the map
in(19); its kernel is O. The image contains1/t from f, and its
derivatives generate every negative power, so it is onto. The extension
is nonsplit by the infinite t-divisibility argument. Nevertheless

    V[t^-1]=O[t^-1] direct_sum O[t^-1]*exp(t)

is a SEMISIMPLE connection. This is a parameter-zero lattice defect,
not the finite-pole defect excluded by compactness: every function in
V is entire. It is a module countercontrol, not an exceptional phase
or a claimed complete Gauss--Manin realization.

The coefficient-ring boundary is equally explicit. Replace exp(t) by
exp(e*t) and f by sum_(m>=0)e^m*t^m/(m+1)!; the relation becomes
t*f'=(e*t-1)f+1 without division by e. On R=C[e,b]/(eb), the scalars
making this series polynomial form J=(b), nonzero but not containing1.
Thus a nonzero polynomial-state ideal is insufficient over rings,
exactly as(6)--(8) require.
