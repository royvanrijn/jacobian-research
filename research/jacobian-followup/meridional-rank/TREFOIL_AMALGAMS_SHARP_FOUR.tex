\begin{theorem}\label{thm:knots}
For each of the four named knots
\[
                  13a_{3145},\quad13a_{3147},\quad
                  13a_{3148},\quad13a_{3149},
\]
the meridional rank and bridge number are both four.
\end{theorem}

The lower certificate uses a simple three-sheeted branched cover.
Its group has rank exactly two, although its abelianization is
cyclic and the commutator subgroup is perfect. Exact presentation
rewrites identify the group as an amalgam of two trefoil groups
along injective peripheral $\Z^2$ subgroups. This proves
nonabelianity by the classical amalgam normal-form theorem,
and an independent finite certificate now gives explicit
noncommuting permutations on 25 points for every cover group.
The general cover bound then gives $\mr\ge4$. Independently
replayed four-seed diagram certificates give $b\le4$ through
the published equality of Wirtinger number and bridge number
\cite{wirt}.

The names belong to the previously frozen 293-name panel and
remain in the external project's open list fetched on
17 November 2026 \cite{deposit}. They are outside its 243
deposited certificates. Both archived KnotInfo releases and an
independent HT census give literally identical PDs after the
standard zero/one-based label shift. These facts establish
provenance and project-list scope; they do not establish
worldwide novelty or exclusion from every previously known
family. No such priority claim is made. The uniform arguments
are written working proofs, unformalized and externally
unreviewed. Full classical MRC remains open.

\section{Why noncyclicity of this cover is enough}

Let $\Gamma=\pi_1(S^3\setminus K)$ and let
$\phi:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow S_r$ take every meridian to a
transposition. Complete the associated connected exterior cover
to the simple branched cover $Y_\phi$.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rank}
If $\Gamma$ is generated by $d$ meridians, then
$\pi_1(Y_\phi)$ is generated by at most $d-r+1$ elements.
Consequently
\[
                    \mr(K)\ge r-1+\rank\pi_1(Y_\phi).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use a presentation on the $d$ specified meridians, with any
complete set of relations. Its lifted graph has $r$ vertices.
For each generator, its transposition gives two exchanged-sheet
edges and $r-2$ fixed-sheet loops. Branched filling kills each
fixed-sheet meridian loop and the two-edge meridian-square
cycle. Thus the two exchanged-sheet edges become oppositely
oriented copies of one edge.
These are legitimate filling relations for every chosen
generator: the meridians are conjugate, and their lifted
filling loops are conjugates of the same boundary meridian
loops. No extra filling beyond the branched completion is
being imposed. There remain exactly $d$ edges
on $r$ vertices. They form a connected graph because the
monodromy is transitive. Kill a spanning tree with $r-1$ edges.
The remaining $d-r+1$ edges generate; lifted relators impose
relations but add no generators. This proves the assertion for
every meridional generating family, not only a bridge diagram.
\end{proof}

For $r=3$, a nonabelian cover group has rank at least two and
therefore gives $\mr\ge4$. This is the nonabelian counterpart
of the retained scalar identity
$\dim Z^1_{\rm ad}=2+b_1(Y_\phi;k)$. Cyclic homology can miss
the group-rank gain.

\section{The four exact two-generator normal forms}

The retained onto $S_3$ colouring is unique up to sheet
relabelling on each of these four diagrams. Its cover
presentation is obtained by lifting every Wirtinger relator
from all three sheets. Fixed-sheet loops vanish, exchanged
edges are inverse, and two spanning-tree edges are set to one,
as in Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}.

Only exact group isomorphisms are used in simplification:
free/cyclic reduction, inversion and relator conjugation,
duplicate removal, one-occurrence Tietze elimination,
invertible Nielsen substitutions, and substitution of a
subword using another retained relator. Every latter move
keeps the relation used, so it preserves the normal closure
in both directions. The complete moves and inverse maps are
retained and independently replayed.

For $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$ define
\begin{align}
 A_\epsilon&=\langle a,b\mid
 b^3a^2b^3=a,\quad a^{3\epsilon}ba^{3\epsilon}=b^2\rangle,
                                                        \label{eq:A}\\
 B_\epsilon&=\langle a,b\mid
 b^2ab^2=a^2,\quad (a^{3\epsilon}b)^3=b^2\rangle.
                                                        \label{eq:B}
\end{align}
The verified transport is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 K&\pi_1(Y_\phi)&H_1(Y_\phi;\Z)\\\hline
 13a_{3145}&A_{+1}&\Z/37\\
 13a_{3147}&A_{-1}&\Z/35\\
 13a_{3148}&B_{+1}&\Z/37\\
 13a_{3149}&B_{-1}&\Z/35.
\end{array}
\]

For the third case a targeted extra basis change is needed:
in the retained structural basis substitute
$b_{\rm old}=b_{\rm new}a^{-2}$, whose inverse is
$b_{\rm new}=b_{\rm old}a^2$. Two further retained-relator
shortenings give \eqref{eq:B}. Relative to the original
two-generator checkpoint, the new generator is
$a^2b_{\rm original}a^2$. This is an invertible basis change,
not a quotient or an added relation. The other three final
normal forms already match \eqref{eq:A} or \eqref{eq:B}.

The abelianizations follow directly from the exponent matrices,
whose first Smith factors are one. Their determinants have
absolute values 37, 35, 37 and 35. These homology counts alone
do not distinguish the groups from cyclic groups of those orders.

\section{Explicit finite commutator certificates}

Identify the 25 points with $\F_5^2$, numbering $(x,y)$ by $x+5y$.
Let the permutations $p,q$ of $\F_5$ have image lists
\[
 p=(3,0,2,1,4),\qquad q=(4,1,3,2,0).
\]
These are image lists, not cycle notation. Define
\[
 \alpha(x,y)=(p(x),y+2),\qquad
 \delta(x,y)=(q(x),y+3).
\]
Then $\delta^2=\alpha^3=f$, where $f(x,y)=(x,y+1)$, and
$\mu=\delta\alpha^{-1}$ is $(x,y)\mapsto(x+1,y+1)$.
In particular, the peripheral image $\langle\mu,f\rangle$
is the regular translation group $C_5^2$. The vertex image
$\langle\alpha,\delta\rangle$ is $A_5\times C_5$, of order 300:
its first-coordinate projection is $\langle p,q\rangle=A_5$,
and the central translation $f$ separates the second coordinate.

For $\epsilon=\pm1$, the invertible linear permutation
\[
 F_\epsilon(x,y)=(x-y,(1+\epsilon)x-y)
\]
sends the translations $\mu,f$ to $f^\epsilon,\mu^{-1}$,
respectively. The following images satisfy the model relators:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 \text{model}&a&b\\\hline
 A_\epsilon&\alpha&F_\epsilon\alpha F_\epsilon^{-1}\\
 B_\epsilon&\alpha&(-F_\epsilon)\delta(-F_\epsilon)^{-1}.
\end{array}
\]
Here $-F_\epsilon$ negates both output coordinates. These formulas
can be checked directly on all 25 points; equivalently, the
peripheral translations satisfy the two gluing identifications
in Propositions~\ref{prop:A} and~\ref{prop:B} below.

The certificate uses the original retained two-generator
checkpoint, before the structural rewrites. Its first generator
is always $\alpha$. The second is the displayed model image
for $13a_{3145}$ and $13a_{3147}$, is
$\alpha^{-2}b_{\rm model}\alpha^{-2}$ for $13a_{3148}$,
and is $\alpha^2b_{\rm model}$ for $13a_{3149}$.
With $[A,B]=ABA^{-1}B^{-1}$ and composition acting rightmost
first, the literal checkpoint certificates give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 K&\text{degree}&\text{relators checked}&[A,B](i)\\\hline
 13a_{3145}&25&6&0\mapsto11\\
 13a_{3147}&25&6&5\mapsto9\\
 13a_{3148}&25&6&0\mapsto3\\
 13a_{3149}&25&6&0\mapsto2.
\end{array}
\]
Every relator fixes every point, while the last column proves
noncommutation. For example, the first case moves $(0,0)$ to
$(1,2)$. Thus all four groups are nonabelian, giving rank two
from the retained two-generator presentations. This finite
proof of nonabelianity needs neither amalgam normal form nor
an appeal to residual finiteness.

\texttt{finite\_cover\_certificates.json} contains all eight
permutation image lists and all 24 literal checkpoint words.
The standalone standard-library checker
\path{check_finite_cover_certificates.py} evaluates these
words and the moved-point witnesses. It checks the algebra;
the named-knot and upper-bound dependencies are checked separately.
The fuller independent replay evaluates words point by point
in reverse letter order, extends the images to all eleven
original cover generators, and checks all 39 original branched
relators for each knot. Independent planar arc traces rebuild
those words. Four corruptions are rejected: a malformed
permutation, a false appended relator, a commuting pair, and
an incorrect moved-point image.

These are additional finite proofs for the same four retained
cases, not additional named equalities. Neither minimal action
degree nor the order of the full two-generator image is claimed.

\section{Peripheral injections in the trefoil group}

Consider
\[
                 T=\langle d,a\mid d^2=a^3\rangle,
 \qquad f=d^2=a^3,\qquad \mu=da^{-1}.
\]
The element $f$ is central. Abelianization sends $d$ to three,
$a$ to two and $f$ to six in $\Z$, so $f$ has infinite order.
Quotienting by $f$ gives $C_2*C_3$; the image of $\mu$ is a
cyclically reduced word of length two. Every nonzero power
has nonempty reduced form in that free product.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:peripheral}
The commuting pair $(\mu,f)$ generates a copy of $\Z^2$ in $T$.
The group $T$ is infinite and nonabelian.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $\mu^u f^v=1$, passage to $C_2*C_3$ forces $u=0$.
Then the infinite order of $f$ forces $v=0$. Noncommutation
of $d,a$ is already visible in $C_2*C_3$.
\end{proof}

For the trefoil interpretation, $(\mu,f)$ is a meridian and
regular-fibre pair. The algebraic proof above supplies every
injectivity fact needed below, without importing a peripheral
incompressibility theorem.

The normal-form theorem for free products with amalgamation
\cite{serre} says that if the edge subgroup injects into both
vertex groups, each vertex group embeds into the amalgam.
One constructs normal forms using coset representatives for
the edge subgroup in the two factors: a nonempty alternating
reduced sequence cannot represent identity. This is the only
general amalgam fact needed here; it is an explicit classical
input, not a conclusion inferred from finite controls.

\section{The two peripheral amalgams}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:A}
The group $A_\epsilon$ is the amalgam of
\[
 T_1=\langle d,a\mid d^2=a^3\rangle,\qquad
 T_2=\langle e,b\mid e^2=b^3\rangle
\]
with peripheral identifications
\[
 \mu_1=f_2^{-1},\qquad f_1=\mu_2^\epsilon,
 \quad
 (\mu_1,f_1)=(da^{-1},a^3),\quad
 (\mu_2,f_2)=(eb^{-1},b^3).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The edge basis matrix is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\epsilon\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
of determinant $\epsilon$, so it identifies the two copies of
$\Z^2$. The first edge relation gives $d=b^{-3}a$ and the
second gives $e=a^{3\epsilon}b$. Substitute in the two vertex
relations. They become
\[
 b^{-3}ab^{-3}=a^2,\qquad
 (a^{3\epsilon}b)^2=b^3,
\]
which are precisely \eqref{eq:A}. Conversely those formulas
define $d,e$ and recover every vertex and edge relation, proving
an isomorphism of the presentations.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:B}
The group $B_\epsilon$ is the amalgam of
\[
 T_1=\langle d,a\mid d^2=a^3\rangle,\qquad
 T_2=\langle b,e\mid b^2=e^3\rangle
\]
with peripheral identifications
\[
 \mu_1=f_2,\qquad f_1=\mu_2^{-\epsilon},
 \quad
 (\mu_1,f_1)=(da^{-1},a^3),\quad
 (\mu_2,f_2)=(be^{-1},b^2).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The edge basis matrix is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-\epsilon\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
again of determinant $\epsilon$. Eliminate
$d=b^2a$ and $e=a^{3\epsilon}b$. The vertex relations become
$b^2ab^2=a^2$ and $(a^{3\epsilon}b)^3=b^2$, exactly
\eqref{eq:B}; the inverse definitions recover the full
amalgam presentation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:groups}
All four cover groups are infinite and nonabelian, and have
rank exactly two.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:peripheral} gives both edge injections. Amalgam
normal form embeds a nonabelian trefoil vertex group, proving
the lower rank bound two. The displayed two-generator
presentations give the upper rank bound two.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of the four knot equalities and provenance}

Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} and Corollary~\ref{cor:groups} give
$\mr(K)\ge2+2=4$. For each source PD, four meridional seed arcs
propagate to all thirteen arcs by Wirtinger colouring moves.
The independent replay constructs these arcs by tracing the
planar diagram from every undercrossing exit, rather than
using the producer's union-find parser. The source parser is
used only to transport the stored certificate indices. The
new arc relations, all three starting-sheet branched words
and the complete seed propagation are then checked directly.

The published theorem $b=\omega$ for diagram Wirtinger number
\cite{wirt} turns these certificates into $b(K)\le4$.
Together with $\mr\le b$ this proves Theorem~\ref{thm:knots}.
The lower argument concerns meridional rank, not merely the
geometric bridge count, because Lemma~\ref{lem:rank} applies
to any meridional generating family.

The PD for every named case agrees literally with KnotInfo
snapshots 2026.10.5 and 2026.11.2. The independent census lookup
\texttt{K13a3145}, and similarly for the other three, has the
same PD after adding one to every edge label. The census and
archived full planar-walk checks are retained in
\texttt{splice\_knot\_provenance.json}, with pinned SnapPy 3.3.2,
Spherogram 2.4.1 and \texttt{snappy\_15\_knots} 1.2.1.
No numerical hyperbolicity or approximate recognition is used.

The archived rows have bridge interval $[3,4]$. The external
main-branch open list fetched on 17 November contains all four;
its 243 deposited named certificates contain none. This is a
precise source-status observation, not a claim that every
publication or known family has been excluded. Blank Conway
or Montesinos metadata is not used to prove non-membership.
The original sixteen-target prefix remains unchanged: these
four cases are elsewhere in the frozen 293-name panel.

\section{Why the homological and solvable tests failed}

The exact normal-form work also proves three more of the
26 degree-three cover groups cyclic, of orders two, two and
four. Thus their complete classification for the tested rank
gate is: nineteen cyclic groups of order two, two of order
four, one trivial, and the four rank-two amalgams above.
All 26 cover-group ranks are now determined.

For each of the four amalgams, a separate finite
Reidemeister--Schreier calculation computes the integral
homology of the universal abelian cover. Its degree is 37 or
35, and every Smith factor of the reduced matrix is one:
\[
                   H_1([G,G];\Z)=0.
\]
Here $G=\pi_1(Y_\phi)$, and the cyclic quotient is the full
abelianization, as independently checked from the two exponent
relations. The reduced homology matrices have 38 or 36 columns;
lifted-word reconstruction and every unimodular Smith step
are replayed, not just a black-box invariant-factor output.

\begin{corollary}
The commutator subgroup of each of these four cover groups
is nontrivial and perfect. Every solvable quotient of the
cover group is cyclic.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Zero first homology means $G'=[G',G']=G''$. It is nontrivial
because $G$ is nonabelian. In a solvable image, the image of
$G'$ is both perfect and solvable, hence trivial. The image
therefore factors through the cyclic abelianization.
\end{proof}

This excludes every solvable coefficient-group search on
these cover groups as a nonabelianity test, not just the
earlier $S_3,A_4,D_{10},D_{14}$ proposals. Their ordinary
homology is cyclic in every field, so scalar lift bounds
reach at most three. The same four knots admit no
representation satisfying the original nonzero-common-form
binary criterion, by the retained rank-two equivalence and
their upper-four bound. The amalgam certificate supplies
information that these completed affine and abelian routes
cannot supply. No universal finite-quotient detection claim
is made.

A separately frozen follow-up exhausts all 21 onto $S_4$
transposition colourings on the same 293-name panel. For
degree four, any nontrivial cover group would already give
sharp four by Lemma~\ref{lem:rank}. In every case, however,
independent folded-word reconstruction and explicit Tietze
eliminations remove all ten generators and leave the trivial
group. Thus the degree-four group-rank gate gives no further
case. There are no degree-five bases, and the four-generator
connected-transposition-graph argument excludes higher
degrees. All simple-cover group-rank tests of degrees at
least three on this fixed panel are now exhausted: precisely
the four cases above pass. Double-cover ranks and other
representation mechanisms remain open. A trivial cover
group does not itself supply a three-bridge presentation of
the branch knot.

and splice plans/results, with
