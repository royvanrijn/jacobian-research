# HC4 structural audit, transport and polynomiality

This follow-up audits ingredients of [Tang's HC4 normal-form claim](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00156v1)
and proves a sharp extension of its characteristic calculation to rational
potentials. The main additions are a denominator bound in every dimension,
an explicit family separating rational from polynomial Hessian metrics, and
an exact local Frobenius certificate. A [subsequent complete written structural review](STRUCTURAL_REVIEW.md)
now reproduces the external HC4--JC2 equivalence as HCTANG2. Its
[applications](APPLICATIONS.md), HCTANG3, prove HC4 through potential degree
101, exclude marked-collision Meng--Yang graphs through degree 48, and give
a finite constant-covector recognition certificate. Neither conjecture is
settled, and no novelty priority is claimed.

The later [all-degree graph application](../hc4-polynomial-graphs/README.md)
uses the structural alternative and an elementary no-affine-plane obstruction
to exclude the entire standard polynomial-graph collision route. This
replaces the bounded marked-graph frontier without any plane degree import.

## Sharp denominator bound for rational transport

Let K have characteristic zero, n>=1, X=(X1,...,Xn), and let
h=P/Q in K(X,w), with P,Q polynomials and Q(X,0) nonzero. Suppose the full
(n+1)-variable Hessian of h has rank n, and

\[
 A(X)=\operatorname{Hess}_Xh(X,0)
\]

is invertible over K(X). Define

\[
 v=A^{-1}\nabla_Xh_w(X,0),\quad
 D(w)=\det(I-wDv),\quad q(w)=Q(X-wv(X),w).
\]

**Theorem.** In K(X)[w],

\[
 \boxed{D(w)\mid q(w)^n.}                         \tag{1}
\]

The exponent n is optimal as a uniform bound. In particular, if q is a
nonzero constant in w, then Dv is nilpotent. This includes polynomial h by
taking Q=1. A rational reduction with q nonconstant needs additional
information before nilpotence can be inferred.

**Characteristic identity.** Over an algebraic closure, the gradient image
has irreducible hypersurface closure. Let R be its reduced equation and
set C=grad R(grad h). Generic smoothness and density imply C is nonzero.
Differentiating the relation gives Hess(h) C=0. The derivation
C dot grad annihilates grad h and therefore annihilates C. Consequently
its mth power on any rational function g equals the sum of
C_i1 ... C_im times the corresponding mth partial derivative of g.
For a component of grad h these expressions vanish for every m>=1.
Taylor expansion in s therefore gives grad h(Z+s C(Z))=grad h(Z).
This is a rational identity: the denominator at s=0 is nonzero in the
function field, and its embedding into the formal Laurent series field
is injective. This argument works algebraically in characteristic zero.

On w=0 the Hessian kernel is generated by (-v,1), because its first n rows
have invertible block A. The slice's gradient image has dimension n, so it
is dense in the full gradient image; hence C is nonzero there generically.
Its last coordinate is consequently nonzero. Evaluating the line at the
parameter w/C_w(X,0) gives

\[
 \nabla_Xh(X-wv(X),w)=\nabla_Xh(X,0).             \tag{2}
\]

The identities descend to K. Differentiating (2) in X gives

\[
 B(w)(I-wDv)=A,
 \quad B(w)=\operatorname{Hess}_Xh(X-wv(X),w),
\]

so

\[
 \det B(w)D(w)=\det A.                          \tag{3}
\]

**Divisibility.** Every entry of B has denominator dividing q^3. Thus
q^(3n) det B is polynomial in w. Equation (3) shows that every irreducible
factor of D divides q. But D has degree at most n and D(0)=1; the
multiplicity of any factor of D is at most n. This proves (1), without
assuming q is squarefree or P/Q reduced.

One can improve the intermediate Hessian denominator exponent from 3n to
2n+1, although this is not needed for (1). Put g=grad_X Q and

\[
 M=Q\operatorname{Hess}_XP-P\operatorname{Hess}_XQ
       -(\nabla_XP)g^T-g(\nabla_XP)^T.
\]

Then Hess_X(P/Q)=(QM+2Pgg^T)/Q^3, and determinant multilinearity gives

\[
 \det(QM+2Pgg^T)
   =Q^{n-1}(Q\det M+2P g^T\operatorname{adj}(M)g).
\]

This is valid for singular M too; it does not require using its inverse.

**Sharpness and a rational failure of the apex conclusion.** For every n,

\[
 h(X,w)=\frac{\sum_i X_i^2}{2(1+w)}
\]

has full Hessian rank n, slice metric A=I, v=-X, q=1+w and
D=(1+w)^n. Hence no exponent smaller than n works uniformly. Its gradient
image has equation

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n U_i^2+2U_{n+1}=0.
\]

A translation direction d would force
sum d_i U_i+d_(n+1)=0 for all U1,...,Un, so d=0. In dimension four this
is a rational rank-three singular-Hessian potential with no nonzero
translation direction, even though the slice metric is the constant I3.
The transported Hessian determinant has a pole that cancels D in (3).
The global polynomial hypothesis prevents exactly this cancellation.

## A family with nilpotence and a rational Hessian metric but no planar image

Let K0(T) be any polynomial of degree at least three and write k=K0'. Set

\[
 t=x/y,\quad
 V=(t^2/2,t,K_0(t)),\quad
 f=t+\frac12\bigl(z-y k(t)\bigr)^2,
 \qquad y\ne0.
\]

**Theorem.** With N=DV and H=Hess(f),

\[
 N^2=0,\quad \operatorname{rank}N=1,\quad
 \det H=-y^{-4},\quad HN=(HN)^T.
\]

The image of V lies in no affine plane. Moreover, **every polynomial
potential whose Hessian symmetrizes this N is affine**, so none supplies
a nonsingular polynomial Hessian metric. The rational metric above is
therefore a strict enlargement of the admissible class.

**Proof of the identities.** Let

\[
 a=(1,-t,0)^T,\quad dt=a/y,\quad
 d=(t,1,k)^T,\quad s=z-yk(t).
\]

Then N=d(dt)^T and (dt)^T d=0. Furthermore

\[
 ds=(-k',-k+tk',1)^T,
\]

\[
 H=\begin{pmatrix}0&-y^{-2}&0\\-y^{-2}&2ty^{-2}&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
       +(ds)(ds)^T-\frac{s k''}{y}aa^T.
\]

Both ds and a annihilate d, so Hd=-(dt)/y. Thus
HN=-(dt)(dt)^T/y. Direct expansion gives det H=-y^(-4).
The checker verifies these expressions with k,k',k'' as independent
symbols, so the calculation imposes no degree bound on K0. Nonplanarity
follows from the linear independence of 1,t,t^2,K0(t).

**Polynomial obstruction.** Suppose F0(x,y,z) is polynomial and its
Hessian symmetrizes N. Since N=d(dt)^T and dt is nonzero, symmetry says
Hess(F0)d is proportional to dt. Put F(t,y,z)=F0(ty,y,z).
Contracting Hess(F0)d with (t,1,0) and (0,0,1) yields

\[
 \partial_y(F_y+kF_z)=\partial_z(F_y+kF_z)=0,
\]

so F_y+kF_z depends only on t. Every coefficient of y^j z^ell in F
has t-degree at most j. If the z-degree L is positive, its top coefficient
is independent of y, hence a nonzero constant by that degree constraint.
If L>=2, the coefficient of y z^(L-1) must then be -L c k(t), which has
t-degree at least two, contradicting the bound one. Thus L<=1. Its only
z term is a constant multiple of z, and the displayed differential equation
forces the remaining part to be affine in y. The same coefficient bound
makes it a constant plus (a t+b)y. Hence F0 is affine.

### The associated finite etale map and its source

F=Id+V has Jacobian determinant one and generic degree two. Put
Delta=Y^2-2X in the target. It is a finite etale map

\[
 \operatorname{Spec}K[x,y,z,y^{-1}]
       \longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Spec}K[X,Y,Z,\Delta^{-1}].
\]

Indeed Delta(F)=y^2. Adjoining r with r^2=Delta gives the inverse
description

\[
 y=r,\quad x=r(Y-r),\quad z=Z-K_0(Y-r).
\]

The cover is free of rank two and etale because 2r is a unit. For
K0=t^3/3 an exact collision is

\[
 F(0,1,0)=F(-2,-1,-8/3)=(0,1,0).
\]

Its source has the nonconstant unit y. It is not affine three-space,
and the formulas have poles on y=0. The example is a check on attempts
to replace global polynomial hypotheses by identities on an open set;
it is not a Keller counterexample on affine space.

## Exact local Frobenius gate

Let V be rational in three variables, N=DV nilpotent of rank two, and
assume H=Hess(f) is generically invertible with HN symmetric. Here f may
be rational. At a regular point a constant source/target conjugation
puts N into J3; a further change in its centralizer puts H into the
anti-diagonal matrix R. This follows by taking a finite square root of
the invertible polynomial in J3 representing R^(-1)H.

Write K_(i,j,l)=partial_j partial_l V_i and
T_(i,j,l)=partial_i partial_j partial_l f at that point. The last two
indices of K commute, and T is fully symmetric.
Differentiating tr N, tr N^2/2, tr N^3/3 and the three skew entries of HN
gives 18 homogeneous linear equations in 28 variables. The matrix has
rank 17. An exact five-row combination proves

\[
 K_{3,2,2}-K_{3,3,1}=0.                         \tag{4}
\]

For an image-annihilating one-form omega with omega=dX3 at the point,
differentiating omega N=0 gives
partial_l omega_1=-K_(3,2,l) and
partial_l omega_2=-K_(3,3,l). Thus (4) is the coefficient of
omega wedge d omega. The image distribution is involutive.

The literal certificate in [jet-certificate.json](jet-certificate.json)
is checked independently with stdlib Fraction by [check_jet.py](check_jet.py).
This independent check verifies the row combination, not the geometric
normalization or the entire external theorem.

As a useful negative control, let u=y-x^2 and

\[
 V_0=(u,z+2xu,-u^2).
\]

Its Jacobian has rank two and cube zero, but its image is annihilated by
omega=2u dx+dz and omega wedge d omega=-2 dx wedge dy wedge dz.
Consequently no generically nonsingular Hessian metric, even rational,
can symmetrize DV0. A degree-bounded coefficient search for such a
potential would be unnecessary.

For our [HC4 moving-pivot programme](../../archive/non-elliptic/HC4_MENG_YANG_GRAPH_OBSTRUCTIONS.md),
the usable addition is precise: when a reduction produces a rank-n rational
singular-Hessian auxiliary potential with an invertible transverse slice,
its transported denominator must support every nonzero eigenvalue of Dv.
An inverse over a rational function field does not eliminate those poles.
This test applies only with the theorem's rank and slice hypotheses;
it is not a criterion for every full-rank HC4 potential.
