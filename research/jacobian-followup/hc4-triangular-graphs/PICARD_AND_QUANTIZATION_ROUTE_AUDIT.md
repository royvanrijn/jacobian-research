## 1. The generic Picard calculation does not annihilate puncture classes

Use the actual all-irreducible target frame of
[LR0/1](IRREDUCIBLE_TARGET_FRAMES_AND_PUNCTURE_EXTENSIONS.md).
Put k=C(P), A=C[x,y] tensor_C[P] k, C=Spec A, and let Cbar be its smooth
projective completion over k. Let S be the closed puncture points over k;
their number h counts monodromy orbits, not geometric punctures.

A is a localization of the polynomial UFD, and is a regular
one-dimensional ring. Thus Pic(C)=0. The standard divisor restriction
sequence, directly obtained by extending divisors and restricting
rational functions, is

    0 -> A*/k* -> Div_S(Cbar) -> Pic(Cbar) -> Pic(C) -> 0.

The constant field is k because P is primitive. In this supplied frame
A*/k*=0 by LQ1. Consequently the actual divisor-class group Pic(Cbar)
is freely generated by the closed puncture points. Its degree-zero
subgroup is the kernel of

    Z^h -> Z, (a_i) |-> sum a_i [k(s_i):k],

and has rank h-1. This assertion concerns actual line bundles/divisor
classes; no identification with every k-point of a Picard scheme is
being silently made. No Brauer/descent theorem is imported.

In particular EVERY nonzero degree-zero divisor supported on S has
a nontorsion class: if a positive multiple were principal, its
rational function would be a nonconstant unit of A. Thus Pic(C)=0
does not make puncture differences torsion or zero. It says that
punctures GENERATE the projective Picard group.

There is a useful test of the proposed height-zero shortcut. Let
f=Q on Cbar, d=deg(f), and F_infty its pole divisor. The actual mate
makes df nowhere zero on C. Hence K_0=div(df) is supported on S and

    E=d K_0-(2g-2) F_infty

is a degree-zero puncture divisor. At a pole of f of order e its
coefficient is -d(e+1)-(2g-2)e, which is negative for g>=1.
Thus E is nonzero in the remaining LR frame g>=2 and its divisor
class is nontorsion by the preceding sequence.

This is consistent with the known NONSPLIT puncture extension of LR1.
It does not prove any particular height formula. On a surface
compactification a degree-zero horizontal divisor is orthogonal to
the fiber class; the Hodge-index inequality permits negative square.
It does not force square zero. Boundary classes removed on A2 can
still carry these projective classes. No theorem linking the actual
coframe to height zero was verified in the targeted literature search.

Also, A*/k*=0 is a statement over k. It must NOT be replaced by
geometric unit-freeness after a finite constant-field extension:
norms of arbitrary unit divisors can cancel. The positivity argument
for canonical sections in LQ1 is a separate, specially marked argument.
