# The trace-zero algebra and the first energy defect

Claims HC4MYKS0/1. Written all-degree plane arguments extending
[KR1/2](QUADRATIC_SOURCE_RECONSTRUCTION_AND_QUARTIC_RETIREMENT.md).
Full HC4/JC2 remains open. No new mathematical checker is needed for
these geometric deductions; the retained algebra controls are not replayed.

## 1. The quadratic image is an algebra: HC4MYKS0

Use B=C[P,Q], R=C[x,y], F=Frac(B), L=Frac(R), d=[L:F]>=3, and
N=ker(Tr:R->F). Let Z=Spec(S) be the actual finite normalization, with
source X=Spec(R) and disjoint omitted boundary primes E_i. For their
distinct target images D_j write ell_j=sum_(E_i over D_j)e_i*f_i.
Let J be the set of images with ell_j=1. Such an image has precisely one
omitted prime, necessarily unramified with residue degree one. Denote
these primes by F_1,...,F_c, where c=|J|, and form the partial completion

    V = X union F_1 union ... union F_c inside Z.

Then, as ACTUAL EMBEDDED B-subalgebras of L,

    image(Sym^2_B N -> R) = B[N] = Gamma(V,O_V).        (1)

In particular the quadratic image is already a normal finitely generated
smooth affine B-algebra, and V->Spec(B) is etale. Closure under products,
associativity, normality, finite generation and etaleness are not further
recognition gates once the actual embedded N and field multiplication
are supplied. Polynomial source existence is still required for a proposed
finite cover; none of these statements creates that source from abstract
module data or bare monodromy-group/cycle information.

### Affineness and smoothness of the partial completion

The retained PF2BATT1 geometry makes Z regular except for finitely many
cyclic quotient surface singularities on its boundary. Such a singularity
is locally Q-factorial: pull a Weil divisor to the regular finite quotient
cover, choose a local equation upstairs and take its norm; a positive
multiple of the original divisor is Cartier. The same argument in completed
local rings descends by faithful flatness, since an invertible completed
divisorial ideal is an invertible module over the local ring. There are
only finitely many singular points, so a common positive multiple of any
specified sum of boundary primes is an effective Cartier divisor on Z.

Apply this to the sum of all boundary primes other than the F_a. Its
complement is V. The complement of an effective Cartier divisor is affine
over its ambient scheme: locally on the latter it is the principal open
D(f). Affineness of a morphism is local on the target. Since Z is affine,
V is affine. This argument does not require the chosen divisor to be
globally principal, or the surface to be globally factorial.

Every added F_a is unramified along its ENTIRE length by PF2BATT1:
boundary disjointness and purity exclude a hidden branch point on it.
Thus V is smooth and its map to the target is etale. In particular its
ring C=Gamma(V,O_V) is normal and of finite type and is a W=D_B-submodule
of R. The already cached Borisov--Gabber--Vasiu Corollary11.8(3), printed
pp44-45, separately corroborates affineness and regularity of the maximal
etale completion. It is not a new theorem import needed by this proof.

### Identification of the actual embedded algebra

Normality gives the valuative description

    C = {r in R : v_(F_a)(r)>=0 for every a}.          (2)

Trace zero forces regularity in the sole missing unramified coordinate
over each D_j in J: all retained coordinates are regular and there is
no other omitted coordinate to cancel its polar part. Hence N is contained
in C. The generic rank of C is d; the semisimple full-support IC socle I
of R must therefore lie in C, by the same generic-rank argument used in
KR1. The quotient C/I is a submodule of the semisimple boundary module T.

At the generic point of an image in J, filling its one unramified missing
factor replaces its Laurent field by the regular DVR, so C/I has zero
part there. Over every other image the partial completion makes no change,
so C/I has all of T_j. Semisimplicity, with no point-supported summand in
T, makes this a GLOBAL equality. KR1 identifies precisely the same
embedded quotient for A=image(Sym^2_B N->R). Thus C=A.

Now B and N lie in the ring C, while C=A is spanned by products from N.
It follows that B[N]=C=A, proving (1). In particular S is contained in
B[N], although I must still not be identified with the finite algebra S.

### Maximality, higher products and the exact remaining obstruction

The trace kernel of C is still N. The operation which replaces a source
ring by the algebra generated by its trace-zero elements is therefore
IDEMPOTENT here: applying it to C gives C again. Products of three,four
or arbitrarily many elements of N cannot recover any of the omitted F_a.

More precisely, V is the LARGEST partial completion of X inside Z that
preserves the embedded trace kernel N. Filling any other omitted prime
removes some elements of N: in a ramified factor N contains fractional
poles, and in an unramified factor sharing an image with another omitted
factor it contains a pole compensated on that other factor. These are the
flat local constructions of KR1; faithful flatness ensures a genuine
element of the localized original N has such a pole. Filling all or part
of that boundary prime cannot preserve N. No independent isolated
codimension-two boundary is available, by KMFI94.

The localization sequence, X=A2 factoriality and R*=C* give

    Pic(V)=Cl(V)=direct_sum_(a=1)^c Z[F_a],
    V*=C*,
    C is factorial iff c=0 iff C=R.                   (3)

Indeed all divisor classes are boundary-supported; a relation among the
F_a would be the divisor of a function invertible on X and hence constant.
Smoothness identifies Pic and Cl. Thus polynomiality and factoriality
coincide for this ACTUAL completion: if c=0 it is the given plane, and
if c>0 its Picard group obstructs it from being a plane. This is not a
general characterization of all factorial etale surfaces, nor proof that
an abstract reconstructed algebra has a polynomial-source open at all.

Under c>0 the open immersion X->V cannot be a principal localization
C->C[f^-1] with a nonconstant f: such an f would be a nonconstant unit
of R. Nontrivial invertible boundary ideals are essential. Higher products,
normalization or a scalar denominator inversion do not remove this defect.

## 2. The first energy trace sees the entire quadratic defect: HC4MYKS1

For EVERY r in R and EVERY sole missing unramified prime F_a over D_j,

    v_(D_j)(Tr(r))<0 iff v_(F_a)(r)<0,

and in the polar case these valuations are EQUAL. In a faithfully flat
completed DVR splitting, there is one omitted degree-one field factor.
Its trace is the identity. All other factors are retained unramified
DVR factors and contribute regular traces. They cannot cancel the polar
part of the omitted factor. This proves both assertions and gives

    B[N]={r in R : Tr(r) is regular along every D_j in J}. (4)

Consequently, for ANY polynomial source coordinate system x,y, the union
of the polar supports of Tr(x),Tr(y), RESTRICTED TO J, is exactly J.
At every omitted prime at least one source coordinate has a pole;
otherwise the inverse rational coordinate map would extend over that
prime and, by separatedness, place it back in X. A different polynomial
source coordinate system cannot make both first traces regular there.

Now take ANY cotangent Keller potential

    h(z,w)=D(z)+w dot K(z),  z=(x,y),
    eta=K(z), xi=grad(D)+DK^t*w,
    G=(z,w) dot grad(h)-h = xi dot z-D(z).

The xi are independent gradient-target variables, as in KO3. The energy
has a pole on EVERY omitted prime. Over a loss-one image the preceding
trace calculation remains valid after adjoining xi, so its FIRST trace
Tr(G) already has a pole there, with the same negative valuation. Hence

    Tr(G) polynomial in the gradient target
      implies J is empty
      implies R=B[N]=image(Sym^2_B N->R).              (5)

This works in every generic degree d>=3, for every D and source frame.
For arbitrary HC4 potentials its use is through the retained Tang
classification. It does NOT prove the first trace regular, nor make its
regularity an inverse criterion. Ramified missing factors can cancel a
first trace; KO3's higher marked moments remain necessary unless some
new global argument controls those cancellations. An unramified omitted
prime sharing an image with other missing sheets is not in J and is not
covered by the no-cancellation assertion (4).

## 3. A tested route which does not exclude the completion

Since V->A2 is etale, its pulled-back volume is nowhere zero and EXACT:
d(P*dQ)=dP wedge dQ. This alone does not contradict a nontrivial Picard
group or a proper A2 open. The retained UADANVOL1 already proves that
for EVERY m>=2 the smooth Danielewski surfaces u^m*v=P(z), with P
square-free, have an explicit global regular primitive of their canonical
volume form. For P=z^2-1 they contain the actual A2 chart

    (u,t) -> (u, 2*t+u^m*t^2, 1+u^m*t),

whose complement is the other affine line u=0,z=-1. Its inverse is
t=(z-1)/u^m on u!=0 and t=v/(z+1) on z+1!=0; these agree and cover
that complement. This is the same
kind of geometric completion, with nontrivial Picard group and scalar
units. The exponent-one surface is excluded by its nonzero volume period;
the higher-exponent surfaces pass exactness. Credit for that distinction
and the uniform primitive belongs to [UADANVOL1](../universal-keller-log-picard/PROOF.md), not this note.

No etale map from these higher-exponent surfaces to A2 is supplied here.
A regular primitive is not automatically P*dQ for two global functions
with independent exact differentials. Thus neither this family nor the
actual completion theorem proves or disproves HC4. The next useful input
must constrain that global coordinate pair, the embedded field
multiplication, or the marked energy moments; abstract algebra closure,
further products of N and volume exactness have already been accounted for.
