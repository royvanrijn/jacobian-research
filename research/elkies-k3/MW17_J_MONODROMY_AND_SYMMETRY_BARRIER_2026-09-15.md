# Full S24 monodromy rules out symmetry-based duplication on the four parents

For each of the four retained arithmetic MW17 parents, the degree24
j-map has geometric monodromy **S24**. A different original parameter
with the same generic j-value requires degree23 over the original
parameter field. Therefore no quadratic base change can acquire an
extra parameter symmetry preserving the elliptic fibration's j-map.

A zero-preserving fibration symmetry, or one translated by an inherited
section, cannot turn one anti-invariant direction into two independent
ones. This closes a structural construction route on these four parents;
it is not a bound on arbitrary quadratic-twist rank. The
[positive two-gain objective](CORRELATED_QUADRATIC_GAINS_2026-09-12.md)
remains open.

## 1. Exact critical-value certificate

Write the usual Weierstrass invariants as c4(t), c6(t), and put

```
D=c4^3-c6^2,     j=1728*c4^3/D,
H=3*c4'*c6-2*c4*c6'.
```

The [checker](scripts/verify_mw17_j_monodromy.py) reads only the retained
generic equations, using the full long-Weierstrass invariant formulas
for the recovered Curve302 model. It verifies, at the following good
primes, all the degree, squarefreeness, coprimality and critical-image
conditions stated below:

| Parent | Witness prime |
|---|---:|
| Direct11952 Q80 | 131 |
| Published R17 | 131 |
| Recovered Curve302 MW17 | 149 |
| X1092 class1 | 151 |

At each witness:

- c4, c6, D and H have degrees8,12,24,18, are squarefree, and the
  required pairwise common-root exclusions hold;
- infinity is smooth and unramified for j;
- all18 roots of H have distinct j-values, disjoint from0 and1728.

To check the last condition exactly, reduce j in Fp[t]/(H) and form its
18-by18 multiplication matrix. Its characteristic polynomial has degree18
and is squarefree, with neither0 nor1728 a root. A second computation
uses modular matrix-power traces and Newton identities and produces the
same characteristic polynomial. The polynomial also annihilates j modulo H.
Thus no approximation or numerical comparison of critical values is used.

The rational coefficients are integral at the chosen prime, all necessary
leading coefficients remain nonzero, and the displayed nonzero gcd and
discriminant conditions therefore certify the characteristic-zero
conditions. A bad reduction is skipped, not interpreted as an exclusion.

The derivative identity

```
(1728*c4^3)'*D - (1728*c4^3)*D' = -1728*c4^2*c6*H
```

shows that the full branching is

```
j=0:       eight ramification points of index3,
j=1728:    twelve ramification points of index2,
other:     eighteen distinct branch values, each with one index2 point.
```

The ramification contributions sum to16+12+18=46=2*24-2.
The degree18 of H and the nonzero leading coefficients ensure that no
extra ramification is hidden at infinity.

## 2. Why this branching forces S24

Work over C. The connected j-cover has transitive monodromy G<=S24.
Loops around0 and1728 give permutations a,b of orders3 and2. Each of
the other18 branch loops gives a transposition. These loops generate G
and their product is identity. This is the standard monodromy description
of a connected branched cover; see
[Milne, Lectures on Etale Cohomology, section21](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/LECc.pdf).

Let N be the normal subgroup generated by all18 transpositions. Modulo N,
the branch-loop product says ab=1. The image of a=b^(-1) has order dividing
both3 and2, hence is identity. Therefore G=N.

A permutation group generated by transpositions is a product of symmetric
groups on the connected components of its transposition graph. Since G
is transitive, that graph is connected and G=S24. This argument does not
assume primitivity and does not infer monodromy from one Frobenius cycle.

The same finite certificate also proves there is no rational-function
factorization of j into two maps of degrees greater than1: the point
stabilizer S23 is maximal in S24.

## 3. A distinct parameter costs degree23

Let k be Q, a number-field extension, or an algebraic closure of Q. Set
K=k(t), F=k(j(t)). Geometric S24 monodromy means the normal closure of
K/F acts on its24 conjugate parameter values as the full symmetric group.
The stabilizer of t is S23, transitive on the23 other values.
Consequently any distinct conjugate u with j(u)=j(t) has

```
[K(u):K]=23.
```

Equivalently, after removing the diagonal u=t from the equation
j(u)=j(t), the remaining degree23 equation is irreducible over k(t).
This is a function-field assertion; it does not exclude coincident
j-values at particular rational specializations. The correspondence of
subfields and stabilizers is the ordinary
[fundamental theorem of Galois theory](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09DW).

For any finite extension L/K of degree less than23, an F-automorphism of
L must therefore fix t: its image of t cannot be a distinct conjugate.
Thus

```
Aut_F(L)=Aut_K(L)       whenever [L:K]<23.
```

In particular, for a nonconstant quadratic cover C -> P1_t with deck
involution sigma, the only base automorphisms of C preserving j(t) are
identity and sigma. This includes automorphisms that were not initially
assumed to descend to the original t-line, and imposes no genus or
branch-reduction restriction on C.

## 4. Consequence for duplicating a new section

A zero-preserving automorphism of the pullback elliptic fibration induces
an automorphism of C preserving its j-map. By section3 its action on the
base is identity or sigma. The elliptic curve has nonconstant j, so its
geometric origin-preserving fibre automorphisms are only+1 and-1.
After composing with the canonical deck action where necessary, these
are all possibilities.

For an anti-invariant point P, sigma(P)=-P. Every such symmetry therefore
sends P to+P or-P. Composing with translation by an inherited section T
sends it to+/-P+T, giving the same one-dimensional direction modulo M.
Translation by an already independent new section is not a method for
constructing that section. No statement here excludes unrelated surface
automorphisms that change the elliptic fibration itself.

There is a related isogeny barrier. For a24-I1 parent the geometric
mod-ell representation is SL2(F_ell) for every prime ell, by the
simple-j-pole and transvection argument already proved in the
[all-prime division theorem](../elliptic-curves/notes/DET1092_ALL_PRIME_DIVISION_AND_MULTISECTION_THEOREM_2026-09-09.md),
sections2--3. That argument depends on the24-I1 configuration, so applies
to these four parents with the same verified hypotheses.

A line in E[ell] has orbit size ell+1 under SL2(F_ell). It cannot become
Galois-stable over a quadratic extension, since ell+1 cannot divide2.
Hence no prime-degree isogeny exists over that extension. A least-degree
isogeny to a nonisomorphic curve in characteristic zero has cyclic kernel:
a noncyclic kernel contains some full E[m], allowing division through[m]
and a smaller isogeny. A cyclic kernel would contain a stable subgroup
of prime order, already excluded. Thus generic isogeny transport also
cannot connect to a nonisomorphic elliptic curve over these quadratic
function fields. Multiplication maps do not supply independent directions.

## 5. What a different symmetry construction would require

A symmetric parent would need to fail the S24 critical-value condition,
for example through actual coincidences among the18 additional critical
values. Failure of the certificate is only a necessary opportunity, not
proof of a symmetry or of correlated gain.

There is a standard character-based sufficient setup on a different
parent. Suppose a rational elliptic surface E0/k(u) has

```
rank E0(k(u))=8,     rank E0^(u)(k(u))>=9,
```

and its quadratic base change u=t² is an actual K3 with its arithmetic
rank-at-least17 certified. If a linear delta(u) additionally satisfies

```
rank E0^(delta)(k(u))>=1,
rank E0^(u*delta)(k(u))>=1,
```

then over this parent's single quadratic extension w²=delta(t²), the
last two characters give independent new directions. The conic
w²=delta(t²) must have a rational point to meet the infinite-base
requirement. All nonzero branch and smoothness hypotheses must hold.
This is the usual biquadratic character decomposition over the lower
u-line; it becomes degree two over an independently established MW17
t-line parent. Merely relabelling the existing degree-four construction
over an MW17 parent would not satisfy these hypotheses.

No retained equation in this work supplies the crucial8+9 symmetric
parent, and none of the four existing parents can play that role under
a rational change of its original parameter. Any such construction would
require a new actual arithmetic parent and must respect the existing
rational-marking gate. No parent search is initiated by this note.
