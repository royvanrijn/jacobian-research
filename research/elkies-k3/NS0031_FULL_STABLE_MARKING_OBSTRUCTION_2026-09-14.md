# NS0031: the full stable group and a corrected rational-marking obstruction

No K3 surface over \(\mathbf Q\) with geometric Picard rank 19 has its full
Néron–Severi lattice equal to NS0031 and rationally marked. The obstruction
uses the **full** stable projective period
group, including the determinant-minus-one reflection that invalidated the
September 4 argument. Its canonical modular curve has genus 10 and no
rational noncuspidal non-CM point.

The [earlier note](NS0031_QQ_MARKING_OBSTRUCTION_2026-09-04.md) retains the
counter-witness and the valid norm-one calculation. An empty norm-one cover
does not prove this theorem. The new ingredient is a rational genus-2
quotient whose rational points can be determined completely.

## 1. The full projective group

Use the literal lattice, without similarity rescaling,

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&4\\0&74&1\\4&1&-2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A_T\simeq\mathbf Z/1184\mathbf Z.
\]

Write its basis as \(e_0,e_1,e_2\). The unique even overlattice of index four
has basis

\[
e=e_0/4,\qquad h=e_1-e_0/4,\qquad f=e_2+e_0/4.
\]

It is \(T_0=U\oplus\langle74\rangle\), with quadratic form
\(ef+37h^2\), and

\[
T=\{(e,h,f)\in T_0:e-h+f\equiv0\pmod4\}.
\]

Uniqueness follows from the cyclic discriminant group: it has just one
subgroup of order four, and that subgroup is isotropic. Consequently every
isometry of \(T\) extends to \(T_0\).

The even Clifford order of \(ef+37h^2\) is the Eichler order
\(R_0(37)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf Z&\mathbf Z\\37\mathbf Z&\mathbf Z\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
For example, the products of the isotropic generators give the diagonal
idempotents and the two off-diagonal generators of this order. An isometry
induces an automorphism of the order, hence an inner automorphism of
\(M_2(\mathbf Q)\) normalizing it. At primes other than 37 a normalizer
preserves its maximal order; at 37 it preserves or interchanges the two
maximal orders whose intersection is \(R_0(37)\). After a rational scalar
is removed, its determinant squareclass is therefore 1 or 37. This proves
the exhaustive positive projective normalizer description

\[
\Gamma_0(37)\ \sqcup\ W\Gamma_0(37),\qquad
W=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\37&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here the determinant-one part really is \(\Gamma_0(37)\): a local
normalizer preserving both maximal orders is an Eichler unit up to scalar;
the integral units of positive determinant have determinant one. The
displayed groups also preserve \(T_0\), proving the converse.

At level four put

\[
C=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a+b&b\\b&a\end{pmatrix}
 :a,b\in\mathbf Z/4,\ a^2+ab-b^2\text{ odd}\right\}.
\]

This is the nonsplit Cartan, of order 12; its determinant-one subgroup has
order 6. The stabilizer of \(T\) inside \(\Gamma_0(37)\) reduces to the
Cartan normalizer. Exhausting all 48 elements of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z/4)\)
gives 12 stabilizing residues. On the cyclic discriminant group they act by

| Residue | Number | Discriminant multiplier | Signs at 2 and 37 |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Cartan, determinant one | 6 | 1 | \((+,+)\) |
| Other normalizer coset, determinant one | 6 | 223 | \((-,+)\) |

The principal kernel is checked on an exact finite generating set: conjugate
\(\Gamma_0(592)\) by \(\operatorname{diag}(4,1)\). This is projectively
\(\Gamma(4)\cap\Gamma_0(37)\), and every generator has trivial
discriminant action. Thus the residue calculation proves the subgroup
statement for all elements, not just for the chosen lifts.

In the retained trace-zero Clifford embedding the adjoint action of \(W\)
has discriminant multiplier \(-1\). Its negative is exactly the stable
reflection

\[
-\operatorname{Ad}(W)=
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\4&1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It fixes a positive plane and has determinant \(-1\). Passing from an
orthogonal isometry to its projective action allows this common minus sign;
it does not allow the different signs \((-,+)\) or \((+,-)\) on the two
primary discriminant groups. The result is

\[
\boxed{\ P O^+(T)^*=\Gamma\sqcup W\Gamma,\qquad
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\rm ns}(4)\cap\Gamma_0(37).\ }
\]

For use in the replay, a generator of the projective Cartan is
\(r=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}10&-3\\37&-11\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
with \(r^3=1\). There are no further normalizer cosets by the overlattice
argument above.

## 2. Its canonical model over Q

A complex group alone does not specify the arithmetic twist. At 2, enumerate
all 96 elements of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf Z/4)\), allowing every odd
determinant. Exactly 24 stabilize \(T\); the 12 Cartan elements act as +1
on \(A_{T,2}\), and the other 12 act as -1. This calculation extends to
the full local groups: the principal level-four kernel is topologically
generated by the upper and lower elementary matrices of parameters 4 and
148 and the two diagonal matrices with diagonal entries 5 and 1. All four
act trivially on \(A_{T,2}\). Gaussian elimination and
\(\overline{\langle5\rangle}=1+4\mathbf Z_2\) prove the generation claim.

At 37 the Eichler units act as +1 on \(A_{T,37}\), and the Fricke coset
acts as -1. Elsewhere the level is maximal. The projective image of the
stable orthogonal level consists of the two **simultaneous** sign choices
\((+,+)\) and \((-,-)\). This includes determinant units, and fixes its
canonical model, rather than choosing a twist from the complex equation.

Define \(Y_n=X_{\rm ns}(n)\times_{X(1)}X_0(37)\), using the full
Cartan in GL, and let \(w_{{\rm ns},n}\) be the nontrivial Cartan-normalizer
involution. In \(w_{37}\), transport the Cartan structure by the
37-isogeny. The required curve over \(\mathbf Q\) is

\[
\boxed{\ C_4=Y_4/\langle w_{37}w_{{\rm ns},4}\rangle.\ }
\]

The product matters: the pure 37-Fricke action has local discriminant signs
\((+,-)\). Analytically its representative can be taken as \(NW\), where
\(N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}28&3\\37&4\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
has the non-Cartan normalizer residue. The product has representative \(W\).

To connect this arithmetic curve to K3 markings, use the period map with
an auxiliary full level at least three. [Rizov, §3.9 and Theorem 3.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0508018)
proves that this map is defined over \(\mathbf Q\) to the canonical
orthogonal Shimura model. Restricting the fixed primitive NS classes gives
the rank-three subdatum. Its complex stabilizer is the discriminant kernel
([Dolgachev, Proposition 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9502005v2)).
Keep the orientation of the determinant line until the last step. With
orientation, the integral stabilizer lies in SO and acts trivially on both
primary discriminant groups. The computed local level is exactly the full
GL Cartan level at 2 and Eichler-unit level at 37, so its canonical split
adjoint curve is \(Y_4\). Removing the auxiliary congruence level is defined
over Q by functoriality of the canonical models.

The remaining orientation cover is a single quadratic torsor. Its Galois
character is \(\det(T_\ell(1))\), independent of \(\ell\): at good
Frobenius elements the determinants agree by compatibility of K3 cohomology,
and continuity and density give agreement on Galois. Thus its two signs are
global; one must not allow unrelated signs at 2 and 37. The orientation
deck transformation is represented by the integral isometry R fixing NS.
In the odd-dimensional adjoint realization
\(g\mapsto\det(g)g\), it is \(-R=\operatorname{Ad}(W)\). It has
discriminant signs \((-,-)\), hence acts on \(Y_4\) as the Q-defined
normalizer operation \(w_{37}w_{{\rm ns},4}\). Forgetting orientation
therefore descends the period map to precisely \(C_4\).

Thus a full rational NS0031 marking gives a rational point of this specified
Q-curve even when its orientation is defined only over a quadratic field;
no rational lift to \(Y_4\) is assumed. Multiple primitive-embedding
components give the same implication after choosing a complex marking of
the stated literal complement.

An exact modular-symbol calculation gives \(g(C_4)=10\). More explicitly,
conjugation by \(\operatorname{diag}(1/4,1)\) realizes the geometric curve
as an \(S_3\) quotient of \(X_0(592)\), generated projectively by

\[
\begin{pmatrix}40&-3\\592&-44\end{pmatrix},\qquad W_{592}.
\]

The cuspidal homology dimensions are 142 before quotient, 46 after the
order-three quotient, and 20 after both generators. Thus the norm-one
genus is 23 and the full genus is 10. At level four this geometric
description is not used on its own to descend the curve to Q.

## 3. A genus-two quotient over Q

Reduction of Cartan structures modulo two gives a degree-four morphism

\[
C_4\longrightarrow C_2=Y_2/\langle w_{37}w_{{\rm ns},2}\rangle
\quad\text{over }\mathbf Q.
\]

At level two, conjugation by \(\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1)\) identifies
the full-level cover with \(X_0(148)\) over Q. Its quotient generators are
\(R_2=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}20&-3\\148&-22\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
and \(W_{148}\). These automorphisms are Q-rational: translation by
\(1/2\) acts on q-expansions by \(q\mapsto-q\), and
\(R_2\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1/2\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}20&7\\148&52\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
is a 4-Atkin–Lehner representative. They generate \(S_3\); the invariant
cuspidal homology has dimension four, so the quotient has genus two.

Let \(f=f_{37a}(q^2)\), \(g=f_{148a}(q)\), with the normalized newforms
of \(y^2+y=x^3-x\) and \(y^2=x^3-x^2-5x+1\). The replay verifies that
these two differentials descend: for the oldform it uses the level-lowering
map of parameter two and the level-37 Hecke projector; for the newform it
checks the invariant two-dimensional homology eigenspace. They span the
quotient differentials. Put

\[
u=g/f,\qquad v=\frac{q\,du/dq}{f}.
\]

The weight-12 identity
\([q(g'f-gf')]^2=g^6-4g^4f^2+4f^6\) is checked beyond the Sturm bound
228, using precision 300. The ratio of the two differentials is the
degree-two canonical map, so the identity proves the Q-model

\[
\boxed{\ C_2:v^2=u^6-4u^4+4.\ }
\]

## 4. Complete rational-point calculation

The change \(s=1/u,\ Y=v/u^3\) gives
\(Y^2=4s^6-4s^2+1\). Hence \((s^2,(Y-1)/2)\) lies on
\(E:y^2+y=x^3-x\), with \(E(\mathbf Q)=\mathbf Z P\), \(P=(0,0)\).
This full Mordell–Weil statement is proved in the replay, including the
generator and trivial torsion.

Let \(K=\mathbf Q(a)\), \(a^3-4a+2=0\), and \(\theta=a/2\).
Then \(4\theta^3-4\theta+1=0\), and

\[
4s^6-4s^2+1=4(s^2-\theta)(s^4+\theta s^2+\theta^2-1).
\]

The usual 2-descent Kummer map is \(x-\theta\) modulo squares in K.
Since \(E(\mathbf Q)/2E(\mathbf Q)=\{O,P\}\), a rational point must lift
to one of the two genus-one curves

\[
Z_\delta:\quad z^2=(s^4+\theta s^2+\theta^2-1)/\delta,
\qquad \delta\in\{1,-\theta\}.
\]

Indeed \(s^2-\theta=\delta t^2\) makes the displayed quotient a square.
The exceptional value \(s=\infty\) is included projectively. The two
curves have K-points \((1,\theta/(2\theta-1))\) and
\((0,1/(2\theta))\), respectively, so elliptic Chabauty applies to their
maps \(s:Z_\delta\to\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf Q}\).

The [Magma input](scripts/certify_ns0031_genus2_rational_points.m) proves,
unconditionally, the full group \(\mathbf Z/2\oplus\mathbf Z\) for
each elliptic curve over K, then uses elliptic Chabauty at 5 with auxiliary
primes \(3,7,11,13,17,19\). The retained output is:

| \(\delta\) | Rank bounds | Full MW proved | Exact upper bound | Rational s-images |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 1, 1 | true | 6 | \(-1,1,\infty\), each twice |
| \(-\theta\) | 1, 1 | true | 6 | \(0,-1/2,1/2\), each twice |

Both residual-coset lists are empty. The returned index conditions are
11 and 1; the proved full MW groups have index one, discharging both.
These are the exact upper bounds of [Magma's elliptic Chabauty procedure](https://magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/magma/handbook/text/1568),
with both proof flags from [MordellWeilGroup](https://magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/magma/handbook/text/1570).
The run used Magma 2.29-10, with its unconditional class-group defaults;
the input rejects versions before 2.29. No analytic-rank hypothesis or
height bound enters the conclusion.

Transforming back, all twelve rational points are

\[
C_2(\mathbf Q)=\{(0,\pm2),(\pm1,\pm1),(\pm2,\pm2),\infty_+,\infty_-\}.
\]

## 5. The images are cuspidal or CM

Use Elkies's normalization of \(X_0(37)^+=E\), with cusp O and
\(P=(0,0)\). The degree-two modular forgetful map is

\[
X=\frac{u^4-2u^2-uv}{2},\qquad
Y_E=\frac{u^6-3u^4-u^3v+uv}{2}.
\]

It satisfies \(Y_E^2+Y_E=X^3-X\) and
\(dX/(2Y_E+1)=-2\,du/v=-2f_{37a}(q^2)dq/q\).
Moreover \(\infty_-\) maps to O, with
\(X=q^{-4}+2q^{-2}+5+9q^2+\cdots\). These fix the translation and sign:
Elkies's differential is \(-dX/(2Y_E+1)\), not its negative. Equality of
the pulled-back differentials makes two such maps differ by a constant,
and their common cusp image makes that constant zero.

Substitution gives the following complete image table. The CM discriminants
are those in [Elkies, Appendix, level 37, equations (98)–(104)](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~elkies/xisog.pdf).

| Points on \(C_2\) | Image on E | Type |
|---|---|---|
| \(\infty_-\), where \(v/u^3=-1\) | O | cusp |
| \(\infty_+\), where \(v/u^3=1\) | \(2P=(1,0)\) | CM, -4 |
| \((0,\pm2)\) | \(P=(0,0)\) | CM, -11 |
| \((1,1),(-1,-1)\) | \(3P=(-1,-1)\) | CM, -3 |
| \((1,-1),(-1,1)\) | \(-P=(0,-1)\) | CM, -7 |
| \((2,2),(-2,-2)\) | \(-4P=(2,2)\) | CM, -27 |
| \((2,-2),(-2,2)\) | \(6P=(6,14)\) | CM, -67 |

The non-CM rational 37-isogeny point \(-6P=(6,-15)\) does not occur.
Changing the sign normalization would incorrectly interchange it with
\(6P\); this is why the differential and cusp checks are part of the proof.

Finite-level maps preserve the underlying period and carry precisely the
same CM/non-CM distinction. A noncuspidal rank-19 K3 marking has a non-CM
period; a CM period in this one-dimensional orthogonal datum has Picard
rank 20. Every rational point on \(C_4\) maps to the above list. Therefore

\[
C_4(\mathbf Q)_{\rm noncuspidal,nonCM}=\varnothing,
\]

which proves the claimed rational rank-19 marking obstruction. This does
not classify the rational CM lifts to \(C_4\), exclude singular K3
specializations containing NS0031, or exclude markings over larger fields.
The certified formal \(\mathbf Z_7\) branch and geometric F017 corridor
remain valid within their original scopes.
