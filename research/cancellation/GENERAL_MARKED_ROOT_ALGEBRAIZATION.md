# Programme 2: general marked-root algebraization

This note starts the extension of the
[boundary-cancelled incidence lemma](CONTROLLED_BOUNDARY_SUSPENSIONS.md)
from one monogenic marked root to finite Gorenstein algebras, complete
intersections, several marks, towers, and resolvents.  The first outcome is
an intrinsic determinant-line criterion.  It is a theorem once its listed
gates are checked, but it is not an existence theorem for those gates.

The main change of language is

\[
 \boxed{\partial_\tau E
 \quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
 \text{the trace section of }\omega_{B/A}
 \text{, equivalently the different divisor}.}
 \tag{0.1}
\]

For a complete intersection this section is again explicit: it is the
determinant of the relative equation Jacobian.  Multiple-root and resolvent
constructions change the finite algebra, not the algebraization criterion.

Work over a characteristic-zero field \(k\).

## 1. Scope and unavoidable gates

Let

\[
 A=k[y_1,\ldots,y_d],\qquad Y=\operatorname{Spec}A.
\]

The proposed input is a finite \(A\)-algebra \(B\), a rational affine chart
on \(\operatorname{Spec}B\), and a divisor ledger.  Three restrictions are
forced if the output is to be one polynomial Keller map
\(\mathbb A^d\to\mathbb A^d\).

1. **Generic separability.**  The selected generic algebra must be etale.
   For a generically nonreduced algebra the trace discriminant vanishes and
   the different is not a generic unit.  Such an algebra cannot be the
   function-field cover of a nonzero-Jacobian map in characteristic zero.
2. **One field component.**  The function algebra of the irreducible source
   is a field.  A generic product algebra can describe several charts or a
   disconnected source, but not one affine space without selecting a
   component.
3. **Rationality and polynomiality.**  The selected field must be
   \(k\)-rational, and the pulled-back target coordinates must be
   polynomials.  A correct different ledger only proves a rational
   zero--pole cancellation; it does not remove denominators from the
   coordinate functions.

Thus "arbitrary finite flat Gorenstein algebra" is meaningful as the
ambient algebraic formalism.  It is not true that every such algebra is the
normalization cover of one Keller map.  The realizable input is a selected
normal, generically etale, rational component satisfying the gates below.

There is a second immediate exclusion.  If a complete fibre of a Keller map
is identified with

\[
 \operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_A\kappa(y)),
\]

then that fibre algebra is reduced: a Keller map is etale.  Nonreduced
Gorenstein fibre points may occur only on the omitted boundary of the finite
cover, not in the complete affine fibre.

## 2. The Gorenstein replacement for a marked derivative

Let \(A\to B\) be finite flat and Gorenstein.  Its relative dualizing module

\[
 \omega_{B/A}=\operatorname{Hom}_A(B,A)
 \tag{2.1}
\]

is an invertible \(B\)-module.  The trace defines a canonical section

\[
 \tau_{B/A}\in\omega_{B/A}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

After choosing a local generator \(\lambda\) of \(\omega_{B/A}\), write

\[
 \tau_{B/A}=\delta\lambda.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Then \((\delta)\) is the different ideal on that trivializing open.  Replacing
\(\lambda\) by \(h\lambda\) replaces \(\delta\) by \(h^{-1}\delta\).
Consequently neither factor is intrinsic, but their product in the chart
ledger is.

When the map is generically etale, the different is an effective Cartier
divisor and

\[
 \omega_{B/A}\simeq\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}B}(R_{B/A}).
 \tag{2.4}
\]

This is the determinant-line version of the controlled divisor
\(\Delta^e\) in the one-root lemma.  It permits several irreducible
ramification components and does not require a global equation for their
sum.

### Complete-intersection specialization

Suppose

\[
 B=
 A[t_1,\ldots,t_c]/(E_1,\ldots,E_c)
 \tag{2.5}
\]

is finite flat and \(E_1,\ldots,E_c\) are a regular sequence.  Put

\[
 \Delta_{\rm CI}
 =
 \det\left(\frac{\partial E_i}{\partial t_j}\right)_{1\le i,j\le c}
 \in B.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

The Kähler different
\(\operatorname{Fitt}_0^B\Omega_{B/A}\), the Noether different, and the
dualizing different agree and are generated by \(\Delta_{\rm CI}\).
For \(c=1\), equation (2.6) is exactly \(E'(\tau)\).

This is the first practical generalization: an arbitrary
relative-dimension-zero finite complete intersection has one computable
determinant in place of the marked-root derivative, even when \(B\) is not
monogenic.

## 3. A general affine algebraization datum

For the first theorem assume that \(B\) is also a normal domain.  Put

\[
 Z=\operatorname{Spec}B,\qquad K=\operatorname{Frac}B,
\]

and let \(\pi:Z\to Y\) be the finite map.  Passing from a nonnormal
Gorenstein algebra to its normalization does not automatically preserve
flatness or the Gorenstein property.  Those conditions must be rechecked
before using the following definition.

A **general marked-algebra affine datum** consists of:

1. an isomorphism of \(k\)-fields
   \[
    \theta:K\xrightarrow{\sim}k(x_1,\ldots,x_d);
    \tag{3.1}
   \]
2. the affine source ring \(R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_d]\) and source volume form
   \[
    \xi=dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_d;
    \tag{3.2}
   \]
3. a rational generator \(\nu\) of the canonical line of \(Z\);
4. the different coefficient \(\delta\in K^*\), defined by
   \[
    \pi^*(dy_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_d)=\delta\nu;
    \tag{3.3}
   \]
5. the chart coefficient \(a_\theta\in k(x_1,\ldots,x_d)^*\), defined by
   \[
    \theta(\nu)=a_\theta\xi;
    \tag{3.4}
   \]
6. a polynomiality certificate
   \[
    f_i:=\theta(y_i)\in R\quad(1\le i\le d);
    \tag{3.5}
   \]
7. the height-one determinant ledger
   \[
    \operatorname{div}_R\theta(\delta)
    +\operatorname{div}_R(a_\theta)=0;
    \tag{3.6}
   \]
8. for any declared complete fibre, a reconstruction certificate saying
   that the whole finite fibre of \(\pi\) lies in the affine chart obtained
   below.

The definition does not require the canonical or different lines to be
globally trivial on \(Z\).  If \(\nu\) is replaced by \(h\nu\), then
\(\delta\) is replaced by \(\delta/h\) and \(a_\theta\) by
\(\theta(h)a_\theta\), so (3.6) is unchanged.

If rational target coordinates are used as well, their canonical chart
coefficient is subtracted from the left side of (3.6).  This recovers the
three-term source--core--target ledger of the existing lemma.

## 4. Determinant-line algebraization criterion

> **Proposition 4.1 (general marked-algebra criterion).**
> A general marked-algebra affine datum defines a polynomial map
> \[
>  F=(f_1,\ldots,f_d):\mathbb A^d_k\longrightarrow\mathbb A^d_k
> \]
> with
> \[
>  \det DF\in k^*.
> \tag{4.1}
> \]
> The induced morphism \(j:\mathbb A^d\to Z\) is an open immersion and
> \(F=\pi\circ j\).  Hence
> \[
>  F^{-1}(y)=j(\mathbb A^d)\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(y).
> \tag{4.2}
> \]
> A declared finite fibre of \(B/A\) is the complete Keller fibre exactly
> when all its scheme points lie in \(j(\mathbb A^d)\).

### Proof

Equations (3.3) and (3.4) give, in the common function field,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 df_1\wedge\cdots\wedge df_d
 &=\theta(\delta)\theta(\nu)\\
 &=\theta(\delta)a_\theta
   \,dx_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dx_d.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \det DF=\theta(\delta)a_\theta.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Polynomiality (3.5) puts the left side in \(R\).  Ledger (3.6) says that
the right side has zero valuation at every height-one prime of the UFD
\(R\), hence it lies in \(R^*=k^*\).  This proves (4.1).

Every element of \(\theta(B)\) is integral over \(\theta(A)\subset R\), and
therefore integral over \(R\).  Since it lies in \(\operatorname{Frac}R\)
and \(R\) is normal, it belongs to \(R\).  Thus \(\theta\) induces
\(j:\mathbb A^d\to Z\) and \(F=\pi\circ j\).

The Keller condition makes \(F\) etale and hence quasi-finite.  Fibres of
\(j\) are contained in fibres of \(F\), so \(j\) is quasi-finite.  It is
birational by (3.1), and a birational quasi-finite morphism to the normal
scheme \(Z\) is an open immersion.  Formula (4.2) and the fibre assertion
follow.  \(\square\)

### What the proposition does and does not prove

The proof centralizes the determinant argument, including a nonprincipal
different.  It does not produce:

- the rational field chart (3.1);
- polynomiality (3.5);
- a finite list of valuations sufficient to prove (3.6);
- or the full-fibre reconstruction assertion.

Those are the classification and construction problems.  As in the
one-root lemma, the ledger is not the polynomiality theorem.

## 5. Computing the chart coefficient for a complete intersection

In presentation (2.5), view \(Z\) inside affine space with coordinates
\((y_1,\ldots,y_d,t_1,\ldots,t_c)\).  The canonical residue form is

\[
 \nu_{\rm res}
 =
 \operatorname{Res}
 \frac{
  dy_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dy_d
  \wedge dt_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dt_c}
 {E_1,\ldots,E_c}.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Choose any \(c\) ambient coordinates \(w=(w_1,\ldots,w_c)\) for which

\[
 M_w=\det\left(\frac{\partial E_i}{\partial w_j}\right)
 \ne0
 \tag{5.2}
\]

in the function field, and let \(z=(z_1,\ldots,z_d)\) be the complementary
coordinates.  Up to the fixed ordering sign,

\[
 \nu_{\rm res}
 =
 \frac{dz_1\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_d}{M_w}.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

For a rational chart \(z=z(x)\), \(w=w(x)\) satisfying the equations,

\[
 a_\theta
 =
 \pm
 \frac{\det(\partial z_i/\partial x_j)}
      {M_w}.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

Combining (2.6) and (5.4) gives the explicit ledger candidate

\[
 \det DF
 =
 \pm
 \Delta_{\rm CI}
 \frac{\det(\partial z_i/\partial x_j)}
      {M_w}.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

Although (5.5) equals the final Jacobian rationally, its factors have
independent geometric meanings:

- \(\Delta_{\rm CI}\) is intrinsic ramification;
- \(M_w\) records the residue-coordinate patch;
- and \(\det(\partial z/\partial x)\) is the affine chart ledger.

Factoring these three terms before expanding the \(f_i\) is the proposed
search method.

## 6. Multiple marks as finite algebras

Let the generic etale algebra have geometric sheet set \(\Omega\) and
monodromy group \(G\le\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)\).

For \(m\ge1\), the ordered-distinct marking set is

\[
 \Omega^{[m]}
 =
 \{(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_m):\omega_i\ne\omega_j
 \text{ for }i\ne j\}.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

This finite \(G\)-set defines a generic finite etale algebra.  Its integral
model is obtained by:

1. taking the corresponding idempotent factor of the generic tensor power;
2. selecting a \(G\)-orbit if one field component is desired; and
3. normalizing \(A\) in that field.

It is generally wrong to use the whole tensor power
\(B^{\otimes_A m}\): it contains diagonal markings and may acquire
embedded or nonnormal structure along collisions.  Removing the diagonals
gives the right generic open, but its finite model is the normalization of
the selected closure.

If \(G\) acts \(m\)-transitively, the stabilizer of one ordered tuple is a
subgroup \(H_m\), and the selected field is \(L^{H_m}\) in a Galois closure
\(L\).  For the generic \(S_N\) root cover,

\[
 H_m=S_{N-m},\qquad
 [S_N:H_m]=\frac{N!}{(N-m)!}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

For a general monodromy group there may be several tuple orbits.  Each orbit
is a different candidate cover and must pass Proposition 4.1 separately.

## 7. Flags and the additive different ledger

Consider a tower

\[
 A=B_0\subset B_1\subset\cdots\subset B_r=C
 \tag{7.1}
\]

in which every step is finite flat, generically etale, and Gorenstein.
Finite duality gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \omega_{C/A}
 &=\operatorname{Hom}_A(C,A)\\
 &\simeq
 \operatorname{Hom}_{B_{r-1}}
   (C,\operatorname{Hom}_A(B_{r-1},A))\\
 &\simeq
 \omega_{C/B_{r-1}}
 \otimes_{B_{r-1}}\omega_{B_{r-1}/A}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
\]

Iterating and using compatible local generators gives, up to a unit,

\[
 \delta_{C/A}
 =
 \delta_{C/B_{r-1}}
 \delta_{B_{r-1}/B_{r-2}}\cdots\delta_{B_1/A}.
 \tag{7.3}
\]

Hence the ramification ledger is additive:

\[
 R_{C/A}
 =
 R_{C/B_{r-1}}
 +\rho_{r,r-1}^*R_{B_{r-1}/B_{r-2}}
 +\cdots+
 \rho_{r,1}^*R_{B_1/A}.
 \tag{7.4}
\]

This is the correct formalism for flags of marked subalgebras.  It allows
different boundary factors to be cancelled by different chart variables.
It also supplies a diagnostic: a proposed flag that omits one relative
different cannot have a complete determinant ledger.

For a Galois closure with group \(G\), subgroup chains

\[
 G=H_0\supset H_1\supset\cdots\supset H_r
 \tag{7.5}
\]

give the opposite field tower

\[
 L^{H_0}\subset L^{H_1}\subset\cdots\subset L^{H_r}.
 \tag{7.6}
\]

Thus root flags, block flags, and flags of resolvent data are the same
operation at the generic level.

## 8. Resolvents and nonsymmetric quotient covers

A resolvent should be treated intrinsically as a finite \(G\)-set, not as a
particular resolvent polynomial.  Every transitive \(G\)-set is \(G/H\) for
a subgroup \(H\le G\), and its cover is the normalization of \(A\) in
\(L^H\).  A primitive element may later give a one-variable equation, but
the choice is not part of the cover.

This includes:

- one or several ordered roots;
- unordered blocks and partitions;
- pair-sum, orientation, and classical solvability resolvents;
- quotients by normal subgroups;
- and non-Galois quotients by nonnormal subgroups.

Nothing in Proposition 4.1 requires \(G=S_N\).  Nonsymmetric groups instead
create three new gates.

1. **Flat Gorenstein integral model.**  Normalization in \(L^H\) is finite,
   but flatness and Gorensteinness over \(A\) are not automatic in dimension
   greater than one.
2. **Rational quotient field.**  The field \(L^H\) must admit (3.1).
   This is a genuine rationality problem, not a consequence of finite
   generation.
3. **Affine quotient chart.**  An invariant quotient need not be affine
   space.  Its class group and canonical character can obstruct a
   polynomial chart even when the different ledger is correct.

For a linear quotient these gates include the familiar distinction between
reflection quotients, which have polynomial invariant rings, and general
finite-group quotients.  The programme should not call a quotient cover a
Keller construction until an actual polynomial source and target chart has
been exhibited.

## 9. Relation to the existing one-root lemma

For

\[
 B=A[\tau]/(E(\tau;y)),
\]

take the standard dualizing generator \(\lambda\).  Then

\[
 \tau_{B/A}=E_\tau\lambda,\qquad
 \delta=E_\tau.
\tag{9.1}
\]

If \(E_\tau=u\Delta^e\), equation (3.6) becomes

\[
 e\,\operatorname{div}(\Delta\circ\alpha)
 +\operatorname{div}(J_\alpha)
 -F^*\operatorname{div}(J_\beta)=0,
\tag{9.2}
\]

which is exactly the divisor form of the boundary-cancelled incidence
lemma.  Proposition 4.1 therefore generalizes its determinant and
finite-normalization conclusions.  The existing lemma retains more
family-specific data: a chosen core map, a controlled-discriminant
normalization, and an explicit collision reconstruction.

The
[root-incidence derivative split for \(A_4\)](../extended-geometry/A4_ROOT_INCIDENCE_DERIVATIVE_SPLIT.md)
is already a first resolvent-style experiment in this language.  It splits
the inverse different \(1/P'(T)\) between two incidence coordinates, proves
generic field recovery, and then finds a surviving orientation pole.  Its
failure is polynomiality/affine-chart failure, not determinant-line
failure.

## 10. First research packages

The programme should proceed through bounded packages rather than attempt
arbitrary finite algebras at once.

### Package A -- codimension-two complete intersections

Start with a rank-four algebra

\[
 B=A[u,v]/(E_1,E_2)
\]

given by two quadrics.  Compute

\[
 \Delta_{\rm CI}
 =
 E_{1,u}E_{2,v}-E_{1,v}E_{2,u},
\]

factor its height-one valuations, and search for rational affine charts for
which (5.5) is constant.  A surviving chart must then pass denominator
clearing for every target coordinate.  This is the smallest test not
globally organized around one displayed marked-root equation.

### Package B -- ordered-pair and block covers

For each existing degree-four and degree-five root family:

1. construct the generic \(G\)-sets of ordered pairs and unordered
   two-blocks;
2. decompose them into \(G\)-orbits;
3. compute the corresponding fixed fields and differents;
4. test rationality before attempting polynomiality; and
5. record whether the tower ledger (7.4) splits into independently
   cancellable boundary characters.

This distinguishes a new cover from a representation of the old
single-root field.

### Package C -- the \(A_4\) orientation tower

Use the subgroup structure

\[
 1\triangleleft V_4\triangleleft A_4
\]

to test whether the single orientation denominator in the current \(A_4\)
experiment refactors into a tower ledger.  If it does, the exact next
question is whether two independent source masks can cancel the relative
differents while retaining polynomial reconstruction.  The existing
target-only pullback obstruction does not answer either question.

### Package D -- nonsymmetric resolvent screening

For a candidate subgroup \(H\le G\), reject it before symbolic expansion
unless all of the following are available:

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
\text{gate} & \text{certificate}\\ \hline
\text{generic component} & \text{a transitive }G\text{-orbit }G/H\\
\text{integral model} & \text{finite flat Gorenstein normalization}\\
\text{rationality} & \operatorname{Frac}B_H\simeq k(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\\
\text{different} & \text{Cartier factors or a CI Jacobian determinant}\\
\text{ledger} & \text{zero coefficient at every chart valuation}\\
\text{polynomiality} & \theta(A)\subset k[x_1,\ldots,x_d]\\
\text{newness} &
\substack{\text{field/monodromy or boundary invariant}\\
\text{not inherited from the old seed}}
\end{array}
\tag{10.1}
\]

The last row is essential if the goal is fundamentally new families rather
than another presentation of one polynomial seed.

## 11. Current status

The determinant-line criterion, the complete-intersection formula, and the
tower identity are rigorous reductions.  No new polynomial Keller map is
claimed here.

The leading construction question is now precise:

> Find a normal finite flat Gorenstein \(A\)-domain \(B\), not merely a
> monogenic root cover already used in the repository, together with a
> rational affine chart satisfying the complete divisor ledger and
> polynomiality gate of Proposition 4.1.

The preferred first search is Package A, with Package C as the first
group-theoretic test.  Multiple marks and arbitrary resolvents become useful
only after their selected fixed fields pass the rationality and affine-chart
gates.
