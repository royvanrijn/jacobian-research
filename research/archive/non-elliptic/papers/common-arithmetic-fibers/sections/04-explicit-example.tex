\section{An explicit arithmetic fiber}\label{sec:example}

It is useful to watch the general mechanism act on one familiar piece of
arithmetic.  The example below is not needed for the theorem, but it makes
the promise of ``preserving the algebra itself'' visible: an everywhere
locally soluble algebra with no rational point enters the construction and
emerges as the complete fiber of an explicit Keller map.

Consider the squarefree quintic
\[
 P_5(T)=\CAFExplicitPolynomialFactorized
 =\CAFExplicitPolynomialExpanded.
\]
Berend and Bilu introduced this polynomial in their study of polynomials
having a root modulo every integer \cite{BerendBilu}.
It has no rational root: the rational-root theorem excludes a root of
\(T^3-19\), while \(T^2+T+1\) has discriminant \(-3\).  On the other hand,
\(\Spec\Q[T]/(P_5)\) has a point over every completion of \(\Q\).  The cubic
factor has a real root.  For a prime
\(p\notin\{2,3,19\}\), if \(p\equiv2\pmod3\), cubing is an automorphism of
\(\mathbb F_p^\times\), so \(T^3-19\) has a simple root modulo \(p\); if
\(p\equiv1\pmod3\), then \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) contains a nontrivial cube
root of unity, giving a simple root of \(T^2+T+1\).  At the exceptional
primes, Hensel witnesses are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
p&\text{factor and approximate root}&\text{check}\\ \hline
2&T^3-19,\ T=1&-18\equiv0\pmod2,\quad 3\in\mathbb Z_2^*\\
3&T^3-19,\ T=-2&v_3(-27)=3>2v_3(12)=2\\
19&T^2+T+1,\ T=7&57\equiv0\pmod{19},\quad15\in\mathbb Z_{19}^*.
\end{array}
\]

We now feed this algebra into the formulas above.  No search is needed: the
translation \(a=0\) is admissible, and
\[
 G(S)=P_5(S)-P_5(0)
 =\CAFExplicitSeed.
\]
Thus \((g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4,g_5)=(-19,-19,1,1,1)\).  Put
\[
 t=\CAFExplicitT,\qquad q=\CAFExplicitQ,
\]
and define \(F=(F_1,F_2,F_3)\) by
\begin{align}
 F_1={}&\CAFExplicitFOne,\notag\\
 F_2={}&\CAFExplicitFTwo,\notag\\
 F_3={}&\CAFExplicitFThree.
 \label{eq:explicit-quintic-map}
\end{align}
This is the output rescaling
\(\operatorname{diag}\CAFExplicitIntegralScaling\) of the quadratic-gauge
map, so
\[
 \det DF=\CAFExplicitIntegralJacobian,\qquad
 \gdeg(F)=\CAFExplicitDegree.
\]
At the target \(y=\CAFExplicitIntegralTarget\), whose normalized predecessor
is \(\CAFExplicitNormalizedTarget\), the inverse equation is
\[
 \CAFExplicitInverseIdentity.
\]
The squarefree fiber theorem therefore gives
\[
 \boxed{F^{-1}(1,0,-38)
 \simeq\Spec\Q[T]/\bigl((T^3-19)(T^2+T+1)\bigr).}
\]
This full Keller fiber has points over \(\mathbb R\) and every
\(\mathbb Q_p\), but no rational point.  The displayed map, literal fiber,
rank, finite \'etaleness, and these local and global point statements are
also included in the Lean development.  Composing \(F\) with the diagonal
target scaling
\(\operatorname{diag}\CAFExplicitIntegralToJacobianOneScaling\) gives
determinant \(\CAFExplicitJacobianOneJacobian\) and sends the target to
\(\CAFExplicitJacobianOneTarget\).
The displayed coefficients, inverse identity, scalings, and targets in this
section are generated from one JSON specification.  The Lean correspondence
theorems compare the generated definitions with the formal map and inverse
definitions literally, rather than checking only the resulting Jacobian and
fiber claims.  Thus the example is both a piece of the story and an exact
regression of the general construction.
