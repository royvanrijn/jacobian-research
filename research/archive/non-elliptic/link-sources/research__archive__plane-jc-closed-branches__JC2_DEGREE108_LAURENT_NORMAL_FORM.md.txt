# Degree-108 JC2 residue as a Laurent normal-form problem

> **Archived derivation.** The terminal obstruction is closed by `PJ72B1` and
> this note is no longer an active frontier.

## Scope

This note continues `JC2_DEGREE108_BELYI_REDUCTION.md`.  For the
no-vertical-edge `(8,28),(3,2)` residue write

\[
P=A(X)s^2+B(X)s+C(X),\qquad
Q=D(X)s^3+E(X)s^2+F(X)s+G(X)
\]

with

\[
[P,Q]_{X,s}=-X^2s^4.
\]

The five coefficient equations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
-2AD'+3DA'&=-X^2,\\
-2AE'-BD'+3DB'+2EA'&=0,\\
-2AF'-BE'+3DC'+2EB'+FA'&=0,\\
-2AG'-BF'+2EC'+FB'&=0,\\
-BG'+FC'&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{0.1}
\]

The point of this note is that the first three equations are not a generic
polynomial deformation problem.  They are the first two graded layers of the
Lie algebra of Laurent source vector fields preserving the Jacobian density
`-X^2 s^4`.  The first layer has exactly two scalar moduli after the Newton
bounds; the second homogeneous layer has exactly three.  At the third layer
there is **no nonzero regular source symmetry**.  Thus the last two equations
contain the first genuine normal-form obstruction.

No Belyi map needs to be reconstructed for these statements.

## 1. Top equation and a seven-variable closure system

Write

\[
A=Xa(X),\qquad D=X^2d(X),
\]

where `deg a=7` and `deg d=10`.  The top equation becomes

\[
\boxed{ad+X(2ad'-3da')=1.}
\tag{1.1}
\]

If

\[
a=\sum a_iX^i,\qquad d=\sum d_jX^j,
\]

then

\[
\sum_{i+j=n}(1+2j-3i)a_id_j=\delta_{n0}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

After fixing `a_0d_0=1`, this recursively determines every `d_n` from the
seven nonconstant coefficients of `a`; the condition `deg d<=10` is the finite
closure system `d_11=...=d_17=0`.  This is an alternative zero-dimensional
presentation of the five Belyi top types.

## 2. Complete first deformation layer

Define

\[
\boxed{\phi=\frac{2AE-3BD}{X^2}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The order conditions imply `phi in K[X]` and `phi(0)=0`.  The second equation
of (0.1), together with the top equation, is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
B=\phi\left(A'-\frac AX\right)-\frac12\phi'A,
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
E=\phi\left(D'-\frac{3D}{2X}\right)-\frac34\phi'D.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

A useful integrating-factor form is

\[
B=-\frac{A^3}{2X^2}
\left(\frac{X^2\phi}{A^2}\right)'.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Because `deg A=8`, `deg B<=8`, `ord_0 A=1`, and `phi(0)=0`, (2.4) gives the
**complete** polynomial form

\[
\boxed{
\phi=c\left(\frac{A^2}{X^2}-a_0^2\right)+tX,
\qquad a_0=(A/X)(0).
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Indeed `X^2 phi/A^2` has derivative `O(X^-14)` at infinity.  Subtract its
constant limit; the remaining numerator has degree at most three, so the
remaining part of `phi` has degree at most one.  The condition `phi(0)=0`
fixes its constant term as in (2.5).

The resonant term has a simple meaning.  Under the top equation,

\[
\phi=\frac{A^2}{X^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
B=0,\qquad E=\frac A2.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Thus its highest part is a triangular target shear; it is not a new Belyi
modulus.

The degree condition `deg B=8` forces

\[
\boxed{t\ne0.}
\tag{2.7}
\]

## 3. Density-preserving source symmetry

For an arbitrary polynomial `phi`, put

\[
V_\phi=
\frac{\phi(X)}s\partial_X
-\left(\frac{\phi(X)}{2X}+\frac{\phi'(X)}4\right)\partial_s.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\partial_X(JV_\phi^X)+\partial_s(JV_\phi^s)=0,
\qquad J=-X^2s^4.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Moreover (2.2)--(2.3) are exactly

\[
V_\phi(As^2)=Bs,
\qquad
V_\phi(Ds^3)=Es^2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

So the complete `s^3` Wronskian equation is the first graded
Jacobian-density-preserving source deformation.

## 4. Complete homogeneous second layer

Let `(C_0,F_0)` be any particular solution of the `s^2` equation; for example
one may take the coefficients supplied by

\[
\frac12V_\phi^2(As^2),\qquad
\frac12V_\phi^2(Ds^3).
\]

Every homogeneous difference `(\widehat C,\widehat F)` is parameterized by a
single polynomial `f(X)`:

\[
\boxed{
\widehat C=fA'-\left(\frac23f'+\frac{4f}{3X}\right)A,
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\widehat F=fD'-\left(f'+\frac{2f}{X}\right)D.
}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\widehat F=-\frac{D^2}{X^2}
\left(\frac{X^2f}{D}\right)'.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The degree bounds give the complete homogeneous form

\[
\boxed{f=c_2\frac{D}{X^2}+\alpha+\beta X.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The resonant mode is again trivial at the target level:

\[
f=\frac D{X^2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\widehat C=-\frac13,\qquad \widehat F=0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The low modes are generated by the second density-preserving Laurent field

\[
W_f=
\frac f{s^2}\partial_X
-\left(\frac{f'}3+\frac{2f}{3X}\right)\frac1s\partial_s,
\tag{4.6}
\]

which satisfies `div(J W_f)=0`.

## 5. No regular third-layer source symmetry

The next density-preserving Laurent field would have to be

\[
U_h=
\frac h{s^3}\partial_X
-\left(\frac{h'}2+\frac hX\right)\frac1{s^2}\partial_s.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Again `div(J U_h)=0`.  But its action on the leading term of `P` is

\[
U_h(As^2)=
\left[hA'-\left(h'+\frac{2h}{X}\right)A\right]s^{-1}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

To remain inside the Laurent window `s^0,s^1,s^2`, the bracket in (5.2) must
vanish.  Its only rational solutions are

\[
h=c\frac A{X^2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since `ord_0 A=1`, no nonzero solution of (5.3) is regular at `X=0`.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{there is no nonzero regular homogeneous deficit-3 source mode.}}
\tag{5.4}
\]

This is the first level at which the Wronskian hierarchy has a genuine
cokernel rather than coordinate/target freedom.

## 6. The explicit level-3 residue

Eliminating `G'` between the last two equations of (0.1) gives the necessary
polynomial identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R
=B^2F'-2BEC'-BFB'+2AFC'=0.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus the remaining no-vertical-edge problem is now sharply stated:
substitute the complete layers (2.5) and (4.1)--(4.4) into `R`.  There is no
third source mode with which to remove a nonzero remainder.  Any nonzero
normal-form component of `R` eliminates **all five** top Belyi dessins at
once.

This is substantially smaller than reconstructing five degree-21 Belyi maps.

## 7. Universal leading edge

The resonant part of (2.5) has one lower `X` degree in `B`, so the leading
coefficients depend only on `t`.  If

\[
A\sim A_8X^8,\qquad D\sim D_{12}X^{12},
\]

then

\[
\frac{B_8}{A_8}=\frac{13}{2}t,
\qquad
\frac{E_{12}}{D_{12}}=\frac{39}{4}t.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The top `X^19` coefficients of the final three Wronskian equations uniquely
force

\[
\frac{C_8}{A_8}=\frac{169}{16}t^2,
\qquad
\frac{F_{12}}{D_{12}}=\frac{507}{16}t^2,
\qquad
\frac{G_{12}}{D_{12}}=\frac{2197}{64}t^3.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Consequently the entire leading vertical edge is the common approximate root

\[
\boxed{
P_{\rm top}=A_8X^8\left(s+\frac{13t}{4}\right)^2,
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
Q_{\rm top}=D_{12}X^{12}\left(s+\frac{13t}{4}\right)^3.
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This agrees with, rather than contradicts, the approximate-root structure in
the degree-108 reduction.  Its value here is that it follows from the
Wronskian hierarchy alone and is independent of the five dessins.
