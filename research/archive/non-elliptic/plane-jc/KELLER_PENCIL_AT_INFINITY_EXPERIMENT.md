# The quartic linear pencil at infinity: an input obstruction

## Status

The surviving geometric-degree-four conductor packets do **not** determine
the topology at infinity of the linear pencil

\[
f_{[\alpha:\beta]}=\alpha P+\beta Q.
\]

This is already visible in the exact finite-free quartic countermodel used
to calibrate the \(3+1\) cusp and \(2+2\) connector. A nonlinear target
shear preserves the finite normalization, both packets, and
\(dP\wedge dQ\), but changes the genus and the monodromy-at-infinity zeta
function of a generic linear-pencil member.

The calculation is exact and is replayed by
[`cas/experiment_quartic_keller_pencil.py`](cas/experiment_quartic_keller_pencil.py).
It is an under-determination result and a resolved-pencil calibration. It
does not construct or exclude a quartic Keller map: the two displayed maps
have a common nonconstant Jacobian determinant.

## 1. Two target-equivalent realizations of the same packet

Put

\[
 P=y,\qquad
 Q_0=x^4-x^3+xy,\qquad
 Q_2=Q_0+y^2.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The target automorphism

\[
 A(u,v)=(u,v+u^2)
\tag{1.2}
\]

satisfies

\[
 (P,Q_2)=A\circ(P,Q_0).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Consequently the finite covers are isomorphic over the corresponding
target coordinates. At \((u,v)=(0,0)\),

\[
 Q_i(x,0)=x^3(x-1),
\tag{1.4}
\]

so both have the clean \(3+1\) fiber. For \(F_0=(P,Q_0)\), the target
value \((1/8,-1/64)\) has fiber polynomial

\[
 x^4-x^3+\frac18x+\frac1{64}
 =\frac{(8x^2-4x-1)^2}{64}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

For \(F_2=(P,Q_2)\), the same source fiber lies over \((1/8,0)\).
Thus the \(2+2\) connector is preserved by the shear.

The determinant is preserved as well:

\[
 \det\frac{\partial(P,Q_0)}{\partial(x,y)}
 =
 \det\frac{\partial(P,Q_2)}{\partial(x,y)}
 =-4x^3+3x^2-y.
\tag{1.6}
\]

In particular, both maps fail the Keller condition on
\(y=-4x^3+3x^2\). Their role is to test exactly which information the
packet retains.

## 2. The unsheared symbolic pencil

On the chart \(\beta\ne0\), write \(s=\alpha/\beta\). With
\(g=x^4-x^3\), polynomial division gives

\[
 h_s(x)=\frac{g(x)-g(-s)}{x+s}\in\mathbb C[s,x].
\tag{2.1}
\]

The triangular change

\[
 X=x+s,\qquad Y=y+h_s(x)
\tag{2.2}
\]

then gives the exact identity

\[
 Q_0+sP=XY+g(-s)=XY+s^4+s^3.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus every direction with \(\beta\ne0\) has generic fiber
\(\mathbb C^\ast\): its smooth completion has genus zero and two punctures.
In the cohomological convention

\[
 \zeta_\infty(t)
 =\prod_j\det(1-t\Phi_j^\infty)^{(-1)^j},
\tag{2.4}
\]

the monodromy is the identity on both \(H^0\) and \(H^1\), so

\[
 \boxed{\zeta_{\infty,F_0,[\alpha:\beta]}(t)=1
 \quad(\beta\ne0).}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Use \(\mathbb P^1_X\times\mathbb P^1_Y\). The rational map \(XY\) has
two base points, \((X,Y)=(\infty,0)\) and \((0,\infty)\). One blowup at
each resolves it. The boundary graph is

```text
H_X(1) -- D_X(infinity, pole 1) -- D_Y(infinity, pole 1) -- H_Y(1),
```

where each \(H\) is dicritical of degree one. Each pole component has two
boundary intersections and hence open Euler characteristic zero. The
A'Campo product is therefore \(1\).

The unique exceptional pencil direction is \([1:0]\), for which \(f=P=y\).
Its generic fiber is \(\mathbb A^1\), with genus zero and one puncture. The
pole component has order one and open Euler characteristic one, hence

\[
 \zeta_{\infty,P}(t)=1-t.
\tag{2.6}
\]

## 3. The target-sheared symbolic pencil

For \(F_2\), the \(\beta\ne0\) members are

\[
 Q_2+sP=y^2+(x+s)y+x^4-x^3.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Their Newton polygon at infinity is the triangle

\[
 \Delta=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(4,0),(0,2)\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The only compact face at infinity has polynomial \(x^4+y^2\), whose two
partials have no common zero in \((\mathbb C^\ast)^2\). Hence every
symbolic \(s\) is nondegenerate at infinity. The triangle has area four,
eight boundary lattice intervals, and one interior lattice point. Its
edge at infinity has lattice length two. The generic fiber therefore has

\[
 g=1,\qquad r=2,\qquad b_1=2g+r-1=3.
\tag{3.3}
\]

The ordinary-blowup resolution can be read directly at the unique
projective base point \([0:1:0]\). In the \(y=1\) chart the leading ideal
is generated by \(z^2+x^4\) and \(z^4\). Two shared blowups separate the
branches \(z/x^2=\pm i\). At either separated point the remaining base
ideal is \((r,x^4)\), giving a chain with pole orders \(3,2,1\) and then a
degree-one dicritical:

```text
                         V3+ -- V2+ -- V1+ -- H+(1)
                        /
L(infinity, pole 4) -- E2(pole 4) -- E1(pole 2)
                        \
                         V3- -- V2- -- V1- -- H-(1)
```

Here \(E_2\) has four boundary intersections and open Euler characteristic
\(-2\); \(L\) and \(E_1\) each have one and hence open Euler characteristic
\(1\); all intermediate \(V\)'s have open Euler characteristic zero.
The A'Campo product is

\[
 (1-t^4)(1-t^2)(1-t^4)^{-2}
 =\boxed{\frac1{1+t^2}}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Equivalently, the eigenvalues on \(H^1\) are \(1,i,-i\), while \(H^0\)
contributes the cancelling factor \(1-t\). The exceptional direction
\([1:0]\) is still the coordinate polynomial \(P=y\), with zeta \(1-t\).

## 4. Consequences for the proposed Keller-pencil experiment

The comparison proves:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
&F_0&F_2=A\circ F_0\\ \hline
\text{finite-normalization packet}&3+1,\ 2+2&3+1,\ 2+2\\
dP\wedge dQ&\text{same}&\text{same}\\
\text{generic pencil fiber}&(g,r)=(0,2)&(g,r)=(1,2)\\
\zeta_\infty&1&(1+t^2)^{-1}.
\end{array}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore the cusp/connector packet cannot be fed directly into a Newton or
constructible-sheaf formula: it does not specify the Newton boundary of
\(\alpha P+\beta Q\). It also shows that the topology of the **linear**
pencil is not invariant under general polynomial left equivalence. It is
automatically invariant under affine target changes, which merely
reparameterize \(\mathbb P^1\), but a nonlinear target shear changes the
linear system.

For a hypothetical Keller map the equality

\[
 dP\wedge d(Q+P^2)=dP\wedge dQ
\tag{4.2}
\]

shows that the same ambiguity remains compatible with the constant-Jacobian
equation. The example above does not prove that a shear changes the pencil
of an unknown Keller map, but it prevents the packet or Jacobian equation
alone from defining the proposed invariant.

## 5. Input required for a genuine packet test

A resolved Keller-pencil certificate must add, on one fixed smooth
completion:

1. the complete boundary graph, intersection matrix, and canonical class;
2. \(\operatorname{ord}_E(P)\) and \(\operatorname{ord}_E(Q)\) on every
   boundary prime;
3. the initial residue sections of \(P\) and \(Q\) whenever their orders
   tie, so the directions where
   \(\alpha\operatorname{in}_E(P)+\beta\operatorname{in}_E(Q)\) cancels are
   known;
4. the full infinitely-near base clusters after every such cancellation;
5. the maps from horizontal components to the value line, including their
   dicritical degrees; and
6. the conductor pairing that identifies packet endpoints.

The existing quartic records provide item 6 and fragments relevant to item
1, but not items 2--5. Once those data are supplied, the exceptional
directions form a finite constructible stratification of \(\mathbb P^1\);
adjunction gives genus, horizontal degrees give punctures, and the pole
multiplicities and open Euler characteristics give the zeta function.

Thus the next valid experiment is not “compute a symbolic zeta function
from the packet.” It is:

\[
\boxed{\text{enhance each quartic packet to a map-decorated pencil
certificate, or prove that no such enhancement exists.}}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The Newton/constructible-sheaf formulas are then an appropriate backend,
not a substitute for the missing map-decorated boundary input.

## 6. Reproduction

Run

```bash
.venv/bin/python plane-jc/cas/experiment_quartic_keller_pencil.py \
  --output artifacts/generated-results/quartic_keller_pencil_calibration.json
```

The output verifies the target shear, the two packet factorizations, the
common Jacobian determinant, the symbolic normal form, both resolved
boundary graphs, and the A'Campo products. With the repository's pinned
Python/SymPy environment, the generated JSON has SHA-256
`2705c5a1b92f5b4f3a469c12238580e1c9cdb59a8cd692a347da3549e3a8cd1c`.

For the Newton-polyhedron and constructible-sheaf backend, see Y. Matsui
and K. Takeuchi,
[*Monodromy zeta functions at infinity, Newton polyhedra and constructible
sheaves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.3149).
