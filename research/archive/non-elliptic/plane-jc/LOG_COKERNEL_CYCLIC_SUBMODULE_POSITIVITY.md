# Positivity of isolated `Fitt_1` corrections

> **Status.**  Local algebra theorem.  Let `R` be a two-dimensional regular
> local ring with infinite residue field and let a `2 x 2` injective bundle
> matrix have nonzero determinant `g`.  If its cokernel is cyclic at every
> height-one point of `(g)`, then the cokernel contains a copy of `R/(g)` and
> the quotient has finite nonnegative length.  Its length is at least the
> colength of `Fitt_1`.  Consequently an isolated noncyclic logarithmic
> packet can only add a positive point class to the complete cyclic Cartier
> packet.  Combined with the F2 complete-chain identity, this excludes every
> one-component simple-inertia E8 completion.  The theorem does not address
> a positive-dimensional `Fitt_1` locus, extra affine components, inertia
> greater than two, or `k>1`.

Exact thick-node and unibranch model regressions are in
[`verify_log_cokernel_cyclic_submodule_positivity.py`](../scripts/verify_log_cokernel_cyclic_submodule_positivity.py).

## 1. Local cyclic-submodule theorem

Let `(R,m)` be a two-dimensional regular local domain with infinite residue
field, and let

\[
 \Theta:R^2\longrightarrow R^2                 \tag{1.1}
\]

be injective with determinant `g!=0`.  Put

\[
 M=\operatorname{coker}\Theta.                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Assume that `M_p` is cyclic for every height-one prime `p` containing `g`.
Equivalently, `Fitt_1(M)` is not contained in any height-one prime of the
determinant divisor.

### Theorem 1.1

There is an exact sequence

\[
 \boxed{
 0\longrightarrow R/(g)\longrightarrow M
 \longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow0,}           \tag{1.3}
\]

where `Q` has finite length.  Moreover the injection may be chosen so that

\[
 \boxed{
 \ell_R(Q)\ge
 \ell_R\bigl(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_1(M)\bigr).} \tag{1.4}
\]

The right side is interpreted only when `Fitt_1(M)` is `m`-primary.  If it
is the unit ideal, both sides may be zero.

### Proof

The determinant curve has finitely many height-one generic points.  At each,
the generators of `M` that fail to generate form a proper linear condition.
Because the residue field is infinite, one primitive vector in the target
free module has a class `v in M` that generates `M_p` at every such point.
Complete `v` to a basis of the target `R^2`.

The determinant kills `M`, so `g` kills `v` and gives a map

\[
 R/(g)\longrightarrow M.                        \tag{1.5}
\]

It is an isomorphism at every height-one point of `(g)`.  Its kernel is
therefore supported only at `m`.  But `R/(g)` is a one-dimensional
Cohen--Macaulay hypersurface and has no nonzero finite-support submodule.
Thus (1.5) is injective, and its quotient `Q` is finite.

In the chosen target basis write

\[
 \Theta=
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}.          \tag{1.6}
\]

Killing the class of the first target generator leaves the second generator
with relation ideal `J=(c,d)` (after transposing conventions, the same proof
uses the complementary row).  Hence

\[
 Q\simeq R/J.                                    \tag{1.7}
\]

Since `J` is generated by entries of `Theta`,

\[
 J\subseteq\operatorname{Fitt}_1(M).             \tag{1.8}
\]

Both ideals are `m`-primary when the defect is isolated, and (1.4) follows.
\(\square\)

## 2. Geometric form

For a logarithmic differential between rank-two bundles on a smooth surface,
the theorem applies in the completed local ring at every isolated
`Fitt_1` point.  The cyclic submodule in (1.3) is the local form of the
kernel-line Cartier packet

\[
 K\otimes O_D(D).                                \tag{2.1}
\]

The local sequences glue with its transition units away from the finite
defect set.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{ch}_2(T^{\log})
 =\deg_D(K)+\frac12D^2+sum_p\ell(Q_p),
 \qquad \ell(Q_p)\ge0.}                         \tag{2.2}
\]

When `Fitt_1` is primary at `p`,

\[
 \ell(Q_p)\ge\ell_p(O_X/\operatorname{Fitt}_1). \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the sign ambiguity that arises from comparing arbitrary normalization
sequences disappears once the comparison is made with the determinant and
kernel-line cyclic submodule supplied by the actual bundle matrix.

The diagonal thick-node matrix gives the familiar exact sequence

\[
0\to R/(u^av^b)
 \to R/(u^a)\oplus R/(v^b)
 \to R/(u^a,v^b)\to0,                           \tag{2.4}
\]

whose positive quotient has length `ab`.  The smooth-fold matrix

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}r&0\\t^N&r\end{pmatrix}         \tag{2.5}
\]

similarly contains `R/(r^2)` with positive quotient `R/(r,t^N)` of length
`N`.  These are the two local geometries needed by the cusp-attachment
ledger.

## 3. Consequence for the E8 simple-inertia atlas

The extraction root and every affine row have generic Smith form with one
unit entry, so their `Fitt_1` ideals are units at all determinant generic
points.  On a completed proper surface, any remaining `Fitt_1` support is
therefore finite.  Theorem 1.1 applies at every point of it.

The complete-chain theorem gives total available point budget `u-1`.  At
the multiplicity-three E8 cusp, the order gate gives total

\[
 \sum_p\ell(O_X/\operatorname{Fitt}_1)_p
 \ge2F.                                         \tag{3.1}
\]

For simple inertia, `F=R=(d-u)/2`.  By (2.3), effectivity is not an extra
hypothesis: the actual local cokernel quotients have at least this length.
Consequently a necessary condition is

\[
 u-1\ge2R.                                       \tag{3.2}
\]

Every row of the order-`240` orbifold atlas violates (3.2).  Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{Every one-component simple-inertia E8 completion is excluded.}}   \tag{3.3}
\]

This removes the negative-`Fitt_1` escape left in the complete-chain note.
For the full `(75,125)` problem, the remaining cusp branches are now:

1. E8 meridian inertia greater than two;
2. a completion with more than one affine nonproperness component;
3. the non-E8 `k=1` strata; and
4. the charts `k=2,...,24` if `k=1` is not forced.

The subsequent
[`global affine-ramification budget`](F2_AFFINE_GLOBAL_RAMIFICATION_BUDGET.md)
shows that the first item is a real claim boundary rather than a loose
qualification.  In degree six there is a unique cubic-inertia `A_6` action
that saturates the scalar point budget, and an exact SNC local model has
generic Smith form `diag(t,t)` on a contracted component.  Its `Fitt_1`
locus is one-dimensional, so Theorem 1.1 does not apply there.

## Reproduction

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/verify_log_cokernel_cyclic_submodule_positivity.py
```

The checker verifies the exact positive quotients and the lower comparison
with `Fitt_1` for thick nodes and noncyclic unibranch matrices through a
bounded family.  The general argument is the local algebra proof above.
