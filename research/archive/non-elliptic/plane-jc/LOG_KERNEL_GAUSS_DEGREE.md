# Gauss-degree formula for a contracted cyclic logarithmic packet

> **Status.**  Let a proper cyclic determinant packet be contracted to one
> target point.  This note proves that its logarithmic kernel line is the
> tautological line of a morphism `gamma:D->P^1`; hence
> `K=gamma^*O(-1)` and `deg_D(K)=-deg_D(gamma^*O(1))<=0`.  Combining this
> with the cyclic kernel-line formula gives
> `ch_2(coker)=D^2/2-deg(gamma)`.  For the F2 extraction-root packet the
> contribution is therefore `27-deg(gamma_root)`, with a nonnegative and
> geometrically countable remaining degree.  The subsequent
> tangential-coordinate theorem computes that degree as zero for the F2 root;
> neither theorem closes the global Chern ledger.

The exact degree bookkeeping and F2 specializations are replayed by
[`verify_log_kernel_gauss_degree.py`](../scripts/verify_log_kernel_gauss_degree.py).

## 1. Contracted cyclic setup

Let `X` be a smooth surface and

\[
 \theta:E\longrightarrow F
\]

an injective map of rank-two vector bundles with determinant divisor `D`.
Assume `D` is proper, `Fitt_1(coker(theta))=O_X`, and
`coker(theta)=i_*L` for a line bundle `L` on `D`.  Let

\[
 K=\ker(\theta|_D).
\]

Suppose moreover that

\[
 E|_D\simeq V\otimes O_D                         \tag{1.1}
\]

for a two-dimensional vector space `V`.  In the logarithmic map setting,
this holds when the support of `D` is contracted to a target point: trivialize
the target logarithmic cotangent bundle near that point and pull the basis
back to `D`.

Because the restricted map has locally free kernel and image of rank one,
`K` is a line subbundle of the trivial bundle in (1.1).  It therefore defines
a morphism

\[
 \gamma:D\longrightarrow \operatorname{Gr}(1,V)=P(V)\simeq P^1, \tag{1.2}
\]

including when `D` is reducible or nonreduced, and

\[
 \boxed{K\simeq\gamma^*O_{P(V)}(-1).}             \tag{1.3}
\]

This is the logarithmic kernel-direction, or Gauss, map.

## 2. Positivity on the Cartier curve

Put

\[
 e(\gamma)=\deg_D\gamma^*O_{P^1}(1).             \tag{2.1}
\]

The line bundle in (2.1) is generated by two global sections.  Since a
Cartier divisor on a smooth surface is Cohen--Macaulay and has finitely many
associated components, a general linear combination of those generators is
a nonzerodivisor.  It gives an exact sequence

\[
 0\longrightarrow O_D\longrightarrow
 \gamma^*O_{P^1}(1)\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow0, \tag{2.2}
\]

where `Q` has finite length.  Thus

\[
 e(\gamma)=\operatorname{length}(Q)\ge0,
 \qquad \deg_D(K)=-e(\gamma)\le0.                \tag{2.3}
\]

On a reduced rational component this is the familiar pencil
`[a:b]` without common zeros: its kernel is `O(-d)` and its Gauss degree is
`d`.  Formula (2.2) is the version that remains valid for the full thickened
Cartier packet.

## 3. Chern formula

The kernel-line theorem gives

\[
 \operatorname{ch}_2(\operatorname{coker}\theta)
 =\deg_D(K)+\frac12D^2.
\]

Using (2.3),

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{ch}_2(\operatorname{coker}\theta)
 =\frac12D^2-e(\gamma).}                         \tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently the constant-direction case is the largest possible cyclic
contribution.  It equals `D^2/2` exactly when `gamma^*O(1)` has degree zero.
For a connected reduced packet this means that the kernel direction is
constant; the nonreduced case is best retained in the precise degree-zero
form unless its nilpotent Gauss data have also been checked.

## 4. F2 extraction-root specialization

For `D_root=3E+18L`, the local coordinates of `PF2UCE1` give

\[
 (T,z)=(WU^5,W^3U^{18})\cdot\text{units}.
\]

Both reduced components `E` and `L` map to the same target point.  The
target covector `dz` generates the kernel near their node, so the local Gauss
map has no base point or direction change there.  Globally, define

\[
 e_{\rm root}=\deg_{D_{\rm root}}
              \gamma_{\rm root}^*O_{P^1}(1)\ge0. \tag{4.1}
\]

Since `D_root^2/2=27`, (3.1) becomes

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{ch}_2(\mathcal T^{\log}_{\rm root})
 =27-e_{\rm root}\le27.}                        \tag{4.2}
\]

In particular the root contribution vanishes exactly when `e_root=27` and
is negative when `e_root>27`.  These are alternatives in the Chern ledger,
not contradictions by themselves.

## 5. Claim boundary and next calculation

The theorem requires a cyclic packet with unit `Fitt_1` and a trivialized
pulled-back source bundle on its determinant curve.  It does not apply
unchanged to noncyclic nodes or determinant components mapping onto a target
curve.

For F2 the remaining local calculation is now sharply finite: express the
kernel direction as a coprime pair of rational functions in a parameter on
`E` and on `L`, include its nilpotent jets through multiplicities `3` and
`18`, and count the zero divisor of a general pencil.  The carrier endpoint,
the extraction node, every non-toric special point, and the outgoing/purity
attachments must all be included.  That count is `e_root`.

For the actual F2 root, the stronger
[`tangential-coordinate theorem`](LOG_TANGENTIAL_KERNEL_TRIVIALIZATION.md)
avoids this count: `f^*z` is divisible by the full determinant equation, so
the fixed target covector `dz` trivializes the kernel and `e_root=0`.
