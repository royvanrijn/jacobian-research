# Nonlinear Wronskian-to-`JC2` programme for the final `[4]` branch

> **Archived programme.** Its proof obligation was superseded by the completed
> Wronskian reduction and relative-nilpotent master theorem.

## Status

This note records the remaining global reduction after `HC4RSD69`.  The local
identities below have been checked at a generic jet.  The final moving-frame
normalization is deliberately stated as the next proof obligation rather than
silently promoted as a theorem.

## 1. One polynomial generator

For the remaining linearly-dependent rank-three form

\[
A=yP(x,w)+zQ(x,w)+R(x,w),\qquad J(P,Q)=0,
\]

the polynomial Lüroth/generative-polynomial theorem gives a closed polynomial
\(h\in K[x,w]\) and univariate polynomials \(p,q\) such that

\[
P=p(h),\qquad Q=q(h).
\]

After dividing \((p',q')\) by its gcd, write the primitive pair

\[
(a(h),b(h)),\qquad \gcd(a,b)=1.
\]

The top Hessian kernel is generated by

\[
D=b(h)\partial_y-a(h)\partial_z.
\]

Its projective motion is measured by the Wronskian

\[
\Theta(h)=a(h)b'(h)-b(h)a'(h).                 \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus \(\Theta=0\) is exactly the fixed-kernel case.

Because \(a,b\) are coprime, choose \(\alpha,\beta\in K[h]\) with

\[
b\alpha-a\beta=1.
\]

Then

\[
u=ay+bz,\qquad t=\alpha y+\beta z
\]

is a polynomial passive-coordinate change of determinant \(-1\), with

\[
Du=0,\qquad Dt=1.
\]

The top determinant coefficient therefore integrates globally to

\[
\psi=tC(x,w,u)+E(x,w,u).                       \tag{1.2}
\]

## 2. Tangent derivation on the active plane

Put

\[
\tau=h_x\partial_w-h_w\partial_x.
\]

Then

\[
\tau h=0,
\qquad
\tau a=\tau b=\tau\alpha=\tau\beta=0,
\]

so \(\tau\) also fixes the adapted passive coordinates \(u,t\).

At a generic active point choose an affine frame with \(dh=(1,0)\), and a
constant passive frame with \((a,b)=(1,0)\) at that point.  Write
\(\Theta\) for the resulting first projective derivative of the kernel line,
and let

\[
\Lambda=T(\tau,\tau).
\]

Generic rank three means \(\Lambda\ne0\).

A complete generic-jet expansion of the pencil gives

\[
[s^2]\det(S+sT)
=3\Theta\Lambda\,t\,C_u^2+\text{terms independent of }t. \tag{2.1}
\]

Hence, on a genuinely moving chart \(\Theta\ne0\),

\[
C_u=0.
\]

The remaining part of the same coefficient gives

\[
\tau C=0.                                             \tag{2.2}
\]

Since the chosen generator \(h\) is closed, (2.2) puts \(C\) in the
one-variable field/ring generated by \(h\); write

\[
C=H(h).                                               \tag{2.3}
\]

## 3. The last two coefficients

The same generic-jet expansion yields

\[
[s]\det(S+sT)
=-\Lambda E_{uu}\bigl(\Theta E_u+B(h)\bigr)^2,       \tag{3.1}
\]

where \(B(h)\) is the connection-corrected derivative of the affine
coefficient \(H\).  Nonzero \(\det S\) therefore forces

\[
E_{uu}=0.
\]

Write

\[
E=uL(x,w)+M(x,w).                                    \tag{3.2}
\]

After this substitution, the constant determinant identity at the same jet is

\[
\det S
=(\tau L)^2\bigl(\Theta(h)L+B(h)\bigr)^2.     \tag{3.3}
\]

The affine-generator theorem `HC4RSD69` is exactly the special case where
\(h\) itself is affine-linear, in which (3.3) immediately contradicts
\(\Theta\ne0\).

## 4. Why the nonlinear case should reduce to `JC2`, not to an absolute contradiction

Assume the moving-frame calculation globalizes with no extra factor in (3.3).
Since the left side is a nonzero constant, after a harmless scalar extension
we obtain

\[
(\Theta L+B)\tau L=c\in K^*.
\]

But \(\tau h=0\), so for

\[
G=\frac{\Theta(h)}{2}L^2+B(h)L                    \tag{4.1}
\]

we get

\[
\tau G=c.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
J(h,G)=c.                                             \tag{4.2}
\]

Thus a genuinely moving `[4]` kernel would manufacture a plane Keller pair.
This is exactly the desired endpoint:

\[
\boxed{\text{moving nonlinear `[4]` branch }\Longrightarrow `JC2`.}
\]

This also explains why trying to prove that every nonlinear generator is
impossible is too strong.  The natural obstruction is the unresolved plane
Jacobian problem itself.

For example, the nonlinear probe \(h=xw\) cannot survive, because \(h\) has a
critical point and hence cannot satisfy \(J(h,G)\in K^*\) with any polynomial
\(G\).

## 5. Exact remaining proof obligation

The next step is not another degree calculation.  It is to verify (2.1),
(3.1), and especially (3.3) in the determinant-one moving frame

\[
(n,\tau;\partial_u,D),
\]

where \(nh=1\) and the active and passive frame determinants are both units.
The generic-jet computations show that all Hessian-of-\(h\) connection terms
enter only through \(\Lambda\) and cancel from (3.3).  A coordinate-free
moving-frame derivation would promote the Wronskian-to-Keller mechanism to an
all-degree theorem.

## 6. Literature connection

The use of a single generator \(h\) is justified by the polynomial
Lüroth/generative-polynomial theorem for algebraically dependent bivariate
polynomials.  See the closed/generative-polynomial literature of
Arzhantsev--Petravchuk and the classical Noether--Schinzel sharpening of
Lüroth.  The singular-Hessian/quasi-translation reduction is compatible with
De Bondt's classification programme and the rank-three dependence criterion of
Jin--Guo.
