# Positive-density primes exclude every rational numerator return

The two nonrational branch points of the existing elliptic slice are
independent of its rational Mordell--Weil group. Kummer theory and
Chebotarev turn this into a prime-distribution statement: a fixed
positive-density set of active primes passes all four earlier
quadratic eligibility tests, but neither finite branch residue belongs
to the reduction of any rational point. At these primes the local
sieve on the rational image has only its pole branch. This result does
not establish recurrence of pole returns or an all-primes Haar limit.

## 1. The fixed curve, points and external interfaces

Use the translated model and rational group

\[
 E_0:y^2=x^3-10656x^2+27091730x,
 \qquad \Gamma=E_0(\mathbb Q),
 \qquad F(x)=128x-25865.
\]

Write `q_0=2709173`, `d=51730=2*5*7*739`, and

\[
 L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d),\qquad
 B=L(\sqrt2,\sqrt{5q_0}).                         \tag{1.1}
\]

The three defining squareclasses of `B` are independent, so
`[B:Q]=8`. Their quadratic discriminants are prime to three; hence
`B/Q` is Galois and unramified at three. Retain the independent
rational points

\[
 P=\left(\frac{373248}{49},\frac{59459616}{343}\right),\qquad
 Q=\left(\frac{3442688}{529},\frac{304670016}{12167}\right),
 \qquad T=(0,0),                                  \tag{1.2}
\]

and the finite branch points

\[
 R_\pm=\left(\frac{25865}{128},
                    \pm\frac{639735\sqrt d}{2048}\right).
                                                        \tag{1.3}
\]

Here `R_+` denotes the branch point, not the rational point `P+T`
called `R` in some local-count notes. ETC3a in
[the complete three-adic classification](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_COMPLETE_THREE_ADIC_CLASSIFICATION.md)
proves independence of `P,Q`; EBT1 in
[the branch-twist note](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_BRANCH_TWIST.md)
proves that `R_+` is nontorsion and conjugates to `-R_+` over `L/Q`.
The rational rank is two and its full torsion group is generated by
`T`. In particular

\[
 \Gamma_0=\langle P,Q,T\rangle\subset\Gamma,
 \qquad I=[\Gamma:\Gamma_0]<\infty.                \tag{1.4}
\]

No value of `I` and no full Mordell--Weil basis is needed here.

The external Kummer interface is Corvaja--Demeio--Javanpeykar--
Lombardo--Zannier, *On the distribution of rational points on ramified
covers of abelian varieties*, Compositio Mathematica 158 (2022),
2109--2155, DOI
[10.1112/S0010437X22007746](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X22007746).
In its section 6.2, `K_{Z,m}=K(A[m],[m]^{-1}Z)` and
`N_m=Gal(K_{Z,m}/K(A[m]))`. Theorem 6.9, printed p.2144
(PDF p.37), bounds `[A[m]:N_m]` by a constant depending only on
`A/K,Z`, for every `m`, provided the cyclic group generated by `Z`
is Zariski dense. Remark 6.10 on printed p.2145 explicitly allows
`Z` to be divisible. We use this finite-level statement; we do not
assume independence of the Galois actions at different auxiliary
primes. The
[retained published PDF](sources/cdjlz-2022-ramified-covers.pdf) and
[version metadata](sources/cdjlz-2022-ramified-covers.json)
pin the directly read interface.

Two further primary interfaces are Milne's *Modular Functions and
Modular Forms*, version 1.31, Corollary 12.15, printed/PDF p.125
(CM implies integral `j`), and *Abelian Varieties*, January 2, 2022,
Proposition 12.1, printed/PDF p.19 (Poincare reducibility), and
Proposition 20.7 and Corollary 20.8, printed/PDF p.44 (finite etale
multiplication and unramified division extensions at good primes
where the multiplier is invertible). Their retained copies are
[MF](sources/milne-modular-functions-131.pdf),
[AVs](sources/milne-abelian-varieties-2022.pdf), with
[MF metadata](sources/milne-modular-functions-131.json) and
[AVs metadata](sources/milne-abelian-varieties-2022.json).
The rational-prime density assertion uses the classical Chebotarev
theorem, for example Milne ANT300 Theorem 8.31, printed p.140
(PDF p.148) of the already
[retained source](sources/milne-ant300.pdf).

## 2. A positive-density exclusion inside the eligible primes

**Theorem EKX1 (Kummer exclusion of both numerator branches).** There
is a Chebotarev set `P_Kum` of rational primes of positive natural
density such that every `p in P_Kum` is good for `E_0`, is two modulo
three, splits completely in `B`, and satisfies

\[
 \overline R_+,\overline R_-
       \notin \operatorname{im}\bigl(\Gamma\longrightarrow
                                      E_0(\mathbb F_p)\bigr).
                                                        \tag{2.1}
\]

Consequently, for every affine rational point with
`x=a/c^2`, `gcd(a,c)=1`, `c>0`,

\[
 \boxed{p\nmid 128a-25865c^2\quad(p\in P_{\rm Kum}).}          \tag{2.2}
\]

In particular, the positive-prime numerator condition is absent on
this fixed set for the entire rational elliptic slice, even though
`p` passes the numerator eligibility test for every one of its four
global squareclasses.

**Endomorphisms and nondegeneracy.** Direct invariant arithmetic gives

\[
 c_4=516402336,\qquad
 \Delta_E=2^9 5^2 7^3\cdot1889\cdot q_0^2,
\]
\[
 j(E_0)=\frac{268964296422901398497088}
                   {118888422368172129575}\notin\mathbb Z.
                                                        \tag{2.3}
\]

For example, `c_4=739 mod1889` and `v_1889(Delta_E)=1`, so
`v_1889(j)=-1`. Milne MF Corollary 12.15 excludes complex
multiplication, and hence `End_{Qbar}(E_0)=Z`, using the endomorphism
classification in the same source, section 12, printed/PDF p.123.

The point `Z=(P,Q,R_+) in E_0^3(B)` is independent modulo torsion:
a relation `uP+vQ+wR_+` torsion, subtracted from its conjugate under
an automorphism nontrivial on `L`, makes `2wR_+` torsion. EBT1 gives
`w=0`, and independence of `P,Q` then gives `u=v=0`.

Here this independence implies the exact nondegeneracy needed for
the Kummer theorem. Indeed, let `H` be the identity component of the
Zariski closure of the cyclic group generated by `Z`. If `H` were a
proper abelian subvariety of `E_0^3`, Poincare reducibility would give
a nonzero rational endomorphism of `E_0^3` that kills `H`. Composing
with a suitable coordinate projection and clearing denominators
gives a nonzero homomorphism

\[
 \phi:E_0^3\longrightarrow E_0,\qquad
 \phi(U,V,W)=[u]U+[v]V+[w]W,
\]

because `Hom(E_0^3,E_0)=Z^3`. Some nonzero multiple of `Z` belongs
to `H`, since an algebraic group has finitely many connected
components. Thus `phi(Z)` would be torsion, contrary to the
independence just proved. This argument can be made over the
algebraic closure, and therefore proves Zariski density over `B`
as well.

**Choice of the auxiliary prime.** Apply the stated Kummer theorem to
`A=E_0^3/B` and `Z`. Let `c_K` be its finite index bound. Fix any
prime satisfying

\[
 \ell>\max(c_K,3),\qquad \ell\nmid I.              \tag{2.4}
\]

Such primes exist because `I` is a fixed finite integer. This is an
existence choice; we have not computed `c_K`, `I`, a qualifying
numerical `ell`, or the division field below. Put

\[
 M=B(E_0[\ell],[\ell]^{-1}P,[\ell]^{-1}Q,
                                      [\ell]^{-1}R_+).
                                                        \tag{2.5}
\]

The Kummer kernel

\[
 N=\operatorname{Gal}(M/B(E_0[\ell]))
       \ \cong\ E_0[\ell]^3                      \tag{2.6}
\]

is full: its index is at most `c_K`, but every nontrivial index of
a subgroup of the finite `ell`-group `E_0[ell]^3` is at least
`ell>c_K`. In particular the translations

\[
 \mathcal C=\{(0,0,t):0\ne t\in E_0[\ell]\}
        \subset N                                \tag{2.7}
\]

all exist, and `#C=ell^2-1`.

**Galois normality and the congruence at three.** The field `M` is
Galois over `Q`. The field `B` is Galois, the points `P,Q` are
rational, and every rational Galois automorphism sends `R_+` to
either `R_+` or `-R_+`. The full sets of division points in (2.5)
are therefore preserved, since division points of `-R_+` are the
negatives of division points of `R_+`.

The subset `C` is stable under conjugation in `Gal(M/Q)`. To check
this without assuming a split extension, let `h` induce `A_h` on
`E_0[ell]` and send `R_+` to `epsilon_h R_+`, with
`epsilon_h in {1,-1}`. Conjugating a translation `(t_1,t_2,t_3)`
then sends it to
`(A_h t_1,A_h t_2,epsilon_h A_h t_3)`. Choices of division lifts
only introduce affine constants that cancel in this conjugation
of a pure translation. Thus the first two coordinates remain zero
and the third remains nonzero.

Moreover, `M/Q` is unramified at three. The curve has good reduction
at three, `B/Q` is unramified there, and `ell!=3`. Over each local
integer ring of `B` above three, properness extends all three points
to sections of the abelian scheme. The multiplication map `[ell]`
is finite etale, so its fibres and the associated division fields
are unramified, precisely as in Milne AVs Corollary 20.8. This
argument also covers rational points with nonintegral affine
coordinates. It follows that

\[
 M\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)=\mathbb Q,              \tag{2.8}
\]

since the nontrivial quadratic field `Q(zeta_3)` is ramified at
three.

**Rational-prime Chebotarev.** In the product group

\[
 \operatorname{Gal}(M(\zeta_3)/\mathbb Q)
   =\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)\times
                       \operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)/\mathbb Q),
\]

take the conjugacy-stable subset `C times {tau}`, where `tau` is
nontrivial. Chebotarev gives a set of primes with natural density

\[
 \delta_{\rm Kum}=\frac{\ell^2-1}{2[M:\mathbb Q]}>0.
                                                        \tag{2.9}
\]

Remove the auxiliary prime `ell`, the finitely many primes of bad
reduction, and exceptional constants for the local branch formulas; this does not change the
density or the Chebotarev description up to finite differences.
These are `P_Kum`. Their Frobenius fixes `B`, hence the primes split
completely there, while `tau` enforces `p=2 mod3`.

At a chosen prime above such `p`, Frobenius is the identity on
`E_0[ell]`, fixes all `ell`-division points of `P,Q`, and translates
every `ell`-division point of `R_+` by a nonzero `t`. Reduction at
`p!=ell` identifies these finite etale fibres with the corresponding
fibres over `F_p`. Therefore

\[
 \bar P,\bar Q\in[\ell]E_0(\mathbb F_p),\qquad
 \bar R_+\notin[\ell]E_0(\mathbb F_p).             \tag{2.10}
\]

The same nonmembership holds for `-bar R_+`. The order-two point
`bar T` is also an `ell`-multiple because `ell` is odd. The map
`Gamma -> E_0(F_p)/ell E_0(F_p)` thus kills `Gamma_0`; its image
has order dividing `I`, by (1.4), and also a power of `ell`.
Condition (2.4) forces the image to be zero. All of `Gamma_red`
therefore lies in `ell E_0(F_p)`, proving (2.1). This finite-index
argument is essential: division of two independent generators alone
would not suffice if the auxiliary prime divided their index.

Finally, if `p` divided the integer in (2.2), then `p` could not
divide `c`, because `gcd(a,c)=1` and `p!=2`. Thus `x` would be
integral at `p` and reduce to `25865/128`. The reduced curve equation
would put the rational point in one of the two branch residues,
contradicting (2.1). If `p` divides the denominator `c`, the same
coprimality already gives `128a-25865c^2=128a!=0 modp`.
This proves the numerator assertion also for points with denominators.
\(\square\)

## 3. The actual local sieve is pole-only on this set

**Corollary EKX2 (positive-density pole-only primes).** For
`p in P_Kum`, every rational point outside the finite excluded base
points has `v_p(F)<=0`. Its matching fibre is locally soluble at
`p` if and only if either it does not reduce to `O`, or its formal
depth at `O` is divisible by three.

Indeed, positive valuation would require one of the excluded branch
residues. Off `O` the valuation is zero. At `O`, a point of formal
depth `n>=1` has `v_p(F)=-2n`, so ELV1 is exactly `3|n`.
The same residue support exclusion holds in the closure of the
rational image, since reduction has finite image.

In the notation of
[ETP4--ETP5](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_TRIANGULAR_PRIME_SIEVE.md), after
also removing its fixed initial prime cutoff, the exact predictable
hazard at such a prime is consequently

\[
 h_p=b_p\,\mathbf1_{\{\operatorname{red}_p=O\}},\qquad
 b_p=1-\frac{p^{2-\delta_p}}{p^2+p+1},\qquad
 \delta_p=3\lceil e_p/3\rceil-e_p.                \tag{3.1}
\]

No finite zero-branch offset contributes. In particular, fix any
existing feasible prefix `S,a` and enlarge the triangular prime cutoff
to include `S` and its finite local quotient restrictions. For an EKX
prime beyond this cutoff, (3.1) remains the `F_<p` conditional failure
kernel on the event imposing that prefix and any preceding tests.
There is no assertion that the conditional pole-return probability
is positive or bounded below. The earlier
[EPE1--EPE2 eligibility tests](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_PRIME_ELIGIBILITY.md)
allow numerator returns here: complete splitting in `B` makes both
`d` and each `a in {1,2,5q_0,10q_0}` locally square. EKX1 shows that
these quadratic conditions do not guarantee occurrence in the actual
rational reduction image.

More quantitatively, let `N_a` be the set of active rational primes
at which some rational point in class `a` has `v_p(F)>0`.
EPE2 gives the containing numerator-eligible Chebotarev set density
`rho_a=1/4` for `a=1` and `rho_a=1/8` for the other three classes.
The common subset `P_Kum` is disjoint from every `N_a`. Hence

\[
 \overline{\operatorname{dens}}(\mathcal N_a)
              \le \rho_a-\delta_{\rm Kum}<\rho_a.             \tag{3.2}
\]

Here the bar denotes upper natural prime density. No existence of
a natural density for the occurring numerator-prime support is
asserted; the strict gap follows by subtracting the known-density
excluded subset from its known-density eligible superset.
This prime set has positive density for one fixed auxiliary division
field. The theorem gives neither its density within all primes with
the exclusion property nor any independence across different
auxiliary fields. It does not prove that pole returns recur, that
`e_p=1` on a specified set, that the pathwise hazard sum diverges,
or that an all-places fibre count is infinite or has zero density.
No numerical value of (2.9) is claimed. The proof uses the retained
Kummer and Chebotarev interfaces, rather than a finite search or a
new family of moving factors; no artificial finite checker is added.

## 4. Every feasible smaller-prime prefix extends at these primes

**Corollary EKX3 (no new finite obstruction at an EKX maximum).**
Let `p_*` be a triangular cutoff as in ETP1, and let
`p in P_Kum`, `p>p_*`. For every finite set
`S subset {q prime:q<p}` and every `a in A`,

\[
 \boxed{\mu_{S\cup\{p\},a}\ \ge
       s_{p,e_p}\,\mu_{S,a},\qquad
       s_{p,e_p}=\frac{p^{2-\delta_p}}{p^2+p+1}>0.}           \tag{4.1}
\]

In particular every feasible smaller-prime prefix extends positively
at this prime, for every formal depth `e_p`; the assertion does not
assume `e_p=1` and does not depend on a lower bound for the starting
positive coefficient.

**Proof.** The class restriction and all tests in `S` are measurable
in the lower-primary sigma-algebra `F_<p`, by the choice of the
triangular cutoff. EKX2 and ETP4 give conditional local survival
`1` off the pole residue and `s_{p,e_p}` at the pole. Both values
are at least `s_{p,e_p}`. Integrating this bound over the
class-`a` event satisfying `S` gives (4.1). Correlations among
those earlier tests are retained throughout. \(\square\)

Call a finite prime set `S` an inclusion-minimal class-`a` obstruction
when `mu_{S,a}=0` and `mu_{S',a}>0` for every proper subset
`S'` of `S`. If its largest prime exceeds `p_*`, that largest prime
cannot belong to `P_Kum`: remove it, apply (4.1) to the remaining
feasible prefix, and obtain a contradiction. This conclusion only
restricts where a minimal obstruction can end. It does not eliminate
minimal obstructions whose largest prime lies outside `P_Kum`, and
does not turn successive one-prime extension statements into an
all-primes soluble set of positive measure.

## 5. Compatibility with any fixed division data unramified at three

**Corollary EKX4 (exclusion after a prescribed finite splitting condition).**
Let `C/Q` be any fixed finite Galois extension unramified at three,
and put `K_C=BC`. There is a positive-density Chebotarev set of
rational primes which split completely in `C`, are two modulo
three, and satisfy every conclusion of EKX1--EKX3. In particular
neither finite zero is supported by any rational reduction at these
primes.

**Proof.** The nondegeneracy of `(P,Q,R_+)` is geometric, so it
continues to hold over `K_C`. Reapply the same Kummer interface
over that field and choose

\[
 \ell>\max(c_C,3),\qquad\ell\nmid I,
\]

where `c_C` is its index bound and `I` is the fixed index (1.4).
Use

\[
 M_C=K_C(E_0[\ell],[\ell]^{-1}P,[\ell]^{-1}Q,
                                          [\ell]^{-1}R_+).
\]

Its kernel over `K_C(E_0[ell])` is `E_0[ell]^3`.
The field `K_C/Q` is Galois, so the same argument proves
`M_C/Q` Galois and proves conjugacy stability of the `ell^2-1`
translations `(0,0,t!=0)`. Since `K_C` is still unramified at
three, the good-reduction division argument gives
`M_C cap Q(zeta_3)=Q`. Chebotarev applied to these translations
and the nontrivial action on `zeta_3` yields the density

\[
 \delta_C=\frac{\ell^2-1}{2[M_C:\mathbb Q]}>0.                 \tag{5.1}
\]

All such Frobenius elements fix `K_C`, and therefore `C`. The
remaining reduction and finite-index arguments of EKX1 are
unchanged. EKX2--EKX3 then apply after their fixed initial cutoff.
\(\square\)

For this fixed `C`, the primes splitting `K_C` and two modulo
three themselves have density `1/(2[K_C:Q])`. The constructed
subset consequently has exact positive relative density

\[
 \frac{\delta_C}{1/(2[K_C:\mathbb Q])}
    =\frac{\ell^2-1}{\ell^6[K_C(E_0[\ell]):K_C]}.              \tag{5.2}
\]

There is no uniformity in `C`, its Kummer bound, the chosen auxiliary
prime or this relative density.

For example, `C` may be a prescribed finite Galois compositum of
torsion and division fields on this good-at-three curve whose
multipliers are prime to three: Milne AVs Corollary 20.8 verifies
the required unramifiedness. Including `Q(E_0[m])` for `3` prime
to `m` also imposes `p=1 mod m`: this torsion field contains
`mu_m` by the nondegenerate Galois-equivariant Weil pairing and
canonical elliptic selfduality. The exact retained pairing interface
is Milne AVs, section 16 opening, printed/PDF p.29. This example
imposes full torsion splitting, which is stronger than only the
congruence.

The unramifiedness hypothesis cannot simply be dropped. If `C`
contains `Q(zeta_3)`, complete splitting in `C` forces
`p=1 mod3`, which is incompatible with the desired active primes.
In particular the full third-division field contains `zeta_3` by
the same Weil pairing and is not covered by this corollary. No claim is
made that unramifiedness at three is necessary for every individual
extension, or that arbitrary finite-place prefix conditions can
be encoded by the permitted splitting fields. This fixed-division-field
argument provides no distribution bound for the varying-residue-prime
formal depths `e_p,k_p`.
