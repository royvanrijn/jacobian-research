# The exact joint sieve at two, three, five and seventeen

Adding the complete five-adic test to the existing joint sieve changes
only the cyclotomic quadratic class. Its coefficient is multiplied by
`16/31`; the other three class coefficients remain unchanged. The
total retained coefficient is `2300089/10392564`. The finite component
at five is correlated with the two-adic data, so its separate local
density is not multiplied into the earlier total.

## 1. The finite group at five

Use the curve and exact points of
[J2171](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_JOINT_2_3_17_SIEVE.md):

\[
 E_0:y^2=x^3-10656x^2+27091730x,
 \qquad P,\ S,\ T=(0,0),\ R=P+T.
\]

Write `E_0(5)` for the subgroup of points with nonsingular reduction,
and `E_n(5)` for the formal subgroups. The integer translation
`X=x-3552` identifies these with the corresponding subgroups of the
integral short model used in the other notes.

**Lemma J235171 (the five-adic finite quotient and formal image).**
One has

\[
 E(\mathbb Q_5)/E_1(5)\simeq C_4\times C_2,
\]

with generators the images of `P,T`, respectively. The point `S`
has exact formal depth one and generates the full formal group in
its closed integer span. Consequently the rational five-adic closure
is all of `E(Q_5)`. In the coordinates `mR+nS+cT`, the finite
quotient coordinates are

\[
 (m\bmod4,\ m+c\bmod2).                             \tag{1.1}
\]

In particular this point is in the formal disc precisely when
`m=0 mod4` and `c=0`.

**Proof.** Reduction gives `y^2=x^2(x-1)`. Its nonsingular points
are

\[
 O,\quad(1,0),\quad(2,2),\quad(2,3),
\]

a cyclic group of order four generated by `bar P=(2,2)`.
Its double is `(1,0)`. The reduction theorem gives
`E_0(5)/E_1(5)=C_4`.

Here is a direct proof that there are exactly two component cosets.
Put `b=27091730`, so `v_5(b)=1`. Points with negative `x` valuation
are formal, and points with unit `x` have nonsingular reduction.
For a point with `k=v_5(x)>0`, translation by `T` gives

\[
 x(Q+T)=b/x(Q).
\]

If `k=1`, this coordinate is a unit. If `k>=2`, the term `b`
uniquely has smallest valuation in `x^2-10656x+b`. The curve
equation makes `k+1` even, so `k>=3` is odd; the translated
coordinate has negative valuation `1-k`. Thus every point with
singular reduction belongs to `T+E_0(5)`, including `T` itself.
Since `T` has order two and is not in `E_0(5)`,
`E(Q_5)=E_0(5) direct-sum <T>`. This proves the finite quotient
without a reduction-type classification.

The exact coordinates of `S` give

\[
 v_5(x(S))=-2,\quad v_5(t(S))=1,\quad
 t(S)/5=1\pmod5,\qquad t=-X/y.                     \tag{1.2}
\]

Its closed span is therefore `E_1(5)` by the formal filtration.
Together with `P,T`, this gives the whole local group. Since
`R=P+T` and `S` is formal, the finite coordinates are (1.1).
\(\square\)

The primary group input is Milne, *Elliptic Curves* (2006),
Chapter II, Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 5.9, printed pp.62--63 and 67:
[primary book](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/ectext6.pdf),
[retained copy](sources/milne-elliptic-curves-2006.pdf).
The local root test
[ETF3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_TWO_FIVE_ADIC_CLASSIFICATION.md) says that
all nonformal points are soluble at five. In `E_1(5)` exactly the
depths divisible by three are soluble. Their conditional Haar mass is

\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}(1-5^{-1})5^{1-3k}=\frac1{31}.         \tag{1.3}
\]

## 2. The joint image retains one finite correlation

**Lemma J235172 (joint five-adic refinement).** Conditional on the
global class `a=1` and local solubility at `2,3,17`, the five-adic
formal disc has probability `1/2`. Its formal coordinate is independent
of the earlier data. The other three global classes never meet the
formal five-adic disc and are automatically soluble at five.

**Proof.** The known subgroup `<R,S,T>` has odd index by ETF1.
It therefore gives every actual finite two-primary image, now including
the finite quotient at five. At two choose the normalized additive
coordinate `u` on `E_4(2)`. Its coefficients have the form

\[
 u=a_Rm+a_Sn,\qquad a_R,a_S\in\mathbb Z_2^*.
\]

The two coefficients are units because `R,S` both have depth four.
The change from `(m,n)` to `(m,u)` is an automorphism of `Z_2^2`.
In these variables the old two-primary data depend only on
`u,m mod2,c`: the residue at three is `cA+(n mod2)B`, with
`n mod2=u-m mod2`; the seventeen-adic `C_2` coordinate is `m+c`;
and the global class is

\[
 a=(5q_0)^{m\bmod2}(10q_0)^c,\qquad q_0=2709173.
\]

Thus `m mod4` remains uniform among its two lifts after fixing its
parity and all the old data. Class one has `m` even and `c=0`.
By (1.1) exactly one of those two lifts, `m=0 mod4`, is formal
at five. For each of the other three classes either `m` is odd or
`c=1`, so the formal disc is impossible.

The higher three-primary and seventeen-primary parts of the old
image are those proved in J2171. Five adds no new finite component
of either order. Its new five-primary part is full by (1.2).
At every finite level, integer Chinese remainder approximation in
the coefficients of `R,S,T` realizes the product of these different
primary images. The two-primary image is already exact by the
odd-index assertion, the three- and seventeen-primary images are
already exact by J2171, and the five-primary image is full. Hence
the actual rational group cannot enlarge any of them.

This proves independence of the formal five-primary coordinate
from the earlier data while preserving the finite correlation
(1.1). It is not an inference from the separate local marginals.
\(\square\)

## 3. Exact coefficients and the Hasse upper bound

**Theorem J235173 (the complete four-prime coefficient).** Let
`M_(2,3,5,17;a)(B)` count admissible full field targets of class `a`
on the existing elliptic slice, soluble at these four primes. Then

\[
 M_{2,3,5,17;a}(B)=c_a C_E\log B+O_E(\sqrt{\log B}),
                                                               \tag{3.1}
\]

where the coefficients are normalized relative to the whole slice:

| Class `a` | Quadratic field | `c_a` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1` | `Q(sqrt(-3))` | `820/866047` |
| `2` | `Q(sqrt(-6))` | `205/2456` |
| `5q_0` | `Q(sqrt(-40637595))` | `1/84` |
| `10q_0` | `Q(sqrt(-81275190))` | `1/8` |

Their sum is

\[
 \boxed{M_{2,3,5,17}(B)=\frac{2300089}{10392564}C_E\log B
                                      +O_E(\sqrt{\log B}).}    \tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently the all-places Hasse count satisfies

\[
 \boxed{A_E(B)\le\frac{2300089}{10392564}C_E\log B
                                      +O_E(\sqrt{\log B}),}    \tag{3.3}
\]

and its class-one subcount has upper coefficient `820/866047`
at the same error order.

**Proof.** In class one the five-adic retention factor is, by
(1.3) and J235172,

\[
 \frac12+\frac12\cdot\frac1{31}=\frac{16}{31}.
\]

Multiply the old class-one coefficient `205/111748` of J2174 by
this factor. All other coefficients of J2174 remain unchanged.
The loss from their old total is

\[
 \frac{205}{111748}\frac{15}{31}=\frac{3075}{3464188},
\]

giving the displayed sum. Apply
[ESQ3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_SHARP_FINITE_LOCAL_ERROR.md) for the
square-root logarithmic error in each fixed finite-place class and
sum the four counts. Every Hasse target passes these four tests,
giving (3.3). \(\square\)

The exact coefficient is for this finite prefix, not an all-places
asymptotic. It leaves the simultaneous tests at the remaining primes
and the Haar-limit/tail obligation of
[EFA3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_FINITE_LOCAL_ASYMPTOTICS.md) unresolved.
The certified class still has coefficient `1/8`; five is automatic
in that class. The new class-one coefficient remains positive,
consistent with [ECO1](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_FINITE_LOCAL_CLASS_ONE.md).
