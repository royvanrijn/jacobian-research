# A uniform obstruction to preserving one leaf during Hurwitz induction

A Hurwitz algorithm cannot always keep a chosen sheet as a leaf while
trying to give its DT factor a second leaf. Deleting that chosen sheet
produces a lower-degree polynomial tuple with one transposition.
Its generated group is invariant under every elementary move that
preserves the chosen leaf. If this lower group is imprimitive, the
desired second leaf is impossible under that restriction.

There is an explicit full-alternating counterfamily in every odd
polynomial degree at least seven. Initially it has no removable DT.
A short unrestricted braid does extract two leaves, but necessarily
passes through a stage where the chosen sheet occurs in two factors.
This is an obstruction to a restricted induction strategy, rather
than a distinction between unrestricted Hurwitz orbits.

## The chosen-leaf deletion

Consider a polynomial tuple of DTs on N=2r+1 symbols with full-cycle
product P. A chosen symbol x is a leaf when exactly one tuple entry
moves it. Write that unique entry as

\[
                         A=(x\,u)e,\qquad e=(v\,w),
\]

with its four symbols distinct. All other entries fix x.
Delete x by replacing A with e and retaining all other entries,
now as permutations of the remaining N-1 symbols. Denote this
tuple by D_x.

Its expanded graph is the tree obtained by deleting the leaf x,
so its product is a full cycle on those N-1 symbols. In fact that
product is obtained by deleting x from P, independently of the
position of A in the tuple.

To verify the latter assertion write P=L A R, with L,R fixing x.
Then

\[
 P=(x\,L(u))\,Q,\qquad Q=L e R,\qquad Q(x)=x.
\]

Thus L(u)=P(x), and Q=(x P(x))P. Its restriction is precisely P
with x omitted from its cyclic order.

## Lemma 1. Deletion commutes with every leaf-preserving elementary move

If a positive or negative elementary Hurwitz move retains x as a
leaf, its deletion is the same elementary move on D_x.
Consequently the generated lower subgroup is literally invariant
along any sequence that keeps x a leaf at every intermediate tuple.

**Proof.** Moves between entries fixing x commute with deletion
immediately. Let B fix x and use
Q(a,b)=(b,b^-1ab). Since all upper entries are DTs, inverses in
the following formulas may be replaced by the same involutions.

For (A,B), a positive move gives
(B,B^-1 A B). The unique x entry is still the second factor and
has the decomposition

\[
 B^{-1}AB=(x\,B^{-1}u)\,(B^{-1}eB).
\]

Deletion therefore gives the positive move on (e,B).

For (A,B), a negative move gives (ABA,A). It retains x as a leaf
exactly when B fixes u. In that case B commutes with (x u), as
does e, and hence ABA=eBe. Deletion gives (eBe,e), the required
negative move on (e,B).

For the reversed pair (B,A), the positive move retains the leaf
exactly when B fixes u, and the negative move always retains it.
The same two calculations give the corresponding positive and
negative moves on (B,e). This covers all elementary cases. ∎

Simultaneous relabeling carries the lower group to a conjugate.
Thus its imprimitivity is also invariant if sheet labels are
reassigned while the chosen leaf is carried along as part of
the data.

## Theorem 2. An imprimitive deletion blocks this strategy

If the lower group generated by D_x is imprimitive, no sequence
of elementary Hurwitz moves keeping x a leaf at every stage can
make its unique DT have two exclusive leaf ends.

**Proof.** Lemma1 preserves the lower group. If that DT acquired
a second leaf y on its other edge, the remaining transposition e
in D_x would itself have leaf y. The other lower entries all fix y;
their product is a full (N-2)-cycle on the remaining symbols,
fixing y. Thus the transitive lower group would contain a cycle
of type (N-2,1).

A transitive group of degree M containing an (M-1)-cycle is
primitive. Indeed, the block containing that cycle's fixed point
is invariant under the cycle. If it contains any other point,
it contains the entire long orbit and is the whole set. Otherwise
it is a singleton, forcing all blocks to be singletons. Apply
this with M=N-1 to obtain a contradiction. ∎

The theorem concerns the DT containing the chosen x. It does not
prevent a different color from becoming removable, a different
leaf from being selected, or a path that temporarily spreads x
across two factors.

## Theorem 3. Full-alternating counterfamilies in every degree

For r>=3 put N=2r+1, x=2r, u=2r-2, and define

\[
 A=(x\,u)(0\,1),\qquad
 B_i=(2i-2\,\,2i)(2i-1\,\,2i+1),\quad1\le i\le r-1.
\tag{1}
\]

Then the tuple (A,B_1,...,B_(r-1)) has full-cycle product and
generates A_N. It initially has no removable DT. Deleting x
has the full imprimitive group

\[
                             C_2\wr S_r.
\tag{2}
\]

Therefore Theorem2 applies uniformly, including r=3.

**Proof.** The transposition edges are exactly the path

\[
 x,\ 2r-2,\ 2r-4,\ldots,0,\ 1,\ 3,\ldots,2r-1.
\]

The tree-product lemma gives a full-cycle product. Its only
leaves are x and 2r-1, in distinct factors A and B_(r-1).
No factor initially has both leaves.

The generated group is transitive. In odd degree a nontrivial
block size is at least three. A DT moving blocks would move at
least two such blocks, hence at least six symbols. Every generator
would instead have to fix every block setwise, contradicting
transitivity. Thus the group is primitive.

Let v=2r-4. The supports of A and B_(r-1) intersect only in u,
and direct multiplication gives

\[
                          (AB_{r-1})^2=(x\,v\,u).
\tag{3}
\]

This is a 3-cycle fixing at least four symbols, even when r=3.
Jordan's prime-cycle theorem gives A_N; every generator is even,
so the group is exactly A_N. The imported formulation is already
retained in the
[primary-source ledger](alternating_hurwitz_source_ledger.json).

After deleting x, A becomes (01). The r blocks

\[
                      \{0,1\},\{2,3\},\ldots,\{2r-2,2r-1\}
\]

are preserved as a block system. The B_i induce the adjacent
transpositions of those blocks, generating a diagonal S_r.
Conjugating (01) by its block permutations supplies the independent
flips within every block. They generate C_2^r, and together with
S_r generate the whole wreath product (2). Conversely all original
lower generators belong to that wreath product. This proves the
exact lower group and its transitive imprimitivity. ∎

## Proposition 4. A leaf-handoff braid escapes the restriction

For seven distinct labels there is a two-move identity

\[
 Q^{-2}\bigl((x\,u)(ab),(u\,v)(cd)\bigr)
                  =\bigl((u\,v)(ab),(x\,v)(cd)\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

If all other entries fix x, then x is a leaf before and after
this replacement, but both intermediate factors move x after
the first inverse move. Its leaf color has been handed to
the other branch.

For the counterfamily (1), the explicit word

\[
             (-1,-2,\ldots,-(r-2),-(r-1),-(r-1))
\tag{5}
\]

has length r and makes one DT removable, with leaf ends x and
2r-1. It necessarily violates the leaf-preserving restriction.

**Proof.** One inverse move gives
((x v)(cd),(x u)(ab)); a second gives (4).

In (5), the first r-2 inverse moves bring A to the right unchanged.
Each crossed B_i fixes u, so these moves preserve the chosen leaf.
The other entries become A B_i A. All still fix both x and the
opposite leaf 2r-1.

The final pair is A,B_(r-1). Set v=2r-4, c=2r-3 and y=2r-1.
Its seven symbols x,u,v,0,1,c,y are distinct for r>=3.
Applying (4) gives

\[
                         (u\,v)(01),\qquad(x\,v)(c\,y).
\]

Only the last factor moves x or y, so it has two exclusive leaf
ends. At the intermediate step x is moved by both factors.
This proves the claimed escape and explains why it does not
contradict Theorem2. ∎

The lower group after the escape is S_(2r): it contains the
single transposition left by deleting x, and the other factors
give a (2r-1)-cycle fixing the second leaf. Hence it is primitive;
a primitive group containing a transposition is symmetric.
For completeness, conjugates of that transposition define a
nonempty invariant edge graph. Its connected components are
blocks, so primitivity makes it connected, and edge transpositions
of a connected graph generate the full symmetric group.

Thus the unrestricted braid changes the lower deletion group
from C_2 wr S_r to S_(2r). That change is impossible while the
chosen leaf is retained at every elementary step.

## Verification and the remaining frontier

The [checker](verify_fixed_leaf_hurwitz_obstruction.py) verifies
deletion equivariance for all allowed elementary moves and all
leaf choices in the complete degree-seven DT domain. It also
checks the uniform family, its exact block generators, the
3-cycle certificate, absence of an initially removable factor,
and every stage of the escape (5) in retained controls.

The [receipt](fixed_leaf_hurwitz_obstruction_verification.json)
and [source ledger](fixed_leaf_hurwitz_obstruction_source_ledger.json)
pin the exact inputs. The lower wreath groups in the first
controls are certified by complete finite group closure as
well as the written all-degree group argument.

This does not distinguish unrestricted full-alternating braid
orbits or prove arbitrary-tree leaf extraction. It rules out
a specific natural restriction on such a proof and supplies
a concrete move that any more flexible strategy may use.
The global extraction and extension-connectivity questions
remain open in this work.
