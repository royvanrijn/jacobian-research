# Primitive discriminant -23 norms: class obstructions and pointwise sums

Let
\[
 Q_0(x,y)=x^2+xy+6y^2,\qquad
 P=\{Q_0(x,y)>0:\gcd(x,y)=1\},
\]
and let \(R_P(N)=\#\{(a,b)\in P^2:a+b=N\}\) count ordered value pairs.
Put \(\chi=\chi_{-23}\) and
\[
 J_{-23}(N)=\prod_{\substack{p\mid N\\p\ne23,\ \chi(p)=-1}}
                                      \frac{p-1}{p-2}.
\]
Here 2 is split, so every prime in the product is odd.

**Theorem CN3.** There are fixed constants \(c_1,c_2>0,N_0\) such that
for every \(N\ge N_0\) with \(529\nmid N\),
\[
 c_1\frac{N J_{-23}(N)}{\log N}
    \le R_P(N)\le c_2\frac{N J_{-23}(N)}{\log N}.           \tag{CN3.1}
\]
For every positive multiple of 529 the count is exactly zero. Thus all
sufficiently large primitive-local admissible targets have the full
quadratic-semigroup logarithmic counting scale. No numerical cutoff,
unweighted leading constant or complete unrestricted binary exception
classification is provided.

The individual primitive norm set has genuine class-group obstructions
invisible to local tests. The proof below bypasses those obstructions in
an additive lower bound using four fixed primes and exact valuations.
The analytic inputs remain the existing quadratic splitting-semigroup
proof, its fixed-period extension and its finite-predicate unweighting.
No nonabelian prime-distribution estimate is used. These are internally
reviewed deductions with exact finite certificates, without formal
verification, external review or an established priority claim.

## Individual primitive norms when the ideal class group is C3

**Lemma CN3Individual.** Let a maximal imaginary quadratic order have
ideal class group \(C_3\). For a positive integer \(n\), let \(s\) be the
number of distinct split prime factors. Let \(r\) count those nonprincipal
split primes \(p\mid n\) for which \(3\nmid v_p(n)\). Then \(n\) is a
primitive principal norm if and only if no inert prime divides it, every
ramified valuation is at most one, and \(r\ne1\). When the first two
conditions hold, its exact signed coordinate representation count is
\[
             R^*(n)=2^{s-r+1}\frac{2^r+2(-1)^r}{3}.        \tag{CN3.2}
\]
It is zero if either prime-factor condition fails.

**Proof.** Primitive ideal factorization must orient each entire split
prime power to one of its two conjugate prime ideals. Choosing orientations
independently for copies of the same prime would permit rational-prime
divisibility and is incorrect. Inert primes are excluded; a ramified
exponent at least two also puts the generator in a rational prime ideal.
A ramified ideal class has order dividing two, hence is trivial in \(C_3\).
A nonprincipal split power contributes zero class when its exponent is
zero modulo three, and otherwise permits the two signs \(\pm1\).

There are zero-sum choices for \(r=0\), none for \(r=1\), opposite pairs
for even \(r\ge2\), and a triple of equal signs followed by opposite pairs
for odd \(r\ge3\). The number of zero-sum sign choices is
\(\{2^r+2(-1)^r\}/3\), by the character filter on \(C_3\).
The other \(s-r\) split powers have two class-zero orientations each.
Different orientations give different ideals, and every principal ideal
has two generators. The unit group has order two since the two imaginary
quadratic fields with extra units have class number one. This proves
(CN3.2), including \(n=1\), and the membership criterion. \(\square\)

For discriminant \(-23\) this class group can be calculated directly.
Write \(\omega=(1+\sqrt{-23})/2\), so its polynomial is
\(T^2-T+6\). Minkowski's bound
\((2/\pi)\sqrt{23}<4\) leaves only ideal representatives of norms 1,2,3.
Both 2 and 3 split, with
\(\mathfrak p_2=(2,\omega)\), \(\mathfrak p_3=(3,\omega)\), and
\[
 (\omega)=\mathfrak p_2\mathfrak p_3,\qquad
 (1+\omega)=\overline{\mathfrak p}_2^3.
\]
There is no element of norm 2, because
\(4Q_0=(2x+y)^2+23y^2\). Thus \([\mathfrak p_2]\) has exact order
three, \([\mathfrak p_3]=-[\mathfrak p_2]\), and the Minkowski
representatives exhaust the three classes. The ramified prime is
principal, generated by \(2\omega-1\) of norm 23. The exact primary
Minkowski and ideal/form correspondence statements are Milne, v3.08,
Theorems 4.3 and 4.29; their source edition is retained in the ledger.
The usual reduced forms are \((1,1,6),(2,1,3),(2,-1,3)\).

For this field the individual primitive local criterion is precisely
absence of inert factors and \(v_{23}(n)\le1\). At 2 the algebra is split.
At 23 the unit part must be a square; a product of split primes has that
property since \(\chi(p)=(p/23)\), including \(p=2\). Squares of units
lift over \(\mathbb Z_{23}\), and multiplication by \(\sqrt{-23}\)
handles valuation one. All other unit local norms lift as usual.
Consequently
\[
       2^{3a+1},\ 2^{3a+2}\quad(a\ge0)                    \tag{CN3.3}
\]
are locally admissible individual norms with no primitive global
representation. This is an individual-value obstruction, not an eventual
additive obstruction.

## Four explicit primes absorb every remaining class contribution

Take
\[
                  F=3\cdot13\cdot29\cdot31=35061.
\]
All four primes are split and nonprincipal. The nonprincipal form
\(Q_1=2x^2+xy+3y^2\) represents them at
\((0,1),(2,1),(-2,3),(-4,1)\). Their principal nonrepresentation is a
complete small check: \(4Q_0=(2x+y)^2+23y^2\) leaves only \(|y|\le2\)
for primes at most 31. At \(|y|=1\), the possible square values
\(4p-23\) are \(-11,29,93,101\); none is a square. At \(|y|=2\),
the only candidates require squares 6 or 8. At \(y=0\) a prime is
not a square. The checker also verifies splitting and the displayed
\(Q_1\) witnesses independently.

If an integer with no inert factor and ramified valuation at most one
has two of these distinct primes to exponent exactly one, its \(r\) in
(CN3.2) is at least two. It is therefore a primitive principal norm,
regardless of every other split-prime class and exponent. This is the
fixed-prime absorption property used here. Mere divisibility does not
suffice: \(351=3^3\cdot13\) has just one active nonprincipal prime
power and is not a primitive norm.

Let \(C\) contain 1 and integers supported on odd split primes; 2 and
23 are excluded from this analytic core. Choose squarefree \(a_0,b_0\mid F\),
each a product of two distinct primes. Require residual cofactors \(x,y\in C\)
to satisfy \((xy,F)=1\). Then both
\[
                        a_0x,\qquad t b_0y\quad(t=1,2)     \tag{CN3.4}
\]
are primitive principal norms: the four assigned odd exponents remain
exactly one. A factor 2 may add an active prime but cannot remove either
of the two fixed ones.

The restriction to valuations zero and one at 2 in (CN3.4) is used only
for a lower-bound subset. It is not a classification of \(P\): for
example \(2\notin P\) while \(8\in P\), and arbitrary valuations of
the split prime 2 occur.

## The quadratic core estimate and the finite-profile extension

For \(L=46\), \(H=\ker\chi\subset(\mathbb Z/L)^*\), put
\(V=\tau\mathbf1_C\) and use normalized shifted families
\(F_B(m)=V(Bm)/V(B)\), \(G_D(l)=V(Dl)/V(D)\).
For \(t=1\), the weighted line estimate is literally the
[existing subgroup theorem](ASYMMETRIC_SUBGROUP_SPLITTING.md), (H2),
with index two. For \(t=2\), the fixed-coefficient extension is the same
explicit transfer as [CN2Filter](PRIMITIVE_NORM_CLASS_NUMBER_TWO.md),
with \(L=46\), period 92 and no genus labels:
\[
 w(m)=\mathbf1_{2\mid M-um}\,
  \mathbf1_{((M-um)/2,46)=1,\ \chi((M-um)/2)=1}.
                                                               \tag{CN3.5}
\]
Its support enforces the 2-part of \(um\equiv M\pmod{2vka}\), so the
varying modulus is just \(vka\), prime to 46. Expand the filter modulo
92 on the first unit variable. The two character factors, bounded kernel
norms and retained smooth scalar phases are exactly the existing ones.
Kernel indices need only be units of character value one, not integers
with split prime support. The principal twists reassemble by the
pointwise identity \(F_B\xi=F_B\) for \(\xi\in\{1,\chi\}\).

Thus the old BGLM and fixed-period box inputs and their (H17)--(H18)
error hierarchy apply unchanged up to fixed constants. For both \(t=1,2\)
there are fixed \(\eta,\theta>0\), with \(\theta\) chosen last, giving
uniform power-error weighted estimates in all good shifts and coefficients
\(B,D,u,v\in C\) at most \(M^\theta\), all coefficient gcds and all
target valuations. Small exact-divisibility/valuation predicates remain
covered after decreasing the power budget. No new distribution estimate
at an even varying modulus is assumed.

In full, the weighted main is
\[
 \sum_{\substack{um+tvl=M\\m,l\ge1}}F_B(m)G_D(l)
 =\frac{M}{utv}\Gamma_{t;u,v}(M)\rho_{F_B}\rho_{G_D}
       \prod_{p\nmid46}\frac{D_p}{M_{A,p}M_{B,p}}
                  +O(M^{1-\eta}).                         \tag{CN3.5a}
\]
The good-prime radial factors have exactly the definitions in (H3)--(H4)
on the line \(ux+tvy=M\); they retain any coefficient/gcd zero factors.
At a good prime the unit multiplier \(t\) does not change valuations.

The fixed density, including coefficients, is the actual finite count
\[
 \Gamma_{t;u,v}(M)=\frac{tL}{|H_{tL}|^2}
       \#\{(a,b)\in H_{tL}^2:ua+tvb=M\pmod{tL}\}.
\]

Allowed coefficients permute \(H_{tL}\), so this is independent of
\(u,v\). The real normalization is \(M/(utv)\), as in CN2.4--CN2.8.
For even \(M\) at \(t=1\), or odd \(M\) at \(t=2\), and \(23\nmid M\),
\[
 \Gamma_t(M)=\frac{46}{121}s(M),\qquad
 s(M)=\frac{22-2(M/23)}4\in\{5,6\}.                       \tag{CN3.6}
\]
Indeed \(s(M)\) counts ordered nonzero square residues whose sum is
\(M\pmod{23}\); 2 is itself a square modulo 23. The parity restriction
accounts for the factor 46, and the two second-variable lifts at period
92 cancel the larger denominator. For \(23\mid M\) the density is zero.

We need the unweighted estimate with a fixed local block, not merely the
weighted main. Prescribe at every \(q\mid F\) a pair of exact exponents
\((\epsilon_q,\delta_q)\in\{0,1\}^2\) for the two core variables on
\(m+t l=M\). On this line the weighted local masses are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{profile}&\text{target condition}&\text{mass}\\\hline
 (0,0)&q\nmid M&(q-2)/q\\
 (0,0)&q\mid M&(q-1)/q\\
 (1,0)\text{ or }(0,1)&q\nmid M&2(q-1)/q^2\\
 (1,1)&v_q(M)=1&4(q-2)/q^2\\
 (1,1)&v_q(M)\ge2&4(q-1)/q^2.
 \end{array}                                               \tag{CN3.7}
\]
All other listed-profile/target combinations have zero mass. These
identities follow by choosing the first residue modulo \(q^2\), with
the second determined by the line. Exact exponent one has local
weight \(V(q)=2\). In the common case write both values as \(q\)
times units; the first-variable measure contributes \(1/q\), followed
by exclusion of two or one residue classes. The coefficient \(t\) is a
\(q\)-adic unit. For each allowed case the mass is uniformly positive
in the target valuation, including arbitrary depth.

**Lemma CN3Profile.** For a fixed profile whose local masses (CN3.7)
and fixed density (CN3.6) are positive, its ordered unweighted core-pair
count is comparable to \(M J_{-23}(M)/\log M\), for every sufficiently
large allowed target, uniformly in those target valuations.

**Proof.** Every exact profile predicate is a finite combination of
divisibility predicates covered by the shifted weighted family above.
Equivalently factor \(m=a_0x,l=b_0y\), with \(a_0,b_0\) the products
of assigned exponent-one primes, and impose \((xy,F)=1\). On this
support the weights factor exactly as
\(V(a_0x)=V(a_0)V(x)\), \(V(b_0y)=V(b_0)V(y)\).
Common coefficients are removed when they divide the target, exactly
as in (H15). All are fixed \(C\)-factors, so finite density is unchanged.

Keep all primes in \(F\) as a fixed local block throughout the positive
finite-block sieve and restoration in section 6 of the subgroup theorem.
Exclude them from every variable roughness/directive/common-cofactor
pool. This preserves their exact exponents after restoring the values;
an initial coprimality condition alone would not suffice. It removes
only finitely many Euler factors, whose masses (CN3.7) have a common
positive lower bound. Outside \(F\), the shifted radial profiles are
ordinary \(V\), with rough dimension two and restored exponent one.
The uniform small-power weighted errors cover the same sieve tests;
all fixed profile products eventually fit the budget. Recoverable
patterns and normalized residual weights are unchanged. This is the
existing positive unweighting argument with a finite fixed block,
not an inference from a weighted asymptotic alone. The target factor
\(J_{-23}\) ignores \(F\) because all four primes split. It follows
that the resulting scale is \(M J_{-23}(M)/\log M\). \(\square\)

## A positive profile for every target

Take a target \(M\) coprime to 23 and of the parity appropriate for
\(t=1\) or 2. Let \(S=\{q\mid F:q\mid M\}\).

* If \(|S|\ge2\), choose two primes from \(S\) as common profile
  \((1,1)\), and leave the others unused \((0,0)\).
* If \(S=\{p\}\), use \(p\) as \((1,1)\), one other prime as
  \((1,0)\), another as \((0,1)\), and leave the fourth unused.
* If \(S\) is empty, assign two primes first-only \((1,0)\) and the
  other two second-only \((0,1)\).

Each side has two distinct active primes. A single-sided prime never
divides the target; a common prime always does. Every unused prime
has positive unit-unit mass whether or not it divides the target.
Thus all local masses are positive, even at 3 and at arbitrarily large
target valuations. Chinese remaindering combines them with (CN3.6).

If \(c=(a_0,b_0)\), then \(c\mid M\), and normalization gives
\(u=a_0/c,v=b_0/c,M'=M/c\). The coefficients are coprime, and every
coefficient prime is coprime to \(M'\). Common primes may divide \(M'\)
to arbitrary depth, already covered by the uniform profile estimates.
Both target parity and \(\chi(M)\) are unchanged by \(c\), because
\(c\) is odd and \(\chi(c)=1\); hence the fixed density remains positive.
All 16 divisibility patterns produce coefficients at most 899.

By Lemma CN3Profile, each selected subset has
\(\gg M J_{-23}(M)/\log M\) pairs. Every pair gives primitive principal
norm values by (CN3.4). There are only 16 templates and two parity
choices: take the maximum cutoff and the minimum positive constant
over this finite family. This proves a uniform lower bound for all
sufficiently large \(23\)-unit targets.

## Ramification, upper bound and scope

A primitive \(23\)-unit value is a nonzero square modulo 23. A value
divisible by 23 has exact valuation one, with square unit quotient.
Since \(-1\) is nonsquare modulo 23, a sum divisible by 23 must have
both values divisible by 23. Dividing them by 23 shows that a sum
cannot have valuation at least two. This proves the obstruction at 529.
It is the only primitive additive local obstruction: at 2 the algebra
is split, and the good-prime local sums and nonzero-square sums at 23
cover every remaining target.

For an admissible \(N\), write \(N=23^e M\), \(e=0,1\), \(23\nmid M\).
The lower-bound construction gives both values coprime to 23. Multiplying
them by \(23^e\), or their generators by \((\sqrt{-23})^e\), preserves
primitivity and gives an injective lift of the ordered value pairs.
This fixed scaling changes constants by bounded factors and leaves
\(J_{-23}\) unchanged.

For the upper bound retain only that no odd inert prime divides either
primitive norm. Drop every class constraint, ramified valuation bound
and restriction at 2. The existing upper block sieve excludes residues
0 and \(N\) at inert primes; they merge precisely at divisors of the
target. It has dimension one and enhancement \((p-1)/(p-2)\), giving
\(\ll N J_{-23}(N)/\log N\). This direct upper bound avoids an
incorrect finite decomposition by the unbounded split 2-adic valuation.
Together with the lower bound it proves Theorem CN3. \(\square\)

The advance over the general pair-primitive quaternary theorem is the
sharp logarithmic order for distinct values in this class-number-three
case. Individual class obstructions persist, as (CN3.3) proves, while
fixed exact prime factors remove them on a sufficient additive subset.
This is a concrete extension of the existing method, not a theorem for
arbitrary class groups, nonmaximal orders or simultaneous norm conditions.

## Exact bounded binary exceptions

**Proposition CN3Finite.** For every \(2\le N\le2^{24}=16777216\),
\[
 N\in P+P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                  529\nmid N\text{ and }N\notin E,
\]
where
\[
\begin{split}
E=\{&
3,4,5,6,8,10,11,15,17,21,22,23,25,43,55,61,69,73,\\
&92,115,138,166,184,230,253,345,391,483,506,575,667,713,\\
&943,989,1150,1265,1403,1679,1863,3818\}.
\end{split}
\]
All 40 integers are actual global nonrepresentations. The assertion that
there are no further exceptions is proved only through the displayed
bound; the unrestricted exact-exception gap remains open.

The [bounded sumset checker](verify_primitive_norm_minus23_sumset.py) and
[receipt](primitive_norm_minus23_sumset_checks.json) establish the full
finite assertion by two primitive membership constructions. For any form
\(a x^2+bxy+cy^2\) of discriminant \(-23\),
\[
                 4aQ=(2ax+by)^2+23y^2.
\]
Simultaneous sign reversal permits \(y\ge0\) for value membership.
For every \(y\le\lfloor\sqrt{4aB/23}\rfloor\), the checker enumerates
the complete integer interval
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{-h-by}{2a}\right\rceil
 \le x\le
 \left\lfloor\frac{h-by}{2a}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 h=\lfloor\sqrt{4aB-23y^2}\rfloor,
\]
retaining positive values with \(\gcd(x,y)=1\).
It independently reconstructs every flag through \(B=2^{24}\)
by the C3 prime-power criterion, with split classes labelled using
the other reduced form. The flags agree everywhere, yielding 2265203
primitive values. Trial factorization through 10000 and a separate
signed square-box enumeration through 1000 challenge the class and
coordinate computations.

The packed flag is shifted by every one of the 876 primitive anchors
at most 5000. Every marked sum is valid. Of the 16745501 locally
admissible targets, 683 remain after this sweep. Each receives a
complete search over \(1\le a\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor\), using
the complete membership flag. The receipt stores 643 explicit residual
witness triples; the remaining 40 are exhausted nonrepresentations.
The final coverage mask equals the entire local mask with precisely
\(E\) removed: 16745461 represented targets and 31714 separately
excluded multiples of 529. Since summands are positive, none above
\(B\) can contribute to a target in this interval.
Controls reject deleted represented targets, injected exceptions and
local exclusions, and corrupted individual class/exponent membership.
This proves the finite proposition, not a numerical analytic cutoff.

The ramified exceptions differ from the unit-target exceptions for an
exact arithmetic reason. Put \(U=\{n\in P:23\nmid n\}\).
The principal ramified generator \(\sqrt{-23}\) gives
\[
 P\cap23\mathbb Z=23U,\qquad
        23M\in P+P\ \Longleftrightarrow\ M\in U+U.       \tag{CN3.8}
\]
The first identity follows by dividing a primitive generator of
ramified valuation one by \(\sqrt{-23}\), and conversely multiplying
a 23-unit primitive generator. Both preserve primitiveness at all
other primes and change the ramified valuation between zero and one.
For the second identity, nonsquareness of \(-1\pmod{23}\) forces
both summands of a 23-divisible target to be ramified; division applies
to each. These are unrestricted identities.

For example \(29=6+23\in P+P\), while \(667=23\cdot29\notin P+P\)
by the complete finite check: the representation of 29 uses a ramified
summand, and \(29\notin U+U\). The 17 members of \(E\) coprime
to 23 are
\[
3,4,5,6,8,10,11,15,17,21,22,25,43,55,61,73,166.
\]
The other 23 members are 23 times
\[
1,3,4,5,6,8,10,11,15,17,21,22,25,29,31,41,43,50,55,61,73,81,166.
\]
This distinguishes ramified behavior without inferring a complete
unrestricted sumset from bounded evidence.
