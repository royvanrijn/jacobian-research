# Complete three-adic classification on the elliptic exceptional slice

Every admissible point of the existing elliptic slice has an explicit
three-adic solubility test. The test treats all four residue discs and
all formal depths. It sharpens the earlier upper sieve and gives an exact
leading count for targets soluble at three alone. Other places remain
separate conditions.

## 1. The pointwise classification

Retain

\[
 E:T^2=X^3-10758382X+6600759744,\qquad x=X+3552,
\]
\[
 T^2=x(x^2-10656x+27091730),\qquad
 v=\frac{3(x+432)}{6400},\qquad
 z=\frac{3(128x-25865)}{819200},\quad
 \kappa=\frac{42649}{16384}.                         \tag{1.1}
\]

The admissible open and the quadratic and cubic factors are those of
[the explicit local criterion](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_EXPLICIT_LOCAL_CRITERION.md).
In particular, `z!=0`, and translating the cubic gives

\[
 g(w)=h(4/5+w)=w^3-zw+z\left(\frac{10}{9}z-\kappa\right).
                                                               \tag{1.2}
\]

All statements below apply to admissible points of `E(Q_3)`, including
the local images of rational points. The fibre factors have matching
discriminant squareclasses there.

Write `O` for the identity. The other three points of `E(F_3)` have
`T=0` and `X=0,1,2`. At a point in the identity disc put

\[
 t=-X/T,\qquad n=v_3(t)\ge1.
\]

At a point in a finite residue disc put `m=v_3(T)>=1`.
All valuations have `v_3(3)=1`.

**Theorem ETC1 (complete local test at three).** An admissible matching
fibre is soluble over `Q_3` precisely in the following cases.

| Reduction in `E(F_3)` | Necessary and sufficient condition |
| --- | --- |
| `O` | `t` is a cube in `Q_3`, equivalently `3|n` and `t/3^n=1 or -1 mod9` |
| `(X,T)=(0,0)` | Never |
| `(X,T)=(2,0)` | `m>=2` |
| `(X,T)=(1,0)`, `m>=2` | `m>=3` |
| `(X,T)=(1,0)`, `m=1` | Put `k=v_3(z)>=4`. One requires `3|k` and `kappa z/3^k=1 or -1 mod9` |

The quadratic factor is nonsplit at three everywhere on this admissible
local family. Thus solubility in this table is exactly the existence of
a `Q_3` root of `h`.

## 2. Cube units and the two finite residue discs

**Lemma ETC2 (the Cardano criterion in an unramified quadratic algebra).**
Let `F=Q_3(sqrt(x))`, interpreted as the split quadratic algebra when
`x` is a square. Put

\[
 \eta_\pm=\frac{z}{3840}
                 \left(5200-x\ \pm\frac{T}{\sqrt x}\right).
                                                               \tag{2.1}
\]

In the split case these are the two components of one element of
`Q_3 times Q_3`. The cubic has a root over `Q_3` if and only if this
element is a cube in `F`. The algebra `F` is split or unramified
quadratic. In an unramified field of residue degree `f=1 or 2`, a
nonzero element `3^a u`, with `u` a unit, is a cube if and only if

\[
 3\mid a,\qquad u^{3^f-1}=1\pmod9.                 \tag{2.2}
\]

For a split algebra apply the degree-one test to both components.

**Proof.** The equation of `E` gives

\[
 (5200-x)^2-T^2/x=2(128x-25865),
\]
\[
 \eta_++\eta_-=-z(10z/9-\kappa),\qquad
 \eta_+\eta_-=(z/3)^3.                              \tag{2.3}
\]

Both components are nonzero on the admissible open. The quadratic
algebra containing them has squareclass `x` because their difference
is the nonzero rational multiple `zT/(1920 sqrt(x))`.

If `eta_+=u^3` in `F`, put `v_*=z/(3u)`. Identities (2.3) imply that
`u+v_*` is a root of `g` in `F`. A cubic with a root in a field of
degree at most two is reducible over `Q_3`, and hence has a `Q_3`
root; this also handles the split algebra componentwise.

Conversely let `w` be a `Q_3` root, and let `u,v_*` solve
`U^2-wU+z/3=0`. Then their cubes are `eta_+,eta_-`. Moreover
`u^3-v_*^3=(u-v_*)(w^2-z/3)`, and the last rational factor is nonzero:
otherwise `w` would be a repeated cubic root. Thus the quadratic
algebra generated by `u-v_*` is precisely `F`, and the required cube
roots lie in `F`.

The valuation of `x` is even. For negative valuation this follows from
the dominant `x^3` term of the elliptic equation; for positive valuation
the factor `x^2-10656x+27091730` is a unit. Thus `F` is split or
unramified, since the residue characteristic is odd.

Finally, in an unramified field, Hensel lifting separates a unit into a
root of unity of order `3^f-1` and an element of `1+3 O_F`. Cubing is
bijective on the first factor, and

\[
 (1+3b)^3=1+9(b+3b^2+3b^3).
\]

The polynomial in parentheses has derivative one modulo three, so
Hensel's lemma shows `(1+3 O_F)^3=1+9 O_F`. This proves (2.2).
The only imported local lifting result is the usual simple-root Hensel
lemma, in [Milne, ANT 3.00](sources/milne-ant300.pdf), Proposition 7.31,
printed p.113. No numerical logarithm is used. \(\square\)

We now prove the finite-disc rows of ETC1. At every local point the
quadratic discriminant is `D=-3x/1600` of odd valuation, so it supplies
no local point. The `X=0` disc is the Eisenstein obstruction of ETS3
in [the earlier three-adic sieve](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_THREE_ADIC_SIEVE.md).

In the `X=2` disc one has `x=2 mod3`. The elliptic equation and `3|T`
force `x=5 mod9`: modulo nine the quadratic factor in (1.1) is
`x^2+2`. Here `F` is unramified quadratic, `v_3(z)=1`, and the rational
part of `eta` is

\[
 A=\frac{(128x-25865)(5200-x)}{1048576000}=1\pmod9.
                                                               \tag{2.4}
\]

The other part is a unit multiple of `T sqrt(x)`, of valuation `m`.
Modulo nine its eighth power is `1+8(eta-A)`. The two integral basis
components `1,sqrt(x)` are independent modulo nine. Thus (2.2) holds
exactly when `m>=2`.

In the `X=1` disc with `m>=2`, the elliptic equation forces
`x=67 mod81`. Indeed the quadratic factor has a simple root in the
`x=1 mod3` class, and its value at 67 is
`3^5*151*719`. Consequently

\[
 z=27u,\qquad u=7\pmod9,\qquad
 (5200-x)/3=1\pmod9.                               \tag{2.5}
\]

Now `x` is a local square. Each component of `eta` has valuation three,
and the rational part of `eta/27` is `-1 mod9`. Its remaining part
has valuation `m-1`, with unit leading coefficient. When `m=2` its
residue is therefore `-1+3a`, with `a` a unit, which is neither 1 nor
-1 modulo nine. When `m>=3` the residue is -1. The degree-one case of
(2.2) proves precisely the stated distinction.

## 3. The branch annuli and the identity disc

Still in the `X=1` disc, suppose `m=1`. Put `S=5200-x` and
`b=T/sqrt(x)`. Both have valuation one, so `S^2-b^2` has valuation
at least three. By (2.3), `k=v_3(z)>=4`.

The Newton edge of (1.2) gives root valuation `k/3`, so no local root
exists unless `k=3r`, with `r>=2`. Write `z=3^{3r}u` and `w=3^r U`.
The scaled monic polynomial is

\[
 U^3-3^r uU+u(10\,3^{3r-2}u-\kappa).              \tag{3.1}
\]

Its linear coefficient is in `9 Z_3`, its constant is a unit, and
its discriminant has valuation three. More generally a monic cubic
`U^3+aU^2+bU+c` with `a,b in9 Z_3` and `c` a unit has discriminant
valuation three: only `-27c^2` has that valuation. By the exact cubic
root test FMA3a in
[the ambient classification](FIXED_MAP_AMBIENT_LOCAL_CLASSIFICATION.md),
it has a local root exactly when it has one modulo nine. Unit cubes
modulo nine are 1 and -1. Applied to (3.1), this is exactly the last
row of ETC1.

For the identity disc, ETS4 already shows that `3|n` is necessary.
Put `n=3r` and rescale the original cubic by `W=3^{-4r}U`. The
resulting monic cubic has quadratic and linear coefficients of
valuations `1+4r` and `1+2r`, respectively, and unit constant

\[
 c=3^{4n}\left(\frac{10v^2}{9}
               -\frac{10s_0v}{9}+\frac{125}{1024}\right),
 \qquad s_0=\frac{1816347}{819200}.                  \tag{3.2}
\]

Write `t=3^n a`, with `a` a unit. The elliptic equation implies
`t^2 x=1 mod27`; the shift from `X` to `x` preserves this congruence.
Also `v/(3x/6400)=1 mod9` and the last two terms of (3.2), relative
to its first, have positive valuations at least two. Hence

\[
 c=\frac{a^{-4}}{160^3}\pmod9.                    \tag{3.3}
\]

Again the discriminant valuation is three, so FMA3a reduces local
solubility to `c=1 or -1 mod9`. Since `160^3=1 mod9`, and the unit
group modulo nine is cyclic of order six, (3.3) gives exactly
`a=1 or -1 mod9`. This proves the identity-disc row and completes
ETC1.

## 4. Stronger finite upper bounds

Retain the matching-target coefficient `C_E` and the finite local
group filtration `E_n` from EEC1 and ETS1. Let `H` be the closure of
`E(Q)` in `E(Q_3)`. The rational witnesses in ETS1 show that

\[
 H_0=E(\mathbb Q_3)[2]+E_2\ \subseteq H,
 \qquad [E(\mathbb Q_3):H_0]=3.                    \tag{4.1}
\]

Here all four local two-torsion points exist because the reduced
cubic has three simple roots. To see the precise first equality,
`2P_2` of ETS1 has depth two and generates every finite quotient
`E_2/E_N`. Its closed span is `E_2`. Odd powers of three applied to
`P_2` then tend to its local two-torsion representative. Together
with the rational `T_0` this gives all four local two-torsion points.
The rational closure is in fact determined by a finite certificate.

**Lemma ETC3a (finite three-saturation and the exact local image).**
Let

\[
 P_1=\left(\frac{199200}{49},\frac{59459616}{343}\right),\qquad
 P_2=\left(\frac{1563680}{529},\frac{304670016}{12167}\right)
\]

in `(X,T)` coordinates, and `T_0=(-3552,0)`. The subgroup
`Gamma=<P_1,P_2,T_0>` has finite index in `E(Q)`, and this index is
prime to three. Its three-adic closure, and that of `E(Q)`, are

\[
 \boxed{H=H_0=E(\mathbb Q_3)[2]+E_2.}              \tag{4.2}
\]

This proves saturation at three only; no full generator basis or
saturation at other primes is asserted.

**Proof.** Use the translated equation (1.1), so the two points have
`x` coordinates `373248/49` and `3442688/529`. The following complete
good-reduction certificate is checked by finite-field group arithmetic.

| Prime | `#E(F_p)` | `bar P_1` | `bar P_2` | `[#E/3]bar P_1` | `[#E/3]bar P_2` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `41` | `48` | `(39,37)` | `(0,0)` | `(37,37)` | `O` |
| `53` | `60` | `(21,16)` | `(33,51)` | `(13,45)` | `(13,8)` |

In each last pair the nonzero point has order three; at 53 the two
points are negatives. Thus if `aP_1+bP_2` belongs to
`3E(Q)+<T_0>`, reduction gives

\[
 a=0\pmod3,\qquad a-b=0\pmod3.                   \tag{4.3a}
\]

Indeed multiplication by `#E(F_p)/3` kills both a triple and rational
two-torsion in these two groups. The two displayed linear conditions
have determinant -1 modulo three and force `3|a,b`.

They first prove independence. Any integral relation modulo rational
torsion has both coefficients divisible by three; division by three
gives another such relation because `E(Q)` has no three-torsion.
Iterating forces both coefficients to vanish. The rank-two theorem
and exact rational torsion group from the elliptic arithmetic note
then show that `Gamma` has finite index.

They also prove three-saturation: if `3Q=aP_1+bP_2+epsilon T_0`, write
`a=3a_0,b=3b_0`. The point
`Q-a_0P_1-b_0P_2-epsilon T_0` is killed by three and hence is zero.
Thus `Q in Gamma`. A finite quotient with this property has order
prime to three.

All generators of `Gamma` lie in `H_0`. For `P_1`, the translated
coordinate has `v_3(x(P_1))=6`, and the exact two-torsion addition
formula is `x(P_1+T_0)=27091730/x(P_1)`. Hence `P_1+T_0` has formal
depth three. For `P_2`, its `T` coordinate has valuation two, so its
difference from the local two-torsion point in the same reduction
disc belongs to `E_2`: translation preserves depth because the
elliptic derivative at that two-torsion point is a unit. The earlier
paragraph shows that `<P_2,T_0>` is already dense in `H_0`.
Consequently the closure of `Gamma` is exactly `H_0`.
Since `E(Q_3)/H_0` has order three, a nonzero rational image in this
quotient would give an order-three quotient of `E(Q)/Gamma`, which
is impossible. Hence the rational closure is exactly (4.2).
\(\square\)

**Theorem ETC3 (refined finite-quotient upper bounds).** For each fixed
integer `J>=1`, the Hasse-target count on this slice satisfies

\[
 A_E(B)\le\left(\frac{55}{156}
                   +\frac{45}{26\,27^J}\right)C_E\log B
                     +O_{E,J}(\sqrt{\log B}).      \tag{4.3}
\]

In particular the first quotient gives coefficient `5/12`.

**Proof.** Take the quotient by `E_{3J}`. In the rational image `H_0`,
each of the four reduction discs has one quarter of the points.
The `X=0` disc is discarded. The `X=2` disc is retained in full, and
the `X=1` disc retains its third consisting of depths `m>=3`.
In the identity disc the conditional fraction at depth `n>=2` is
`(2/3)3^{-(n-2)}`. Among each tested annulus of depth divisible by
three, the unit-cube condition retains exactly one third.

This last assertion is a finite quotient count: for `n>=1`, Milne's
parameter-addition estimate from ETS1 identifies `E_n/E_{n+2}` with
the nine residues of `t/3^n modulo9`. Six are units, and exactly two
are cubes. For `n=3k<3J` this test is visible in the stated quotient.
Retaining the untested tail `E_{3J}` gives total fraction

\[
 \frac14+\frac1{12}
 +\frac14\left\{
      \sum_{k=1}^{J-1}\frac29\,3^{-(3k-2)}
                          +3^{-(3J-2)}\right\}
   =\frac{55}{156}+\frac{45}{26\,27^J}.            \tag{4.4}
\]

For comparison, in the full ambient local group, retaining the entire
`X=1` disc, one third of `X=2`, and the tested identity annuli gives
the smaller upper fraction

\[
 \frac{53}{156}+\frac{15}{26\,27^J}.               \tag{4.5}
\]

The torsion-aware lattice count and the generic two preimages `P,-P`
of a target are exactly as in ETS1. Each finite retained union of
cosets has its specified fraction of `C_E log B`, with the stated
fixed-`J` boundary error. This proves (4.3). \(\square\)

**Corollary ETC4 (limiting Hasse upper proportion).**

\[
 \boxed{\limsup_{B\to\infty}
      \frac{A_E(B)}{C_E\log B}\le\frac{55}{156}.}    \tag{4.6}
\]

Take the upper limit in `B` first for each fixed `J` in ETC3, and
then let `J` increase. No uniform error or density-zero conclusion is
asserted.

## 5. Exact counts for solubility at three alone

Let `L_3(B)` count distinct admissible matching targets of height at
most `B` that have a `Q_3` source point. Requiring both factors to be
fields changes this count by only a finite number, by the genus-four
argument of the elliptic arithmetic note.

**Theorem ETC5 (the local-only logarithmic count).**

\[
 \boxed{L_3(B)=\frac{55}{156}C_E\log B
                                      +o_E(\log B).}           \tag{5.1}
\]

This counts solubility at three, without imposing any other finite
place. The normalized Haar measure in the full ambient group
`E(Q_3)` is instead `19/156`; the rational closure is the subgroup
(4.2), whose normalized measure is `55/156`.

**Proof.** In a finite residue disc centered at a local two-torsion
point, `T` is a uniform local coordinate: the derivative of the
elliptic cubic in `x` is a unit. Its additive measure agrees with
Haar measure up to the same fixed normalization, since
`dx/(2T)=dT/f_E'(x)` and `f_E'(x)` is a unit. Translation from `E_r`
to that point preserves depth, so the conditional depth distribution
begins at `r=2` in the rational closure `H_0` and at `r=1` in the
full ambient group.

The identity-disc cube condition has conditional measures

\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac29\,3^{-(3k-2)}=\frac1{13}
 \quad(H_0),\qquad
 \sum_{k\ge1}\frac29\,3^{-(3k-1)}=\frac1{39}
 \quad(E(\mathbb Q_3)).                            \tag{5.2}
\]

For `H_0`, the conditional measures in the four discs
`O,X=0,X=1,X=2` are consequently

\[
 (1/13,\ 0,\ 1/3,\ 1).
                                                               \tag{5.3}
\]

Their average is `55/156`.

In the full group the `X=2` measure is `1/3`, and the `X=0` measure
is zero. In the `X=1` disc the points with `m>=3` have measure `1/9`.
It remains to count its `m=1` part. The two points above `z=0` are
defined over `Q_3`, and their `T` coordinates are `r,-r`, with
`v_3(r)=1`. Indeed

\[
 x_R=\frac{25865}{128},\qquad
 T_R^2=\frac{10585532408369625}{2097152},
\]

whose squareclass is `51730=1 mod3`. The two balls
`r+9 Z_3` and `-r+9 Z_3` partition the `m=1` part. On either ball,

\[
 v_3(z)=v_3(T-r)+2.                                \tag{5.4}
\]

For (5.4), subtract the elliptic equations at `x` and `x_R`.
Their difference quotient in `x` is a unit, while `T+r` has
valuation one. Since `z=3(x-x_R)/6400`, the assertion follows.
Moreover `z/[9(T-r)]` is a unit analytic function. At the potentially
allowed depths `ell=v_3(T-r)=4,7,10,...`, its residue modulo nine
is constant on each annulus: its ratio to its value at the center is
one modulo nine. Therefore the normalized-unit condition in ETC1
retains exactly one third of each such annulus.

Conditional on either ball, the allowed measure is

\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}\frac29\,3^{-(4+3j-2)}=\frac1{39}.
\]

The two balls together have conditional measure `2/3` in the `X=1`
disc. Its total good measure is thus

\[
 \frac19+\frac23\frac1{39}=\frac5{39}.              \tag{5.5}
\]

The full-group disc measures are `(1/39,0,5/39,1/3)`, averaging
to `19/156`. This is the ambient local comparison, not the rational
point distribution identified in ETC3a.

To pass from these local measures to (5.1), approximate the soluble
set from inside and outside by finite unions of cosets of `E_N`.
The omitted tails shrink to the identity and the two branch points;
they have Haar measure tending to zero. The finite exclusions from
admissibility also have measure zero. Every fixed finite union has
the exact leading target coefficient given by its fraction in the
rational finite-quotient image, by EEC1. The two approximations
therefore squeeze the normalized count to `55/156`, the local measure
in the proven rational closure.
Negation preserves the soluble set, so the generic division by two
for target images preserves these proportions. This argument supplies
`o(log B)` directly. The square-root error theorem
[ESQ3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_SHARP_FINITE_LOCAL_ERROR.md), using an
additional primary bound for two p-adic elliptic logarithms, sharpens
this to `O_E(sqrt(log B))` with the same coefficient.
\(\square\)

## 6. Evidence and remaining scope

The [exact checker](verify_elliptic_exception_complete_three_adic_classification.py)
controls the Cardano identities, all cube-unit residues modulo nine,
the finite-disc congruences, independently normalized cubic root
tests on declared bounded rational inputs, the complete reduction
groups and three-saturation certificate at 41 and 53, and the rational
finite quotient and measure constants. Its receipt is
[elliptic_exception_complete_three_adic_classification_checks.json](elliptic_exception_complete_three_adic_classification_checks.json).
The all-valuation statements follow from the written proofs, rather
than from extrapolation of those bounded controls.

The exact local test at three and its local-only count do not settle
the additional conditions at two, five, or the varying inert primes
in the all-primes criterion. No everywhere-locally-soluble asymptotic,
Hasse infinitude, generator saturation at primes other than three,
formal verification or external review is asserted.
