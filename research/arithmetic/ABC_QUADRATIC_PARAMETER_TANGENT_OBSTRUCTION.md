# Quadratic parameter changes and independent tangent norm classes

A pair of rational parameter maps of degree at most two can preserve
the quadratic field function and produce two independent generic cubic
norm classes. Such a pair cannot realize two positive weighted tangent
witnesses. In genus three the base curve and the two directions can be
identified explicitly. Each direction fails a fixed valuation test at
\(2\) in every nondegenerate rational specialization of the real model.

Use the weighted tangent elliptic models and deck involutions of
[WM1](ABC_WEIGHTED_CUBIC_TANGENT_ELLIPTIC_MARKS.md#the-double-cover-and-its-three-marks).
Each model is \(E_b:y^2=x^3+b^2\), with \(b=4k(k+1)\).
The three symmetric profiles, up to reordering their prime vertices,
are the arithmetic progression profile. For them use the normalization
of [FC1](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_FIELD_COLLISIONS.md#the-fixed-elliptic-double-cover)
on \(E_1\) with \(T=(-1,0)\), parameter \(t=p/h\), and
\[
 U^2=f(t)=1+6t^2-3t^4,\qquad t=\frac{x+1}{y}.
\]
The three prime directions are \(p-h,p,p+h\), so positivity up to
common scaling requires \(|t|>1\).

## Two degree-two projections in genus three

**Theorem QP1.** Let \(C/\mathbb Q\) be a smooth geometrically integral
hyperelliptic curve of genus three with two degree-two maps to
weighted tangent models, each intertwining the hyperelliptic involution
with the target deck involution. Suppose the two elliptic pullbacks in
\(\operatorname{Jac} C\) are distinct. Then both profiles are symmetric.
After the normalization above, the two parameters satisfy one of
\[
 t_1^2+t_2^2=2,\qquad
 t_1^2+t_2^2+6t_1^2t_2^2=0. \tag{1}
\]
The hyperelliptic base is the normalization of the corresponding
relation in (1), and \(C\) is obtained from its function field by
adjoining \(\sqrt{f(t_1)}\). Both models occur. Only the first base has
real points, and neither model gives a real point with both tangent
directions positive. The first model has two independent generic
cubic norm classes, even over the algebraic closure.

Let \(\eta\) be the hyperelliptic involution and let \(\beta_i\) be the
involution of the degree-two map \(\pi_i\). Write \(j_i=\pi_i^*\) and
\(p_i=\pi_{i*}\) on Jacobians, so \(1+\beta_i=j_i p_i\) and
\(p_i j_i=[2]\). The involutions act on the polarized orthogonal complement
of the sum \(A\) of the two elliptic images by \(-1\).
As in [GT5](ABC_RECIPROCAL_GENUS_TWO_TANGENT_OBSTRUCTION.md#classification-with-two-degree-two-projections),
the rational endomorphism rings are \(\mathbb Z\), with a nonzero
rational homomorphism between the factors only if the factors are
rationally isomorphic. In that case identify the two factors. The
pullback of the polarization by the sum map to \(A\) has diagonal
entries \([2]\) and integer off-diagonal entry \(n\).
Positivity and \(\dim A=2\) give \(|n|<2\).
If the factors are nonisogenous, the off-diagonal maps are zero.

When \(n=\pm1\), the actions of the two involutions on \(A\), in the
coordinates of the two factors up to isogeny, are
\[
 (P,Q)\longmapsto(P+nQ,-Q),\qquad
 (P,Q)\longmapsto(-P,nP+Q).
\]
Their product has order three on \(A\) and acts trivially on its
orthogonal complement. The action of the hyperelliptic curve
automorphism group on its Jacobian is faithful by
[strong Torelli](sources/serre-strong-torelli-2001.pdf).
Thus the two involutions generate \(S_3\) on \(C\). This subgroup
acts faithfully on the hyperelliptic base because the only possible
kernel is the central involution \(\eta\) and \(S_3\) has trivial center.

Over the algebraic closure choose a coordinate \(x\) on the base
in which the group is generated by \(x\mapsto\zeta_3x\) and
\(x\mapsto1/x\). The stable eight-point branch set must consist of
the orbit \(\{0,\infty\}\) and either one orbit of size six or both
orbits of size three. In the latter case the curve is
\(Y^2=x(x^6-1)\) and either lift of \(x\mapsto1/x\) has order four,
since it acts by \(Y\mapsto\pm\sqrt{-1}\,Y/x^4\). In the former case
the curve is \(Y^2=x(x^6+a x^3+1)\) with \(a\ne\pm2\).
The elliptic involution is the lift with \(Y\mapsto Y/x^4\):
it fixes four points over \(x=\pm1\), whereas the other lift fixes
none. Its quotient has coordinates \(u=x+1/x,\ v=Y/x^2\) with
\(v^2=u^3-3u+a\). The coefficient of \(u\) is nonzero, so this
quotient cannot have \(j=0\). This excludes \(n=\pm1\).
The two forms of the branch set also occur in the automorphism
strata of [Shaska–Thompson, Sections 5.3.2 and 5.4.2](sources/shaska-thompson-genus-three-bielliptic.pdf).
The orbit argument uses only the subgroup, including when the full
automorphism group is larger.

The vanishing off-diagonal pairings imply that \(\beta_1,\beta_2\)
commute because \(j_1p_1j_2p_2=j_2p_2j_1p_1=0\). They are
distinct and generate a group of order four. They induce distinct
involutions on the hyperelliptic base because \(\eta\) is neither
one of them nor their product. In particular their product acts
as \(+1\) on the one-dimensional orthogonal complement, where
\(\eta\) acts as \(-1\).

On the first elliptic quotient the other involution descends to
a map with linear part \(-1\), from the same orthogonality identities.
It is distinct from the target deck involution because the two
lifts of that deck involution would be \(\eta\) and \(\eta\beta_1\).
Two commuting distinct involutions \(P\mapsto S-P\) and
\(P\mapsto T-P\) have product a nonzero rational 2-torsion translation.
The same holds on the second elliptic quotient. A weighted model
has rational 2-torsion exactly when \(b\) is a rational cube, which
forces a symmetric profile by the coefficient calculation in
[FM4](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_COLLISION_MOMENT.md#asymmetric-weighted-profiles).
In the normalized \(E_1\) model that translation is by \(T\), and
acts on the parameter as \(\tau(P+T)=\tau(-P)=-\tau(P)\).

Put \(z=t_1^2\) and \(w=t_2^2\). Each generates the quotient field
of the hyperelliptic base by the two induced involutions. Therefore
\(w\) is a rational Möbius transformation of \(z\). The base itself
has function field
\[
 \mathbb Q(z)(\sqrt z,\sqrt w).
\]
The zeros and poles of \(z,w\) are rational. Their two branch sets
must share exactly one point, since their compositum is geometrically
biquadratic and the base has genus zero. Indeed, there must be
three distinct branch places in a biquadratic cover of genus zero
by Riemann–Hurwitz, each of inertia order two.

Write \(G(z)=1+6z-3z^2\). The target covers give
\(G(w)/G(z)\) square on the base. Its class in
\(\mathbb Q(z)^\times/\mathbb Q(z)^{\times2}\) therefore lies in
the span of \(z,w\). The odd-order zeros from \(G(z)\) are two
irrational conjugates. The zeros from \(G(w)\) must cancel them
since the span branches only at rational places.
Consequently the Möbius map \(w(z)\) permutes the roots of \(G\).
The ratio \(G(w)/G(z)\) now has even divisor and must be a square
already over \(\mathbb Q(z)\), since the geometric classes of
\(z,w\) are independent.

The shared rational branch point means \(w\) sends at least one of
\(0,\infty\) to one of \(0,\infty\). If it fixes \(\infty\), the
nonidentity permutation of the two roots is \(w=2-z\).
If it fixes \(0\), that permutation is \(w=-z/(6z+1)\), using
the sum \(2\) and product \(-1/3\) of the roots.
If it sends \(0\) to \(\infty\), fixing the roots gives
\(w=2+1/(3z)\), for which \(G(w)/G(z)=-1/(3z^2)\) is nonsquare.
Swapping the roots gives \(w=-1/(3z)\), which shares both branch
places. The case sending \(\infty\) to \(0\) follows by inversion.
The two survivors have square ratios \(1\) and \(1/(6z+1)^2\);
they give (1). Their base fields are geometrically biquadratic as
indicated, and the extensions by \(\sqrt{G(z)}\) give eight simple
branch points over the base, since the two irrational places of
\(G\) are unramified in it. This proves the genus and the claimed
degree-two maps in each case.

In the second model, a real point with finite parameters would
require \(t_1=t_2=0\). Above that point the normalization has
\(t_2/t_1=\pm\sqrt{-1}\), so there is no real point.
An infinite parameter would require the other square to be
\(-1/6\). In the first model both squares are nonnegative, and
their sum \(2\) prevents both exceeding \(1\). Thus the positive
directions cannot coexist.

In the first model, each norm function gives the pullback of a point
of order three on its elliptic factor after dividing its divisor by
three, as in [GT2](ABC_RECIPROCAL_GENUS_TWO_TANGENT_OBSTRUCTION.md#independent-norm-functions-on-the-reciprocal-cover).
These pulled-back classes are nonzero by degree-two pushforward
and lie in opposite eigenspaces for \(\beta_1\): the first is fixed,
while the second is negated. This proves geometric independence.

## The dyadic obstruction and specializations

**Theorem QP2.** Every rational pair \(t_1^2+t_2^2=2\) has both
parameters odd 2-adic units and both parameters 3-adic units.
Consequently neither projection can have the field of a genuine
prime progression. For each nondegenerate specialization into a
quadratic field, each norm class has valuation nonzero modulo
three at the split primes over \(2\), and zero modulo three at all
odd primes. There are infinitely many distinct real quadratic fields
in which the two classes are independent and their span has
virtual-unit subspace of dimension one.

To see the local conditions on the parameters, a negative minimum
of their valuations at \(2\) or \(3\) would require two squares,
at least one a unit, to sum to zero modulo \(4\) or \(3\),
respectively, after multiplication by a suitable even power of the
prime. The unit squares make this impossible. The equation then
forces both parameters to be units at each prime.
By FC1, \(f(t)\) is a local square at \(2,3\) on these integral
parameters. At \(2\) it has valuation exactly two since
\(t^2=1+8j\) gives \(f(t)=4(1-48j^2)\).
In a genuine progression with all primes greater than \(3\),
\(t=p/h\) has negative valuation at \(2,3\), and the field has
local squareclass \(-3\) at both. This already excludes a genuine
progression from either projection.

Write a parameter as \(t=a/b\) in lowest terms, with \(b>0\), and
put \(F=b^4+6a^2b^2-3a^4\).
As in [CT1](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_NORM_RANK_AND_CONDUCTORS.md#the-tangent-identity-and-the-lost-cubic-direction),
one of its edge classes is represented, up to inversion, by
\[
 \alpha=\frac{2a^3-3a^2b-b^3+b\sqrt F}{2},\qquad
 \operatorname N(\alpha)=(a(a-b))^3.
\]
Assume \(a(a-b)\ne0\) and exclude split global specializations
from the quadratic-field assertion. At \(2\) both \(a,b\) are
odd and \(v_2(\sqrt F)=1\). The sum of \(\alpha\) and its
conjugate has valuation one and both terms have valuation at least
one. The norm gives the two valuations \(1,3v_2(a-b)-1\) in some
order, so neither is zero modulo three. At an odd prime \(q\) the
element is integral. If \(q\mid a(a-b)\), it does not divide
\(2a^3-3a^2b-b^3\), by coprimality and reduction at \(a=0\) or
\(a=b\). Hence only one prime over \(q\) can divide
\(\alpha\), that prime has residue degree one, and the norm
makes its valuation a multiple of three. All other odd prime
valuations vanish. The test therefore imposes exactly one
linear condition on the span whenever the two classes are independent.

The circle has a rational point, so its base is a rational parameter
line. Adjoin \(\zeta_3\) and the two cube roots to the generic
quadratic field. This is Galois over the rational function field by
the cube norms, and its total degree is \(36\) by QP1.
[Hilbert specialization](sources/zywina-quantitative-hit-2010.pdf)
preserves that degree for infinitely many rational parameters.
For each fixed squareclass \(d\), the rational parameters lie on
the twist \(dU^2=f(t_1)\) over the circle, a smooth genus-three
curve after normalization. [Faltings](sources/faltings-1983-endlichkeitssaetze.pdf)
gives finitely many in each field. The fields are real because
\(G(z)=4-3(z-1)^2>0\) on \(0\le z\le2\).

An exact specialization is \(t_1=7/5,\ t_2=1/5\) in
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{193})\). With the same choice of \(\sqrt F\),
the two classes are \(-87+5\sqrt{193}\) and
\(-69+5\sqrt{193}\). Their valuations at the two primes over
\(2\) are \((1,2)\) and \((5,1)\), respectively, with that
ordering of the primes. The classes are independent, and the
virtual-unit line in this fixture is generated by their product.
The field has class number one. The
[Sage checker](verify_quadratic_parameter_tangent_obstruction.py)
replays the Möbius identities and this specialization.

## Consequence for rational parameter maps

**Corollary QP3.** Let two nonconstant rational maps of degree at
most two from \(\mathbb P^1_s\) to weighted tangent parameter lines
give the same nonsquare quadratic function field over \(\mathbb Q(s)\).
If the pulled-back generic norm classes are independent, no real
parameter gives two positive coherent tangent witnesses.
Thus such maps cannot construct families of two genuine weighted
prime triangles carrying independent cubic class directions.

The smooth common cover \(C\) has genus at most three, since
the pullback of the four elliptic branch places by a map of degree
at most two has at most eight odd-multiplicity branch places.
It has genus at least one because it maps nontrivially to the
elliptic models. The maps of \(C\) have the same degrees as the
two parameter maps, by adjoining the common quadratic field.
They intertwine the deck involutions.

In genus one the pullbacks between Jacobians are nonzero isogenies.
By the rational Hom calculation of GT5, the two tangent curves are
rationally isomorphic. The two pullbacks from the identified elliptic
curve are rationally proportional. More explicitly, composing with
one pullback's dual shows that the other has proportionality
factor with square equal to the ratio of their degrees. With degrees
in \(\{1,2\}\) they therefore have the same degree and differ only by
sign. The marked cyclic order-three subgroup is unique as in GT5.
Thus the norm classes agree up to inversion, using their divisor
classes and then the cube norms to remove a possible constant.

In genus two or three both maps have degree two. Equal elliptic
images give the same generic norm direction by the injectivity
and divisor argument of GT5, which applies in either genus.
Distinct images fall within GT5 or QP1, respectively, and these
exclude simultaneous positivity. The conclusion concerns rational
parameter maps of the indicated degrees. The occupied rank-one
norm-record moment varies field and anchor data beyond this route.
