# Rank-zero genus-three Jacobians with unbounded elementary three-Sha

This note gives an explicit HLP gluing family, an unbounded three-isogeny
Selmer ratio, and a rank-zero quadratic-twist selection. The rank selection
uses Smith's 2025 preprint, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3, as an external
theorem. The result concerns split hyperelliptic Jacobians. It does not
determine their entire three-primary Sha, assert finiteness of that group,
or construct geometrically simple examples. Historical priority and
independent mathematical review remain unestablished.

It achieves the general rank-controlled large-Sha construction objective.
The separate task of controlling the genus-two companion rank in the
original Mordell cover [HG8](GENUS_THREE_MARKED_TORSION_DEFORMATIONS.md)
remains open; the present elliptic factors are different.

## Explicit gluing family and growing Selmer ratio

For an even integer (sge0), put

\[
 h=-\frac{3(s^2+31)}{2(s^2+49)},\quad b=2h+3=\frac{54}{s^2+49},\quad
 a=h^2+12h+9,\quad \Delta=a^2-4b^3=(h+1)(h-3)^3.
\]

Define elliptic curves
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_h&:y^2=x^3+(hx+h+1)^2,\\
 E'_h&:y^2=U(U^2+aU+b^3),\\
 F_h&:y^2=x(x-1)(x-\lambda),\qquad\lambda=\frac{a^2}{4b^3}.
 \end{split}
\]
The normalized three-isogeny (\phi:E_h\to E'_h) has kernel
generated by ( (0,h+1) ). Explicitly,
\[
 U=x+\frac{4h(h+1)}x+\frac{4(h+1)^2}{x^2}-(4h+3),\qquad
 y'=y\left(1-\frac{4h(h+1)}{x^2}-\frac{8(h+1)^2}{x^3}\right).
\]
Let (\psi:E'_h\to E_h) be its dual. Its kernel is the group
scheme (\mu_3).

For the hyperelliptic equation put
\[
 u=-\frac{7h+5}{2},\qquad v=\frac{(h+1)s}{2},
\]
\[
 L_s(t)=u(1-t^2)-2vt,\qquad M_s(t)=v(1-t^2)+2ut,
\]
\[
 G_s(t)=b\left(16b^2L_s(t)^2M_s(t)^2-\Delta(1+t^2)^4\right),
 \qquad C_s:y^2=G_s(t). \tag{14}
\]
Write (\omega_{\ge5}(m)) for the number of distinct prime divisors
at least five of a nonzero integer (m), using its absolute value, and put
\[
 W(s)=\omega_{\ge5}(s^2-5)+\omega_{\ge5}(s^2+49)
                            -\omega_{\ge5}(s^2+43).
\]

**Proposition HG13.** The curves in (14) are smooth of genus three and
their Jacobians admit degree-eight dual isogenies with

\[
                 E'_h\times E'_h\times F_h,
\]
whose composites are [2]. The global Selmer ratio of (\psi) satisfies
\[
                         c(\psi)\ge3^{W(s)-2}. \tag{15}
\]
Moreover (W(s)/\log\log T\to1) in probability as the even integers
(s\in[0,T]) are ordered by size. In particular (c(\psi)) is unbounded
in this rational gluing family.

**Geometry.** Direct substitution gives
\[
 u^2+v^2=-\frac a{2b},\quad
 h+1=\frac{5-s^2}{2(s^2+49)},\quad
 h-3=-\frac{9(s^2+43)}{2(s^2+49)},\quad
 a=-\frac{27(s^4+62s^2+529)}{4(s^2+49)^2}.
\]
These show that all elliptic factors are smooth, with (a,b,\Delta)
nonzero for integer (s). In HLP Proposition 14 use (A_1=A_2=a),
(B_1=B_2=b^3), (A_3=-1-\lambda), (B_3=\lambda).
The discriminant product root is (R=\Delta(1-\lambda)), and the
twisting factor is zero. Their conic and quartic can be taken as
\[
 2b^3(X^2+Y^2)+aZ^2=0,
\]
\[
 W_0^2Z^2=-\frac{a^2b^3}{2}(X^4+Y^4)
                         +\frac{a^2(a^2+4b^3)}{16b^3}Z^4.
\]
The conic has rational point ((u:v:b)). Its parametrization is
((X,Y,Z)=(L_s,M_s,b(1+t^2))); substituting gives the right-hand side
(a^2G_s(t)/16). The ordinate change (y=4W_0Z/a) proves (14).
HLP's proposition supplies the polarized gluing and smoothness. The
leading coefficient can vanish only if (b=(u^2-v^2)^2), whereas
(v_3(b)=3), so (14) really has degree eight for every integer (s).

**Local ratios.** At a prime (p\ge5), the only possible bad reductions
of (E_h,E'_h) are above (s^2-5,s^2+49,s^2+43). These three integers
are pairwise coprime outside 2 and 3. The local ratio for the dual
isogeny is

| Condition on (p\ge5) | (c_p(\psi)) |
|---|---:|
| (p\mid s^2-5) | 3 |
| (p\mid s^2+49) | 3 |
| (p\mid s^2+43), with ( (-3/p)=1 ) | 1/3 |
| (p\mid s^2+43), with ( (-3/p)=-1 ) | 1 |
| None of these | 1 |

Here is a direct reduction check. For (h=m/n), use integral marked
two-torsion models with coefficients
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &A&B\\ \hline
 E_h&m^2-3n^2&n^3(2m+3n)\\
 E'_h&m^2+12mn+9n^2&n(2m+3n)^3.
 \end{array}
\]
Their quadratic discriminants are ((m+n)^3(m-3n)) and
((m+n)(m-3n)^3). In our parametrization (2m+3n=108), up to
factors supported at 2 and 3. At (m+n=0) the multiplicative
discriminant orders have ratio 3:1, and the node has square tangent
slope. At (n=0) they have ratio 6:2, again with square slope.
At (m-3n=0) they have ratio 1:3, with tangent squareclass (-3).
The marked models have unit (c_4) in each case. The local table now
follows from the split multiplicative Tamagawa ratio; nonsplit
multiplicative reduction gives ratio one. This also fixes the direction
of the dual isogeny, which is essential to obtaining Sha on the factor
that is actually glued.

By BKLOS Propositions 10.4 and Theorem 10.5, both factors at 2 and 3
are at least (1/3). The real local factor of (\psi) is one since
(\mu_3(\mathbb R)) is trivial. Multiplication of the table proves (15).
Counting all prime divisors of (s^2+43) in the negative term only
weakens the bound; that term must not be used as an equality.

**Unboundedness, rather than a finite experiment.** Each of
(s^2-5,s^2+49,s^2+43) is irreducible over (\mathbb Q). For odd primes
away from its fixed discriminant, its number of roots modulo (p) is
(1+\chi(p)), with a fixed nonprincipal quadratic character (\chi).
Williams' Theorem 1, summed over the corresponding fixed residue
classes, gives
\[
                  \sum_{p\le z}\frac{\rho(p)}p=\log\log z+O(1).
\]
This remains true when restricting (s) to even integers, since two
is invertible modulo these primes. Take (z=T^{1/8}). The sum of the
indicators (p\mid f(s)), (p\le z), has this mean and variance
(O(\log\log T)+O(z^2/T)): for distinct primes the Chinese remainder
theorem gives joint density (\rho(p)\rho(q)/(pq)), with error
(O(\rho(p)\rho(q)/T)). At most a bounded number of distinct prime
divisors of (f(s)), uniformly (s\le T), exceed (z), since
(|f(s)|\ll T^2). Chebyshev's inequality proves
(\omega(f(s))/\log\log T\to1) in probability. Applying this separately
to the three polynomials and taking the intersection proves the stated
normal order of (W). No independence between the three polynomials
is required. This completes HG13.

## Rank zero and arbitrarily many elementary Sha directions

**Theorem HG14.** For every integer (r\ge1), the explicit family

\[
                    C_{s,d}:d y^2=G_s(t)
\]
contains infinitely many rational isomorphism classes whose Jacobians
(J_{s,d}) satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{rank}J_{s,d}(\mathbb Q)=0,\qquad
                 (\mathbb Z/3)^{2r}\subseteq\Sha(J_{s,d})[3].
\]
For a suitable fixed even integer (s), the eligible squarefree twist
parameters (d) have positive density. This uses the external Smith
theorem specified above and makes no BSD or Sha-finiteness assumption.

**Proof.** Choose even (s) with (W(s)\ge r+3), possible by HG13.
Then (c(\psi)\ge3^{r+1}). Fix this (s), and abbreviate (E'_h)
and (F_h) by (E') and (F).

We first impose compatible even root numbers. Put
(P(s)=s^4+62s^2+529). For even (s), this is odd, greater than one,
and prime to three. Choose a prime (q\ge5) dividing it. The exact
congruences
\[
 P(s)\equiv864\pmod{s^2-5},\quad
 P(s)\equiv-108\pmod{s^2+49},\quad
 P(s)\equiv-288\pmod{s^2+43}
\]
show that (E_h,E') have good reduction at (q). At that prime
(a) vanishes and (b) is a unit. Hence (\lambda=a^2/(4b^3))
has positive valuation, the Legendre curve (F) is multiplicative,
and its conductor exponent at (q) is one.

Let (S) contain 2,3 and all bad primes of (E_h,E',F). Write
(\tau=w(E')) and (\eta=w(F)). Impose the following finite local
conditions on squarefree (d):

* (d\equiv1\pmod8), and (d) is a local square at every odd prime
  of (S\setminus\{q\});
* (d) is a unit at (q), with ( (d/q)=\tau\eta );
* (\operatorname{sgn}(d)=\tau).

These conditions define a nonempty reduced arithmetic progression
together with a sign, so their squarefree members have positive density.
They are odd fundamental discriminants prime to both conductors.
The coprime-twist root-number formula gives
\[
               w(E'^{(d)})=\tau\operatorname{sgn}(d)=1,
\]
\[
       w(F^{(d)})=\eta\operatorname{sgn}(d)(d/q)=1.
\]
The prime (q), where the companion is bad but (E') is good, makes
these two choices independent.

For the isogeny Selmer ratio, every finite bad prime of (E_h,E')
has unchanged local squareclass. At all remaining finite primes the
local ratio is one under every twist, by BKLOS Theorem 6.2, with
2 and 3 already fixed. Only the real local ratio can change; it is
one for positive (d) and (1/3) for negative (d). Therefore
\[
                         c(\psi_d)\ge c(\psi)/3\ge3^r.
\]

Smith's Corollary 1.3 says that the rank-positive exceptions among
root-number +1 twists of either fixed elliptic curve form a set of
size (o(H)) up to height (H). Their union is still (o(H)).
Our local conditions have positive density among the signed squarefree
integers, so a density-one subset of them has
\[
                  \operatorname{rank}E'^{(d)}(\mathbb Q)
                  =\operatorname{rank}F^{(d)}(\mathbb Q)=0.
\]
Thus the glued Jacobian has rank zero. This step uses fixed curves
after (s) is chosen; no uniformity in Smith's theorem as (s) varies
is asserted or needed.

Exclude the finitely many squareclasses (d=1,-3). The two kernel
characters of the isogeny pair are the characters of (d) and (-3d),
so both rational kernels are trivial. Cassels' formula then reads
\[
          |\operatorname{Sel}_{\psi_d}(E'^{(d)})|
           =c(\psi_d)|\operatorname{Sel}_{\phi_d}(E_h^{(d)})|
           \ge3^r.
\]
The same two characters are the composition factors of (E_h^{(d)}[3]),
so that group has no nonzero rational point. Since (E_h^{(d)}) is
isogenous to the rank-zero curve (E'^{(d)}), its rational group is
finite of order prime to three. The quotient
(E_h^{(d)}(\mathbb Q)/\psi_dE'^{(d)}(\mathbb Q)), killed by three,
is therefore zero. The Kummer exact sequence identifies the displayed
Selmer group with (\Sha(E'^{(d)})[\psi_d]\subseteq\Sha(E'^{(d)})[3]).

Finally, the degree-eight gluing and its dual have composites [2].
Their twist-compatible Kummer maps, as in HG5, identify full
three-primary Sha with the direct sum for the three elliptic factors.
There are two copies of (E'^{(d)}), giving the subgroup of dimension
at least (2r). Distinct squarefree twist classes give infinitely many
rational forms (the finite automorphism group identifies at most
finitely many twists). This proves HG14.

## Exact non-asymptotic fixture and verification scope

The parameter pair (s=8,d=57) already gives rank zero and
((\mathbb Z/3)^4\subseteq\Sha(J_{8,57})[3]), without using Smith's
theorem for this individual example. Minimal elliptic models for the
two distinct factors, in (a)-invariants, are
\[
 E'^{(57)}:[1,1,0,-546524405,-4917455709459],
\]
\[
 F^{(57)}:[0,-1,0,-32816655601327594443617,
                        -2284648019026739769616608032943135].
\]
Exact mwrank descent, with rank databases disabled, gives upper and
lower rank zero for both. The global ratio (c(\psi_{57})=9), and
both rational three-torsion kernels vanish. Cassels' formula and the
rank-zero Kummer quotient give at least two elementary directions on
each copy of (E'^{(57)}), hence four on the Jacobian. No complete
Sha order or equality of primary groups is inferred.
