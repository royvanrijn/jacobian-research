# Consecutive Hasse failures on one fixed Keller line

Let \(\Phi\) be the fixed Jacobian-one map and let \(h(n)\) indicate that
its fibre over \(y_n=(-1,32n/9,(8n+1)/3)\) is everywhere locally soluble
and has no rational point. Only positive integer parameters enter the
count. The fixed-map criterion describes these parameters by local
conditions and exclusion of integer cubes.
[Fixed map and rational classification](../../unpublished/fixed-map-hasse-failures/sections/03-local-global.tex)

**Theorem (consecutive Hasse failures).** Assume the all-conductor
Dirichlet zero-free and weighted-dilation inputs of the fixed-shift
arithmetic model comparison. Then
\[
 \sum_{1\le n\le X}h(n)h(n+1)
 = C\,\frac{X}{\log X}
   +O\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log X)^{1+1/49152}}\right),
 \qquad
 C=\frac1{117\sqrt3}
     \prod_{p\equiv2\ (3)}\left(1-\frac{2p}{p^3-1}\right)>0 .
\]
The product includes \(p=2\), whose factor is \(3/7\).
For instance \(2456=2^3\cdot307\) and \(2457=3^3\cdot7\cdot13\)
are consecutive Hasse-failing parameters. Their \(3\)-adic units
are \(8\) and \(1\) modulo \(9\), respectively.

## The comparison model

Let \(\chi=\chi_{-3}\), and call \(p\equiv2\bmod3\) inert. For a
positive integer \(n\) put \(u_3(n)=n/3^{v_3(n)}\) and define
\[
 g(n)=1_{3\mid v_3(n)}1_{u_3(n)\equiv\pm1\ (9)}
                      \prod_{p\ {\rm inert}}1_{3\mid v_p(n)} .
\]
The classification gives \(h(n)=g(n)1_{n\ {\rm not\ a\ cube}}\).
A positive integer is a rational cube exactly when it is an integer
cube, by reducing the fraction for its cube root. Thus removing pairs
with a cube costs \(O(X^{1/3})\). We work with \(g\) until the last step.

Use the fixed shift \(1\), outer scale \(X\), modulus \(18\) and bank
class \(1\bmod18\). Put \(L=\log X\), \(y=\exp(L^{2/5})\),
\(E=\exp(\sqrt L)\) and \(V_y=\prod_{p\le y}(1-p^{-1})\). Write \(n=rv\),
where \(r\) is the full part supported on primes at most \(y\) and \(v\)
is rough. Let \(M_{R,K}\) be the manuscript's cutoff Hankel model of
fixed depth \(K\) for the subgroup \(H=\{1,7,13\}\) of the units modulo
\(18\). Its semigroup is generated by \(p\equiv1\bmod3\) and its two
principal characters are \(1,\chi\). Define the truncated indicator and model by
\[
 \mathcal A(r,v)=
 1_{3\mid v_3(r)}
 1_{(r/3^{v_3(r)})v\equiv\pm1\ (9)}
 \prod_{p\ {\rm inert}}1_{3\mid v_p(r)},\qquad
 \mathcal D(n)=\mathcal A(r,v)\frac{(1+\chi(v))M_{R,K}(v)}{V_y}.
\]
[Fractional rough models, including the rough means and record tail](../../results/pointwise-binary-splitting-semigroups/model-preparation.tex)

For (C1), nonnegativity and the depth-independent eventual bound one
follow from the manuscript's cutoff and \(M_{R,K}/V_y\ll_K L^{-3/10}\)
on its nonzero range. The bound follows from the pole exponent \(1/2\)
and \(\log y=L^{2/5}\). For (C2), multiplication by a bank prime
\(p\le y\) congruent to \(1\bmod18\) changes only \(r\), preserves
the valuation masks and acts trivially on the unit test modulo \(9\).
It also preserves \(g(n)\), including when repeated.

For (C3), freeze a small-prime record \(r\le E\) and put \(T=Y/r\)
at the scale of the rough mean. The exponent masks in \(\mathcal A\)
are then fixed and the residual unit test is periodic modulo \(9\).
Absorb it into the bounded periodic test function. On rough \(v\),
the indicator \(\prod_{p>y,\ {\rm inert}}1_{3\mid v_p(v)}\) differs
from the split-prime semigroup indicator only if some inert
\(p>y\) has \(p^3\mid v\). On the support \(v\ll T\), their total
difference in absolute value is
\(\ll T\sum_{p>y}p^{-3}\ll T/y^2\). This is smaller than
\(T L^{-A}\) for every fixed \(A\). The split-prime rough-mean
lemma applies at \(T\ge X^{1/2}/E\), with period enlarged by at
most nine and the same smooth weight. It pairs the split-prime
indicator with \((1+\chi(v))M_{R,K}(v)/V_y\) on the rough
population. This proves (C3) at the chosen depth. The projected
rough character is independent of inert cube factors in the frozen record.

## The sparse record bound

Here is the (C4) verification at scale \(X/L\). For an arbitrary
nonnegative bank-record weight bounded by \(L^B\), discard records
above \(E\) on either side using the Rankin record-tail bound.
A bank record is bounded by the full small-prime part, and the
endpoint weights are bounded by one, so the discarded contribution
is \(XL^{O_B(1)}e^{-cL^{1/10}}\). For each retained pair \(r_0,s_0\),
the moduli are coprime by the shift. Divisibility defines a single
progression of density \(1/(r_0s_0)\). Exactness excludes one bank
root modulo \(p\) on that progression when \(p\mid r_0s_0\), and
two when \(p\nmid r_0s_0\). The resulting product, including the
progression density, is exactly the joint bank law (C4).

On a \(g\)-side sift inert primes up to \(w\), and on a model side
up to \(y\), dropping \(p=2\) for this upper bound. Use the necessary
condition \(v_p\ne1\) on an active side. At a nonbank inert prime,
one side excludes \(p-1\) roots modulo \(p^2\). If both sides are
active their excluded roots are disjoint because the shift is one.
All inert primes are units at the bank progression modulus. Thus
the local removed fraction is \(\nu_p(p-1)/p^2\), where \(\nu_p\)
is the number of active sides. Excluding valuation two as well
is unnecessary for this upper bound. It will be imposed in the
model main term by the exact cubic valuation rule.

Apply the upper-bound fundamental lemma with level \(D=X^{1/100}\)
and \(w=X^{1/(100s_0)}\) for a sufficiently large fixed \(s_0\).
Both the bank atoms modulo \(p\) and inert atoms modulo \(p^2\)
remove fractions at most \(2/p\), so Mertens' bound for
\(\sum_{p\le t}(\log p)/p\) gives the upper sieve-dimension condition
with dimension two, uniformly in the records. The local retained
fractions are bounded away from zero. The sieve acts on prime labels even when an
atom uses \(p^2\). For squarefree \(d\le D\), the number of
CRT root classes for its atom is at most \(2^{\omega(d)}d\).
The progression endpoint errors therefore sum to
\(O(D^2(\log D)^{O(1)})\) per record, including the factor
\(3^{\omega(d)}\) in Richert's Theorem 11.7. Multiplying by the
\(E^2\) possible retained pairs and \(L^B\) remains
\(X^{1/50+o(1)}\), smaller than the \(X^{1/2}L^{C_B}\)
error allowed by (C4).

At an inert prime the sifting factor differs from
\((1-p^{-1})^{\nu_p}\) by a factor \(1+O(p^{-2})\).
Inert-prime Mertens products thus give denominators of orders
\(\log w\), \((\log w\log y)^{1/2}\), and \(\log y\) for
\(gg\), \(g\mathcal D\), and \(\mathcal D\mathcal D\).
Each model side supplies \(O_K(L^{-3/10})\), and
\(\log w\asymp L,\ \log y=L^{2/5}\). All endpoint choices have
the required factor \(O_K(L^{-1})\). Multiplying by the interval
length and bank law and summing with the given record weights
proves (C4). The exceptional-record and endpoint error exponents
are independent of \(K\).

## Evaluation of the model

Let \(I_y=\{p\le y:p\ {\rm inert}\}\). On the small inert valuation
mask write \(d(n)=\prod_{p\in I_y}p^{v_p(n)/3}\). Let \(c_H\) be the
positive one-variable constant for the split-prime semigroup.
Outside the negligible large small-prime records, the rough
logarithm is \(L+O(\sqrt L)\), and the model is
\[
 \mathcal D(n)=\lambda_y B_y(n)(1+O_K(L^{-1/2})),\qquad
 \lambda_y=\frac{2c_H}{\sqrt L}(1-1/3)^{-1}
                              \prod_{p\in I_y}(1-p^{-1})^{-1},
\]
where
\[
 B_y(n)=
 \prod_{p\in I_y}1_{3\mid v_p(n)}
 \,1_{3\mid v_3(n)}\,1_{u_3(n)\equiv\chi(d(n))\ (9)} .
\]
Here \(B_y=0\) when \(d(n)\) is undefined. Indeed all split primes
in \(r\) have character one, so on the small valuation mask
\(\chi(r/3^{v_3(r)})=\chi(d(n)^3)=\chi(d(n))\). The conjunction
of \(\chi(v)=1\) and \(u_3(n)\equiv\pm1\bmod9\) is therefore
exactly the displayed unit test. This retains the coupling
between the small inert exponents and the \(3\)-adic sign.

For clarity, the finite-cutoff predicate \(B_y(n)B_y(n+1)\) can
be evaluated by CRT and the fundamental lemma with an error
\(O_A(X/L^A)\) for every fixed \(A\). Truncate \(d(n),d(n+1)\) at
\(E\) and the two powers \(3^{v_3}\) at \(E\). The discarded
integer counts are \(O(X/E^2+X^{1/3})\) for the cube divisors and
\(O(X/E+1)\) for the powers of three. The discarded normalized
Haar mass is \(O(E^{-1})\) by the corresponding sums of
\(d^{-3}\) and the \(3\)-adic tail. There are at most
\(O(E^2(\log E)^2)\) retained cells. The fixed cube divisors and
the prescribed \(3\)-adic units give a single progression if the
congruences are compatible. Its modulus is at most \(9E^7\).
At each inert prime in \(I_y\), exact valuations exclude one root
in the progression if the prime divides one of the two cubed
prefixes, and two otherwise. In the former case this is the
root for the next \(p\)-power after removal of the prescribed
power. A cell with both residues excluded at two is empty.
For the other cells the product sifting densities are exactly
the local valuation measures. Upper and lower fundamental-lemma
weights of level \(D=X^{1/100}\) give relative error smaller than
any inverse fixed logarithmic power since \(\log D/\log y\asymp
L^{3/5}\). The masses across cells sum to at most one. The
endpoint errors, with at most \(2^{\omega(d)}\) roots per
squarefree sieve atom, sum conservatively to \(X^{1/50+o(1)}\).
This proves the Haar evaluation with the claimed accuracy.

At an inert prime, the mass for both valuations to be multiples of
three is
\[
 \delta_p=1-\frac2p+\frac{2(1-p^{-1})}{p^3-1}.
\]
The first term is the mass with both endpoints units. In either
of the two disjoint nonunit cases the nonunit valuation is a
positive multiple of three, giving the geometric sum. This works
also at two.

At three, two units congruent to \(\pm1\bmod9\) cannot be
consecutive. If \(v_3(n)=j\in3\mathbb Z_{>0}\), then
\(n+1\equiv1\bmod9\), so \(\chi(d(n+1))\) must be \(1\).
For fixed sign on the divisible side the mass of its valuation
and unit is \(3^{-j-2}\). If instead \(v_3(n+1)=j>0\),
then \(n\equiv-1\bmod9\), so \(\chi(d(n))\) must be \(-1\),
with the same mass on the divisible side. Summing the divisible-side
masses in either case gives
\[
 \sigma_3=\sum_{\ell\ge1}3^{-3\ell-2}=\frac1{234}.
\]
Before the \(3\)-adic test, the joint inert valuation law is
invariant under swapping the endpoints, by \(x\mapsto -x-1\)
in each inert factor. Under that law the mass with
\(\chi(d(n+1))=1\) plus the mass with \(\chi(d(n))=-1\) equals
the entire inert mass. Hence the *coupled* Haar average is
\(\sigma_3\prod_{p\in I_y}\delta_p\). It follows that
\[
 \sum_{X\le n<2X}\mathcal D(n)\mathcal D(n+1)
 =X\lambda_y^2\sigma_3\prod_{p\in I_y}\delta_p
       \bigl(1+O_K(L^{-1/2})\bigr).
\]
Indeed the errors of the CRT evaluation and the large-record
discard are smaller than every fixed inverse logarithmic power
on the scale \(X\), and the displayed main term is of order
\(X/L\) by the constant calculation below.

## The constant and the count

The split-prime Dirichlet series \(F_H(s)\) has the exact identity
\[
 F_H(s)^2=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi)(1-3^{-s})
                       \prod_{p\ {\rm inert}}(1-p^{-2s}).
\]
This follows prime by prime, with the split Euler factors both
contributing \((1-p^{-s})^{-1}\). The normalization
\(\Gamma(1/2)^2=\pi\) gives
\[
 c_H^2=\frac{L(1,\chi)}{\pi}(1-1/3)
                       \prod_{p\ {\rm inert}}(1-p^{-2}).
\]
Consequently the combined inert factor in the pair constant is
\[
 \frac{(1-p^{-2})\delta_p}{(1-p^{-1})^2}
     =1-\frac{2p}{p^3-1}.
\]
All factors are positive and \(1+O(p^{-2})\). The tail beyond
\(y\) changes the coefficient by \(O(1/y)\).
Finally \(L(1,\chi)=\int_0^1(1+x+x^2)^{-1}\,dx=\pi/(3\sqrt3)\).
The remaining prefactor
\(4L(1,\chi)(1-1/3)^{-1}\sigma_3/\pi\) is \(1/(117\sqrt3)\).

With (C1) through (C4) verified, the comparison theorem replaces
the model by \(g(n)g(n+1)\) on the dyadic interval with error
\(O_K(X/L^{1+\delta})\), where
\(\delta=1/(8192\varphi(18))=1/49152\). This dominates the
model evaluation error. Sum the dyadic estimate above \(\sqrt X\)
and bound lower endpoints trivially by \(O(\sqrt X)\).
The sum of the dyadic main terms has coefficient \(C\) on
\(X/\log X\), with conversion error \(O(X/(\log X)^2)\).
The cube exclusion costs \(O(X^{1/3})\) on the whole interval
and preserves the saving. This proves the theorem.
