# One norm class on three cubic twists

A coherent tangent progression gives a nonzero
Selmer class shared by three distinct cubic twists.
The class comes from rational norm points on two
twists. On the third twist, the corresponding
generic genus-one curve has index three and splits
over the same pure cubic field as the extra section.

## Three local images in a common quadratic algebra

Take the squarefree tangent prime family of
[CT4](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_NORM_RANK_AND_CONDUCTORS.md#squarefree-dyadic-fields-and-rank-two-conductors)
with \(h=6k\), \(k\ge5\) prime,
\(R=3p^4-6p^2h^2-h^4\) squarefree, \(D=-R\)
and \(K_D=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\). Write
\[
        b_e=4^e(3k)^2D,\quad
        E_e:\ y^2=x^3+b_e,\quad E_e':\ y^2=x^3-27b_e,
                      \qquad e=0,1,2 .
\]
Let \(\phi_e:E_e\to E_e'\) and
\(\widehat\phi_e:E_e'\to E_e\) be the dual degree-three
isogenies, and write \(S_e=\operatorname{Sel}_{\widehat\phi_e}(E_e')\).
Identify the three ambient cohomology groups with
\[
        \{[\gamma]\in K_D^\times/K_D^{\times3}:
                           N\gamma\in\mathbb Q^{\times3}\}.
\]
With the sign of \(\sqrt D\) fixed, the Kummer map from
\(E_e\) in this identification is represented by
\(y+2^e(3k)\sqrt D\) on the ordinary affine points.
This convention is the conjugate choice of
[Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman, Proposition 18 and Remark 19](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.05759#S3.SS1).
The norm condition is equivalently the
anti-invariant cubeclass condition.

**Proposition CS1.** The local images in this
common identification have the following description.

- At \(2\), the image is zero for \(e=0,1\) and
  the full one-dimensional anti-invariant space
  for \(e=2\).
- At \(3\), let \(e_*\) be the unique \(e\in\{0,1,2\}\)
  such that \((2^e k p^2)^2\equiv7\bmod9\).
  The image is full for \(e=e_*\) and is the same
  one-dimensional line for both other \(e\).
  With \(\lambda=\sqrt{-3}\) and \(U_i=1+\lambda^i
  \mathcal O_{\mathbb Q_3(\zeta_3)}\), that line
  is the image of \(U_3\).
- At \(k\), the image is zero when \(k\equiv2\bmod3\).
  When \(k\equiv1\bmod3\), choose a split component
  and a root \(s_0=\sqrt{b_0}\) in \(\mathbb Q_k\).
  The image in \(\mathbb Q_k^\times/\mathbb Q_k^{\times3}\)
  is the line generated by \(2^{e+1}s_0\).
  All three lines are distinct when \(2\) is a
  noncube modulo \(k\), and agree otherwise.
  Their intersection with the unit line is zero.
- At each prime \(q\ge5\) dividing \(R\) the
  ambient anti-invariant space is zero.
  At all other finite \(q\notin\{2,3,k\}\) the
  curve has good reduction and the image is the
  anti-invariant unit space. The archimedean
  cohomology is zero.

At \(2\), \(R\equiv3\bmod8\), so \(D\equiv5\bmod8\)
and \(K_{D,2}\) is the unramified quadratic extension.
Its anti-invariant classes are the units detected in
\(\mathbb F_4^\times\). Write \(b_e=4^e B\) with
\(B=(3k)^2D\equiv5\bmod8\). A nonintegral abscissa
has even valuation, and after removing a rational
cube the descent value reduces to a rational unit,
so gives zero. At \(e=0\), an even integral abscissa
would give \(y^2\equiv5\bmod8\). At \(e=1\), an even
abscissa \(x=2u\) would give
\((y/2)^2=2u^3+B\), also impossible modulo eight.
Odd abscissas in either case give a trivial
residue class. At \(e=2\), the abscissa \(x=4\)
has a point with \(y=4v\) and \(v^2=4+B\equiv1\bmod8\).
Writing \(\tau=(\sqrt D-1)/2\), the descent value
divided by the cube \(8\) is
\((v+3k)/2+3k\tau\). The residue of \(\tau\)
generates \(\mathbb F_4/\mathbb F_2\)
and this is a nontrivial cubeclass.

At \(3\), \(R/3\equiv p^4\bmod9\) and the local
quadratic field is \(\mathbb Q_3(\zeta_3)\).
Write \(b_e=27 B_e\), so
\(B_e\equiv-(2^e k p^2)^2\bmod9\), and fix
\(\sqrt{b_e}=3\lambda u\) with \(u\) a rational
\(3\)-adic unit. Here \(U_1^3=U_4\), since
\(U_1=\mu_3 U_2\) and the formal logarithm
sends \(U_2^3\) to \(\lambda^4\mathcal O\).
The anti-invariant part has dimension two,
from the odd terms at orders one and three.
A cubeclass may be tested on the ratio of a
descent value and its conjugate, since this
squares its anti-invariant class.
For a point with \(3\)-adic unit abscissa,
the ordinate is a unit and the ratio has
a nonzero term of order three, so gives
the line from \(U_3\). Such a point exists
at \(x=1\). A nonintegral abscissa gives
a trivial class by the same ratio, whose
difference from one has order at least four.
An abscissa of valuation greater than one
is impossible since it would make \(v_3(y^2)=3\).
For \(x=3u_0\), a point requires \(u_0\equiv1\bmod3\),
and \(y^2=27(u_0^3+B_e)\). If
\((2^e k p^2)^2\equiv4\bmod9\), then
\((u_0^3+B_e)/3\equiv2\bmod3\), so there is no
such point. If the square is \(1\bmod9\),
the ordinate of any such point has valuation
at least three. In that case \(y/\sqrt{b_e}\)
has order at least three in \(\lambda\), so the
ratio again lies in the line. If the square is
\(7\bmod9\), \(x=3\) gives a point with ordinate
of valuation two, since the reduced square after
dividing by \(3^4\) is \(1\bmod3\).
The ratio has a surviving term of order one,
which gives the full anti-invariant space.

At \(k\), \(D\equiv-3p^4\bmod k\). In the split
case use Kummer evaluation at \((0,\sqrt{b_e})\)
to get the generator \(2^{e+1}s_0\), or its inverse.
The description by the order-three points for
bad reduction away from \(3\) follows from
[Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman, Propositions 29–30](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.05759#S4.SS1).
In the inert case there is no such order-three
point, so the image is zero. The split generator
has valuation one, and its unit changes by \(2\)
at each step. This proves the line comparison.
The unit description at good primes is Proposition 29.
At a ramified quadratic place away from \(3\)
the conjugation acts trivially both on valuations
and on residue unit classes, so the
anti-invariant space vanishes there.

## The common global class

**Proposition CS2.** The nonzero tangent class
\([\alpha_-]=[\alpha_+]\) belongs to all three
\(S_e\) for all sufficiently large squarefree
prime triangles of either sign. It is realized
by the displayed rational norm points for
\(e=0,1\). On the third twist it is a Selmer class.
If \(k\) is inert, or if \(k\) splits and \(2\)
is a noncube modulo \(k\), the full intersection
\(\bigcap_e S_e\) is the space of virtual-unit
classes with zero local class at \(2,k\) and
local class in the \(U_3\) line at \(3\).

For \(e=0\) this is the class of the consecutive
rational points. The rational third-colour
point on \(E_1\) gives
\([\alpha_{13}]=[\alpha_-]+[\alpha_+]=2[\alpha_-]\)
by [CT1](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_NORM_RANK_AND_CONDUCTORS.md#the-tangent-identity-and-the-lost-cubic-direction).
Thus the line is in both \(S_0,S_1\).
Its valuations are divisible by three everywhere.
In particular at \(k\) it is in the common
unit part of the local images, which is zero.
At least one of the first two twists requires
the \(U_3\) line at \(3\), and the third twist
always contains that line. At \(2\) its zero
class also persists. All other local images
are identical, so the class belongs to \(S_2\).
It is nonzero in the imaginary case by CT4.
In the real case a cube root would be integral
of norm \(p_1p_2\), and could not be rational.
For a nonrational cube in a real quadratic
field the doubled irrational coefficient
has absolute value at least \(d/4\) by
[RV1](REAL_COHERENT_NORM_VIRTUAL_CLASS_RANK.md).
Here that coefficient is \(h\asymp d^{1/4}\),
which is smaller for large \(d\).
The full intersection statement follows
by intersecting the listed local conditions:
the entire local class is zero at \(k\)
under each of its two stated hypotheses.
At the other bad places all valuation
classes are automatically zero.

## A Selmer ratio and a conductor count

**Corollary CS3.** Put \(\varepsilon_D=\mathbf1_{D<0}\)
and \(\varepsilon_k=\mathbf1_{k\equiv2\bmod3}\).
Then
\[
 \dim S_e-\dim\operatorname{Sel}_{\phi_e}(E_e)
 =-\mathbf1_{e<2}+\mathbf1_{e=e_*}
                    -\varepsilon_k-\varepsilon_D .
\]
For sufficiently large \(Y\) there are
\(\gg Y^{1/5}/(\log Y)^4\) distinct conductors
at most \(Y\) of curves of the third twist
\(E_2\) with \(\dim S_2\ge1\) and
\(\dim\operatorname{Sel}_{\phi_2}(E_2)\ge3\).

The local ratio of \(\widehat\phi_e\) at a place
is its local image size divided by its kernel
size. The kernel has size three when
\(-3D\) is a square in that completion,
otherwise size one. It has size three
at \(2,3,k\). At the archimedean place
the local ratio is \(1/3\) if \(D<0\)
and one otherwise. At all other places
the ratio is one by good reduction or
the ramified calculation. The displayed
formula follows from the local image sizes
of CS1 and the global isogeny-Selmer ratio
in [Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman, Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.05759).
There is no rational three-torsion on either
model: at any prime \(q\ge5\) dividing \(R\)
the coefficient has valuation one, so it
is not a square, and its multiple by
\(-4\) is not a cube. These exclude the
two possibilities in the three-division
polynomial of a Mordell curve.

Use the imaginary sign, with \(p\equiv1\)
and \(k\equiv2\bmod9\). This is a compatible
fixed congruence in the sieve of CT4:
the three vertex primes are units at
\(3\), and \(v_3(R)=1\) is unchanged.
It gives \(e_*=1\), \(\varepsilon_k=\varepsilon_D=1\).
The ratio is \(3^{-2}\) at \(e=2\),
and CS2 gives the lower bound for \(S_2\).
At every \(q>3\) the conductor is unchanged
by this fixed twist, since the valuation of
\(b_e\) there and the reduction types agree.
The factors at \(2,3\) are bounded.
The bounded conductor fibres of CT5
therefore give the claimed count.

## The explicit genus-one curve on the third twist

**Proposition CS4.** In the notation of CS2 put
\(\alpha_-=a+\nu\sqrt D\), where \(a=u_-/2\)
and \(\nu=h/2\). The plane cubic
\[
 \nu U^3+3aU^2V+3\nu DUV^2+aD V^3=4\nu W^3            \tag{1}
\]
is everywhere locally soluble for the squarefree
prime triangles of CS2. It has a point over
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)\). Over the function field
of the cubic tangent surface its generic
version has period and index exactly three.

The left side is the \(\sqrt D\)-coefficient
of \(\alpha_-(U+V\sqrt D)^3\). Its three roots
as a binary cubic are distinct, since it
is the difference of two nonzero cubes
of independent linear forms over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}\). Thus (1) is smooth.
For \(W\ne0\) it represents the class
\([\alpha_-]\) on \(E_2\), by reading the
rational part as the ordinate and taking
the norm to get the abscissa.
Conversely a point in the Kummer image
gives this equation by lifting a cube root.
One may take the elliptic point outside
the finitely many special points of the
Kummer formula over a local field.
This is also the covering construction in
[Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman, Proposition 23](https://arxiv.org/html/1610.05759#S3.SS2).
By CS2 the covering is locally soluble.
The point \(U=\sqrt[3]4,\ V=0,\ W=1\)
is defined over the indicated pure cubic
field and belongs to every such curve.

For the generic assertion take \(p=t,h=1\),
\(D=-R(t,1)\) and \(\alpha_-=(u_-+\sqrt D)/2\).
Its norm is \(t^3(t-1)^3\). It is not a cube
in \(\mathbb Q(t,\sqrt D)\): a cube root would
be regular at all finite places and have
pole order at most one at both infinities
of the quartic curve. It would be a
polynomial in \(t\), since \(\sqrt D\)
has pole order two at both infinities.
The one-sided zero at \(t=1\) rules
this out. A rational point of (1) with
\(W=0\) would make
\(\alpha_-(U+V\sqrt D)^3\) rational with
cube norm, hence itself a rational cube.
This is impossible. At \(W\ne0\), it would
give an affine rational point on the
twist of constant \(c=16\).
That twist has rank zero by
[TS4](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_ELLIPTIC_SURFACE.md#the-ranks-of-all-fixed-constant-sextic-twists)
and is torsion-free over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)\)
by TS1, so this is impossible as well.
A smooth genus-one curve with no rational
point and a point over a cubic extension
has period and index three. The
specialized local solubility statement
does not imply specialized global
insolubility.

For \((p,k)=(53,5),(4057,211),(3617,431)\),
the three residues \((2^e k p^2)^2\bmod9\) are,
respectively, \((7,1,4),(4,7,1),(1,4,7)\).
At \(k=211\), \(2^{70}\equiv196\bmod211\),
so all three local lines in CS1 are distinct.
The first example has
\(R=7692843=3\cdot157\cdot16333\);
the other two squarefree controls are in CT4.

## An explicit failure of the Hasse principle

**Theorem CS5.** The coherent triangle on
\((p-h,p,p+h)=(11,17,23)\) gives the plane cubic
\[
 U^3+2204U^2V-560529UV^2-137267324V^3=4W^3.            \tag{2}
\]
It is smooth and has points over every completion
of \(\mathbb Q\) and over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)\).
It has no rational point.

Here \(h=6\), so \(k=1\), and
\(R=186843=3\cdot61\cdot1021\).
The local proof of CS1 and CS2 works unchanged
with \(k=1\) and no exceptional place \(k\):
all primes \(q>3\) outside \(R\) give
good reduction. The norm class is nonzero
because a principal supporting quotient
would give an integral cube root of norm
\(11\cdot17< R/4\) in the imaginary field.
The trace in (1) gives \(a=2204,\nu=3\);
dividing that equation by three gives (2).
Thus local solubility and the cubic point
follow from CS4. To establish global
insolubility it remains to show that
\[
                  E_2:\quad y^2=x^3-26905392
\]
has no nonzero rational point. The
following short descent verifies this
unconditionally.

Let \(\rho^3=124562=2\cdot61\cdot1021\),
\(\theta=6\rho\), and \(T=\mathbb Q(\rho)\).
The two-descent map is the class of
\(x-\theta\) in \(T^\times/T^{\times2}\),
with norm a square. For completeness it is
a homomorphism because along a line the
three abscissa differences from \(\theta\)
multiply to a square, by evaluating the
line-intersection polynomial at \(\theta\).
Its kernel is \(2E_2(\mathbb Q)\):
if \(x-\theta\) is a square, choose
the three conjugate roots \(r_i\) of
\(x-\theta_i\) with product \(-y\).
Writing \(s_1=\sum r_i\) and
\(s_2=\sum_{i<j} r_i r_j\), the point
\((x+s_2,-y-s_1s_2)\) is rational and
doubles to \((x,y)\). The factors
\(x+s_2-\theta_i=(r_i+r_j)(r_i+r_k)\)
give its equation, and the tangent line
of slope \(-s_1\) verifies the doubling.
The same Kummer map is valid over
\(\mathbb Q_2\), where the cubic field
is still a field.

The valuations of \(x-\theta\) are all
even. At \(2,61,1021\) the pure cubic
extension is totally ramified by the
Eisenstein equation, and \(3\) is also
totally ramified with residue degree
one, verified below. Thus the norm
condition gives the valuation parity
there. At any other prime where \(x\)
is integral, the division polynomial
\(X^3-\theta^3\) has distinct roots
modulo the prime. A prime ideal
dividing \(x-\theta\) must be the
unique degree-one ideal from the
root \(x\bmod q\); its valuation is
even by the norm. At primes where
\(x\) is nonintegral, \(y^2=x^3-26905392\)
forces \(v_q(x)\) even, and all
valuations of \(x-\theta\) above \(q\)
are even. The certified class group
of \(T\) is \([3,3]\), so the ideal
square condition leaves only a
unit class. The field has unit
rank one, and the positive norm
condition leaves either \(1\) or
the norm-one fundamental unit
class \([\epsilon]\).

At \(2\) the transformed good model
has \(x=4X,\ y=8Y+4\) and equation
\(Y^2+Y=X^3-420397\). Its group over
\(\mathbb F_2\) has order three, so
multiplying by three carries any
point into the formal subgroup
without changing its two-descent
class. There the old abscissa is
either odd integral or has negative
even valuation; valuation one is
impossible in the original equation
by parity of \(v_2(y^2)\).
For an odd abscissa the equation
requires \(x\equiv1\bmod8\).
For a nonintegral one, write
\(x=2^{-2m}w\) with \(m\ge1\);
the equation similarly requires
\(w\equiv1\bmod8\).
In the completion \(T_2\), \(\rho\)
is an Eisenstein uniformizer and
a unit is a square exactly when
it is a square modulo \(\rho^7\),
by Hensel's lemma and
\(v_\rho(2)=3\).
Thus an odd abscissa gives class
\(1-6\rho\), and the nonintegral
case gives a square after scaling
by \(2^{2m}\).

Residues modulo \(\rho^7\) can be
stored as triples of coefficients
in \(1,\rho,\rho^2\) modulo \(8,4,4\).
The square units have triples
\[
                 (1,0,0),(1,2,1),(1,2,2),(5,0,3).
\]
The norm-one unit and its product
with \(1-6\rho\) have residues
\((3,3,0)\) and \((3,1,2)\).
Neither is a square. Hence
\([\epsilon]\) is excluded by
the local image at \(2\).
The two-descent image is trivial,
so the rational rank is zero.
The good reduction group at \(2\)
has order three, and
prime-to-two torsion injects into it.
There is no rational two-torsion
by irreducibility of \(X^3-\theta^3\)
and no rational three-torsion by
the coefficient valuation one at
\(61\). Thus \(E_2(\mathbb Q)=\{O\}\).

The certificate uses the following
exact PARI/GP replay. The
`bnfcertify` return value \(1\)
certifies the class and unit data,
and the remaining operations are
finite residue calculations.
~~~gp
m=124562; f=x^3-m; rho=Mod(x,f);
B=bnfinit(f,1);
print([B.disc,B.cyc,bnfcertify(B)]);
P=idealprimedec(B,3);
print(vector(#P,i,[P[i].e,P[i].f]));
u=B.fu[1]; if(norm(u)==-1,u=-u);
red(a)=vector(3,i,lift(polcoeff(lift(a),i-1))%[8,4,4][i]);
S=List();
forstep(i=1,7,2,for(j=0,3,for(k=0,3,listput(S,red((i+j*rho+k*rho^2)^2)))));
print(Set(Vec(S)));
print([red(u),red(1-6*rho),red(u*(1-6*rho))]);
~~~

The discriminant and class certificate
output is \([-418923679788,[3,3],1]\),
and the prime above \(3\) has
\((e,f)=(3,1)\).
This proves the global obstruction
by two-descent without using a
rank estimate from point search.

## A Mordell admissibility test over the fixed splitting field

**Proposition CS6.** For all sufficiently large squarefree
pairs of CS2, let \(M=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2,\zeta_3)\)
and let \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q(\zeta_3))\)
send \(r=2^{-1/3}\) to \(\zeta_3r\).
Then the plane cubic (1) has a rational point
if and only if
\[
                P_-\in(\sigma-[\zeta_3])E_0(M).
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
The element \(P_-\) therefore defines the relative
obstruction in cyclic group cohomology for the
twisted operator \([\zeta_3]^{-1}\sigma\).

Restriction of the norm-cube classes from \(K_D\)
to \(K_D M\) is injective. Indeed \(K_D
e\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\)
in this large squarefree family, so \(K_D\cap M=\mathbb Q\).
Over \(K_D(\zeta_3)\) the cyclic cubic extension
is generated by a cube root of \(2\), and the
kernel of restriction on cubeclasses is
generated by the class of \(2\). Restriction
from \(K_D\) to this quadratic intermediate
field is injective by the norm. The class
of \(2\) in \(K_D\) is invariant under conjugation.
It has zero intersection with the anti-invariant
subspace, proving the asserted injection.

The isomorphism \(E_2\to E_0\) over \(M\) is
\((x,y)\mapsto(r^4x,y/4)\). It identifies the
rational points of \(E_2\) with the points \(Q\)
of \(E_0(M)\) satisfying
\(\sigma Q=[\zeta_3]Q\) and \(\tau Q=Q\), with
\(\tau\) complex conjugation. The descent values
under this isomorphism agree over \(K_D M\),
since the rational factor \(4\) is a cube there.
Put \(\lambda=1-[\zeta_3]\).
Over \(M\) the dual isogeny image in \(E_0(M)\)
is \(\lambda E_0(M)\): its source is isomorphic
to \(E_0\) over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), and the
degree-three CM endomorphism is a unit times
\(\lambda\). By injectivity of restriction, the
existence of a rational point on (1) is
equivalent to the existence of such a \(Q\)
congruent to \(P_-\) modulo \(\lambda E_0(M)\).

For all sufficiently large parameters \(E_0(M)\)
is torsion-free. Take a sufficiently large
prime \(q\) with \(v_q(R)=1\).
The type II reduction over \(M\) at \(q\)
has trivial component group and additive
nonsingular group. Prime-to-\(q\) torsion
injects there and vanishes. All rational
torsion over the fixed field \(M\) has
order prime to \(q\) for sufficiently
large \(q\), by reduction at a fixed place
over \(5\) as in TS3. Thus none remains.

If \(P_-=Q+\lambda A\) and \(\sigma Q=[\zeta_3]Q\),
applying \(\sigma-[\zeta_3]\) and cancelling
\(\lambda\) gives \(P_-=(\sigma-[\zeta_3])A\).
Conversely for such an \(A\), \(Q_0=P_--\lambda A\)
has the required \(\sigma\)-eigenvalue.
So does \(\tau Q_0\) because complex conjugation
inverts both \(\sigma\) and \([\zeta_3]\).
Then \(Q=-Q_0-\tau Q_0\) is fixed by \(\tau\)
and is still congruent to \(P_-\) modulo
\(\lambda\), since \(\overline\lambda\)
generates the same ideal and \(3\) is divisible
by \(\lambda\). This proves (3). The norm
of \(P_-\) for the twisted cyclic operator
is zero by \(1+\zeta_3+\zeta_3^2=0\),
and the coboundary image is
\((\sigma-[\zeta_3])E_0(M)\).
This identifies the relative obstruction.

## The remaining specialization question

It is open in this construction whether infinitely
many of the squarefree prime-\(k\) pairs give
nontrivial rational torsors from (1).
CS3 controls the Selmer dimensions and CS5 gives
one failure of the Hasse principle with \(k=1\).
A rank-zero result on infinitely many of the
correctly marked third twists would suffice,
as would a direct obstruction to realizing
the shared class in their rational Kummer
images. The generic rank-zero result of TS4
does not provide that specialized bound.
