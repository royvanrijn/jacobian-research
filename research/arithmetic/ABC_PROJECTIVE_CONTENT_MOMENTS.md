# Moments of the content of projective row tuples

The inverse-square bound for the gcd of the projective
minors gives uniform moments for residue classes. It also
bounds high-content tuples when the modulus is chosen
after the rows are given. A fixed first pair supplies
the divisor catalogue at subpower cost. A catalogue
attached to just the first row can save the cost of
the second anchor.

## Projective classes from one congruence

Let \(\mathcal F\) be a family of \(N\) distinct positive
primitive rows \(A+B=C\), with maximum complete value
between \(X^{h_0}\) and \(X^H\), and individual radicals
at most \(n^{a_0}\), where \(n=X^L\). Use the height-first
quantifiers of (DL4). For two ordered rows put
\[
                [i,j]= A_i C_j-A_j C_i .
\]
The signs of the minors change if a different pair of
complete roles is used. All the minors of distinct rows
are nonzero because the complete rows are primitive.
Use the homogeneous region
\(\|\mathcal S(A,C)\|_\infty\le1\) with real invertible
\(\mathcal S\) and area parameter
\(\mathcal B=|\det\mathcal S|^{-1}\). Thus
\(|[i,j]|\le2\mathcal B\).

For a positive integer \(D\), reduction modulo \(D\)
gives a projective class of each primitive pair
\((A,C)\). Two reductions lie in the same class
if and only if \(D\mid[i,j]\). Indeed a primitive
pair modulo \(D\) is unimodular. Bézout permits
an element of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/D)\) taking
it to \((1,0)\). In those coordinates, any other
primitive pair with zero determinant has second
coordinate zero and first coordinate a unit, so it
is a unit multiple of \((1,0)\). This proves
transitivity also for composite \(D\).

**LC1.** For every fixed \(\eta,\varepsilon>0\) and
all sufficiently small fixed \(L>0\), each
projective class contains at most
\[
           K(D)\ll 1+
                  n^\eta X^\varepsilon
                       \sqrt{\mathcal B/D}
                                                               \tag{LC1}
\]
eligible rows. The bound is uniform in the modulus
and the class. If \(D>2\mathcal B\), each class has
at most one row.

If a class has at least two rows, its content, the
gcd of its pair minors, is a multiple of \(D\).
Apply (DL4) to the family in that class. If there
are zero or one rows, no count is needed beyond
the leading one. In particular large residue
classes are excluded even when their content
is not generated by a prescribed divisor record.
[Projective-minor gcd bound (DL4)](ABC_DIVISOR_LATTICE_LINE_OCCUPANCY.md#dl4-the-gcd-of-the-actual-projective-minors)

Writing \(m_\omega\) for the size of class \(\omega\),
one immediate consequence is
\[
 \sum_\omega (m_\omega)_k
       \le N\min(N,K(D))^{k-1},
 \qquad
 \#\{\omega:m_\omega>0\}\ge N/\min(N,K(D)),
\]
where \((m)_k=m(m-1)\cdots(m-k+1)\) counts ordered
distinct choices. In particular, for a family of
size \(n^{d+o(1)}\) and
\(D\ge\mathcal B n^{-v}\) with fixed \(v\ge0\), the
number of occupied classes has exponent at least
\((d-v/2)_+\), up to arbitrarily small losses.
The usual geometry bound has \(v\) in place of
\(v/2\) in this comparison.

## Content moments with a moving modulus

Fix \(k\ge3\) independently of both limits. For an
ordered tuple \(\boldsymbol i=(i_1,\ldots,i_k)\) of
distinct rows, set
\[
       G(\boldsymbol i)=\gcd_{a<b}|[i_a,i_b]| .
\]
For a real depth \(0<D_0\le2\mathcal B\), let
\(T_k(D_0)\) count tuples with \(G(\boldsymbol i)\ge D_0\).
Use \(K_0\) for the upper bound in (LC1) with \(D_0\)
in place of the modulus.

**LC2.** Uniformly in the depth,
\[
  T_k(D_0)\ll
     X^\varepsilon N(N-1)\min(N,K_0)^{k-2}.
                                                               \tag{LC2}
\]
In particular, if \(N\le n^d\) and
\(D_0\ge\mathcal B n^{-v}\) for fixed \(v\ge0\),
this gives normalized exponent
\[
               2d+(k-2)\min(d,v/2)+\eta
\]
up to the arbitrary inner allowance.

Fix the first two rows. The common content divides
their nonzero minor, an integer of magnitude at most
\(2X^{2H}\). The divisor bound supplies \(X^{o_X(1)}\)
possible contents, uniformly in that pair. For each
possible \(D\ge D_0\), the remaining rows lie in
the first row's projective class modulo \(D\).
By (LC1) this gives at most \(\min(N,K_0)\)
choices at each of the other \(k-2\) slots.
Choose the internal allowances in (LC1) smaller
in terms of \(k\) before fixing the budget. The
divisor factor and the height allowances can then
be absorbed in \(X^\varepsilon\).

One can also prescribe a catalogue \(\mathcal Q_i\)
of positive moduli for every possible first row
\(i\). Count tuples for which some member of
\(\mathcal Q_{i_1}\), at least \(D_0\), divides
every pair minor. Then the bound is
\[
 \sum_i \sum_{\substack{D\in\mathcal Q_i\\D\ge D_0}}
             \bigl(\min(N,K(D))-1\bigr)_{k-1},
                                                               \tag{LC3}
\]
where the class upper bounds are rounded up to
integers and may be truncated to \(N\). In particular,
if \(|\mathcal Q_i|\le n^c\) for every \(i\),
the exponent is at most
\[
                       d+c+(k-1)\min(d,v/2)+\eta.
\]
Here there is no second anchor cost. The catalogue
may depend on the exact first row and the height
but is fixed before choosing the remaining rows.
For instance all divisors of one polynomial-height
integer assigned to the first row give \(c=o_X(1)\)
in the normalization. The anchored exponent improves
on (LC2) if \(c+v/2<d\). When catalogues are not
uniform, the actual sum in (LC3) charges each
catalogue and its depths together.

There is also a normalized positive moment form.
Put \(Z(\boldsymbol i)=G(\boldsymbol i)/(2\mathcal B)\),
so \(0<Z\le1\). For every fixed \(p>0\),
\[
 \frac{1}{N^k}\sum_{\boldsymbol i} Z(\boldsymbol i)^p
       \ll_{k,p,H,a_0,\eta,L,\varepsilon}
       n^\eta X^\varepsilon\,
            N^{-\min(2p,k-2)}(1+\log N).
                                                               \tag{LC4}
\]
In fact (LC2) and the trivial bound \(N^k\) give
the normalized tail bound
\(\min\{1,K N^{-(k-2)}z^{-(k-2)/2}\}\) for
\(0<z\le1\), with \(K\ll n^\eta X^\varepsilon\).
Integrate this tail against \(p z^{p-1}\),
using the trivial bound below \(N^{-2}\) and
the second bound above \(N^{-2}\). The logarithm
is only needed at \(p=(k-2)/2\). This calculation
uses actual integer contents; no statistical
independence of the different pair minors is
assumed.

## An angular version with no window catalogue

Suppose \(C_i\le X\) and write \(r_i=A_i/C_i\).
For \(0<w\le1\), restrict to tuples with
\(\max_{a,b}|r_{i_a}-r_{i_b}|\le w\).
The first row fixes a window
\(|A/C-r_{i_1}|\le w\) containing all the other
rows. The homogeneous transform
\[
                       \bigl((A-r_{i_1}C)/(wX),\ C/X\bigr)
\]
has area parameter \(\mathcal B_w=wX^2\).
Thus (LC1)--(LC3) have the same bounds with
\(\mathcal B_w\) in place of \(\mathcal B\).
There is no cost for enumerating window centres.
In (LC2), \(N(N-1)\) can be replaced by the
actual number of ordered distinct first pairs
at distance at most \(w\) whose minor has a
divisor at least \(D_0\). In (LC3) the first
row may instead have a catalogue of pairs
\((D,w)\); use its associated \(\mathcal B_w\)
in each summand. All these statements retain
the original radical caps and any original
least-repair restrictions.

The estimates apply to row tuples forced to
have substantial common projective content.
They do not force that content at a balanced
occurrence count. A global application needs
an arithmetic source of sufficiently many
high-content tuples, or inexpensive modulus
records giving a bound on their total fibre
size. In particular the raw bounded diffuse
spectra in the determinant test of (DL3)
still do not produce a near-area modulus
from cheap divisor records alone.
[Area and record-price obstruction (DL3)](ABC_DIVISOR_LATTICE_LINE_OCCUPANCY.md#dl3-summing-actual-records)

## A higher-order projective larger-sieve inequality

**LC5.** Keep the genuine three-cap rows of (LC1), and
fix \(k\ge3\). Let \(\mathcal H\) be any nonempty set of
ordered distinct \(k\)-tuples of them, with
\(P=|\mathcal H|\) and with \(A_0\) distinct ordered first
pairs occurring in \(\mathcal H\). Define
\[
                       J=(P/A_0)^{1/(k-2)}.
\]
Use the area parameter \(\mathcal B\) of their common
homogeneous box. One may instead assume each tuple is in
an angular window of width \(w\) about one of its first
two rows and take \(\mathcal B=wX^2\), where \(C\le X\).
Then for every \(\eta,\varepsilon>0\), uniformly at
sufficiently small fixed budgets and large heights,
\[
 \frac{1}{P}\sum_{\boldsymbol i\in\mathcal H}
                   \log_n G(\boldsymbol i)
       \ \le\ \log_n(2\mathcal B)-2\log_n J
                              +\eta+\varepsilon/L .
                                                               \tag{LC5}
\]
All choices of centres in the angular version are fixed
by the first pair and cost no extra catalogue. The
inequality is independent of how the later rows in
\(\mathcal H\) were selected.

For each prescribed first pair enumerate all divisors
of its nonzero minor as in (LC2). For any one such
divisor at least \(2\mathcal B z\), all extending rows
lie in its common class in the same homogeneous box
or in the window fixed by the first pair. If the two
anchor windows both qualify, count their union using
at most a factor two. Thus for \(0<z\le1\) the fraction
of \(\mathcal H\) with \(G/(2\mathcal B)\ge z\) is at most
\[
    \min\!\left(1,
          O\!\left(n^{\eta'}X^{\varepsilon'}
                         J^{-(k-2)}z^{-(k-2)/2}\right)\right)
\]
with arbitrary small fixed allowances. Integrate
against \(p z^{p-1}\) for any fixed \(0<p<(k-2)/2\),
splitting at \(z=J^{-2}\). This bounds the mean
of \((G/(2\mathcal B))^p\) by
\(O_{k,p}(J^{-2p}n^{\eta'}X^{\varepsilon'})\).
Jensen for the logarithm and then a choice of
\(\eta',\varepsilon'\) in terms of \(p,\eta,\varepsilon\)
give (LC5).

In particular, for all distinct tuples of an \(N\)-row
family, \(J=((N-2)_{k-2})^{1/(k-2)}\asymp_k N\) when
\(N\ge k\). If \(m_{p,j,\rho}\) counts its members in the
projective class \(\rho\) modulo \(p^j\), one obtains an
exact sum over all primes and all depths:
\[
 \sum_p \log_n p\,\sum_{j\ge1}
           \frac{\sum_\rho (m_{p,j,\rho})_k}{(N)_k}
 \ \le\ \log_n(2\mathcal B)-2\log_n N
                          +\eta+\varepsilon/L .
                                                               \tag{LC6}
\]
Indeed the left side is the average of \(\log_n G\)
by transitivity of projective congruence. The harmless
constant comparing \(J\) with \(N\) is absorbed in the
allowance. The sums are finite, since the distinct
pair minors are nonzero integers of polynomial height.
For pairwise coprime composite probes one may equally
keep the sum of their log-weights times the probability
that all \(k\) rows pass the same projective class.
That sum is at most the left side of (LC6).

For example, if at most \(K_i\) classes are permitted
at a probe \(D_i\), its contribution to this latter
sum is at least
\[
       (\log_n D_i)\,
              \frac{K_i(\lfloor N/K_i\rfloor)_k}{(N)_k},
\]
because the integer falling factorial is discretely
convex and unused classes can be padded by zeros.
This gives a sufficient clustering inequality for a
counting saving, using actual three-cap rows. The
ordinary projective larger sieve bounds the log-weight
of common prime tests on each *pair* by the height
product. The inverse-square content estimate instead
subtracts twice the log of the extension scale when
averaging over \(k\)-tuples. The two inequalities
concern different coincidence probabilities. Obtaining
a sufficiently large lower bound on the \(k\)-fold
probabilities from the broad valuation profile remains
a separate arithmetic requirement.
[Zywina, Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.3](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~zywina/papers/Quantitative-HIT.pdf)

For comparison, Bhattacharjee's height refinement
in Theorem 1.1 concerns elements of \(\mathcal O_K\).
Our coordinates are primitive rational slopes and
can have growing denominators. Its additive height
bound does not replace the product-of-heights bound
for their two-point differences here. Equations
(LC5)–(LC6) use the three radical caps through the
count in one projective class.
[Bhattacharjee, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24048v1#S1)
