# Two cubic fields from the two marked directions of a prime progression

The ordinary coherent prime progression gives two
different cubic fields through the two marked elliptic
quotients. The primary reflection is unramified over
the mirror and split at \(3\). The other rational
\(3\)-isogeny on the second quotient gives a cubic field
with the original quadratic resolvent and exact wild
ramification at \(3\). At the outer prime marks its
Frobenius is controlled by the cyclic cubic field of
conductor seven.

## The alternate isogeny mark has a valuation obstruction at three

**Theorem PC1.** In the ordinary prime progression family
of [PQ2](ABC_PRIME_PROGRESSION_BIELLIPTIC_CONDUCTORS.md#specialization-to-genuine-prime-progressions),
retain the independent residue tests at \(73,613\)
and \(h\equiv0\bmod18\), with \(p\) odd and prime to
three. Either unit residue of \(p\bmod3\) is allowed,
as are any admissible residues of the three forms
modulo seven. Let \(D\) be the
fundamental discriminant of the base field. The natural
point on the second elliptic quotient has a noncubic
evaluation in the mirror field for the other rational
\(3\)-isogeny. This evaluation has virtual valuations
at every prime except \(3\), where the two valuations are
\(-1,1\). The transported point on \(E_3^{(D)}\) of PQ3
is indivisible by three, and its marked evaluation for
the dual isogeny on \(E_3^{(D)}\) is a cube.

Use \(t=p/h\), the ordinate \(y\) of PQ1, and
\[
 Z=X_2+28=24+4/t^2,\qquad
 L=4Z-28=68+16/t^2,\qquad Y=4y/t^3 .
\]
Then \(Y^2=Z^3-3L^2\). In the mirror field
\(K'=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3D})\) write
\(\rho=\sqrt{-3}\,y\) and define
\[
 \xi=-L/3+\frac{4\rho}{9t^3},\qquad
 \operatorname N_{K'/\mathbb Q}(\xi)=(Z/3)^3 .
 \tag{1}
\]
In the \(D\)-twist coordinates \(Z_D=DZ\),
\(Y_D=D\sqrt D\,Y\), the isogeny with kernel \(Z_D=0\)
has Kummer evaluation
\(Y_D+\sqrt{-3D}(4Z_D-28D)\).
Dividing by the cube \((\sqrt{-3D})^3\)
gives (1). This uses the general model of
[Cohen–Pazuki](sources/cohen-pazuki-2009-three-isogeny.pdf).

At odd primes outside \(3,7\), use the pole or unit
argument of BT2 in the recentered equation. If \(Z\)
has a pole then the ordinate dominates the linear
term and its valuation is divisible by three.
At an integral point, a vanishing evaluation
forces \(Z\) to vanish by the cube norm. Then \(L\)
is a unit and the opposite branch is a unit.
At \(2\), \(K'\) splits since \(K\) has local
squareclass \(-3\). The two members of the sum in
(1) have valuation two, as \(h\) is even. Their
sums on the two branches have smaller valuation
three, and \(v_2(Z/3)=3\) gives valuation six on
the other branch. At \(7\), if \(h\equiv0\bmod7\)
then \(Z/3\) is a unit and both terms are integral.
Otherwise admissibility forces \(p/h\equiv\pm2,\pm3\)
modulo seven. The sextic of PQ1 then has residue
\(5\bmod7\), and \(K'\) is nonsplit at \(7\) because
\(-3\) is a square there. The cube norm suffices
at its single prime.

At \(3\), \(K'\) splits, and
\(\rho/t^3\) is one of the two roots congruent to
\(\pm3\bmod3^5\), using \(h\equiv0\bmod9\).
The numerator \(4\rho/t^3-3L\) is respectively
congruent to \(-192,-216\bmod3^5\).
After division by nine these give valuations
\(-1,1\). This proves both noncubicity and the
failure of virtuality at three.
The transported point on the isogenous twist has
zero dual-isogeny Kummer evaluation by exactness.
If that image were divisible by three, the
original point would be in the image of the dual
isogeny, since the forward isogeny has no rational
kernel on these twists. This would contradict (1).

## An exact wild cubic companion

**Theorem PC2.** Along PC1, there are two cubic fields
\(L_1,L_2\) with the same genuine prime progression
witness. Their discriminants are
\[
 \operatorname{Disc}(L_1)=-D/3,\qquad
 \operatorname{Disc}(L_2)=81D.
 \tag{2}
\]
Their quadratic resolvents are \(K',K\), respectively.
The prime \(3\) splits completely in \(L_1\) and is
totally ramified in \(L_2\). A monic integral polynomial
for \(L_2\) is
\[
 T^3-36(h^2+6p^2)T+288ph^2+1224p^3 .
 \tag{3}
\]
If the homogeneous sextic radicand is \(D\delta^2\),
the order generated by its root has index
\(24|\delta|\) in the maximal order.

The first field is the unramified mirror of the
quotient direction in PQ2. To obtain the second,
adjoin \(\sqrt[3]{\xi}\) to \(K'(\zeta_3)\).
By (1) it yields a nontrivial cyclic cubic that descends
to the mirror of \(K'\), which is \(K\), by the same
simultaneous conjugation argument as in MT2.
At primes away from \(3\) the extension is unramified
by PC1. The compositum \(K'(\zeta_3)\) is split over
\(K\) at the prime above \(3\), with completion
\(\mathbb Q_3(\zeta_3)\). The valuation of the radical
there is \(\pm2\). Inverting and multiplying by a
cube as necessary permits a radicand of valuation one.
Its cubic root is an Eisenstein uniformizer.
The derivative has valuation \(3\cdot2+2=8\)
in the cubic extension, so the relative different
exponent is eight. The relative conductor exponent
of the cyclic degree-three extension is therefore
four, by the conductor–discriminant formula.
This is precisely the conductor \(9\mathcal O_K\)
at the ramified prime above three. The cubic
conductor-discriminant relation gives \(81D\).
The local decomposition group in its normal closure
is all of \(S_3\), with inertia \(S_3\), proving
total ramification in \(L_2\).

The trace polynomial for (1) is
\(X^3-ZX+2L/3\). It is irreducible by the noncubic
Kummer class, as in PA3. Replacing the root by \(3p\)
times itself gives (3). Its polynomial discriminant
is \(2^6 3^6D\delta^2\), either by substitution or by
\(-27(\xi-\bar\xi)^2\) before scaling. Comparing with
\(81D\) gives the index.

## Two kinds of moving-prime Frobenius

**Theorem PC3.** Impose \(p\equiv1\bmod3\) in PC1.
In \(L_2\) the middle mark always splits, and each
outer mark splits or is inert according to its
Frobenius in the cyclic cubic field of conductor seven.
The outer Artin classes in that fixed cyclic field
can be prescribed independently. Their equality is
equivalent to \(7\) splitting in \(K\). The conductor
formulas of PQ2 hold for all these choices.
If their classes are different, both finite
\(3\)-Selmer parities in PQ3 are reversed.

Simultaneously one can independently prescribe
split or inert in \(L_1\) at each of the three marks.
For each sign and each fixed set of prescriptions,
there are \(\gg X^{1/3}/(\log X)^B\) distinct base
discriminants of absolute size at most \(X\)
and distinct pairs (2), for a fixed \(B>0\).

All marks are prime to the polynomial discriminant
of (3), by the incident prime-unit condition.
At the first mark \(q=p-h\), make the change
\(T=6hZ\) modulo \(q\). The reduced polynomial,
up to the invertible factor \((6h)^3\),
is \(Z^3-7Z+7\). At the third mark it is
\(Z^3-7Z-7\), which gives the same splitting by
negating \(Z\). At the middle mark it is
\(Z^3-Z\). The first two polynomials define the
same cyclic cubic field: \(Z^3-7Z+7\) is irreducible
over \(\mathbb Q\) and has discriminant \(49\).
The cyclic conductor–discriminant formula
identifies the field as \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)^+\).

The Artin classes there are the outer residues
in \((\mathbb Z/7)^\times/\{\pm1\}\).
If the classes are equal, the outer residues
must agree, because opposite residues would
force \(p\equiv0\bmod7\).
Thus equality is equivalent to \(h\equiv0\bmod7\),
and the calculation in PC1 makes this equivalent
to splitting in \(K\).
Any two prescribed classes have representatives
with nonzero sum, so taking \(p,h\) as the half-sum
and half-difference gives admissible linear forms
modulo seven.

In the unequal case the twists at seven are
unramified nonsplit multiplicative, so retain
conductor exponent one. Their Tamagawa numbers
are prime to three, as are those of their isogenous
partners, and the local \(3\)-isogeny ratio is one
instead of \(1/3\). This multiplies each ratio of
PQ3 by three and reverses both parity conclusions.
The other local inputs of PQ2–PQ3 are unchanged.

For \(L_1\) the three residue tests are those of
\(7,2,7\) in PS1. Prescribe the corresponding
classes in the fixed pure cubic splitting fields
at the separate prime forms, while keeping the
chosen congruences at seven for \(L_2\).
Their abelian constraints are both the common
cyclotomic condition modulo three, which is
already imposed. The local factors at the
fixed primes are positive by CRT. At the other
primes the three forms have simultaneous unit
values. Thus Frei–Tinková's theorem, as used in
PS2, gives the same order of prime form count.
The PP2–PP3 multiplicity bound and truncation
apply exactly as in PQ4. This gives the distinct
field and paired counts.

## Fields generated jointly by the two cubics

**Corollary PC4.** The two cubic fields of PC2
have a degree-nine compositum of discriminant
\(-3^{11}|D|^4\), whose normal closure has degree
\(36\) and discriminant \(3^{48}|D|^{18}\).
In the families of PC3 their distinct counts
with absolute discriminant at most \(Y\) are
\(\gg Y^{1/12}/(\log Y)^B\) and
\(\gg Y^{1/54}/(\log Y)^B\), respectively.

Both normal closures have group \(S_3\), and
their quadratic resolvents are distinct.
Thus their intersection is rational, and the
normal closure of the compositum is the product.
At primes dividing \(D\) other than \(3\),
each inertia is a tame reflection. The product
inertia acts by simultaneous reflections and
fixes one point on the degree-nine embedding
set, giving exponent four. On the regular
degree-\(36\) action it gives exponent eighteen.
There is no wild prime here because \(D\) is odd.
At \(3\), the first field and its normal closure
are completely split, so the local contribution
to the compositum is three copies of the second
cubic, with total exponent \(3\cdot5=15\).
On the normal closure it is six copies of the
local \(S_3\)-extension, of discriminant exponent
\(3\cdot1+8=11\), hence total exponent \(66\).
The degree-nine field has three real embeddings,
which gives its negative discriminant sign.
The formulas then follow since \(v_3(D)=1\).
The counts follow from PC3 and the exact powers
of \(|D|\), which also distinguish fields within
each sign.

The [checker](verify_prime_progression_two_cubic_companions.py)
checks (1) and the sextic discriminant identity,
both field discriminants and local decomposition
at the three marks on signed fixtures.
