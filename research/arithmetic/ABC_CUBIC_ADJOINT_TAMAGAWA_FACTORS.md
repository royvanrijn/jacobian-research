# Torsion and local factors for the fixed cubic Frey adjoint

Let \(E:y^2=x(x-a)(x+b)\) for a primitive positive triple \(a+b=c\).
Write \(h=\log c\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), and let \(\eta\) be either
of the two primitive cubic Dirichlet characters modulo \(7\), viewed
as a Galois character with value \(\eta(p)\) on arithmetic Frobenius
for \(p\ne7\). For a place \(\lambda\mid\ell\) of \(K\), use the
\(O_\lambda\)-lattice
\[
T=T_\lambda(E,\eta,s)
   =\operatorname{ad}^0(T_\ell E\otimes O_\lambda)
        \otimes \eta\otimes O_\lambda(s),
\qquad V=T\otimes K_\lambda .
\]
We can also use the trace-dual adjoint lattice in this definition.
Here \(\operatorname{ad}^0\) denotes trace-zero endomorphisms with
conjugation action, and \((s)\) is the cyclotomic Tate twist. For
\(p\ne\ell\) set
\[
c_{p,\lambda}(T)=
  \#\left[(H^1(I_p,T)_{\rm tors})^{{\rm Fr}_p=1}\right].
\]
The superscript means fixed points of arithmetic Frobenius on the
finite torsion module before taking its cardinality. This is the
norm of the period-independent prime-to-\(p\) local Tamagawa ideal
for the given coefficient place.
[De La Rochefoucauld, Proposition 4.3](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.926.pdf).
[Diamond–Flach–Guo, §8.3 and equation (57)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02348).

For every integer \(s\) the aggregate local factor satisfies
\[
 C(E,\eta,s):=\prod_\lambda\prod_{p\ne\ell}c_{p,\lambda}(T),
 \qquad 0\le\log C(E,\eta,s)\ll\frac{h}{\log(h+2)}.
\tag{CT1}
\]
The constant is independent of \(s\) and the triple. For \(s=0,1\),
the product of the orders of \(H^0(\mathbb Q,V/T)\) over \(\lambda\)
is bounded by \(27\) and \(27\cdot 49\), respectively, for either
lattice. This covers the fixed cubic adjoint and its Tate dual.

## The local formula over a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\)

Let \(F/\mathbb Q_p\) be any finite extension with residue cardinality
\(q_F\). Suppose \(E/F\) has potentially multiplicative reduction,
\(\ell\ne p\), and the character twist is unramified. Let
\(n=v_F(q_E)>0\) for the Tate parameter of the split Tate curve
with the given \(j\)-invariant, using \(v_F(F^\times)=\mathbb Z\).
The Tate uniformization expresses the Tate module as an extension
of \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) by \(\mathbb Z_\ell(1)\) with Kummer cocycle of
\(q_E\), up to a quadratic twist. That twist disappears from the
trace-zero adjoint. For a choice of Tate basis, a generator \(\tau\)
of the pro-\(\ell\) quotient of tame inertia and a lift \(\varphi\)
of arithmetic Frobenius act on the Tate curve by
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&n\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}q_F&u\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
respectively, with \(u\in\mathbb Z_\ell\). Inertia has this form
because the \(\ell\)-power roots of the unit part of \(q_E\) lie
over the maximal unramified extension. The choice of roots of unity
sets the Kummer coordinate on \(\tau\).
[Dummigan–Watkins, §4 preceding Proposition 4.4](https://magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/~watkins/papers/sp1.pdf).

Write \(e=e_{12}\), \(f=e_{21}\), and \(d=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)\).
The image of \(\tau-1\) in the adjoint lattice is generated by
\(-2ne\) and \(nd-n^2e\). The prime-to-\(\ell\) part of inertia
acts trivially and has vanishing first cohomology with these
coefficients. Evaluation of a cocycle at \(\tau\) computes
\(H^1(I_F,T)=T/(\tau-1)T\). Its torsion is the quotient of the
saturated span of \(e,d\) by this image, of order
\(|O/(2n^2)|\), where \(O\) can be the ring of integers of any
finite coefficient extension of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\). For the
trace-dual lattice use \(d/2\) in place of \(d\). The generators
in that basis are \(-ne\) and \(2n(d/2)-n^2e\), with the same
index.

For odd \(\ell\) either lattice gives the torsion module
\((O/n)^2\) in the \(e,d\) basis. Conjugation by \(\varphi\) on
cochains introduces a factor \(q_F^{-1}\) on this quotient,
because \(\varphi^{-1}\tau\varphi\) maps to \(\tau^{q_F^{-1}}\).
Put \(\xi=\eta(\varphi)q_F^s\), where the local unramified finite
character may have any order. The fixed group is therefore the
kernel on \((O/n)^2\) of the matrix
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}
      \xi-1 & -2u\xi/q_F\\
      0 & \xi/q_F-1
    \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{CT2}
\]
In particular the order is \(|O/\mathfrak m|^{\min(v(n),b_1)+
\min(v(n),b_2)}\), where \(\mathfrak m\) is the maximal ideal,
\(v(\mathfrak m)=1\), and \(b_1,b_2\) are the valuations of the
two Smith invariants of \(B\), with value \(+\infty\) for a zero
invariant. The first valuation is the minimum of the three entry
valuations. If the determinant is nonzero, the second is its
valuation minus the first. If the matrix has rank one, the
second is \(+\infty\), and for the zero matrix both are \(+\infty\).
The kernel order is independent of the Tate basis and the Frobenius
lift because it computes the fixed group on inertia cohomology.

At \(p\ne 2,7,\ell\) dividing \(abc\) over \(\mathbb Q\),
\(n=2v_p(abc)\). If \(\ell\ne2,3\) and \(\eta(p)\ne1\), then
for \(s=0\) the first diagonal entry in (CT2) is a unit, and
the length is \(\min(v(n),v(\eta(p)-p))\). For \(s=1\) the
second diagonal entry is a unit and the length is
\(\min(v(n),v(p\eta(p)-1))\).
The untwisted symmetric-power calculation in Dummigan–Watkins
retains the upper-triangular Frobenius terms. Here (CT2) gives
the two invariant factors for the adjoint with the character
kept in the calculation.
[Dummigan–Watkins, Proposition 4.4 and equations (3)–(4)](https://magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/~watkins/papers/sp1.pdf).

## The total over rational primes

At good primes outside \(2,7,\ell\), inertia acts trivially and
\(H^1(I_p,T)\) is torsion-free. At the other odd primes outside
\(7,\ell\) the preceding index gives
\[
 c_{p,\lambda}(T)\ \le\ |O_\lambda/(2n^2)|,
 \qquad n=2v_p(abc).
\]
At \(7\), with \(\ell\ne7\), choose an inertia element \(g\)
where \(\eta(g)\) is primitive cubic. The adjoint before the
character twist is unipotent at \(g\), for both good and
multiplicative reduction. The determinant of \(g-1\) on the
twisted lattice is \((\eta(g)-1)^3\), independent of \(s\).
Thus \(V^{I_7}=0\) and the coefficient sequence identifies
\(H^1(I_7,T)_{\rm tors}\) with \((V/T)^{I_7}\). It embeds
in the kernel of \(g-1\) on \(V/T\), whose order is the local
norm of that determinant. The product over \(\lambda\nmid 7\)
is at most \(27\).

If \(v_2(abc)>4\), then \(v_2(j)=8-2v_2(abc)<0\) and the
potentially multiplicative calculation applies with
\(n=2v_2(abc)-8\), since \(\eta\) is unramified at \(2\).
Otherwise \(E\) has potentially good reduction at \(2\) and
acquires good reduction over \(\mathbb Q_2(E[5])\) by the
semistable reduction criterion used in the conductor argument.
Let \(I'\) be inertia over this field. The inertia quotient
\(I_2/I'\) has order dividing \(480=|\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_5)|\).
Restriction sends \(H^1(I_2,T)_{\rm tors}\) to zero in
\(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(I',T)\), which is torsion-free.
Inflation–restriction shows that \(480\) kills the torsion.
Also the coefficient sequence embeds that torsion in
\(V/(V^{I_2}+T)\), the quotient of a vector space by a full
lattice of rank at most three. Thus the \(2\)-place product over
\(\lambda\nmid2\) is at most \(480^6\).
[Semistable reduction over the \(5\)-torsion field](ABC_FIXED_CUBIC_ADJOINT_VALUE.md#conductor-and-cuspidality).

For each potentially multiplicative prime outside \(7\), the
product over \(\lambda\nmid p\) of the local bounds is at most
\((2n^2)^{[K:\mathbb Q]}\). For \(r=v_p(abc)\ge1\),
\(2n^2\le8r^2\le(r+1)^4\). Hence
\[
 C(E,\eta,s)\le 27\cdot480^6\prod_{p\mid abc}
                   (v_p(abc)+1)^8 .
\]
To bound the logarithm of the divisor product, put \(M=\log(abc)\).
The primes up to \(\sqrt M\) contribute \(O(\sqrt M\log(M+2))\).
For the larger primes use \(\log(1+r)\le r\) and
\(\sum_{p>\sqrt M}v_p(abc)\le 2M/\log M\) for \(M>1\).
Since \(h\le M\le3h\), this proves (CT1).

## Factors outside the radical

Fix a prime \(p\equiv3\pmod7\) and a prime \(\ell\ne3\)
dividing \(p^2+p+1\). Then \(\ell\ne p,7\), the residue of \(p\)
has order three modulo \(\ell\), and \(\ell\) splits in \(K\).
Use the triple
\[
 (a,b,c)=(1,p^\ell-1,p^\ell).
\]
Here \(\ell\nmid abc\) by \(p^\ell\equiv p\not\equiv1\bmod\ell\),
and \(n=2\ell\) at \(p\). For \(s=0\) there is a unique place
\(\lambda\mid\ell\) at which \(\eta(p)\equiv p\bmod\lambda\).
Equation (CT2) gives \(c_{p,\lambda}=\ell\) and gives one at the
other place. For \(s=1\) the place in this pair is interchanged.
For example \(p=3,\ell=13\) yields the triple
\((1,3^{13}-1,3^{13})\).

These factors occur at infinitely many distinct coefficient
primes outside the corresponding radicals. Given any finite
set of already occurring primes \(\ell\ne3,7\), impose
\(p\equiv1\bmod\ell\) for every prime in the set, in addition to
\(p\equiv3\bmod7\). Dirichlet's theorem gives a prime \(p>3\) in
the resulting reduced class. None of the listed primes divides
\(p^2+p+1\), nor does \(7\). Its \(3\)-part is at most \(3\),
so it has a new prime divisor other than \(3\).

## The global torsion terms

For \(\ell\ne7\), the same inertia element at \(7\) used in the
local bound shows that global \(H^0(\mathbb Q,V/T)\) vanishes
if \(\ell\ne3\), and that its size at the place over \(3\)
is at most \(27\). At \(\ell=7\), use an arithmetic Frobenius
lift at \(3\), with \(\eta(3)\) a primitive cubic root. If
\(E\) is good at \(3\), its full rational two-torsion yields
exactly four points over \(\mathbb F_3\), by the Hasse bound
and divisibility by four. Hence \(a_3=0\), and the adjoint
eigenvalues before twisting are \(1,-1,-1\).
If \(E\) is multiplicative at \(3\), the adjoint eigenvalues
are \(3,1,1/3\) by the Tate module description.
For \(s=0\) the norms of the determinants of
\(\eta(3){\rm Fr}_3-1\) in these two cases are supported at
\(3,13\). For \(s=1\), the determinant has \(7\)-valuation in
its rational norm at most two. Indeed the relevant products
are \((3\eta(3)-1)(-3\eta(3)-1)^2\) in the good case and
\((9\eta(3)-1)(3\eta(3)-1)(\eta(3)-1)\) in the multiplicative
case. Their \(7\)-parts follow from
\(\mathrm N(x\eta(3)-1)=x^2+x+1\).
The determinant is nonzero in each case. The product over places above \(7\) of the invariant-group
orders is at most the \(7\)-part of the corresponding norm,
giving the asserted bounds.

## Selmer rank zero and descent from the cubic field

For any totally real number field \(F_0\), any non-CM elliptic
curve \(E/F_0\), and any finite-order character \(\eta\) trivial
at the real places, the Bloch–Kato Selmer spaces of
\(\operatorname{ad}^0 V_\ell E\otimes\eta(s)\) vanish for \(s=0,1\)
at every coefficient prime. The Bloch–Kato Selmer groups with
coefficients in the corresponding quotient by a stable lattice
are finite at each coefficient prime.

For \(s=0\), restrict to the totally real field cut out by \(\eta\).
Restriction of characteristic-zero \(H^1\) is injective by
inflation–restriction for a finite quotient and preserves the finite
local conditions. Newton and Thorne's theorem gives vanishing there
for the adjoint of every non-CM elliptic curve over a totally real
field at every prime. Trace zero is a direct summand in characteristic
zero. The nonexceptional Frey curves used here are non-CM because each
has an odd multiplicative prime, and a CM elliptic curve has integral
\(j\)-invariant.
[Newton–Thorne, Theorem B(2) and Theorem 5.5](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A3bce2058-88b4-4909-a1fa-579a6be9e657/files/s2514nm30c).

For \(W=\operatorname{ad}^0 V_\ell E\otimes\eta\), both \(H^0(F_0,W)\)
and \(H^0(F_0,W^\vee(1))\) vanish. For the first, the Tate module
over the field cut out by \(\eta\) has open image by Serre's theorem,
so its equivariant endomorphisms are scalars.
[Serre, introduction, assertion (4)](https://www.college-de-france.fr/media/jean-pierre-serre/UPL5874918517843398173_Serre_proprie_te_s_galoisiennes_des_courbes_elliptiques.pdf).

For the second, use a good reduction prime away from \(\ell\) and
the conductor of \(\eta\). Its adjoint Frobenius eigenvalues have
complex modulus one before the Tate twist and modulus the residue
cardinality after the twist. The global Selmer dimension formula
gives
\[
 h^1_f(W)-h^1_f(W^\vee(1)) =
 h^0(W)-h^0(W^\vee(1))+
 \sum_{v\mid\ell}\dim D_{\rm dR}(W_v)/\mathrm{Fil}^0-
 \sum_{v\mid\infty}\dim W^{G_{F_{0,v}}}.
\]
The two sums both equal \([F_0:\mathbb Q]\). At \(\ell\) the
adjoint Hodge–Tate multiset is \(\{-1,0,1\}\), with contribution
\([F_{0,v}:\mathbb Q_\ell]\); at each real place the trace-zero
diagonal line is the fixed space of complex conjugation. This proves
the vanishing for the dual and hence for \(s=1\) for every such
character.
[Bloch–Kato dimension formula, as stated in Bellaïche, Theorem 2.2](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/BSDseminar/refs/BKintro.pdf).

For the finiteness assertion let \(S\) contain the ramified places
and the places at \(\ell\) and infinity. The Selmer group for \(V/T\)
is unramified outside \(S\). On \(G_{F_0,S}\), the map from
\(H^1(V)\) to \(H^1(V/T)\) has kernel a full lattice and finite
cokernel, by the coefficient sequence and finite generation of
\(H^1(T)\) and \(H^2(T)\). For each place in \(S\) the kernel of
the map from the rational to the torsion local-condition quotient
is the image of \(H^1(T)\), a lattice in the rational local quotient.
This follows by propagating the finite local condition by image
from \(V\) to \(V/T\). The map from the global rational space
to the sum of those rational quotients is injective since its
kernel is \(H^1_f(F_0,V)=0\). The preimage of the sum of the local
lattices is therefore a lattice containing the kernel of the
global coefficient map. The part of the torsion Selmer group in
the image of the rational space lies in the finite quotient of
these lattices. The part in the global cokernel is finite as well.

More explicitly for the fixed cubic character, let
\(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)^+\) and \(D_s=(V/T)\otimes\eta^{-1}\), so
\(D_s\) is the untwisted adjoint torsion module with Tate twist
\(s\). If \(\ell\ne3\), restriction identifies
\[
 H^1_f(\mathbb Q,D_s\otimes\eta)
       \simeq H^1_f(F,D_s)^{\eta^{-1}} .
\tag{CT4}
\]
For the global and local cohomology this follows from
inflation–restriction because the extension degree is a unit at
\(\lambda\). At finite places away from \(\ell\), apply the same
argument to restriction on inertia to descend the unramified
rational condition. At places over \(\ell\), apply it to the
kernel of the cohomology map with crystalline period coefficients.
Taking invariants is exact because \(3\) is a unit, so the images of
the rational conditions defining the torsion conditions descend
as well. The indicated eigenspace comes from removing the
character on restriction. Rational Selmer rank zero also holds
at \(\ell=3\), where (CT4) does not give an integral decomposition.

## A restriction on the Hilbert modular comparison

Dimitrov's adjoint special-value formula in Theorem B requires
irreducibility of the tensor induction of the residual two-dimensional
representation upon restriction to the normal closure of the base
field. For the base change of \(f_E\) to \(F\), the residual
representation extends to \(G_{\mathbb Q}\). Upon restriction
to \(G_F\), its three tensor-induction factors are copies of the
same two-dimensional space \(W_0\) with diagonal action. The
subspace \(\bigwedge^2 W_0\otimes W_0\) embeds by antisymmetrizing
the first two factors, and is nonzero and proper in \(W_0^{\otimes3}\)
in every characteristic, including two. Since \(F/\mathbb Q\)
is Galois, this violates the irreducibility requirement at every
coefficient prime. Thus (CT4) identifies the relevant finite groups,
while this particular integral special-value comparison cannot
estimate them in the base-change family.
[Dimitrov, Theorem B and its condition \((\mathrm{LI}_{\mathrm{Ind}\bar\rho})\)](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503134).

## Consequence for the abc valuation problem

Take \(X_E\) from the fixed cubic critical value and put
\[
 Y_E=\frac{\mathrm N_{K/\mathbb Q}X_E}{C(E,\eta,1)}.
\]
Conjugating the choice of \(\eta\) permutes the coefficient
places in \(C\). The value \(Y_E\) is a positive rational number. The proof of
the fixed cubic height estimate together with (CT1) gives,
unconditionally,
\[
 \tfrac12\log Y_E=h+O\!\left(\frac{h}{\log(h+2)}\right).
\tag{CT3}
\]
Full abc is equivalent to bounding \(\tfrac12\log Y_E\) by
\((1+\varepsilon)\log\operatorname{rad}(abc)+O_\varepsilon(1)\)
for every \(\varepsilon>0\). In both directions the logarithmic
error is \(o(h)\) and is absorbed by choosing a smaller
\(\varepsilon\). Multiplication or division by the bounded
global torsion terms has the same equivalence.
[Fixed cubic height estimate and valuation equivalence](ABC_FIXED_CUBIC_ADJOINT_VALUE.md#an-equivalent-valuation-bound).

This controls the product of all \(p\ne\ell\) local factors
and both global invariant-group factors in the expected
Tamagawa number comparison for the twisted adjoint. To turn
\(Y_E\) into a cohomological bound still requires a special-value
comparison for the cubic twist and a uniform size bound for the
finite global Selmer groups or the corresponding congruence term.
The diagonal factors \(p=\ell\) at all variable primes are
computed separately in the Néron determinant normalization.
For the critical Tate twist their product contributes the inverse
square of the radical up to a sublinear logarithmic error.
[Diagonal factors and the remaining size bound](ABC_CUBIC_ADJOINT_DIAGONAL_FACTORS.md).

Diamond–Flach–Guo prove their comparison for
the untwisted adjoint and its Tate twist at coefficient places
in their set \(S_f\). The fixed cubic character requires a separate comparison.
[Diamond–Flach–Guo, equations (57)–(58) and Theorem 8.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02348).
