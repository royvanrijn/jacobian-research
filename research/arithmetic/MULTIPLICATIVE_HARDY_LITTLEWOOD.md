# Hardy–Littlewood laws for multiplicative sets

The leading count for a multiplicative set has three ingredients:
its one-variable density, an archimedean volume, and a joint arithmetic
correction. That correction can require a finite sum of Euler products;
a single product of independent marginal densities need not suffice.

This note proves two regimes. Periodic prime prescriptions, with arbitrary
bounded Euler exponent rules away from the fixed bad modulus, have an
explicit finite-character formula on pairwise-independent affine systems.
This is a classical finite-complexity application with the character and
constant corrections written out. Sets whose exclusions begin at prime
squares admit an elementary Hardy–Littlewood law even for dependent forms.
The existing [binary splitting theorem](SPLITTING_SEMIGROUP_LOCAL_GLOBAL.md)
and [coupled-fiber theorem](COUPLED_SPLITTING_LOCAL_GLOBAL.md) occupy
specific parts of this theory.

Positive local density is the relevant positivity condition. Mere
solubility in every finite quotient can fail for unrestricted exponent
profiles, even when every set has positive natural density.

The results and source applications have internal written review.
No external human review, formal verification, numerical onset or
historical priority claim is asserted.

## 1. Euler indicators and density constants

A multiplicative indicator satisfies \(f(1)=1\),
\(f(n)\in\{0,1\}\), and \(f(mn)=f(m)f(n)\) when \((m,n)=1\).
Write \(f_p(e)=f(p^e)\), \(f_p(0)=1\), and
\[
 F_p(z)=\sum_{e\ge0}f_p(e)z^e,\qquad
 \nu_p=(1-1/p)F_p(1/p),\qquad
 w_p(x)=\frac{f_p(v_p(x))}{\nu_p}.                         \tag{H.1}
\]
The value at \(x=0\) may be set to zero: for a nonconstant affine
form it affects a Haar-null set. Each \(w_p\) has Haar mean one.
The value set need not be a multiplicative semigroup: an allowed
exponent can have a forbidden double.

In the sparse periodic regime fix even \(L\), \(G=(\mathbb Z/L)^\times\),
and a nonempty set \(S\subset G\). Require
\[
 f_p(e)=0\quad(p\mid L,\ e\ge1),\qquad
 f_p(1)=\mathbf1_{p\bmod L\in S}\quad(p\nmid L).
\]
The higher exponent rules may otherwise be arbitrary zero-one rules.
Put \(H=\langle S\rangle\le G\) and \(\rho=|S|/|G|>0\). Then
\[
 \sum_{n\le X}f(n)\sim c_f X(\log X)^{\rho-1},\qquad
 c_f=\frac{\lim_{s\downarrow1}(s-1)^\rho\sum_n f(n)n^{-s}}
                       {\Gamma(\rho)}
     =\frac1{\Gamma(\rho)}
       \prod_p(1-1/p)^\rho F_p(1/p)>0.                    \tag{H.2}
\]
The product is taken in increasing prime order; its first-order
convergence uses the fixed-progression prime theorem.

The Gamma factor is essential. The independent primary normalization
is de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum, *Remarks on the Selberg–Delange method*,
Theorem 1.2 and equation (1.10), with leading coefficient
\(G(1)/\Gamma(\rho)\). At nonpositive integral exponents the reciprocal
Gamma factor vanishes; it cannot be replaced by an Euler residue alone.

## 2. Leading characters and prime-power cosets

For \(\eta\bmod L\), the prime mean of \(f\eta\) is
\[
                  \mu_\eta=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{a\in S}\eta(a).
\]
Exactly
\[
 \Re\mu_\eta=\rho
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \eta(a)=1\text{ for every }a\in S
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \eta\in H^\perp.     \tag{H.3}
\]
Equality of absolute values is a different condition: it allows a
common nontrivial phase. For \(S=\{3\}\bmod4\),
\(\mu_{\chi_{-4}}=-1/2\), although \(\rho=1/2\).

The published LM edition's Lemma 2.5(3), p. 4789, identifies these
absolute and positive-mean conditions; the example above disproves
that identification. Its displayed Lemma 2.8 constant on p. 4790
also omits the Gamma factor. Here the application uses (H.2)–(H.3),
with the independent Selberg–Delange normalization. Subgroup prime
prescriptions have only the means \(\rho\) and zero, so the phase
distinction does not change the earlier subgroup calculations.

For \(\eta\in H^\perp\), define
\[
 r_\eta=\prod_{p\nmid L}
       \frac{F_p(\eta(p)/p)}{F_p(1/p)}.                   \tag{H.4}
\]
These products converge absolutely. At \(p\bmod L\in S\), \(\eta(p)=1\);
else \(f_p(1)=0\), so the ratio is \(1+O(p^{-2})\).
No factor vanishes: \(p\ge3\) and
\(|F_p(\eta(p)/p)|\ge1-1/(p-1)>0\).

**Theorem EulerCosetMarginals.** There is a probability distribution
\(\pi_f\) on \(G/H\) with Fourier transform \(r_\eta\). For each
\(a\in G\),
\[
 \sum_{\substack{n\le X\\n=a\bmod L}}f(n)
 =\frac{c_f}{|H|}\pi_f(aH)X(\log X)^{\rho-1}
       +o(X(\log X)^{\rho-1}).                           \tag{H.5}
\]
In particular, the marginal weights need not be uniform on the
subgroup generated by all allowed prime-power states.

**Proof.** Independently at each good prime choose \(V_p\) with law
\(\mathbb P(V_p=e)=f_p(e)p^{-e}/F_p(1/p)\).
A nontrivial coset \(p^{V_p}H\) requires \(p\notin S\) and \(V_p\ge2\),
an event of probability \(O(p^{-2})\).
Almost surely only finitely many such events occur, so their product
defines a random coset. Its law \(\pi_f\) has Fourier transform (H.4).

Selberg–Delange applied to the fixed characters gives leading
coefficient \(c_fr_\eta\) at \(\eta\in H^\perp\).
Every other character has a strictly smaller real prime exponent.
Orthogonality and Fourier inversion on \(G/H\) now give (H.5).
\(\square\)

For example, take \(L=10\). Allow all exponents at primes \(1\bmod10\),
only exponents zero and two at primes \(3,7\bmod10\), and only zero
at primes \(9\bmod10\) or dividing ten. Here \(S=H=\{1\}\) and
\(\rho=1/4\), but values also occupy \(9\bmod10\). Put
\[
 R=\prod_{p=3,7\bmod10}\frac{1-p^{-2}}{1+p^{-2}},\qquad 0<R<1.
\]
Then \(\pi_f(1)=(1+R)/2\), \(\pi_f(9)=(1-R)/2\), and the other two
unit states have zero mass. A uniform \(H=\{1\}\) mask would lose a
genuine leading contribution.

## 3. The uniform progression interface

For every fixed \(C>0\), the progression formula needed below is
uniform for \(q\le(\log X)^C\), \(L\mid q\), every prime
\(p\le\log\log X\) dividing \(q\), and reduced residues \(a\bmod q\):
\[
 S_f(X;q,a)=\frac q{\varphi(q)}(\log X)^{\rho-1}
   \sum_{\eta\in H^\perp}c_fr_\eta\overline{\eta(a)}
          \prod_{p\mid q}F_p(\eta(p)/p)^{-1}
        +o(E_f(X;q)).                                    \tag{H.6}
\]
The analogous formula holds at \(y\in[\sqrt X,X]\), using \(\log y\).
Here \(S_f(y;q,a)=(q/y)\sum_{n\le y,n=a\bmod q}f(n)\);
characters are induced to \(q\), and \(E_f\) is the source natural
progression normalization.

We give the uniformity argument explicitly, before asserting
class-\({\cal F}\) membership. Put \({\cal E}=H^\perp\) and
\[
 \Delta=\rho-\max\bigl(0,\max_{\eta\notin{\cal E}}\Re\mu_\eta\bigr)>0,
\]
omitting the inner maximum when empty.
For any character modulo \(q\le(\log X)^C\) not induced from
\({\cal E}\), its primitive ancestor has mean \(\mu_\eta\) if it
factors through \(L\), and zero otherwise. Set
\(P_0=\exp(\sqrt{\log X})\). Siegel–Walfisz and partial summation give
\[
 \sum_{P_0<p\le X}f(p)\chi(p)p^{-1-it}
       =\mu_\chi I_X(t)+o(1),\qquad
 I_X(t)=\int_{\sqrt{\log X}}^{\log X}e^{-itv}\frac{dv}{v},
\]
uniformly for \(|t|\le2(\log X)^4\).
All prime divisors of the induced modulus lie below \(P_0\).
At a running endpoint \(Y\ge P_0\), \(qL\le(\log Y)^{2C+O_L(1)}\),
and the prime-theorem error absorbs the fixed logarithmic derivative
cost. An exact primary statement is Green, *Roth's theorem in the
primes*, Proposition 4.2, printed p. 1617, equation (4.4);
its constants are ineffective.

Uniformly in \(t\),
\[
 |\Im I_X(t)|=O(1),\qquad -O(1)\le\Re I_X(t)\le\tfrac12\log\log X.
\]
These follow from the sine integral and splitting the cosine integral
at \(1/|t|\), with integration by parts in its tail. The low-prime
contribution has absolute value at most
\((\rho/2)\log\log X+O(1)\). Hence Halász's distance is at least
\((1-\rho+\Delta/2)\log\log X-O(1)\).
The Halász–Montgomery–Tenenbaum estimate at height \((\log X)^4\),
as stated by Granville–Soundararajan on p. 1192, yields
\[
 X^{-1}\left|\sum_{n\le X}f(n)\chi(n)\right|
          \ll_{C,S,L}(\log X)^{\rho-1-\Delta/4}.
\]
This treats every nonmaximal induced character uniformly, including
fixed nontrivial common phases.

Matthiesen, *Generalized Fourier coefficients of multiplicative
functions*, Corollary 4.2, printed p. 1322, now supplies the
bounded-character progression reduction. It applies to class
\({\cal M}_H\), not class \({\cal F}\).
Take \(H=1,\alpha=\rho/2,k=\lceil16/\rho^2\rceil,k'=2\).
At most \(3k\) primitive characters are retained from the top-\(k\)
lists at \(X,X^{1/2},X^{1/4}\).
Its discarded error is \(O(E_f(X;q)(\log X)^{-\rho/6})\).
Each maximal fixed character has nonzero leading coefficient
\(c_fr_\eta\). The uniform logarithmic gap puts them in the retained
lists for large \(X\) and makes all other retained terms negligible.

There is no sum of \(\varphi(q)\) separate errors. Explicitly,
\[
 E_f(X;q)\asymp
 \frac q{\varphi(q)}(\log X)^{\rho-1}
               \prod_{p\mid q}(1+f(p)/p)^{-1}.
\]
The reciprocal deletion product grows only as a fixed power of
\(\log\log X\); it cannot consume the preceding logarithmic gap.

For maximal characters the required uniform coprimality calculation
must also be checked. Write
\[
 F_{\eta,q}(s)=F_\eta(s)D_{\eta,q}(s),\qquad
 D_{\eta,q}(s)=\prod_{p\mid q}F_p(\eta(p)p^{-s})^{-1}.
\]
The fixed series has \(F_\eta(s)=\zeta(s)^\rho G_\eta(s)\), with
\(G_\eta\) regular near one. Its prime-power correction converges
absolutely for \(\Re s>1/2\). At a prime with an allowed first power
\(\eta(p)=1\); elsewhere the first-order coefficient is zero.
Differentiating the finite Euler product gives, for every fixed \(j\),
\[
 |D_{\eta,q}^{(j)}(1)|
 \ll_{j,L,C}|D_{\eta,q}(1)|
                    (1+\log\log(3q))^j.
\]
The factor and its reciprocal cost at most fixed powers of
\(\log\log(3q)\). The same bounds hold in the shrinking neighborhood
of one used for the Hankel expansion.
Repeating the fixed-branch Selberg–Delange calculation with these
finite-product bounds gives, uniformly in the source range,
\[
 y^{-1}\sum_{\substack{n\le y\\(n,q)=1}}f(n)\eta(n)
 =c_fr_\eta D_{\eta,q}(1)(\log y)^{\rho-1}
                  +o(E_f(X;q)\varphi(q)/q).
\]
The Gamma normalization is the one in de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum
(1.10). A fixed-function asymptotic alone would not justify this
moving-\(q\) step.

Combining these maximal means with the bounded-character reduction
proves (H.6), then stable progression means, then membership in
\({\cal F}\). If \(L\nmid q\), split into the bounded number of
progressions modulo \(\operatorname{lcm}(q,L)\); eventually the prime
supports and \(E_f\) normalizations agree.
Fixed twists inherit stability by the same argument.

Finally, the proof of the 2018 Proposition 5.1, pp. 1354–1355, now
has only fixed exceptional conductors to absorb.
A fixed \(L\)-supported refinement of the primorial gives the
\(W\)-trick independently of inverse-theorem accuracy.
Thus the moving-intercept Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2 apply without
using either incorrect printed identification from Section 2.

## 4. A finite-character Hardy–Littlewood theorem

For fixed \(s\ge2\) and \(1\le i\le s\), take indicators as above with sets \(S_i\),
groups \(H_i=\langle S_i\rangle\), constants \(c_i\), and exponents
\(\rho_i\). Fix filters \(U_i\subset G\).
Let
\[
 \ell_i(t)=b_i\cdot t+a_i(T),\qquad a_i(T)=O(T),
\]
where the fixed nonzero rows \(b_i\in\mathbb Z^d\) are pairwise
linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\), \(d\ge2\).
On a fixed bounded convex positive-volume \(K\), require
\(0<\ell_i(t)\le CT\) for all \(t\in TK\).
All counts below include the filters \(\ell_i\bmod L\in U_i\).

For \(\boldsymbol\eta\in\prod_iH_i^\perp\), define
\[
 \begin{split}
 A_L(\boldsymbol\eta;\boldsymbol a)
 &=L^{-d}\sum_{t\bmod L}
       \prod_i\mathbf1_{\ell_i(t)\bmod L\in U_i}
                         \overline{\eta_i(\ell_i(t))},\\
 B_{p,\boldsymbol\eta}(\boldsymbol a)
 &=\int_{\mathbb Z_p^d}
       \prod_i f_i(p^{v_p(\ell_i(t))})
                    \eta_i(p)^{v_p(\ell_i(t))}\,dt,\\
 {\cal H}_{\ell}(\boldsymbol a)
 &=\left(\frac L{\varphi(L)}\right)^s
    \sum_{\boldsymbol\eta\in\prod_iH_i^\perp}
       A_L(\boldsymbol\eta;\boldsymbol a)
       \prod_{p\nmid L}
          \frac{B_{p,\boldsymbol\eta}(\boldsymbol a)}
                         {\prod_i\nu_{i,p}}.              \tag{H.7}
 \end{split}
\]
The products converge absolutely, uniformly in the intercepts.

**Theorem EulerHardyLittlewood.** With
\(\kappa=s-\sum_i\rho_i\),
\[
 \sum_{t\in TK\cap\mathbb Z^d}
       \prod_i f_i(\ell_i(t))
       \mathbf1_{\ell_i(t)\bmod L\in U_i}
 =\operatorname{vol}(K)\left(\prod_i c_i\right)
    {\cal H}_{\ell}(\boldsymbol a)
    \frac{T^d}{(\log T)^\kappa}
       +o\!\left(\frac{T^d}{(\log T)^\kappa}\right).        \tag{H.8}
\]
The error is uniform in the permitted moving intercepts.
The factor \({\cal H}_{\ell}\) is real and nonnegative.
A uniform positive lower bound for it gives a relative asymptotic
and eventual existence. No numerical rate is asserted.

**Proof.** Apply Matthiesen's Theorem 2.1, arXiv:1606.04482v4,
at \(B_0=0\), using the repaired progression interface above and
\(w(T)=\log\log T\).
Fixed filters are expanded in fixed characters.
Its \(O(T)\) intercept allowance is essential; rescale by a fixed
constant to meet the upper value bound if necessary.

For a maximal character, the coefficient ratio is \(r_{\eta_i}\).
The exact-prefix model removes local factors
\(F_{i,p}(\eta_i(p)/p)\).
A prefix \(w_i\) contributes the phase \(\eta_i(w_i)\), because
\(\overline{\eta_i(\ell_i/w_i)}
=\overline{\eta_i(\ell_i)}\eta_i(w_i)\).
Completing the prefixes gives the raw \(B_{p,\boldsymbol\eta}\).
The coefficient ratios (H.4) cancel the twisted local denominators,
leaving exactly the PRINCIPAL denominators \(\nu_{i,p}\) in (H.7).
There is no further factor \(r_{\eta_i}\) to multiply into (H.7).

Outside a fixed coefficient-prime set, every form is primitive
modulo \(p\) and every pair has independent normals.
Since \(\eta_i(p)=1\) whenever \(f_i(p)=1\), first-order local terms
are unchanged by maximal twists. Events involving higher powers
are confined to \(p^2\mid\ell_i(t)\), of probability \(p^{-2}\).
Inclusion-exclusion gives
\[
       B_{p,\boldsymbol\eta}/\prod_i\nu_{i,p}
                                   =1+O_s(p^{-2})
\]
uniformly in the intercepts and fixed characters.

Prefix completion requires an absolute estimate, not division by
a possibly tiny local factor. Since \(\nu_{i,p}\ge1-1/p\),
\[
 \int v_p(\ell_i(t))
       \prod_j\frac{f_j(p^{v_p(\ell_j(t))})}{\nu_{j,p}}\,dt
 \ll_s e_i(p)+1/(p-1),\qquad e_i(p)=\min_jv_p(b_{ij}).
\]
The right side is \(O_s(1/p)\) outside finitely many primes.
The products of the other principal local factors are uniformly
bounded above. Thus the unnormalized log-prefix moment in the
source \(W\)-model is \(O(\log\log\log T)\).
Its mass beyond \(w_i>(\log T)^{B_2}\) is \(o(1)\), uniformly,
even when a local factor vanishes.
Phases and filters have modulus at most one and do not enlarge
this bound.

The finite-character model therefore becomes (H.7), independently
of the source auxiliary truncations. Remark 2.2 lets its fixed
accuracy tend to zero and proves (H.8).
For nonnegativity, use the joint interpretation below. \(\square\)

If a principal local factor \(B_{p,\boldsymbol1}\) is zero, every
twisted factor at that prime is zero, so \({\cal H}_{\ell}=0\).
Otherwise normalize each principal local root measure and record
the JOINT cosets
\[
             Z_p=(p^{v_p(\ell_i)}H_i)_i.
\]
Outside finitely many primes, a nonidentity record needs a
prime-square event and has probability \(O(p^{-2})\).
Their product \(Z=\prod Z_p\) is well-defined almost surely.
Then (H.7) equals the product of the principal normalized local
factors times the expectation of
\[
 \Phi_L(z;\boldsymbol a)
  =\frac{L^{s-d}}{\prod_i|H_i|}
    \#\{t\bmod L:\ell_i(t)\in U_i,\ \ell_i(t)H_i=z_i\}.
                                                               \tag{H.9}
\]
This proves nonnegativity and explains the needed coupling.
Independent marginal coset distributions cannot replace these joint
prime records.

## 5. Prime-factor semigroups beyond subgroups

For pure prime support, \(f_p(e)=1\) for all \(e\) when \(p\bmod L\in S\),
and is zero for positive \(e\) otherwise. Every allowed prime-power
state lies in \(H=\langle S\rangle\). The coset record is neutral.
Thus (H.7) reduces to
\[
 {\cal H}_{\ell}
  =\frac{L^{s-d}}{\prod_i|H_i|}
       \#\{t\bmod L:\ell_i(t)\in U_i\cap H_i\}
       \prod_{p\nmid L}
        \frac{\mathbb P_t(\ell_i(t)\ne0\ \forall i\in I_p)}
                           {\prod_{i\in I_p}(1-1/p)},     \tag{H.10}
\]
where \(I_p\) is determined by \(p\bmod L\notin S_i\), not by
membership in \(H_i\). The density exponent is \(|S_i|/|G|\),
not \(|H_i|/|G|\).

**Theorem ArbitraryResidueSemigroupImages.** For nonempty \(S\subset G\),
\(H=\langle S\rangle\), and \(U\subset H\), the image of its prime-factor
semigroup modulo \(m\) consists exactly of residues compatible with
\(U\bmod\gcd(m,L)\) and unit at every good forbidden prime dividing \(m\).

**Proof.** Necessity follows from prime factorization.
Extract the allowed prime powers needed for the desired valuation
prefix \(D\). Its residue lies in \(H\).
The remaining unit tail has a prescribed state in \(H\).
Choose a nonempty positive word in \(S\) with that state, of length
at most \(|H|\); a finite-group monoid generated by \(S\) is \(H\).
Realize all but its last letter by allowed primes avoiding \(m\).
The last letter is realized by one CRT/Dirichlet prime, imposing
the required unit tail modulo the remaining prime powers.
Compatibility at \(\gcd(m,L)\) is exactly the stated condition.
The resulting product supplies the desired residue. \(\square\)

**Corollary ArbitraryResidueCoupledLocalGlobal.** Let \(A\) be a fixed
surjective integer matrix with kernel coordinate rows nonzero and
pairwise independent, and take pure prime-factor sets \(C_{S_i}\)
with filters \(U_i\subset H_i=\langle S_i\rangle\).
Use the finite coefficient-prime set \({\cal P}_*\) and joint
\(L\)-root factor from Sections 1–2 of the coupled note, but determine
forbidden good coordinates using \(S_i\).
Positive joint \(L\)-roots and positive unit-root counts at
\(p\in{\cal P}_*\) are equivalent to all-moduli solubility.
They imply eventual global representability uniformly on compact
positive target cones, with natural-scale lower count
\(T^d/(\log T)^{s-\sum_i|S_i|/|G|}\).

**Proof.** The kernel parametrization is a bijection modulo every
modulus. Outside \({\cal P}_*\), at most \(s<p\) proper forbidden
hyperplanes cannot cover parameter space. Roots modulo each good
prime lift while preserving units; a joint \(L\)-root lifts while
preserving the whole subgroup state. CRT combines these conditions,
and the preceding finite-image theorem realizes each coordinate by
an actual \(C_{S_i}\) value. The converse follows by testing the
single finite obstruction modulus.

The positive arithmetic factors in (H.10) have a uniform positive
minimum on admissible targets: finitely many exceptional prime
residues and joint states remain, and the other factors have uniform
summable tails. For each compact set inside the positive real image,
the finite fixed-box cover from the coupled proof applies to (H.8).
Its positive main terms give the lower count and eventual existence.
\(\square\)

This is a classical finite-complexity application, not a new binary
correlation theorem.

For \(S=\{3\}\bmod4\), \(H=G\), so both odd states have equal leading
weight, with exponent \(\rho=1/2\).
Its two-summand finite-congruence criterion is simply that the target
be even. The existing subgroup binary proof is not a literal
specialization: secondary fixed-character branches and the
nonneutral progression frame require additional verification.
No binary asymptotic for this set is inferred here.

## 6. Summable defects: an elementary dense Hardy–Littlewood theorem

A different regime needs no pairwise-independence hypothesis.
Let \(f_i\) be arbitrary multiplicative zero-one indicators such
that \(f_i(p)=1\) outside a fixed finite prime set \(B\).
Higher exponent rules can be arbitrary. Each has a positive natural
density \(\prod_p\nu_{i,p}\).

Take fixed nonzero affine normals in \(d\ge1\) variables, moving
intercepts \(O(X)\), and the same fixed positive bulk \(K\).
Define unnormalized local factors
\[
 \beta_p(\boldsymbol a)
       =\int_{\mathbb Z_p^d}\prod_i
                    f_i(p^{v_p(\ell_i(t))})\,dt.
\]

**Theorem DenseEulerHardyLittlewood.** Uniformly in these intercepts,
\[
 \sum_{t\in XK\cap\mathbb Z^d}\prod_i f_i(\ell_i(t))
   =\operatorname{vol}(K)X^d\prod_p\beta_p(\boldsymbol a)
                         +o(X^d).                       \tag{H.11}
\]
Repeated or dependent forms are permitted.
The Euler tail converges uniformly.
This is an absolute asymptotic; a relative one requires a uniform
positive lower bound on its leading product.

**Proof.** Enlarge \(B\) to contain the prime divisors of every
normal's content. Outside it, failure of any Euler condition implies
\(p^2\mid\ell_i(t)\). For \(p>z\), slicing along a \(p\)-unit normal
coefficient gives
\[
 \#\{t\in XK:p^2\mid\ell_i(t)\}
             \ll X^d/p^2+X^{d-1}.
\]
Only \(p\le\sqrt{CX}\) can contribute, since values are positive and
at most \(CX\). The total normalized large-prime tail is
\(O(\sum_{p>z}p^{-2}+X^{-1/2})\).
Likewise \(1-\beta_p\le s/p^2\) outside the fixed set.

At every prime in \(B\cup\{p\le z\}\), truncate valuations at a
common depth \(k\). The lost normalized count is at most
\[
 O\!\left(\sum_{p\in B\cup\{p\le z\},i}
                  p^{e_i(p)-k}\right)+O_{z,k}(X^{-1}),
\]
uniformly in the intercepts; the same bound holds in Haar measure.
For fixed \(z,k\), the model is periodic modulo a fixed integer.
CRT and lattice-point counting in the fixed convex body give its
volume times the product of truncated local densities, with
\(o(X^d)\) error uniformly over residue offsets.
First send \(X\) to infinity, then \(k\) and \(z\) to infinity.
This proves (H.11). \(\square\)

For squarefree shifts \(n+h_i\), let \(\nu_p(h)\) be the number of
distinct residues \(h_i\bmod p^2\). Then
\[
 \#\{n\le X:n+h_i\text{ squarefree for every }i\}
       \sim X\prod_p\left(1-\frac{\nu_p(h)}{p^2}\right)    \tag{H.12}
\]
when the product is positive.
This is the classical Mirsky pattern formula, explicitly displayed
in Cellarosi–Sinai, JEMS 15 (2013), p. 1349, equation (15).
For repeated shifts it automatically reduces to the correct single
condition. For \(r\)-free shifts replace \(p^2\) by \(p^r\).

## 7. Finite solubility can have zero local density

**Theorem EulerClosureCounterexample.** Positive-density multiplicative
sets and pairwise-independent affine forms can be soluble in every
finite quotient while having no global solution and zero local
density.

**Proof.** Allow all odd-prime powers in all five sets.
At two use
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_1&=\{0\}\cup\{e\ge1:e=1\bmod3\},\\
 E_2&=\{0\}\cup\{e\ge1:e=2\bmod3\},\\
 E_3&=\{0\}\cup\{e\ge1:e=0\bmod3\},\qquad
 E_4=E_5=\{0\}.
 \end{split}
\]
Their natural densities are respectively
\(11/14,9/14,4/7,1/2,1/2\); all prime-density exponents are one.
Use the pairwise-independent forms
\[
                  x,\ y,\ x+y,\ x+2y-1,\ 2x+y-1.         \tag{H.13}
\]
The last two must be odd, forcing \(x,y\) even.
Their finite positive valuations lie in distinct classes one and
two modulo three. Hence
\(v_2(x+y)=\min(v_2x,v_2y)\) is never zero modulo three, contradicting
the third set's rule. No positive solution exists.

For every modulus \(m=2^kq\), \(q\) odd, choose
\(e_1,e_2,e_3\ge\max(k,1)\) in the respective allowed classes.
The actual allowed values
\[
 z_i=2^{e_i}q\ (i\le3),\qquad z_4=z_5=2m-1
\]
satisfy, modulo \(m\),
\[
 z_3-z_1-z_2=0,\qquad
 z_4-z_1-2z_2=-1,\qquad z_5-2z_1-z_2=-1.
\]
Thus every finite quotient has actual-set witnesses.
The associated integer matrix is surjective, and its integer kernel
has the five normals in (H.13), so this is also a coupled-system
counterexample of the required finite-complexity geometry.
There are positive real parameter boxes \(x,y\asymp X\).

In the 2-adic closure the only possible parameters are \(x=y=0\).
That point has Haar measure zero; with finite-valuation indicators
the integrand vanishes everywhere. Therefore \(\beta_2=0\).
The dense theorem correctly predicts zero main term.
All-moduli closure solubility and positive local density are distinct.
\(\square\)

## 8. Proven regimes and remaining correlations

| Regime | Hardy–Littlewood conclusion |
|---|---|
| Euler indicators with periodic positive prime support, coprime to fixed \(L\), independent affine normals | Explicit finite-character law (H.7)–(H.8), classical finite-complexity input |
| Pure prime-factor semigroups for arbitrary residue sets \(S_i\) | Uniform \(H_i=\langle S_i\rangle\) state masks, exponent \(|S_i|/\varphi(L)\), finite coupled congruence criterion |
| Exclusions starting at prime squares outside finitely many primes | Elementary law (H.11), even for one-parameter and dependent patterns |
| Binary subgroup splitting sums, including \(C_{12}+C_{12}\) | Existing proved pointwise theorem with explicit logarithmic remainder and its stated imported inputs |
| General dependent sparse patterns | The formula is a candidate model when its products and scaling are meaningful; a further correlation proof is required |
| Arbitrary infinite bad-prime profiles | Density, tail and scale control are essential; (H.13) refutes all-moduli solubility as a universal positivity test |

The logarithmic exponent \(s-\sum\rho_i\) presumes genuinely distinct
sparse factors. For example \(f(n)^2=f(n)\); a duplicated sparse
form changes the exponent. Binary target collisions and shrinking
real regions require their own scale analysis.
Sets of prime density zero, nonperiodic prime prescriptions and
global class constraints without an Euler-indicator description
are outside the stated theorem.
The finite-character formula supplies the arithmetic model; it does
not resolve every dependent correlation.
