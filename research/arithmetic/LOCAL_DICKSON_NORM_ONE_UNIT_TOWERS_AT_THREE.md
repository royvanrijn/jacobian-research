# Local Dickson towers of norm-one units at three

The local field generated by a Dickson root depends on the
quadratic norm torus and on the depth of its norm-one unit.
For a primitive free unit the completion is determined by
the quadratic algebra, except that the cyclotomic quadratic
has three types distinguished by its cube-root-of-unity
component. In that quadratic the bottom degree-three
quotient can be unramified even when the top character is wild.

Let \(F=\mathbb Q_3\), fix an algebraic closure of \(F\),
put \(m=3^n\), and let \(K\) be a quadratic étale \(F\)-algebra.
For a norm-one unit \(\gamma\) put \(T=\gamma+\bar\gamma\).
When \(K\) is split, write \(\gamma=(g,g^{-1})\); the trace
means \(g+g^{-1}\). Multiplication by \(-1\) changes the
Dickson variable by \(-1\) and leaves all field types
unchanged. In unramified \(K\) we also remove the
norm-one residue torsion of order four, which consists
of \(m\)-th powers for all \(n\). Throughout, a field
generated by a root of \(\mathcal D_m(X,1)-T\) is denoted
\(L_n\); its splitting field is \(N_n\).

## Noncyclotomic quadratic algebras

**Theorem LD1.** Suppose \(K\) is the split algebra,
the unramified quadratic field, or \(F(\sqrt3)\).
Assume that \(\gamma\) is a noncube in \(K^\times\)
(componentwise in the split case). Then \(L_n/F\) is
totally ramified of degree \(m\), with full affine normal
closure. Its local discriminant and the finite character
conductors over the translation-fixed field \(B_n\)
are given by
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 K & v_3(\operatorname{disc}(L_n/F)) &
 \text{conductor exponent of an order-}3^k
              \text{ character of }N_n/B_n\\ \hline
 F\times F,\ \text{unramified quadratic} & nm &2\cdot3^{k-1}\\
 F(\sqrt3) & nm+(m-1)/2 &(3^k+1)/2
 \end{array}
 \qquad(1\le k\le n).
 \tag{1}
\]
In each of these three cases the root fields up to
\(F\)-isomorphism and the normal closures as subfields
of the fixed closure are independent of the primitive
unit. The local tower in
[WD2–WD3](GENUS_THREE_PELL_WILD_DICKSON_FIELDS.md)
is the \(F(\sqrt3)\) case.

For the split or unramified case, normalize the unit to
reduce to one. The \(3\)-adic logarithm identifies the
norm-one principal units with the trace-zero part of
\(3\mathcal O_K\), a free rank-one \(\mathbb Z_3\)-module.
Noncubicity is equivalent to valuation one for
\(\gamma-1\). In the split case take either component.
In the unramified field the module has generator
\(3i\), where \(i^2=-1\). Representatives of the two
primitive unit types are \(4\) and \((1+3i)/(1-3i)\).

In \(K=F(\sqrt3)\), put \(\pi=\sqrt3\). For a norm-one
principal unit \(v\ne1\), its depth \(v_K(v-1)\) is odd.
Indeed conjugation on the grade \(\pi^j/\pi^{j+1}\) is
\((-1)^j\); at even depth the leading term of \(v\bar v-1\)
could not vanish. Cubing a principal norm-one unit raises
its depth by two. For depth \(j\ge3\) this follows from
the unique smallest term \(3(v-1)\) in the binomial
expansion. At depth one the two terms \(3(v-1)\) and
\((v-1)^3\) contribute a nonzero sum on the third grade,
since \(c+c^3\ne0\) for \(c\in\mathbb F_3^\times\) and
\(\pi^2=3\). The unit \(\epsilon=-2-\sqrt3\) has depth one,
so its powers \(\epsilon^{3^r}\) generate each successive
nonzero grade. Successive correction of the leading
coefficient shows that the principal norm-one group
is \(\epsilon^{\mathbb Z_3}\), and a norm-one unit of
residue one is primitive precisely at depth one.
The only other residue is removed by sign.

Consequently, in all three cases two primitive normalized
units differ by raising to an exponent prime to three
and multiplying by an \(m\)-th power of a norm-one unit.
Specifically \(\gamma'=\gamma^b w^m\) for an integer
\(b\) prime to three and norm-one \(w\).
Replacing a radical \(\theta\) of \(\gamma\) by
\(\theta^b w\) preserves the radical fields over \(K\)
and \(K(\zeta_m)\). In the quadratic field cases the
involution over \(F\) which sends \(\theta\) to
\(\theta^{-1}\) continues to act by inversion on the
new radical. Thus it fixes the same degree-\(m\) field.
This proves the stated independence.

For \(F(\sqrt3)\), take the representative \(2+\sqrt3\).
It has trace \(4\) and \(v_3(T+2)=1\). The local affine
group, discriminant and conductors follow by the local
arguments of WD2–WD3. Those arguments use only the norm
condition, this trace valuation and the quadratic
completion \(F(\sqrt3)\).

For the split or unramified case, \(\theta-1\) is an
Eisenstein uniformizer over the normalized component
field, by substituting \(1+Z\) in \(Y^m-\gamma\). The
derivative of the binomial gives different exponent
\(nm\). In the unramified case, conjugation on \(K\)
extends to \(\theta\mapsto\theta^{-1}\); the degree-\(m\)
field is the fixed field of this involution because
\(K(\theta+\theta^{-1})=K(\theta)\) by odd degree.
The extension from that field to \(K(\theta)\) is
unramified quadratic, so the different exponent is
unchanged under this unramified base change. In the
split case the root already generates \(F(\theta)\)
by the same odd-degree argument.

The normalized unit remains noncubic in
\(K(\zeta_m)\) (or \(F(\zeta_m)\) for a component),
because the degree-three binomial extension over the
component field is nonnormal and the cyclotomic
extension there is abelian. For the split case the
full normal closure is the radical field over
\(F(\zeta_m)\). For unramified \(K\) it is the fixed
field of the simultaneous inversion of \(\theta\)
and \(\zeta_m\) over \(K(\zeta_m)\), as in WD2.
In both cases the affine quotient is full.
The translation-fixed field \(B_n\) is the full
cyclotomic field in the split case. For unramified
\(K\) it is the diagonal fixed field of \(K(\zeta_m)\).
It is totally ramified and becomes the full cyclotomic
field after adjoining \(K\). Therefore it has the
same discriminant and relative cyclotomic conductors
as the full cyclotomic tower by unramified base change.

The order-\(3^k\) primitive characters form one orbit
in the permutation representation of the affine group.
The conductor of their induced summand is
\(k3^k-(k-1)3^{k-1}=(2k+1)3^{k-1}\) by the root-field
discriminants. The base \(B_k\) has discriminant exponent
\((2k-1)3^{k-1}\) and residue degree one. Artin conductor
induction as in WD3 gives \(2\cdot3^{k-1}\) at level \(k\).
The first step \(B_{k+1}/B_k\) has conductor \(3^k\),
since the first automorphism changes the full cyclotomic
uniformizer \(\zeta-1\) by an element of valuation \(3^k\)
in the upper field. Every nontrivial character of
\(B_n/B_k\) therefore has strictly larger conductor
than the primitive character being pulled back.
Induction of the restriction, subtracting the base
discriminant as in WD3, preserves its conductor.
This proves (1).

The split case belongs to the rational-unit setting
of Viviani's §5, including Theorems 5.5 and 5.8 on
total ramification and ramification groups over the
cyclotomic base. The use here of norm-one units in
the nonsplit quadratic algebras requires a different
descent. [Viviani, *Ramification groups and Artin
conductors of radical extensions of the rationals*](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.470/).

## The quadratic containing cube roots of unity

**Theorem LD2.** Let \(K=F(\sqrt{-3})=F(\zeta_3)\),
\(\pi=\sqrt{-3}\), and
\[
                       \eta=\frac{1+\pi^3}{1-\pi^3}.
\]
After removing its sign, every norm-one unit is uniquely
\(\gamma=\zeta_3^j\eta^b\), with \(j\in\mathbb Z/3\) and
\(b\in\mathbb Z_3\). Suppose \(b\in\mathbb Z_3^\times\).
Then \(L_n/F\) has degree \(m\), with the following
local discriminant exponent and residue degree
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 &v_3(\operatorname{disc}(L_n/F))& f(L_n/F)\\ \hline
 j=0 & ((2n-1)m-3)/2 &3\\
 j\ne0 & ((2n+1)m-1)/2 &1 .
 \end{array}
 \tag{2}
\]
Its normal closure has group
\[
         C_m\rtimes(1+3\mathbb Z/m)
\]
with translation-fixed field \(B_n=F(\zeta_m)^+\).
For \(1\le k\le n\), the character conductor exponents
of \(N_n/B_n\) are
\[
 \begin{cases}
 (3^n+1)/2,& k=n,\ j\ne0,\\
 0,& k=1\text{ in the remaining cases},\\
 (3^{k-1}+1)/2,& k\ge2\text{ in the remaining cases}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(k\) denotes exact character order \(3^k\).
For fixed \(n\) there are exactly three splitting fields
as subfields of the algebraic closure, indexed by
\(j/b\bmod3\). They become equal after composition with
\(B_{n+1}\).

As in LD1, the nonzero depths of principal norm-one units
are odd. The first grade is generated by \(\zeta_3\),
and the norm-one group at depth at least three is
generated topologically by \(\eta\), because its depth
is three and every cube at those depths raises depth
by exactly two. This proves the asserted decomposition.
In the pure case \(j=0\), \(v_K(\gamma-1)=3\), and
\(v_3(T-2)=3\). For the cubic Dickson polynomial, substituting
\(X=2+3Y\) and dividing by \(27\) gives
\[
                Y^3+2Y^2+Y+(2-T)/27.
\]
The constant term is \(1\bmod3\) because
\((\gamma-1)^2/\gamma=T-2\) and \(\pi^6=-27\).
The reduction is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_3\).
Therefore the first root field is the unramified cubic.
Over \(K\), the first radical \(\theta_1=\gamma^{1/3}\)
lies in the unramified cubic compositum, since it
satisfies a quadratic equation over that cubic composed
with \(K\), and its degree over \(K\) divides three.
The degree cannot be one by irreducibility of the
Dickson cubic. Its residue is one and \(v_K(\theta_1-1)=1\)
in the unramified valuation extension, since cubing at
depth at least two gives depth at least four. Thus,
over \(K(\theta_1)\), \(\theta_n-1\) is Eisenstein for
\((1+Z)^{3^{n-1}}-\theta_1\) if \(n>1\). The radical
field has residue degree three over \(K\), ramification
index \(m/3\), and different exponent \(2(n-1)m/3\).

For \(j\ne0\), \(\gamma-1\) has depth one. Hence
\(\theta_n-1\) is Eisenstein already over \(K\), and
the radical field is totally ramified of degree \(m\)
with different exponent \(2nm\) over \(K\).
In both cases adjoining \(\theta_n+\theta_n^{-1}\)
to \(K\) recovers the radical field, by odd degree.
The nontrivial automorphism of \(K/F\) extends by
inverting \(\theta_n\). Its fixed field is \(L_n\)
of degree \(m\). The quadratic extension from \(L_n\)
to the radical field is ramified, since the absolute
residue degree of the radical field is odd, and is
tame with different exponent one. Transitivity of
the different through \(K/F\) and through \(L_n/F\)
gives (2), after multiplying by the stated residue
degree.

In \(F(\zeta_m)\) the binomial remains irreducible.
For \(n=1\) this follows from the preceding radical
degrees. For \(n\ge2\) the torsion component is already
a cube in \(F(\zeta_m)\), and the cubic radical for the
free component is a nontrivial unramified extension,
so survives the totally ramified cyclotomic base change.
Kummer theory now gives a translation subgroup of
order \(m\). A cyclotomic automorphism \(a\) fixes or
inverts \(\theta_n\) according as \(a\equiv1\) or
\(-1\bmod3\). In the Dickson root action it multiplies
the indices by \(a\) or \(-a\), respectively.
Its kernel on the cyclotomic units is precisely
\(\{1,-1\}\). The claimed affine group and base follow.

At level \(k\) the primitive translations form two
orbits of characters, interchanged by inversion,
since the multiplier group is \(1+3\mathbb Z/3^k\).
Each summand is induced from a primitive character of
the same conductor. In (2) the difference of successive
root-field discriminant exponents is \(2k3^{k-1}\) in
the pure case for \(k\ge2\), and \((2k+2)3^{k-1}\) in
the mixed case for \(k\ge1\) starting with exponent zero.
At level \(k\) the base \(B_k\) has discriminant exponent
\(((2k-1)3^{k-1}-1)/2\) and residue degree one.
Subtracting twice that exponent and dividing by two
gives the primitive conductor at level \(k\). In the
pure case the first character is unramified.

For the pure unit, this conductor is strictly smaller
than the first cyclotomic conductor \((3^k+1)/2\)
of \(B_{k+1}/B_k\), so it persists across all subsequent
cyclotomic base changes by the induction argument
from LD1. For the mixed unit and \(k<n\), an order-\(3^k\)
root of \(\zeta_3^j\) is already present in
\(F(\zeta_{3^n})\). Multiplying the pure radical by
\(\zeta_{3^{k+1}}^j\) gives the mixed radical for the
same free exponent \(b\), and the cyclotomic inversion
inverts both factors. Hence the degree-\(3^k\) cyclic
subextensions over \(B_n\) coincide. This proves (3).

Finally raising the unit to an exponent prime to three
normalizes the free exponent to \(1\bmod3^n\), leaving
only the label \(j/b\bmod3\). The \(m\)-th Kummer
classes of \(\eta\) and \(\zeta_3\) over \(F(\zeta_m)\)
have respective orders \(m\) and \(3\) and are independent.
The first radical field has unramified cubic as its
unique degree-three subfield, and the second is the
totally ramified next cyclotomic step. Thus their
Kummer subgroups for \(\eta,\zeta_3\eta,\zeta_3^2\eta\)
are distinct. The Dickson splitting fields are distinct
as well, since adjoining \(F(\zeta_m)\) recovers these
Kummer fields. On passing to \(B_{n+1}\), the torsion
radical is in the cyclotomic factor and the descended
cyclic fields agree.

## Divisible free exponents and nonsplit affine actions

**Theorem LD3.** Keep \(K=F(\zeta_3)\) and
\(\gamma=\zeta_3^j\eta^b\) after removal of sign.
Let \(s=v_3(b)\), with \(s=\infty\) if \(b=0\),
and \(r=\max(n-s,0)\) with \(r=0\) for \(s=\infty\).
For \(\gamma\ne1\), the local factorization, normal
closure and ray characters are as follows.

- If \(j=0\), there is one irreducible factor of degree
  \(3^r\), and for each \(1\le k\le n-r\) there are two
  factors of degree \(3^{r+k-1}\). The central factor
  is the pure root field of LD2 at level \(r\), or a
  rational root for \(r=0\). Each of the other factors
  has a totally ramified root field and the second
  discriminant formula of (2) at its degree. The
  splitting field over \(B_n\) has cyclic degree \(3^r\),
  with all its character conductors from the pure case
  of (3).
- If \(j\ne0\), the polynomial is irreducible of degree
  \(3^n\) and its root field is totally ramified with
  the second discriminant formula of (2). For \(r=0\)
  the splitting and root fields are both \(B_{n+1}\).
  For \(r>0\), the splitting field over \(B_n\) is
  cyclic of degree \(3^r\). Its primitive characters
  have conductor exponent \((3^n+1)/2\), and its
  characters of smaller exact order use the pure
  formula of (3). If \(0<s<n\), its natural affine
  group extension by the translations is nonsplit,
  even though it acts transitively on the \(3^n\) roots.

For \(\gamma=1\), each primitive cyclotomic degree
\(3^{k-1}\), \(1\le k\le n\), occurs as one factor with
multiplicity two, and \(X-2\) has multiplicity one.
The splitting field is \(B_n\).

Write \(F_i=F(\zeta_{3^i})\) in this proof. Choose
\(\theta_0\) with \(\theta_0^{3^n}=\eta^b\).
For \(r>0\), if \(\beta=\eta^{b/3^s}\), this is a
\(3^r\)-th root of the primitive pure unit \(\beta\).
For \(r=0\), choose \(\theta_0=\eta^{b/3^n}\in K\).
The radical field \(F_n(\theta_0)/F_n\) is cyclic of
degree \(3^r\). For \(r>0\) the free cubic remains
unramified and nontrivial on cyclotomic base change,
as in LD2. The field \(F_n(\theta_0)\) therefore also
has trivial intersection with \(F_{n+1}\) over \(F_n\),
since its unique degree-three subextension is
unramified whereas \(F_{n+1}/F_n\) is totally ramified.

For \(j=0\), the labels of the distinct Dickson roots
are the residues \(i\in\mathbb Z/3^n\) in
\(\theta_0\zeta_{3^n}^i+\theta_0^{-1}\zeta_{3^n}^{-i}\).
Translations add \(3^{n-r}\) times any residue, and
the multipliers form \(1+3\mathbb Z/3^n\).
Modulo translations there is a zero orbit and two
orbits of size \(3^{k-1}\) for each order \(3^k\)
in \(\mathbb Z/3^{n-r}\). This gives the factor degrees.
The zero orbit uses the pure radical field at level
\(r\). For a nonzero orbit choose
\(i=3^{n-r-k}a\) with \(3\nmid a\).
If \(q=r+k-1\), then
\[
 (\theta_0\zeta_{3^n}^i)^{3^q}
   =\zeta_3^a\,
       \begin{cases}
          \beta^{3^{k-1}}, & r>0,\\
          \theta_0^{3^{k-1}}, & r=0 .
        \end{cases}
\]
The right hand side has depth one. Its \(3^q\)-th
radical over \(K\) therefore has the same Eisenstein
different calculation as the mixed unit in LD2.
This also gives a rational root when \(q=0\).
The splitting field is the pure splitting field at
level \(r\) composed with \(B_n\); its conductors
follow from the pure base change in LD2. If
\(\gamma=1\) the labels \(i,-i\) identify and the
listed cyclotomic factors and their multiplicities
follow directly.

For \(j\ne0\), \(\gamma\) has depth one independently
of \(b\). The Eisenstein argument over \(K\) again
gives the degree and different for the root field.
Take \(\theta=\zeta_{3^{n+1}}^j\theta_0\) in the
compositum \(F_{n+1}(\theta_0)\).
Over \(B_n\), the cyclic splitting extension is given
by a product of a primitive character of the pure
degree-\(3^r\) extension and a nontrivial character
of \(B_{n+1}/B_n\). Indeed after the quadratic base
change to \(F_n\) the product is the Kummer character
of the product radical. The two factors descend by
the same simultaneous inversion, and restriction to
\(F_n\) identifies the characters of \(3\)-power order
of the two composita. For \(r=0\) this is just the next cyclotomic
step. For \(r>0\) the two factors are independent
because the first has unramified cubic first layer.
The cyclotomic character has conductor exponent
\((3^n+1)/2\), strictly larger than the conductor
of the pure character. Thus at exact order \(3^r\)
it determines the conductor. Proper powers reducing
the character order kill its cyclotomic factor,
giving the lower pure conductors.

Finally suppose \(0<s<n\). On the Dickson root
indices \(i\bmod 3^n\), elements of the full Galois
group of \(F_{n+1}(\theta_0)\) with cyclotomic
exponent \(a\equiv1\bmod3\) and pure translation \(h\)
act as
\[
      i\longmapsto ai+j(a-1)/3+3^s h\pmod{3^n},
      \qquad a\bmod 3^{n+1},\ h\bmod 3^r .
      \tag{4}
\]
Cyclotomic inversion acts trivially on the root indices
after inverting the radical too, as in LD2.
The kernel of the multiplier map consists of the
translations by \(3^s\mathbb Z/3^n\), since changes
of lift of \(a\) add a multiple of \(3^{n-1}\),
already in that subgroup. This gives the exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow C_{3^r}\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Gal}(N_n/F)\longrightarrow C_{3^{n-1}}
 \longrightarrow 1 .
\]
Every lift of the generator \(4\bmod3^n\) has
translation part congruent to \(j\bmod3\).
Its \(3^{n-1}\)-st power is translation by that
part times \((4^{3^{n-1}}-1)/3\), of valuation
\(n-1\) modulo \(3^n\). It is nontrivial, so no lift
has order \(3^{n-1}\). In fact each lift has order
\(3^n\) and acts transitively, as follows from the
same geometric sum applied to the orbit of zero.
This explains why the root polynomial remains
irreducible even though the radical over \(F_n\)
has degree only \(3^r\).

Turnwald's stated irreducibility criterion already
reduces reducibility of a Dickson translate of degree
\(3^n\) to a cubic trace equation. The local distinction
here also gives the complete factor degrees, different
exponents and the possibly nonsplit group extension.
[Turnwald, *Reducibility of translates of Dickson
polynomials*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-98-04363-9).

The [exact checker](verify_local_dickson_norm_one_unit_towers.py)
checks trace representatives, factor degrees, affine
actions and discriminant exponents in the displayed
types. The conclusions for arbitrary levels and units
use the written valuation and induction proofs.
