# Radical and discriminant in Legendre isogeny fields

The abc radical is recovered, up to an additive \(O(\log(h+2))\)
in logarithmic units, by the largest normalized discriminant of
the fields of definition of the cyclic \(\ell\)-isogenies of a
Legendre curve when \(\ell\asymp h+2\) is prime. The bound is
uniform over the base field, and each extension has relative degree
at most \(\ell+1\). The ramification formulas below apply at every
finite level, including composite and growing levels. The
projective division field at a power of two of size \(h+2\) also
recovers the radical with this error and has a \(2\)-group as its
Galois group over the base.

## Projective division fields at every level

Let \(K\) be any number field, \(d_K=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and
\(t\in K\setminus\{0,1\}\). Write \(h=h(t)\) for the absolute
logarithmic height. For a finite prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\),
take the integer-valued valuation \(v_{\mathfrak p}\) and put
\[
 m_{\mathfrak p}=\max\{|v_{\mathfrak p}(t)|,\,
                          |v_{\mathfrak p}(1-t)|\},\qquad
 w_{\mathfrak p}=\frac{\log\mathrm N\mathfrak p}{d_K}.
\]
Put \(r_K(t)=\sum_{m_{\mathfrak p}>0}w_{\mathfrak p}\) and
\(B=\sum_{\mathfrak p}m_{\mathfrak p}w_{\mathfrak p}\).
The three possible positive depths are \(v_{\mathfrak p}(t)\),
\(v_{\mathfrak p}(1-t)\), and \(-v_{\mathfrak p}(t)\) when
\(v_{\mathfrak p}(t)<0\). Bounding their sums by the finite parts
of \(h(1/t)\), \(h(1/(1-t))\), and \(h(t)\) gives
\(r_K(t)\le B\le 3h+\log 2\).

For a finite extension \(L/K\) use
\[
 \delta(L/K)=\log\operatorname{rd}(L)-\log\operatorname{rd}(K)
       =\frac{\log\mathrm N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak d_{L/K}}
                    {[L:\mathbb Q]},
\]
where \(\operatorname{rd}\) is the absolute root discriminant.
For \(N\ge2\), let \(P_N/K\) be the projective \(N\)-division
field of \(E_t:y^2=x(x-1)(x-t)\), the fixed field of the kernel
of the action on \(E_t[N]\) modulo scalars. At \(\mathfrak p\nmid
2N\) its inertia is generated by a conjugate of a unipotent
matrix and has order
\[
 e_{\mathfrak p}=\frac{N}{\gcd(N,2m_{\mathfrak p})}.             \tag{PD1}
\]
For \(t\) integral at the prime, the model has good reduction
when \(m_{\mathfrak p}=0\). Otherwise its discriminant has
valuation \(2m_{\mathfrak p}\) and \(c_4=16(t^2-t+1)\) is a unit,
so the reduction is multiplicative. If \(t\) has negative valuation,
exchange \(t\) and \(1/t\). Scaling \(x\) by \(t\) identifies the
two curves after a quadratic twist, which does not change the
projective action. In the multiplicative case, over the maximal
unramified local extension the split Tate parameter has valuation
\(2m_{\mathfrak p}\). The torsion basis uses \(\mu_N\) and an
\(N\)-th root of that parameter. Roots of units are unramified
here, since the residue characteristic does not divide \(N\).
Thus inertia is unipotent of the stated order, and its only scalar
is the identity. At good reduction, prime-to-residue-characteristic
torsion is unramified by the finite étale multiplication kernel on
the abelian scheme.
[Tate, §6, in particular the parametrization and formulas (33)–(34)](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN356556735_0023/PPN356556735_0023.pdf).
[The primitive Frey inertia formula](ABC_FREY_REPAIR_PROJECTIVE_INERTIA.md#projective-representations-and-inertia-orders).

The contribution outside \(2N\) to \(\delta(P_N/K)\) is
\[
 D_N^{\mathrm{proj}}=\sum_{\mathfrak p\nmid 2N}
                                  (1-e_{\mathfrak p}^{-1})w_{\mathfrak p}.
\]
For the remaining places, a Galois local extension with
ramification index \(e\) has different exponent
\(d\le e-1+e\,v_{\mathfrak p}(e)\). Indeed, after passing to the
maximal unramified subextension a uniformizer generates the
ring of integers and the different is given by the derivative
of its Eisenstein polynomial. The derivative terms have
distinct valuations modulo \(e\), and the leading term has
valuation \(e-1+e\,v_{\mathfrak p}(e)\).
For a Galois field \(P/K\) of group order \(g\), the contribution
at \(\mathfrak p\) to \(\delta(P/K)\) is \((d/e)w_{\mathfrak p}\).
Every local \(e\) divides \(g\). For a rational prime \(p\), the
sum of the \(v_{\mathfrak p}(e)w_{\mathfrak p}\) terms above
\(p\) is at most \(v_p(g)\log p\), by
\(\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid p} e(\mathfrak p/p)f(\mathfrak p/p)=d_K\).
Also \(\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid p}w_{\mathfrak p}\le\log p\).
Since \(g\le|\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb Z/N)| =
N^3\prod_{\ell\mid N}(1-\ell^{-2})\), it follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0&\le \delta(P_N/K)-D_N^{\mathrm{proj}}\le\log(2N)+3\log N,\\
 0&\le r_K(t)-D_N^{\mathrm{proj}}\le\log(2N)+2B/N.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{PD2}
\]
The second inequality charges the omitted primes by \(\log(2N)\)
and uses \(\gcd(N,2m_{\mathfrak p})\le2m_{\mathfrak p}\)
on the remaining support.

For \(N\) the smallest power of two at least \(h+2\), the
Galois image is a \(2\)-group because \(E_t[2]\) is rational
over \(K\). The tame part \(D_N^{\mathrm{proj}}\) differs from
\(r_K(t)\) by an absolute constant, since only primes above
\(2\) are omitted. The complete \(\delta(P_N/K)\) differs by
\(O(\log(h+2))\) with an absolute constant and
\([P_N:K]\le N^3\ll(h+2)^3\).

For \(K=\mathbb Q\) and \(t=(v/u)^2\) from an admissible
quartic pair, the positive depths away from \(2\) are twice
the valuations on \(u,v\) and the ordinary valuations on
\(u-v,u+v\). For odd \(N\) the \(\gcd\) in (PD1) therefore
gives exactly the same inertia orders as the degree-\(N\)
cyclic quartic lift away from \(2N\). The complete normalized
log discriminants differ by \(O(\log(2N))\), uniformly in
\(N,u,v\). For the cyclic field use the same local different
bound and its absolute degree at most \(N\varphi(N)\).

## The tame discriminant of cyclic isogeny fibres

For each geometric cyclic subgroup \(C\subset E_t[N]\) of
order \(N\), let \(K(C)\) be its field of definition as a subgroup.
The action on these subgroups factors through \(P_N\). The
number of subgroups is \(s_N=N\prod_{\ell\mid N}(1+1/\ell)\),
so each \(K(C)\) has degree over \(K\) at most \(s_N\). Choose
one \(C_i\) from each \(K\)-Galois orbit and write \(L_i=K(C_i)\).
Let \(\overline\delta_N\) be the weighted average of
\(\delta(L_i/K)\) with weights \([L_i:K]/s_N\).

At a prime away from \(2N\), the contribution to this average,
divided by \(w_{\mathfrak p}\), is one minus the fraction
\[
 \frac{\#\{\text{inertia orbits on cyclic subgroups}\}}{s_N}.
\]
Indeed a tame local field factor of residue degree \(f\) and
ramification index \(e\) contributes \(f(e-1)\) to the relative
discriminant exponent. It accounts for \(ef\) geometric points
and \(f\) inertia orbits. Summing this over all local factors
gives the fraction above even when the fibre is disconnected.

For a unipotent generator \(U(b)\) on
\(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Z/\ell^a)\) of order \(\ell^R\), the fraction
of inertia orbits has the following closed formula:
\[
 \Phi_\ell(R)=
 \begin{cases}
  \ell^{-R/2},& R\text{ even},\\[2pt]
  \dfrac{2\ell^{-(R-1)/2}}{\ell+1},&R\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{PD3}
\]
Use the \( \ell^a\) points \([x:1]\), where \(U(b)\) translates
by \(b\), and the \(\ell^{a-1}\) points \([1:y]\) with
\(\ell\mid y\). In the second chart, \(U(b)^n\) fixes \(y\) exactly
when \(nby^2=0\bmod\ell^a\), so the orbit length at depth
\(j=v_\ell(y)\) is \(\ell^{(R-2j)_+}\). For \(R>0\), divide the
orbit count by \(\ell^a\). The affine chart gives \(\ell^{-R}\).
Put \(s=\lceil R/2\rceil\). The second chart contributes
\[
 (1-\ell^{-1})\ell^{-R}\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\ell^j+\ell^{-s}.
\]
The tail includes \(y=0\). Dividing the total by \(1+\ell^{-1}\)
gives (PD3), which is also immediate for the identity \(R=0\).
At composite level, the Chinese remainder theorem gives a
product of projective lines, and the cycle lengths across
distinct prime powers are coprime. Each product of cycles is
one cycle, so the fractions in (PD3) multiply. Thus the exact
fraction at \(\mathfrak p\nmid 2N\) is
\(\prod_{\ell\mid N}\Phi_\ell(v_\ell(e_{\mathfrak p}))\).
It is at most \(e_{\mathfrak p}^{-1/2}\).

Let \(D_N^{\mathrm{iso}}\) be the contribution away from \(2N\)
to \(\overline\delta_N\). It satisfies
\[
 0\le r_K(t)-D_N^{\mathrm{iso}}
       \le\log(2N)+\sqrt{2B\,r_K(t)/N}.                         \tag{PD4}
\]
To see this, use the last orbit bound and Cauchy–Schwarz on
\(\sum_{m_{\mathfrak p}>0}\sqrt{2m_{\mathfrak p}/N}\,w_{\mathfrak p}\).
For prime \(\ell>2\), the error outside \(2\ell\) is in fact at
most \(2r_K(t)/(\ell+1)+B/\ell\), since the fraction in (PD3)
is \(2/(\ell+1)\) when inertia is nontrivial and is one only
when \(\ell\mid m_{\mathfrak p}\). The coefficient \(1/\ell\)
in the latter contribution follows from \(m_{\mathfrak p}\ge\ell\).

In every field \(L_i\subseteq P_N\), transitivity of the
relative discriminant gives \(\delta(L_i/K)\le\delta(P_N/K)\).
In particular
\[
            D_N^{\mathrm{iso}}\le \overline\delta_N
                   \le \max_i\delta(L_i/K)\le \delta(P_N/K).
                                                               \tag{PD5}
\]
At the least prime \(\ell\ge h+2\), Bertrand's postulate gives
\(\ell\ll h+2\). Equations (PD2), (PD4), and the prime-level
error show that \(\max_i\delta(L_i/K)=r_K(t)+O(\log(h+2))\),
and the same holds for the weighted average. Every field in
the average has degree at most \(\ell+1\ll h+2\). If a
\(2\)-group Galois closure is desired for the individual fields,
take \(N\) the smallest power of two at least \((h+2)^2\)
instead. Then the quadratic degree bound \(s_N\ll(h+2)^2\)
suffices and (PD4) gives an absolute error for the tame part,
since only \(2\) is omitted. The full discriminant comparison
still has an \(O(\log(h+2))\) error.

Dvornicich and Paladino, Theorem 4.2, bound the discriminant
of the full division field of any elliptic curve in terms of
its coefficients and the division level. The present
Legendre calculation instead estimates the normalized
discriminant on both sides by the truncated cusp count and
passes to the smaller fields of cyclic-isogeny kernels.
[Dvornicich–Paladino, Theorem 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03429v3).

## Moving the cusp radical into the extension

Let a superscript \({\rm away}\) on a discriminant or radical
mean that only primes of residue characteristic prime to \(2N\)
are counted. For every \(L_i=K(C_i)\) and for \(P_N\) itself,
\[
 \delta(L_i/K)^{\rm away}+r_{L_i}(t)^{\rm away}
                         =r_K(t)^{\rm away}.                  \tag{PD6}
\]
At a base prime of positive cusp depth, all primes above it
have positive depth by valuation scaling. Their weights sum to
\((\sum f_i/[L_i:K])w_{\mathfrak p}\), whereas the tame
discriminant contributes
\((1-\sum f_i/[L_i:K])w_{\mathfrak p}\). At zero depth the
extension is unramified and both contributions vanish.
Here the sum is over local factors at the base prime.

The field realizing the largest tame contribution at prime
level \(\ell\asymp h+2\) has the two properties
\[
 \delta(K(C)/K)=r_K(t)+O(\log(h+2)),\qquad
 r_{K(C)}(t)=O(\log(h+2)).
\]
In fact the maximal tame contribution is at least
\(D_\ell^{\mathrm{iso}}\), so both statements follow from
(PD2), (PD4), (PD6), and the bound \(\log(2\ell)\) for the
total weight above the omitted rational primes over any field.
At the power-of-two isogeny level of size \((h+2)^2\), the
same argument gives \(r_{K(C)}(t)=O(1)\), since only primes
above \(2\) are omitted. On the full projective division field
at the power-of-two level of size \(h+2\), (PD2) and (PD6) give
that bounded radical directly. The height \(h(t)\) is absolute
and is unchanged in these extensions.

## The pointwise modular discriminant obligation

For primitive positive \(a+b=c\), \(t=a/c\) gives
\(h=\log c\) and \(r_{\mathbb Q}(t)=\log\operatorname{rad}(abc)\).
For each \(t\) take \(\ell\) to be the least prime at least
\(h+2\), and set \(D(t)=\max_C\delta(\mathbb Q(C)/\mathbb Q)\),
where \(C\) ranges over cyclic subgroups of order \(\ell\)
of \(E_t\). The full abc conjecture is equivalent to
\[
       h(t)\le(1+\varepsilon)D(t)+O_\varepsilon(1)
           \qquad(\varepsilon>0,\ 0<t<1,\ t\in\mathbb Q).
                                                               \tag{PD7}
\]
Indeed \(D(t)=r_{\mathbb Q}(t)+O(\log(h+2))\), and the error
can be absorbed into an arbitrarily small multiple of \(h\)
in either direction, by first using a smaller \(\varepsilon\).
The same equivalence holds with the degree-weighted average
or with the projective division field at the specified
power-of-two level. More generally, the uniform field
comparison preserves such a pointwise inequality on any
specified range of fields and \(t\), also when both formulations
include the same base-field discriminant term. The Legendre
formula
\(j=256(1-t+t^2)^3/(t^2(1-t)^2)\) has degree six, so the
left side of (PD7) can also be written \(h(j)/6+O(1)\).

The remaining input is a lower bound for the discriminant
of these specialized fields in terms of the height of the
defining Legendre parameter. One version uses the lifts in
(PD6) with bounded normalized cusp radical as the field
degree grows with \(h\). A second version fixes the degree
before varying the input and permits a small fraction of the
height to remain in the cusp radical.

## Fixed levels for the pointwise height inequality

For a fixed \(N\) put
\(D_N(t)=\max_C\delta(\mathbb Q(C)/\mathbb Q)\), with \(C\)
running over cyclic subgroups of order \(N\).
For \(A=\log\operatorname{rad}(abc)\), equations (PD2),
(PD4) and (PD5), and \(A\le B\le3h\) over \(\mathbb Q\),
give
\[
 A-\frac{O(h)}{\sqrt N}-O(\log(2N))
     \le D_N(t)\le A+O(\log(2N)).                      \tag{PD8}
\]
At a fixed prime level \(\ell>2\), the sharper form of
(PD4) improves the lower-bound error to
\(2A/(\ell+1)+3h/\ell+\log(2\ell)\).
For the full projective fields (PD2) gives an
\(O(h/N+\log(2N))\) comparison.

Full abc is equivalent to
\(h(t)\le b_N D_N(t)+C_N\) at an unbounded sequence
of fixed levels \(N\) with \(b_N\to1\).
For any target exponent take one fixed \(N\) with
\(b_N\) sufficiently close to one and use the upper
bound in (PD8). Conversely, apply abc at a sequence
of coefficients tending to one and absorb the
\(O(h/\sqrt N)\) term for all sufficiently large fixed
\(N\). The same reasoning works with the degree-weighted
average or the full projective fields. Thus the height
comparison may use constants depending on a fixed level
and fields of bounded degree, even in the version
supported only on \(2N\operatorname{rad}(abc)\).
