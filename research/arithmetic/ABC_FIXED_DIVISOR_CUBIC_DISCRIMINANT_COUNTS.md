# Uniform fixed-divisor counts for balanced cubic discriminants

Fix \(C>0\). Uniformly for \(N\ge2\) and squarefree
\(x\in[N,2N]\), the number of cubic fields \(K\) with
\(0<|\operatorname{Disc}(K)|\le CN^2\) and
\(x\mid\operatorname{Disc}(K)\) is
\(O_{C,\varepsilon}(N^{4/3+\varepsilon})\).
The same estimate holds for irreducible cubic rings.
It gives the balanced first-moment row bound
\[
\sum_{\substack{N\le y\le 2N\\y\ \mathrm{squarefree}\\(x,y)=1}} h_3(\pm xy)\ll_\varepsilon N^{4/3+\varepsilon},
\]
and the averaged refinement below gives \(O_\varepsilon(N^{29/9+\varepsilon})\) for the balanced two-edge moment. All implicit constants below are uniform in \(x,N\), apart from dependence on the indicated constants.

## Integral representatives and a lattice count

The Levi–Delone–Faddeev correspondence associates a cubic ring to a \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\)-orbit of integral binary cubic forms \(F=aX^3+bX^2Y+cXY^2+dY^3\), preserving the discriminant. Orders in cubic fields correspond to irreducible forms. By the Iwasawa/Siegel description in Bhargava–Shankar–Tsimerman, section 5.2 and equation (15), an orbit with \(0<|\mathrm{Disc}(F)|\le CN^2\) has a representative in a box
\[
|a|\le C_0 R t^{-3},\quad |b|\le C_0 R t^{-1},\quad |c|\le C_0 R t,\quad |d|\le C_0 R t^3,\qquad R=N^{1/2},
\]
for some \(t\ge1\), after adjusting an absolute lower bound for \(t\) into \(C_0=C_0(C)\). Indeed, for each sign fix a real form of absolute discriminant one. The compact and scalar factors give coefficients \(O_C(R)\), the determinant-one diagonal \(\operatorname{diag}(t^{-1},t)\) gives the four displayed weights, and the bounded lower unipotent acts as \(X\mapsto X+uY\) with bounded \(u\), preserving these bounds up to constants. The coefficient \(a\) is nonzero for irreducible forms, so \(t^3\ll_C R\). Replacing \(t\) by a dyadic value costs only a constant in \(C_0\) and \(O_C(\log N)\) boxes. The source is [Bhargava–Shankar–Tsimerman, On the Davenport–Heilbronn theorems and second order terms, section 5.2 and equation (15)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0672).

Set \(q=x/\gcd(x,6)\asymp N\), and retain only \(q\mid\mathrm{Disc}(F)\). For every prime \(p\mid q\), a singular cubic over \(\mathbb F_p\) has a repeated linear root over \(\mathbb F_p\), since any repeated nonconstant irreducible factor has degree at most one. The zero form can be assigned any root. For each \(p\) choose a linear form \(\ell_p\) up to scalar such that \(\ell_p^2\) divides \(F\bmod p\). There are \(\prod_{p\mid q}(p+1)\ll_\varepsilon N^{1+\varepsilon}\) assignments. Each assignment defines a lattice
\[
\Lambda=\{F\in\mathbb Z^4:F\bmod p\in E_p\text{ for }p\mid q\},\qquad E_p=\ell_p^2\mathbb F_p[X,Y]_1 .
\]
The space \(E_p\) has dimension two, so the index, hence covolume, of \(\Lambda\) is \(q^2\).

Use the Euclidean norm \(\|F\|_t=\|F(tX,t^{-1}Y)\|_2\) on coefficient vectors. It is the pullback by \(D_t=\operatorname{diag}(t^3,t,t^{-1},t^{-3})\), which has determinant one. Each displayed box is contained in the ball of radius \(C_1 R\) in this norm. Let \(j\) be the dimension of the real span of the lattice vectors in that ball. If the ball contains an irreducible form then \(j\ge1\). Choose independent vectors \(F_i\), \(1\le i\le j\), with \(\|F_i\|_t=\lambda_i\), where \(\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_j\le C_1R\) are the successive minima of the unit ball. Such a choice exists by selecting a basis in the increasing flag of spans at the distinct minima. The ball contains \(O(R^j/\prod_{i\le j}\lambda_i)\) lattice points. To see this, work in its \(j\)-dimensional linear span in the Euclidean coordinates supplied by \(D_t\). The intersection lattice has covolume \(\gg_j\prod_{i\le j}\lambda_i\) by Minkowski's second theorem applied to the unit ball in that span. It has covering radius at most \(\tfrac12\sum_i\lambda_i\ll_j R\), by taking the centered fundamental parallelepiped of the sublattice generated by the \(F_i\). Its Voronoi cells around lattice points in the ball have disjoint interiors and lie in a ball of radius \(O_j(R)\). Comparing volumes proves the bound. The case \(j=0\) contains only zero.

## Small cusp parameter

Assume \(1\le t\le N^{1/12}\). Fix a lattice assignment with an irreducible form in the ball.

If \(j=1\), the vector \(F_1\) is irreducible because the span has an irreducible form. Its integral discriminant is nonzero and divisible by \(q\). Substitution by \(\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})\) preserves the discriminant, of degree four, so \(\lambda_1\gg q^{1/4}\).

If \(j=2\), the resultant of \(F_1,F_2\) is nonzero. Otherwise they would have a common proper nonconstant factor over \(\mathbb Q\), which divides every form in the span, contradicting irreducibility there. For every \(p\mid q\), their reductions are both divisible by \(\ell_p^2\). The Sylvester map from the pair of binary quadratics \((A,B)\) to the binary quintic \(A F_1+B F_2\) therefore has image of dimension at most four modulo \(p\), so its integral determinant is divisible by \(p^2\). Thus \(q^2\) divides the nonzero resultant. The resultant is invariant under determinant-one substitution and has degree three in each form. Therefore \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\gg q^{2/3}\).

If \(j=3\), write \(n=(n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)\) for the signed integer cofactor vector of the three rows \(F_1,F_2,F_3\). Their rank is at most two modulo every \(p\mid q\), so \(n/q\) is integral. Moreover \(n/q\bmod p\) annihilates \(E_p\). If the row reductions span \(E_p\) this follows from the exact orthogonality of \(n/q\) to the three rows. If their rank is at most one, every \(3\)-row minor is divisible by \(p^2\), so \(n/q\) vanishes modulo \(p\) because \(q\) is squarefree.

For an integral coefficient functional \(v=(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4)\) define
\[
G_v(X,Y)=v_4X^3-3v_3X^2Y+3v_2XY^2-v_1Y^3.
\]
This equals \(v((-Y X_0+X Y_0)^3)\) with \(X_0,Y_0\) formal variables in the input to \(v\). If \(v\) annihilates \(E_p\), then \(G_v\) and both its partial derivatives vanish at the projective root of \(\ell_p\), by evaluation of the displayed cube and its derivatives. Thus \(G_v\bmod p\) is divisible by \(\ell_p^2\). Taking \(G=G_{n/q}\) gives a nonzero integral cubic with \(q\mid\operatorname{Disc}(G)\). The cofactor vector of the rows \(D_t F_i\) is exactly \(D_t^{-1}n\), since \(\det D_t=1\). The coordinates of \(D_tG\) are the reversed coordinates of \(D_t^{-1}n/q\), with multipliers in \(\{-3,-1,1,3\}\). By the exterior product estimate,
\[
\|G\|_t\ll \lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3/q.
\]
If \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3<c_0q^{5/4}\) for a sufficiently small absolute \(c_0>0\), then \(|\operatorname{Disc}(G)|<q\), hence \(\operatorname{Disc}(G)=0\). As \(G\ne0\), over \(\mathbb Q\) it has the form \(G=c_2\ell^2 m\), with primitive integral linear forms \(\ell,m\) and nonzero integer \(c_2\), by Gauss's lemma. The repeated root is rational because it has multiplicity at least two in a cubic. We may use the same weighted norm on linear forms. Multiplication on the unit spheres of linear forms has a positive minimum norm for \(\ell^2 m\), by compactness and the lack of zero divisors. Since the weighted norm of a nonzero integral linear form is at least \(1/t\), it follows that \(\|\ell\|_t^2\ll t\|G\|_t/|c_2|\).

For a prime \(p\mid q\) with \(p\nmid c_2\), both linear factors survive modulo \(p\), and the reduction of \(\ell\) has the unique repeated root of \(G\), namely the prescribed one. Put \(\ell=A X+B Y\) and \(z=(-B,A)\). The gradient \(\nabla F(z)\) for every lattice form in the ball vanishes modulo \(q/\gcd(q,c_2)\). On the other hand, the coordinate vector \((t^{-1}z_1,t z_2)\) has Euclidean norm \(\|\ell\|_t\), and the chain rule gives
\[
|\nabla F(z)|\ll_C t R\|\ell\|_t^2
 \ll_C t^2R\|G\|_t/|c_2|
 \ll_C N^{11/12}/|c_2|.
\]
For sufficiently large \(N\) this is strictly below \(q/|c_2|\le q/\gcd(q,c_2)\). The integral gradient must vanish. Since \(z\ne0\), every such \(F\) has a repeated rational root, contradicting existence of an irreducible form. This proves \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3\gg q^{5/4}\).

If \(j=4\), the determinant of the four independent lattice vectors is a nonzero multiple of \(q^2\). Since the weighted norm is obtained by a determinant-one linear map, Hadamard's bound gives \(\prod_i\lambda_i\gg q^2\).

The four upper bounds on the lattice point count for these cases are \(O_C(N^{1/4})\), \(O_C(N^{1/3})\), \(O_C(N^{1/4})\), and \(O_C(1)\), respectively. The assignment count proves \(O_{C,\varepsilon}(N^{4/3+\varepsilon})\) for each such box.

## Large cusp parameter

Assume \(t\ge N^{1/12}\) and use only \(a\ne0\); nonempty boxes then satisfy \(t^3\ll_C R\). Put \(A=C_0Rt^{-3}\), \(B=C_0Rt^{-1}\), \(C'=C_0Rt\), \(D=C_0Rt^3\), so \(A\ge1\), \(B,C'\gg_C1\), \(D\ll_C N\), and \(q\asymp N\). Fix \(a,b,c\) in the box. The discriminant as a polynomial in \(d\) is
\[
-27a^2d^2+(18abc-4b^3)d+b^2c^2-4ac^3.
\]
Modulo \(p\mid q\), it has at most two roots if \(p\nmid a\), at most one when \(p\mid a,p\nmid b\), and at most \(p\) when \(p\mid a,b\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives at most \(2^{\omega(q)}\gcd(q,a,b)\) root classes, and \(D\ll q\) means each has \(O_C(1)\) representatives. By the divisor formula for the gcd,
\[
\sum_{0<|a|\le A,\ |b|\le B}\gcd(q,a,b)
 \ll\sum_{e\mid q} e(A/e)(B/e+1)
 \ll AB\,\tau(q).
\]
The factor \(A/e\) is valid because zero is excluded in the \(a\) coordinate, even when \(e>A\). Multiplying by the number of \(c\) choices gives
\[
\ll_{C,\varepsilon} N^\varepsilon A B C' \ll_{C,\varepsilon} N^{3/2+\varepsilon}t^{-3}\le N^{5/4+\varepsilon}.
\]
The dyadic box sum finishes the cubic-field and ring counts.

## A range of unequal discriminant and modulus scales

For discriminant bound \(Y\) and squarefree modulus \(x\)
with \(Y^{3/7}\le x\le Y^{3/5}\), the same proof gives
\[
               O_\varepsilon(Y^{1/2+\varepsilon}x^{1/3})
\]
irreducible cubic rings. To check the thresholds set
\(R=Y^{1/4}\), \(q=x/\gcd(x,6)\), and put the small cusp cutoff
at \(t\le c q^{3/8}R^{-1/2}\) with a fixed sufficiently small
\(c\). The rank-three gradient estimate is then strictly below
\(q/|c_2|\). After summing residue assignments, the four lattice
ranks contribute at most \(q^\varepsilon\) times
\[
                q^{3/4}R+q^{1/3}R^2+
                         q^{-1/4}R^3+R^4/q .
\]
In the large cusp the number of \(d\)-lifts per residue contributes
\(1+Rt^3/q\). The box count is consequently at most
\[
     q^\varepsilon(R^3t^{-3}+R^4/q)
                 \ll q^\varepsilon(R^{9/2}q^{-9/8}+R^4/q).
\]
Each term is bounded by \(q^\varepsilon R^2 q^{1/3}\)
on \(R^{12/7}\ll q\ll R^{12/5}\), with the fixed comparability
constants harmless. The remaining dyadic cost is \(Y^\varepsilon\).
For adjacent dyadic boxes \(x\asymp A,\ y\asymp B\) with
\(A\le B\le A^{4/3}\), the resulting signed row bound is
\(O_\varepsilon(A^{5/6}B^{1/2}(AB)^\varepsilon)\).

## Consequences and limits

For each fixed \(x\), the quadratic fields \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\pm xy})\) in the row are distinct. Their fundamental discriminants have absolute value \(O(N^2)\) and are divisible by \(x\). The usual cubic-field correspondence gives twice the number of cubic fields with that discriminant as \(h_3(\pm xy)-1\). The trivial terms cost \(O(N)\). This proves the claimed uniform row bound for either sign. The ordinary first-moment bound and the divisor bound give the sum of the other signed rows \(\ll_\varepsilon N^{2+\varepsilon}\), so the two-edge product moment is \(\ll_\varepsilon N^{10/3+\varepsilon}\). The stronger averaged bound below applies to the same field family.

## An averaged saving from short cubic certificates

Let \(S_x=\sum_y(h_3(xy)+h_3(-xy))\) on the balanced squarefree
coprime row of size scale \(N\). Then
\[
                  \sum_x S_x^2\ll_\varepsilon N^{29/9+\varepsilon}.
\]
Keep \(R=N^{1/2}\), \(q=x/\gcd(x,6)\), and the weighted boxes from
the single-row proof. For any form in such a box, substitution
\(F(X,Y)\mapsto F(X+mY,Y)\) for \(|m|\le t^2\) yields \(\gg t^2\)
distinct forms in a fixed enlargement of the box. Indeed the
corresponding substitution on \(F(tX,t^{-1}Y)\) has parameter
\(m/t^2\), and a nontrivial unipotent cannot stabilize the three
distinct projective roots of a nonsingular cubic. An orbit
represented in the original box is therefore counted by the
enlarged-box form count divided by \(\gg t^2\). Distinct orbits
have disjoint sets of translates. We can split this form majorant
into two terms without assigning an entire orbit to either term.
For \(t\ge N^{1/18}\) the quadratic-in-\(d\) coefficient bound
proved above, whose derivation uses only \(a\ne0\) and \(Rt^3\ll N\),
gives a contribution
\(O_\varepsilon(N^{3/2+\varepsilon}t^{-5})
 =O_\varepsilon(N^{11/9+\varepsilon})\) per \(x\).

For \(t<N^{1/18}\) use the minima argument in the enlarged box.
Count only forms of fundamental discriminant, so they are
primitive (content to the fourth power divides the discriminant).
Rank one now gives at most two forms per assignment. Rank four
gives \(O(1)\) as before. In rank two classify an assignment by
whether \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\) is at least \(q^{2/3+1/9}\), and
in rank three by whether \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3\) is at
least \(q^{5/4+1/36}\). In either large-product case there are
\(O(N^{2/9})\) points per assignment, hence
\(O_\varepsilon(N^{11/9+\varepsilon})\) per \(x\). A short
certificate in the other cases is respectively \(F_1\) of
weighted norm \(\ll J_2=N^{7/18}\), or the nonzero integral
dual cubic \(G_{n/q}\) of weighted norm \(\ll J_3=N^{5/18}\).
Both have the prescribed repeated roots modulo every \(p\mid q\).

Here is a bound over all centers \(x\) for the short nonsingular
certificates at either norm bound \(J=J_2,J_3\). Write the
certificate \(V=cV'\) by content \(c\ge1\). Its nonzero
discriminant, of polynomial size in \(N\), supplies only
\(O_\varepsilon(N^\varepsilon)\) possible divisors \(q\). The
assignment is unique at each prime of \(q\) outside \(c\) and
costs at most \(O_\varepsilon(N^\varepsilon c)\) jointly at
the other primes. For \(J'=J/c\), the weighted coefficient
box for \(V'\) with leading coefficient nonzero has
\(O((J')^4)\) points whenever nonempty. When the leading
coefficient is zero, the second coefficient is nonzero
by nonsingularity. Its box has \(O((J')^3t^3)\) points when
nonempty. In these two cases, respectively, \(J'\gg t^3\)
or \(J'\gg t\), so the indicated lengths cover the shorter
nonzero coordinate. Summing the two bounds with weight \(c\)
gives \(O(J^4+J^3t^3)=O(J^4)\) because \(t^3\ll J\). Thus the
total assignment count over \(x\) is \(O_\varepsilon(J^4
N^\varepsilon)\), with the bounded ambiguity at 2 and 3 harmless.

For a singular certificate use the factorization
\(V=c\ell^2m\) with primitive integer linear forms, as in
the rank-three argument. Put \(e=\gcd(q,c)\). The root at
each prime outside \(e\) is fixed by \(\ell\). There are
\(O_\varepsilon(N^\varepsilon e)\) assignments at the
remaining primes. Also \(\|\ell\|_t^2\ll tJ/e\).
If \(\ell=Y\) up to sign, the leading coefficient of every
form in the lattice is divisible by \(q/e\). The certificate
has \(e\le |c|\ll Jt^3\), so \(RJ=o(q)\) makes the
leading-coefficient bound \(O(R/t^3)\) strictly smaller than
\(q/e\) for all sufficiently large \(N\). There would be no
irreducible form in the box. For any other \(\ell\), its
\(X\)-coefficient is nonzero, so there are \(O(tJ/e)\)
choices in its weighted box. Multiply by the \(e\)-weighted
root count and sum over divisors \(e\mid q\). The per-center
assignment cost is \(O_\varepsilon(tJ N^\varepsilon)\).
After the old per-assignment rank-two and rank-three point
bounds, the per-center exponents for these singular cases
are at most \(1/18+7/18+1/3=7/9\) and
\(1/18+5/18+1/4=7/12\).

We have proved \(S_x\le G_x+B_x\) with nonnegative form-count
majorants. The large cusp, the larger products, the singular
certificates, and the trivial classes give
\(G_x\ll_\varepsilon N^{11/9+\varepsilon}\).
The nonsingular certificates give
\[
 \sum_x B_x\ll_\varepsilon
    N^\varepsilon\bigl(N^{4(7/18)+1/3}+N^{4(5/18)+1/4}\bigr)
    \ll_\varepsilon N^{17/9+\varepsilon}.
\]
We used the old per-assignment point counts in this last bound,
and absorbed the dyadic summation. Now use
\(\sum_x S_x\ll_\varepsilon N^{2+\varepsilon}\) from the
signed ordinary first moment, and the uniform old row bound
\(S_x\ll_\varepsilon N^{4/3+\varepsilon}\). Multiplying the
good term by the first and the bad term by the second proves
the exponent \(29/9\).

This improves the balanced degree-36 estimate in MP11 for any
two sign choices. It also improves the balanced joint minimum
moment. With the squarefree and pairwise coprimality restrictions,
write \(h_{xy}=h_3(\epsilon_1xy)\), and analogously for \(xz,yz\).
For \(3/2\le k\le2\) the result is
\[
 \sum_{x,y,z}^{*} \min(h_{xy},h_{xz},h_{yz})^k
 \ll_{\varepsilon,k} N^{3+(4/9)(k-3/2)+\varepsilon}.
\]
Indeed the new two-edge count bounds the common threshold
tail by \(O_\varepsilon(N^{29/9+\varepsilon}H^{-2})\).
The MP4 triangle bound supplies \(O_\varepsilon(N^{3+\varepsilon}
H^{-3/2})\). These cross at \(H=N^{4/9}\). Sum dyadically,
using \(3/2\le k\le2\). This improves the previous \(k>3/2\)
moment over the full balanced range, with the target
price-free \(N^{3+\varepsilon}\) moment at \(k=2\) still open.
