# Uniform fixed-divisor counts for balanced cubic discriminants

Fix \(C>0\). Uniformly for \(N\ge2\) and squarefree
\(x\in[N,2N]\), the number of cubic fields \(K\) with
\(0<|\operatorname{Disc}(K)|\le CN^2\) and
\(x\mid\operatorname{Disc}(K)\) is
\(O_{C,\varepsilon}(N^{4/3+\varepsilon})\).
The same estimate holds for irreducible cubic rings.
It gives the balanced first-moment row bound
\[
\sum_{\substack{N\le y\le 2N\\y\ \mathrm{squarefree}\\(x,y)=1}} h_3(\pm xy)\ll_\varepsilon N^{4/3+\varepsilon},
\]
and the averaged refinement below gives
\(O_\varepsilon(N^{3+\varepsilon})\) for the balanced two-edge
moment. With unequal kernel lengths \(A,B,C\), it gives the
natural bound \((ABC)^{1+\varepsilon}\) for two edges meeting at
a smallest kernel. These estimates follow by keeping short
cubic certificates within their own successive-minima ranges. All implicit constants below are uniform in \(x,N\), apart from dependence on the indicated constants.

## Integral representatives and a lattice count

The Levi–Delone–Faddeev correspondence associates a cubic ring to a \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\)-orbit of integral binary cubic forms \(F=aX^3+bX^2Y+cXY^2+dY^3\), preserving the discriminant. Orders in cubic fields correspond to irreducible forms. By the Iwasawa/Siegel description in Bhargava–Shankar–Tsimerman, section 5.2 and equation (15), an orbit with \(0<|\mathrm{Disc}(F)|\le CN^2\) has a representative in a box
\[
|a|\le C_0 R t^{-3},\quad |b|\le C_0 R t^{-1},\quad |c|\le C_0 R t,\quad |d|\le C_0 R t^3,\qquad R=N^{1/2},
\]
for some \(t\ge1\), after adjusting an absolute lower bound for \(t\) into \(C_0=C_0(C)\). Indeed, for each sign fix a real form of absolute discriminant one. The compact and scalar factors give coefficients \(O_C(R)\), the determinant-one diagonal \(\operatorname{diag}(t^{-1},t)\) gives the four displayed weights, and the bounded lower unipotent acts as \(X\mapsto X+uY\) with bounded \(u\), preserving these bounds up to constants. The coefficient \(a\) is nonzero for irreducible forms, so \(t^3\ll_C R\). Replacing \(t\) by a dyadic value costs only a constant in \(C_0\) and \(O_C(\log N)\) boxes. The source is [Bhargava–Shankar–Tsimerman, On the Davenport–Heilbronn theorems and second order terms, section 5.2 and equation (15)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0672).

Set \(q=x/\gcd(x,6)\asymp N\), and retain only \(q\mid\mathrm{Disc}(F)\). For every prime \(p\mid q\), a singular cubic over \(\mathbb F_p\) has a repeated linear root over \(\mathbb F_p\), since any repeated nonconstant irreducible factor has degree at most one. The zero form can be assigned any root. For each \(p\) choose a linear form \(\ell_p\) up to scalar such that \(\ell_p^2\) divides \(F\bmod p\). There are \(\prod_{p\mid q}(p+1)\ll_\varepsilon N^{1+\varepsilon}\) assignments. Each assignment defines a lattice
\[
\Lambda=\{F\in\mathbb Z^4:F\bmod p\in E_p\text{ for }p\mid q\},\qquad E_p=\ell_p^2\mathbb F_p[X,Y]_1 .
\]
The space \(E_p\) has dimension two, so the index, hence covolume, of \(\Lambda\) is \(q^2\).

Use the Euclidean norm \(\|F\|_t=\|F(tX,t^{-1}Y)\|_2\) on coefficient vectors. It is the pullback by \(D_t=\operatorname{diag}(t^3,t,t^{-1},t^{-3})\), which has determinant one. Each displayed box is contained in the ball of radius \(C_1 R\) in this norm. Let \(j\) be the dimension of the real span of the lattice vectors in that ball. If the ball contains an irreducible form then \(j\ge1\). Choose independent vectors \(F_i\), \(1\le i\le j\), with \(\|F_i\|_t=\lambda_i\), where \(\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_j\le C_1R\) are the successive minima of the unit ball. Such a choice exists by selecting a basis in the increasing flag of spans at the distinct minima. The ball contains \(O(R^j/\prod_{i\le j}\lambda_i)\) lattice points. To see this, work in its \(j\)-dimensional linear span in the Euclidean coordinates supplied by \(D_t\). The intersection lattice has covolume \(\gg_j\prod_{i\le j}\lambda_i\) by Minkowski's second theorem applied to the unit ball in that span. It has covering radius at most \(\tfrac12\sum_i\lambda_i\ll_j R\), by taking the centered fundamental parallelepiped of the sublattice generated by the \(F_i\). Its Voronoi cells around lattice points in the ball have disjoint interiors and lie in a ball of radius \(O_j(R)\). Comparing volumes proves the bound. The case \(j=0\) contains only zero.

## Small cusp parameter

Assume \(1\le t\le N^{1/12}\). Fix a lattice assignment with an irreducible form in the ball.

If \(j=1\), the vector \(F_1\) is irreducible because the span has an irreducible form. Its integral discriminant is nonzero and divisible by \(q\). Substitution by \(\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})\) preserves the discriminant, of degree four, so \(\lambda_1\gg q^{1/4}\).

If \(j=2\), the resultant of \(F_1,F_2\) is nonzero. Otherwise they would have a common proper nonconstant factor over \(\mathbb Q\), which divides every form in the span, contradicting irreducibility there. For every \(p\mid q\), their reductions are both divisible by \(\ell_p^2\). The Sylvester map from the pair of binary quadratics \((A,B)\) to the binary quintic \(A F_1+B F_2\) therefore has image of dimension at most four modulo \(p\), so its integral determinant is divisible by \(p^2\). Thus \(q^2\) divides the nonzero resultant. The resultant is invariant under determinant-one substitution and has degree three in each form. Therefore \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\gg q^{2/3}\).

If \(j=3\), write \(n=(n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)\) for the signed integer cofactor vector of the three rows \(F_1,F_2,F_3\). Their rank is at most two modulo every \(p\mid q\), so \(n/q\) is integral. Moreover \(n/q\bmod p\) annihilates \(E_p\). If the row reductions span \(E_p\) this follows from the exact orthogonality of \(n/q\) to the three rows. If their rank is at most one, every \(3\)-row minor is divisible by \(p^2\), so \(n/q\) vanishes modulo \(p\) because \(q\) is squarefree.

For an integral coefficient functional \(v=(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4)\) define
\[
G_v(X,Y)=v_4X^3-3v_3X^2Y+3v_2XY^2-v_1Y^3.
\]
This equals \(v((-Y X_0+X Y_0)^3)\) with \(X_0,Y_0\) formal variables in the input to \(v\). If \(v\) annihilates \(E_p\), then \(G_v\) and both its partial derivatives vanish at the projective root of \(\ell_p\), by evaluation of the displayed cube and its derivatives. Thus \(G_v\bmod p\) is divisible by \(\ell_p^2\). Taking \(G=G_{n/q}\) gives a nonzero integral cubic with \(q\mid\operatorname{Disc}(G)\). The cofactor vector of the rows \(D_t F_i\) is exactly \(D_t^{-1}n\), since \(\det D_t=1\). The coordinates of \(D_tG\) are the reversed coordinates of \(D_t^{-1}n/q\), with multipliers in \(\{-3,-1,1,3\}\). By the exterior product estimate,
\[
\|G\|_t\ll \lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3/q.
\]
If \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3<c_0q^{5/4}\) for a sufficiently small absolute \(c_0>0\), then \(|\operatorname{Disc}(G)|<q\), hence \(\operatorname{Disc}(G)=0\). As \(G\ne0\), over \(\mathbb Q\) it has the form \(G=c_2\ell^2 m\), with primitive integral linear forms \(\ell,m\) and nonzero integer \(c_2\), by Gauss's lemma. The repeated root is rational because it has multiplicity at least two in a cubic. We may use the same weighted norm on linear forms. Multiplication on the unit spheres of linear forms has a positive minimum norm for \(\ell^2 m\), by compactness and the lack of zero divisors. Since the weighted norm of a nonzero integral linear form is at least \(1/t\), it follows that \(\|\ell\|_t^2\ll t\|G\|_t/|c_2|\).

For a prime \(p\mid q\) with \(p\nmid c_2\), both linear factors survive modulo \(p\), and the reduction of \(\ell\) has the unique repeated root of \(G\), namely the prescribed one. Put \(\ell=A X+B Y\) and \(z=(-B,A)\). The gradient \(\nabla F(z)\) for every lattice form in the ball vanishes modulo \(q/\gcd(q,c_2)\). On the other hand, the coordinate vector \((t^{-1}z_1,t z_2)\) has Euclidean norm \(\|\ell\|_t\), and the chain rule gives
\[
|\nabla F(z)|\ll_C t R\|\ell\|_t^2
 \ll_C t^2R\|G\|_t/|c_2|
 \ll_C N^{11/12}/|c_2|.
\]
For sufficiently large \(N\) this is strictly below \(q/|c_2|\le q/\gcd(q,c_2)\). The integral gradient must vanish. Since \(z\ne0\), every such \(F\) has a repeated rational root, contradicting existence of an irreducible form. This proves \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3\gg q^{5/4}\).

If \(j=4\), the determinant of the four independent lattice vectors is a nonzero multiple of \(q^2\). Since the weighted norm is obtained by a determinant-one linear map, Hadamard's bound gives \(\prod_i\lambda_i\gg q^2\).

The four upper bounds on the lattice point count for these cases are \(O_C(N^{1/4})\), \(O_C(N^{1/3})\), \(O_C(N^{1/4})\), and \(O_C(1)\), respectively. The assignment count proves \(O_{C,\varepsilon}(N^{4/3+\varepsilon})\) for each such box.

## Large cusp parameter

Assume \(t\ge N^{1/12}\) and use only \(a\ne0\); nonempty boxes then satisfy \(t^3\ll_C R\). Put \(A=C_0Rt^{-3}\), \(B=C_0Rt^{-1}\), \(C'=C_0Rt\), \(D=C_0Rt^3\), so \(A\ge1\), \(B,C'\gg_C1\), \(D\ll_C N\), and \(q\asymp N\). Fix \(a,b,c\) in the box. The discriminant as a polynomial in \(d\) is
\[
-27a^2d^2+(18abc-4b^3)d+b^2c^2-4ac^3.
\]
Modulo \(p\mid q\), it has at most two roots if \(p\nmid a\), at most one when \(p\mid a,p\nmid b\), and at most \(p\) when \(p\mid a,b\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives at most \(2^{\omega(q)}\gcd(q,a,b)\) root classes, and \(D\ll q\) means each has \(O_C(1)\) representatives. By the divisor formula for the gcd,
\[
\sum_{0<|a|\le A,\ |b|\le B}\gcd(q,a,b)
 \ll\sum_{e\mid q} e(A/e)(B/e+1)
 \ll AB\,\tau(q).
\]
The factor \(A/e\) is valid because zero is excluded in the \(a\) coordinate, even when \(e>A\). Multiplying by the number of \(c\) choices gives
\[
\ll_{C,\varepsilon} N^\varepsilon A B C' \ll_{C,\varepsilon} N^{3/2+\varepsilon}t^{-3}\le N^{5/4+\varepsilon}.
\]
The dyadic box sum finishes the cubic-field and ring counts.

## A range of unequal discriminant and modulus scales

For discriminant bound \(Y\) and squarefree modulus \(x\)
with \(Y^{3/7}\le x\le Y^{3/5}\), the same proof gives
\[
               O_\varepsilon(Y^{1/2+\varepsilon}x^{1/3})
\]
irreducible cubic rings. To check the thresholds set
\(R=Y^{1/4}\), \(q=x/\gcd(x,6)\), and put the small cusp cutoff
at \(t\le c q^{3/8}R^{-1/2}\) with a fixed sufficiently small
\(c\). The rank-three gradient estimate is then strictly below
\(q/|c_2|\). After summing residue assignments, the four lattice
ranks contribute at most \(q^\varepsilon\) times
\[
                q^{3/4}R+q^{1/3}R^2+
                         q^{-1/4}R^3+R^4/q .
\]
In the large cusp the number of \(d\)-lifts per residue contributes
\(1+Rt^3/q\). The box count is consequently at most
\[
     q^\varepsilon(R^3t^{-3}+R^4/q)
                 \ll q^\varepsilon(R^{9/2}q^{-9/8}+R^4/q).
\]
Each term is bounded by \(q^\varepsilon R^2 q^{1/3}\)
on \(R^{12/7}\ll q\ll R^{12/5}\), with the fixed comparability
constants harmless. The remaining dyadic cost is \(Y^\varepsilon\).
For adjacent dyadic boxes \(x\asymp A,\ y\asymp B\) with
\(A\le B\le A^{4/3}\), the resulting signed row bound is
\(O_\varepsilon(A^{5/6}B^{1/2}(AB)^\varepsilon)\).

## Consequences and limits

For each fixed \(x\), the quadratic fields \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\pm xy})\) in the row are distinct. Their fundamental discriminants have absolute value \(O(N^2)\) and are divisible by \(x\). The usual cubic-field correspondence gives twice the number of cubic fields with that discriminant as \(h_3(\pm xy)-1\). The trivial terms cost \(O(N)\). This proves the claimed uniform row bound for either sign. The ordinary first-moment bound and the divisor bound give the sum of the other signed rows \(\ll_\varepsilon N^{2+\varepsilon}\), so the two-edge product moment is \(\ll_\varepsilon N^{10/3+\varepsilon}\). The stronger averaged bound below applies to the same field family.

## An averaged saving from short cubic certificates

Let \(1\le A\le B\) and let \(C_0>0\) be fixed. For squarefree \(q\asymp A\) prime to \(6\), let \(M_q\) count \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\)-orbits of primitive irreducible integral binary cubic forms with \(0<|\operatorname{Disc}(F)|\le C_0 AB\) and \(q\mid\operatorname{Disc}(F)\). The interval \(q\asymp A\) has fixed positive lower and upper comparability constants. Then
\[
                        \sum_q M_q^2\ll_{C_0,\varepsilon} (AB)^\varepsilon A B^2.       \tag{CM2}
\]
The result includes either sign and any further subset of this collection. Fundamental discriminants force the corresponding forms to be primitive: a form's content to the fourth power divides its discriminant, and no fundamental discriminant is divisible by a prime fourth power.

We derive (CM2) by counting short vectors in repeated-root lattices. Averaging over \(q\) is essential: the discriminants of the short vectors restrict the possible moduli. The contribution of each size range for the lattice minima is bounded in second moment before combining the ranges.

Use the weighted binary cubic boxes and repeated-root lattices
of the preceding proof with \(R=(AB)^{1/4}\) and \(q\asymp A\).
For each dyadic \(t\), the coefficient bounds are
\[
 (|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|)
   \ll_{C_0} (R/t^3,R/t,Rt,Rt^3).
\]
Here \(t^3\ll_{C_0}R\) in a nonempty irreducible box because
\(a\ne0\). If the lattice vectors in the corresponding
weighted ball span \(j\) dimensions, write
\(P=\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_j\) for the product of the
successive minima. Their point count is \(O_{C_0}(R^j/P)\).
There are \(O_\varepsilon(A(AB)^\varepsilon)\) assignments
of repeated roots for each \(q\).

For each dyadic \(t\), the aggregate number of occurrences of
primitive irreducible forms across \(q\) and all assignments is
\(O_\varepsilon((AB)^{1+\varepsilon})\). There are
\(O_{C_0}(R^4)\) coefficient tuples with \(a\ne0\): whenever a
box contains one, all four extents are bounded below by a
positive constant. The nonzero discriminant of a form allows
only divisor-many \(q\)'s. A primitive form is nonzero modulo
every prime, so its repeated root is unique at each \(p\mid q\).
The assignment is therefore unique. The same aggregate bound
holds for every subset of these occurrences.

### Counting short certificates

Fix \(t\) and a positive height bound \(J\) of polynomial size in \(AB\). A certificate is a nonzero integral cubic \(V\in\Lambda\) with \(\|V\|_t\ll J\). Across all \(q\asymp A\), the number of assignments admitting a nonsingular certificate is
\[
                       \ll_{\varepsilon}(AB)^\varepsilon(J^4+J^3t^3).             \tag{4}
\]
To prove this, write \(V=kV'\) with \(k\ge1\) its content and \(V'\) primitive. For each given \(V\), there are divisor-many \(q\), as \(q\mid\operatorname{Disc}(V)\ne0\). At primes dividing \(q/\gcd(q,k)\) the root is unique. At the remaining primes there are in total \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon\gcd(q,k))\le O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon k)\) choices. In the box for \(V'\), with scale \(J/k\), if its leading coefficient \(a\ne0\) then \(J/k\gg t^3\), and the number of tuples is \(O((J/k)^4)\). If \(a=0\), nonsingularity forces \(b\ne0\), so \(J/k\gg t\) and the number of tuples is \(O((J/k)^3t^3)\). Multiplying by \(k\) and summing proves (4). Also the total number of pairs of a modulus and a root assignment is always \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon A^2)\).

For fixed \(q\), the number of assignments admitting a singular certificate is
\[
                            \ll_\varepsilon (AB)^\varepsilon J t^3.                 \tag{5}
\]
Write \(V=k\ell^2 m\), with \(k\ne0\) an integer and \(\ell,m\) primitive integral linear forms. This factorization follows because a multiple root in a rational cubic is rational, and by Gauss's lemma. The weighted norm for linear forms is also defined by \((X,Y)\mapsto(tX,t^{-1}Y)\). Multiplication has \(\|\ell^2m\|_t\gg\|\ell\|_t^2\|m\|_t\), by compactness of the unit spheres and the absence of zero divisors. Since \(\|m\|_t\ge1/t\), on putting \(e=\gcd(q,k)\) this gives \(\|\ell\|_t^2\ll tJ/e\). Outside primes of \(e\), \(\ell\) determines the repeated root. At primes of \(e\) allow \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon e)\) choices. If the \(X\)-coefficient of \(\ell\) is nonzero, the count of linear forms of weighted size at most \(O((tJ/e)^{1/2})\) is \(O(tJ/e)\): the two coordinate extents have this product and each is bounded below when nonempty. If the \(X\)-coefficient is zero, primitivity requires \(\ell=\pm Y\), and these two choices cost at most \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon Jt^3)\) assignments for each \(e\). Here \(e\le |k|\ll Jt^3\), by the ordinary integer coefficient bound on \(V\). Summing the two branches over \(e\mid q\) proves (5).

### Small cusp and the dyadic minima argument

Assume \(t\le A^{1/14}\). Group every rank case into dyadic \(P\) where needed, and distinguish singular and nonsingular certificates. There are only \(O_{C_0}(\log(2AB))\) possible values of dyadic \(P\), since the minima are at most \(O_{C_0}(R)\) and at least \(t^{-3}\) up to a fixed constant. All bounds that follow allow \((AB)^\varepsilon\), with an arbitrarily small exponent.

Rank zero is irrelevant. In rank one, there are at most two primitive integral vectors in the rational line. Thus the count per \(q\) is \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon A)\), and the aggregate count is \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon AB)\). In rank four, \(P\gg q^2\), as the determinant of the independent lattice vectors is a nonzero multiple of the lattice index \(q^2\). The weighted transformation has determinant one. The bound the lattice point bound and the assignment count give a count per \(q\) of order at most \(A R^4/A^2=B\), with the same bound on aggregate count. For each of these ranks, the product of the supremum per \(q\) and the aggregate count is \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon AB^2)\), which bounds its second moment.

For rank two, the resultant of \(F_1,F_2\) is nonzero if the ball contains an irreducible form. A common nonconstant proper factor over \(\mathbb Q\) would otherwise divide every form in their span. Modulo every \(p\mid q\), the Sylvester map from pairs of binary quadratics to binary quintics via \((S,T)\mapsto S F_1+T F_2\) has image in \(\ell_p^2\mathbb F_p[X,Y]_3\), of dimension at most four. Therefore \(q^2\) divides the nonzero resultant. The resultant is invariant under determinant-one substitution and has bidegree \((3,3)\). Consequently \(P\gg q^{2/3}\). The certificate \(V=F_1\) has \(J=\sqrt P\) up to a fixed constant.

- If the certificate is nonsingular, (4) gives \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon P^2)\) assignments globally, because \(J\gg A^{1/3}\ge t^3\) up to constants. Each has \(O(R^2/P)\) forms, so the product of the supremum and aggregate bounds is
\[
                       \frac{A R^2}{P}\cdot\frac{P^2 R^2}{P}=A R^4=A^2B\le AB^2.
\]
- If the certificate is singular, (5) gives count per \(q\) of order at most \(t^3R^2/\sqrt P\). This is at most \(O(B)\), since \(t^3\le A^{3/14}\), \(P\gg A^{2/3}\), and \(A^{3/14+1/2-1/3} B^{1/2}\le B\). Use the aggregate \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon AB)\) bound, restricted to primitive irreducible counts.

For rank three, the integral dual cubic from the single-row
argument is the short certificate for that assignment. In detail,
let \(n=(n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)\) be the signed integer cofactor vector of the rows \(F_1,F_2,F_3\). It is nonzero and \(v=n/q\) is integral, since the row ranks modulo all \(p\mid q\) are at most two. Moreover \(v\bmod p\) annihilates \(E_p\). If their reductions span \(E_p\), this follows by exact cofactor orthogonality to the three rows, which persists after division by \(q\). If their reductions have rank at most one, each minor is divisible by \(p^2\), and thus \(v=0\bmod p\). Write
\[
            G_v(X,Y)=v((-Y X_0+X Y_0)^3)
                     =v_4X^3-3v_3X^2Y+3v_2XY^2-v_1Y^3,
\]
where the functional \(v\) acts on cubics in \(X_0,Y_0\). At a root of \(\ell_p\), the linear form \(-Y X_0+X Y_0\) is proportional to \(\ell_p(X_0,Y_0)\), so evaluation of the cube and its first derivatives shows \(G_v\) is divisible by \(\ell_p^2\) modulo \(p\). Thus \(V=G_v\) is a nonzero certificate in the very same lattice. Under the determinant-one matrix \(D_t=\operatorname{diag}(t^3,t,t^{-1},t^{-3})\), the cofactor vector becomes \(D_t^{-1}n\), whereas the coordinates of \(D_t G_v\) reverse those of \(D_t^{-1}n/q\) up to the fixed multipliers \(1,-3,3,-1\). Hadamard's bound therefore gives \(\|G_v\|_t\ll P/q\). Use \(J=P/A\) up to a fixed constant.

- If \(G_v\) is nonsingular, \(q\mid\operatorname{Disc}(G_v)\ne0\) implies \(J\gg A^{1/4}\), since the discriminant is invariant under determinant-one substitution and homogeneous of degree four. Hence \(t^3\ll J\), so (4) combines with the trivial global assignment bound to give \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon\min((P/A)^4,A^2))\) assignments. The count in each is \(O(R^3/P)\). The product of the supremum and aggregate bounds is at most a constant times
\[
                 R^6\min(P^2/A^3,\ A^3/P^2)\le R^6=(AB)^{3/2}\le AB^2 .
\]
- If \(G_v\) is singular, (5) gives count per \(q\) of order at most \(t^3 J R^3/P=t^3 R^3/A\ll R^4/A=B\) by the upper bound \(t^3\ll R\) from irreducibility. Again use the aggregate \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon AB)\) bound.

For each branch use the elementary inequality \(\sum_q U_q^2\le (\sup_q U_q)\sum_qU_q\) for nonnegative counts. This proves the target second moment for each dyadic component in the small cusp. The assignment used to count primitive irreducible forms is unique, and the choices of minima/certificates can be made independently for each assignment; no uniqueness of certificate is needed for (4) or (5).

### Large cusp and passage to orbits

Assume \(t\ge A^{1/14}\). For fixed \(a,b,c\), the discriminant as a polynomial in \(d\) is
\[
   -27a^2d^2+(18abc-4b^3)d+b^2c^2-4ac^3.
\]
For \(p\mid q\), it has at most two roots if \(p\nmid a\), at most one if \(p\mid a,p\nmid b\), and at most \(p\) otherwise. Thus CRT bounds the number of classes modulo \(q\) by \(2^{\omega(q)}\gcd(q,a,b)\). The range of \(d\) permits \(O_{C_0}(1+Rt^3/A)\) representatives per class. The divisor formula for the gcd gives
\[
 \sum_{0<|a|\ll R/t^3,\,|b|\ll R/t}\gcd(q,a,b)
 \ll\sum_{e\mid q}e\,\frac{R}{t^3e}\left(1+\frac{R}{te}\right)
 \ll_\varepsilon (AB)^\varepsilon \frac{R^2}{t^4}.
\]
The first extent estimate uses \(a\ne0\), and \(R/t\gg1\) in a nonempty irreducible box. Summing also over \(c\) yields a uniform form count per \(q\) of
\[
                 O_{C_0,\varepsilon}\big((AB)^\varepsilon(R^3t^{-3}+R^4/A)\big).
\]

For each \(t\), the number of orbits represented in (2) is at most \(O(t^{-2})\) times the number of forms in a fixed enlargement of (2). Indeed each orbit with representative \(F\) in (2) has \(\gg t^2\) distinct representatives \(n(m)F\) in the enlarged box for integral \(|m|\le t^2\), where \(n(m)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\m&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Conjugation by \(\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})\) sends \(n(m)\) to \(n(m/t^2)\), whose operator norm is bounded independently of \(t\). The representatives are distinct since a nontrivial unipotent cannot stabilize a set of three distinct projective roots in characteristic zero. Primitivity, irreducibility, and the discriminant are preserved, and different orbits contribute disjoint forms.

Use the weighted count obtained by multiplying enlarged-box form counts by \(O(t^{-2})\) as a majorant for orbits in this range. The uniform bound is \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon(R^3t^{-5}+B t^{-2}))\). By the earlier divisor argument the aggregate bound across \(q\) is \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon AB/t^2)\). Their product is \(O_\varepsilon((AB)^\varepsilon AB^2)\), since \(R^3/t^7\le A^{1/4}B^{3/4}\le B\). This proves the large-cusp second moment. In the small cusp the unweighted form counts already majorize the counted orbits. Summing the \(O_{C_0}((\log(2AB))^2)\) components by Cauchy–Schwarz and adjusting \(\varepsilon\) proves (CM2).

### Class-group and field-count consequences

Let \(x\asymp A\), \(y\asymp B\) be positive squarefree, \(\gcd(x,y)=1\), and use either fixed sign. The quadratic field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{\pm xy})\) has fundamental discriminant of absolute value \(O(AB)\) divisible by \(q=x/\gcd(x,6)\). Each \(q\) accounts for at most four \(x\). For a given \(x\), distinct \(y\) give distinct quadratic fields. By the class field theoretic correspondence between index-three subgroups and cubic fields of fundamental discriminant, their first-moment row \(S_B(x)=\sum_y^* h_3(\pm xy)\) is bounded by \(O(B+M_q)\). The \(O(B)\) term contributes \(O(AB^2)\) to the second moment over \(x\). Thus (CM2) gives
\[
                         \sum_x S_B(x)^2\ll_\varepsilon (AB)^\varepsilon AB^2.
\]
The analogous estimate for \(z\asymp C\) holds for any \(C\ge A\), for the sign on that edge. By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[
            \sum_{x,y,z}^{*}h_3(\epsilon_1 xy)h_3(\epsilon_2 xz)
                     \ll_\varepsilon (ABC)^\varepsilon ABC ,
                     \qquad A\le\min(B,C).
\]
The star may impose all three pairwise coprimality conditions. In particular the balanced MP7 degree-36 family has
\(O_\varepsilon(N^{3+\varepsilon})\) fields by its pair-of-cubic-fields
description. For the restricted family in which the shared kernel
is smallest by size, the same estimate on each dyadic box, and the
discriminant identity in MP7, give
\(O_\varepsilon(Y^{1/18+\varepsilon})\) up to discriminant \(Y\).
The different field-count question with common kernel larger
than a neighbor is not needed for the minimum moment below. This also proves the price-free three-edge minimum moment for every fixed \(0\le k\le2\) in all dyadic kernel shapes, by taking the smallest kernel to be \(x\) and using \(\min(h_{xy},h_{xz},h_{yz})^k\le h_{xy}h_{xz}\).

The argument uses neither a pointwise quadratic torsion estimate nor an averaged cubic-field estimate with ramification conditions. It uses cubic reduction, the classical correspondence, and the elementary lattice and divisibility arguments written above. A counting advance for the unrestricted abc problem still requires extraction or a compatible valuation-price analysis at the resulting separation threshold.

Taking \(A=B=C=N\) in this argument also gives the earlier
\(N^{29/9+\varepsilon}\) estimate and the corresponding
\(3+(4/9)(k-3/2)\) upper exponent for the minimum moment on
\(3/2\le k\le2\).

## Reflection and the unrestricted degree-36 count

Write \(h(d)=|\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt d))[3]|\) for a
signed squarefree \(d\), and put \(h(1)=1\). For \(\theta\ge0\)
consider the signed second-moment estimate
\[
 \sum_{\substack{d\ {\rm squarefree}\\0<\pm d\le Z}}
       h(d)^2\ll_\varepsilon Z^{1+\theta+\varepsilon}.
 \tag{CM3}
\]
The unrestricted degree-36 field count of MP7, with either
edge sign allowed and neighboring kernels allowed to be smaller
than the common kernel, has bound \(O_\varepsilon
(Y^{(1+\theta)/18+\varepsilon})\) if and only if (CM3) holds.
The mixed-sign subfamily with the two neighboring kernels
equal to \(1,3\) in either order already suffices for the
reverse implication.

For the forward transfer from the moment to fields, put
\(x\asymp A,\ y\asymp B,\ z\asymp C\) and \(P=ABC\).
The kernels are positive, squarefree and pairwise coprime.
Each signed product of two kernels of size \(O(AB)\) has
divisor-many ordered representations. Thus the sum of
\(h(\epsilon_1 xy)^2\) over the pairs \((x,y)\) is
\(\ll_\varepsilon (AB)^{1+\theta+\varepsilon}\), and the analogous
sum for \((x,z)\) is \(\ll_\varepsilon(AC)^{1+\theta+\varepsilon}\).
Apply Cauchy–Schwarz over triples, including the trivial third
coordinate multiplicities. The two-edge product sum is
\[
 \ll_\varepsilon P^{1+\varepsilon}
                  \bigl((AB)(AC)\bigr)^{\theta/2}
 \le P^{1+\theta+\varepsilon}.
\]
The cubic-field correspondence bounds the number of ordered
pairs of cubic fields by this product sum. In the regular
\(S_3\times S_3\) action of MP7, every odd prime of \(xyz\)
has inertia of order two and discriminant exponent \(18\).
This gives \(|\operatorname{Disc}|\asymp (xyz)^{18}\), since the
factor at 2 is bounded in fixed degree and \(xyz\) is squarefree.
Sum over dyadic boxes with \(P\ll Y^{1/18}\).

For the reverse transfer take real squarefree \(d>1\) and
its Scholz partner \(-\operatorname{sf}(3d)\), where
\(\operatorname{sf}\) denotes positive squarefree part.
Scholz reflection gives
\(h(d)\le h(-\operatorname{sf}(3d))\le3h(d)\).
The two quadratic radicands occur in
the required two-edge family with common \(x\) and signs \(+,-\):
\[
 (x,y,z)=
 \begin{cases}
 (d,1,3) & 3\nmid d,\\
 (d/3,3,1) & 3\mid d .
 \end{cases}
\]
These signs occur in the mixed orientations of MP4: the
positive edge joins a summand to the target, and the negative
edge joins the two summands. The third edge has signed
radicand \(+3\). The two quadratic characters in the chosen
pair are distinct and nontrivial except for the excluded
case \(d=1\), and hence independent. For each edge of
radicand \(D\) there are \((h(D)-1)/2\) choices of cubic
field of fundamental discriminant. The group and inertia
calculations of MP7 therefore apply. The resulting regular
degree-36 field has discriminant \(\ll d^{18}\).

For \(h(d)>3\), the product of the two choice counts is
\(\gg h(d)^2\). A fixed degree-36 field receives only boundedly
many ordered pairs, including pairs from different \(d\),
because it has boundedly many cubic subfields and the
positive radicand of the first one recovers \(d\). Terms
with \(h(d)\le3\) cost \(O(Z)\) in (CM3). Consequently a
field-count bound at \(Y\asymp Z^{18}\) proves the real
second moment. The map \(d\mapsto\operatorname{sf}(3d)\) on
positive squarefree integers is an involution, changes
the scale by at most three and gives the imaginary moment
by the same reflection inequality.
[Scholz, 1932](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1932.166.201) ·
[Fouvry–Klüners, Scholz's Theorem, equation (1)](https://msp.org/ant/2010/4-5/ant-v4-n5-p01-s.pdf).

The reflected subfamily alone has \(\gg Y^{1/18}\) fields.
Indeed, a real cubic field of positive fundamental
discriminant \(D\le Z\) supplies the first cubic field of a
reflected pair. Its real quadratic radicand \(d\) is at most
\(Z\), and reflection guarantees at least one choice for the
imaginary cubic field. Choose one such partner for each real
cubic field, and apply the same bounded multiplicity and
discriminant bound. Davenport–Heilbronn gives \(\gg Z\) real
cubic fields of positive fundamental discriminant at most
\(Z\). Take \(Z\) a small constant times \(Y^{1/18}\).
Thus the proposed exponent \(1/18\) cannot be lowered even
in the fixed-neighbor imbalance.
[Bhargava–Taniguchi–Thorne, Theorem 1.2 and equation (3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12819v2).

## The degree-nine affine route back to the abc moments

[AFM5](TWO_PRIME_MIRROR_INTERMEDIATE_FIELDS.md#the-full-prime-level-second-moment)
gives another counting formulation of (CM3), with every dyadic and
three-adic quadratic type included. At prime level three the group is
\(G=V\rtimes\{\pm1\}\), \(V=\mathbb F_3^2\), in its affine
degree-nine action; its order is eighteen. The counted fields have
normal closure \(W\), quadratic mirror \(E=W^V\), and
\(W/E\) everywhere unramified and split above three. No splitting
condition at two or three is imposed on the original quadratic field
\(K\). The splitting condition on the relative extension is retained.

Write \(D=\operatorname{disc}K\) and \(\Delta=\operatorname{disc}E\).
The excluded cyclotomic case is \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\).
For the other nontrivial quadratic fields,
\[
 \Delta=\begin{cases}-3D&3\nmid D,\\-D/3&3\mid D,\end{cases}
 \qquad |\operatorname{Disc}L|=|\Delta|^4.
\]
The first equality follows by taking the fundamental discriminant
of the squareclass \(-3D\); it also holds for even \(D\).
The second is AFM5's formula (5). Equivalently, the permutation
representation consists of the trivial character and four induced
pairs of nontrivial characters of \(V\). Each induced pair has
conductor \(|\Delta|\), since its character over \(E\) is unramified.
Thus this equality includes wild quadratic ramification at two.

The fixed-mirror multiplicity has a classical class-field description.
Set
\[
 Q_3(E)=\operatorname{Cl}(E)/
       \bigl(3\operatorname{Cl}(E)+
                    \langle[\mathfrak p]:\mathfrak p\mid3\rangle\bigr),
 \qquad q_E=|Q_3(E)|.
\]
Everywhere unramified \(C_3^2\)-extensions split above three
correspond to surjections \(Q_3(E)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb F_3^2\)
modulo \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)\). Quadratic conjugation
acts by inversion on ideal classes, because the product of an ideal
and its conjugate is principal. These extensions are therefore
Galois over \(\mathbb Q\) with the required scalar action. AFM1's
complement conjugacy gives one degree-nine field per extension.
Consequently the exact contribution of \(E\) is
\[
       \frac{(q_E-1)(q_E-3)}{48}.
\]
Indeed there are \((q_E-1)(q_E-3)\) ordered independent
characters, and \(|\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_3)|=48\).
The classes above three span at most one dimension modulo three:
in the split case the two classes are inverse, in the inert case
the prime is principal, and in the ramified case its class has
order dividing two. In particular
\(h_3(E)/3\le q_E\le h_3(E)\), where
\(h_3(E)=|\operatorname{Cl}(E)[3]|\). A fixed local splitting
condition leaves a second-moment problem, rather than removing
its multiplicity. This is a classical description of the
prime-three specialization, not a new asymptotic estimate.

There is also an exact simultaneous cubic interpretation. Let
\(c_E=(q_E-1)/2\) count cubic fields of fundamental discriminant
\(\Delta\) whose normal closures split above three over \(E\).
Distinct such fields have distinct cyclic cubic closures over \(E\);
any pair generates a \(C_3^2\)-extension. Each such extension has
four cubic subfields up to \(\mathbb Q\)-isomorphism, so its six
unordered pairs all give the same degree-nine field. The contribution
is therefore \(\binom{c_E}{2}/6\). Counting individual cubic fields
controls \(\sum c_E\); the desired bound requires control of
\(\sum\binom{c_E}{2}\) with the common fundamental discriminant
and the relative local condition retained.

### Comparison with inductive field-counting theorems

[Alberts, arXiv:1804.11318v3, Theorem 2.6 and Definition 2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.11318v3)
allow an abelian normal subgroup and infinitely many local restrictions.
Their bound retains a sum of class-group homomorphism counts over
quotient fields. Here \(N=V\) and \(C_G(V)=V\), so that term is
\(|\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(E),C_3^2)|=h_3(E)^2\).
The theorem can encode the ramification restriction but supplies
no independent estimate for that sum.

[Alberts–Lemke Oliver–Wang–Wood, arXiv:2501.18574v1, Theorem 1.11, Lemmas 4.1 and 6.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18574v1)
retain an average of unramified cohomology sizes as input. Their
Lemma 6.3 keeps that multiplicity in the bound by local factors;
Lemma 4.1 bounds it using equivariant class-group homomorphisms.
Theorem 1.11(i) requires its input exponent \(\theta<1/a(T)\).
These are comparisons with the inspected statements, not applications
establishing a new bound for the restricted AFM5 family.

The group calculation explains the difficulty. A nonzero translation
has three orbits of length three, hence index six. An affine
involution \(x\mapsto v-x\) has one fixed point and four pairs,
hence index four. The involutions generate \(G\), since their
products give every translation. Thus \(a(G)=4\), \(a(V)=6\),
and \(G\) is not concentrated in a proper normal subgroup in the
sense of the latter paper. The minimum-index elements already
have the permitted inertia type. Its asymptotic theorem for groups
concentrated in an abelian subgroup does not directly give this
case. This diagnoses the inspected methods; it does not exclude
other methods or an adaptation exploiting additional structure.

For the abc branch, any bound \(N_3(Y)\ll_\varepsilon
Y^{\beta+\varepsilon}\), with \(1/4\le\beta\le17/56\), gives
(CM3) with \(1+\theta=4\beta\), by AFM5 and the finitely many
local cutoff factors. Hence it gives the unrestricted MP7
degree-36 count with exponent \(2\beta/9\), and the degree-54
count below with exponent \(\beta/6\). The target \(\beta=1/4\)
would give \(1/18\) and \(1/24\), respectively; the retained
\(\beta=17/56\) recovers \(17/252\) and \(17/336\).
These implications do not extract the valuation data needed to
prove abc.

The next counting task is to bound pairs of distinct cubic-field
orbits with the same fundamental discriminant \(|\Delta|\le X\),
retaining the relative splitting above three, by
\(O_\varepsilon(X^{1+\varepsilon})\). A simultaneous binary-cubic
orbit count or a new average bound on the unramified lifting
multiplicity would address this task. A count of possible
discriminants or a product of local densities alone does not.
The odd-prime-power Jacobian and isogeny results in
[OPR1–OPR6](ODD_PRIME_POWER_RECIPROCAL_NORM_CURVES.md) remain
separate geometric inputs; they do not supply this upper bound.

## A marginal second moment also bounds the degree-54 fields

Use the same kernel boxes, now on all three edges with the
prescribed signs. The triangle Cauchy–Schwarz inequality of
MP4 bounds the three-edge product sum by
\[
 \sum_{x,y,z}^{*}h(\epsilon_{12}xy)h(\epsilon_{13}xz)h(\epsilon_{23}yz)
 \ll_\varepsilon \bigl((AB)(AC)(BC)\bigr)^{(1+\theta+\varepsilon)/2}
 =P^{1+\theta+\varepsilon},
\]
assuming (CM3). By MP7 the resulting degree-54 fields have
\(|\operatorname{Disc}|\asymp(xyz)^{24}\). Thus their count
is \(O_\varepsilon(Y^{(1+\theta)/24+\varepsilon})\).
At \(\theta=0\) this gives the cubic minimum moment in all
shapes, including the lower torsion thresholds. The same
bound for that moment follows directly from the displayed
product estimate. The unconditional [prime-path second
moment](ABC_QUADRATIC_THREE_TORSION_FERMAT_TAIL.md#moment-consequences-and-remaining-terms)
allows \(\theta=3/14\), giving the field upper exponents
\(17/252\) in degree \(36\) and \(17/336\) in degree \(54\).

The reflected pair that forces the reverse implication in
degree 36 has \(h(3)=1\) on the third edge. It therefore
contributes no cubic triples and only a unit weight to the
cubic minimum moment. The implication from the marginal
second moment gives one proof route for the degree-54 gap.
The three-edge problem can also be studied without an
unrestricted two-edge result.

## Reducing the signed triangle to imaginary quadratic fields

For a sign pattern of negative product, there are zero or two
positive edges. In the mixed case, label the unique negative
edge \(xy\), so the positive edges are \(xz,yz\). Reflect
both positive radicands by Scholz reflection. To express the
result with three pairwise coprime squarefree vertex kernels,
leave the prime factors other than 3 as they are and change
the location of 3 by the involution
\[
       \text{absent}\ \longleftrightarrow\ z,\qquad
       x\ \longleftrightarrow\ y .
\]
The product \(xy\) is unchanged. Membership of 3 is toggled
on both other edges, exactly as required for their new
radicands \(-\operatorname{sf}(3xz)\) and
\(-\operatorname{sf}(3yz)\). The coordinates change by
factors in \(\{1/3,1,3\}\), so the target boxes form a bounded
union of comparable dyadic boxes. Each changed class-group
size is comparable to its old value within a factor of three.
This proves equivalence of the uniform \(k\)-th minimum-moment
bounds for any fixed \(k\ge0\) and of the full product-moment
bounds. The comparison holds for a common upper threshold
on the minimum up to a constant rescaling of that threshold.

The all-negative case also suffices for the degree-54 field
bound. Both reflected weights are at least their real weights,
and the weight on \(xy\) stays fixed. The factors \(h-1\) in
the cubic choice counts therefore do not decrease. Every
tuple with a nonzero transformed choice count has three
independent quadratic characters by MP7: they are distinct
because equality of two negative characters would leave the
third radicand \(-1\), and their product is the character of
\(\mathbb Q(i)\). The change of kernels is injective and
changes \(xyz\) by at most a factor of three. The discriminant
comparison and bounded multiplicities in MP7 then bound the
mixed-sign field count at \(Y\) by a constant times the
all-negative field count at \(O(Y)\). In a family that ranges
over every negative-product sign pattern, the all-negative
family is itself included.
