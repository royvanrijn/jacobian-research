# Simultaneous dihedral quintic extensions in a Jacobian Selmer group

Fix signed squarefree \(d\ne 0,1,5\), put \(k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt d)\),
\(K_0=\mathbf Q(z)\) with \(z=\zeta_5\), and \(B=K_0 k\). Let
\(\iota\) generate \(\operatorname{Gal}(B/K_0)\) and let \(\tau\)
fix \(k\) and send \(z\) to \(z^2\). Write \(\mathcal K\) for the
\(\mathbf F_5\)-subspace of \(B^\times/B^{\times5}\) where
\(\iota=-1\) and \(\tau=2\). For \(A=d w^2\) and
\(J_A=\operatorname{Jac}(y^2=x^5+A)\), we use the Kummer identification
of [Proposition QN1](QUINTIC_NORM_JACOBIAN_SELMER_MOMENTS.md#the-local-terms-and-the-central-class-rank)
for the \(1-z\) isogeny \(\phi\). In particular the local Kummer line
at a split bad prime away from \(2,5\) is generated by \(2w\sqrt d\).
The action here on the Kummer classes is the field automorphism action.

## The cyclotomic character of dihedral fields

Let \(N/\mathbf Q\) be the compositum of finitely many dihedral normal
closures with quadratic resolvent \(k\). The group of \(N/k\) is
elementary abelian of exponent five and \(\operatorname{Gal}(k/\mathbf Q)\)
acts by inversion. The Kummer space of \(NB/B\) is a subspace
\(U\subset\mathcal K\). Conversely, every finite-dimensional subspace
\(U\subset\mathcal K\) gives such a compositum. This is the
\(\tau-2,\iota+1\) description in
[Cohen–Rubinstein-Salzedo–Thorne, §2.3](https://thornef.github.io/ohno-nakagawa3.pdf)
at \(\ell=5\). In the Kummer pairing, conjugation on the Galois
group acts by the quotient of the character on \(\mu_5\) by the
character on \(U\). Thus \(\tau\) centralizes the elementary group
and \(\iota\) inverts it. The quotient by the elementary group has
order eight, prime to five, so a complement exists, and its
\(C_4\) subgroup is central and normal. The fixed field of that
subgroup is \(N\); the elementary quotient lines give its
dihedral subextensions over \(\mathbf Q\). This also proves that
the maximal abelian subfield of \(NB/\mathbf Q\) is \(B\) for
\(U\ne0\).

For an individual \(D_5\) field given by the
[dihedral Fourier norm triple](D5_FOURIER_NORM_INTEGRALITY.md#rational-reconstruction),
the concrete generator of the Kummer line is
\(V_1^5=M_1M_4^2/A_{\rm F}^2\), where \(A_{\rm F}\) denotes the
quadratic Fourier invariant in that note. The rotation sends
\(V_j\) to \(z^j V_j\), so \(V_2/V_1^2\in B\), and the lift of
\(\tau\) fixing \(N\) sends \(V_1^5\) to \(V_2^5\). A reflection
sends it to \(V_4^5\), which is inverse modulo fifth powers because
\(V_1V_4\in \mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\).

## An exact simultaneous embedding criterion

Let \(R\) be the product of rational primes \(p\nmid10\) at
which \(N/k\) ramifies. For \(p\mid R\), let \(L_p\) be the
localization of \(U\) in \(B_P^\times/B_P^{\times5}\) at a prime
\(P\mid p\) of \(B\). Its dimension does not depend on \(P\).
Write \(R_+\) for the product of the \(p\mid R\) that split in
\(k\).

**Theorem DS1.** There exists a positive odd integer \(w\equiv1\bmod25\)
such that
\[
                        U\subseteq\operatorname{Sel}_\phi(J_{d w^2}/K_0)
 \tag{1}
\]
if and only if the following conditions hold:

- At the places over \(2,5\), the elements of \(U\) satisfy the
  local Selmer conditions for \(J_d\).
- For every \(p\mid R\), one has \(\dim_{\mathbf F_5}L_p=1\).

At \(p\mid R\) the second condition means that at a prime of
\(NB/B\) the decomposition group equals the inertia group.
Under these conditions one may take \(w=R^4 q\) for a rational
prime \(q\), with the absolute bounds
\[
                    q\ll |d|^{2776}R^{4168},\qquad
                    w\ll |d|^{2776}R^{4172}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The construction does not require a bound on the number of
dihedral fields generating \(N\). The resulting space \(U\)
meets the image of \(J_{d w^2}(\mathbf Q)\) only in zero.

First consider \(p\mid R\). Tame inertia is cyclic and any
element outside \(\operatorname{Gal}(N/k)\) inverts that group
and has order two. Thus \(p\) is unramified in \(k\) and the
inertia of \(N/\mathbf Q\) has order five. A lift of Frobenius
acts on tame inertia by its \(p\)-power map and on the elementary
group by the sign of its action on \(k\). Consequently
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 p\equiv1\bmod5,\ p\text{ splits in }k & (p\mid R_+),\\
 p\equiv-1\bmod5,\ p\text{ is inert in }k & (p\mid R/R_+).
 \end{array}
 \tag{3}
\]
In both cases \(B/K_0\) splits at the places over \(p\).
The image \(L_p\) contains a ramified class, and local Kummer
duality identifies its dimension with the decomposition rank.
The inertia rank is one. The local condition at \(p\) is a
one-dimensional line for every \(w\), so \(\dim L_p=1\) is
necessary. Necessity at \(2,5\) follows because \(w\) is a fifth
power in both \(\mathbf Q_2\) and \(\mathbf Q_5\) under the
normalization. Rescaling \(x,y\) on the two curves by a local
fifth root of \(w\) identifies the Kummer images, as in QN3.

For sufficiency, prescribe \(w=R^4 q\). At \(p\mid R_+\), choose a
generator of \(L_p\) with valuation four modulo five. Write it
as the class of \(p^4 u_p\), for a local unit \(u_p\).
Here \(B_P=\mathbf Q_p\), and the unit part is specified by its
class in \(\mathbf F_p^\times/\mathbf F_p^{\times5}\). Choose
a residue for \(q\) modulo \(p\) in the class
\[
                \left[\frac{u_p}{2\sqrt d\,(R/p)^4}\right].
 \tag{4}
\]
Then the local Selmer line generated by \(2w\sqrt d\) is \(L_p\).
The equality holds at all primes above \(p\): automorphisms of
\(B/\mathbf Q\) scale the global space by its character, and
carry \(2w\sqrt d\) to itself or its negative. At \(p\mid R/R_+\),
the completion \(B_P/\mathbf Q_p\) is unramified quadratic.
Its Frobenius has \(\tau\)-part \(\tau^2\) and \(\iota\)-part
\(\iota\), hence acts trivially on \(L_p\). On the full local
Kummer space it fixes the valuation line and acts by \(-1\)
on the residue unit line. So \(L_p\) is the unique invariant
line, and \(2w\sqrt d\) spans that line, having valuation four
and invariant class. No residue choice for \(q\) is required at
these primes.

Impose also \(q\equiv R^{-4}\bmod25\) and complete splitting
in \(NB\). The specified residue modulo \(25\) is \(1\bmod5\).
These conditions and (4) are compatible. The intersection of
\(NB\) with \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_{25R_+})\) lies in \(B\),
the maximal abelian subfield of \(NB/\mathbf Q\). Since \(B\)
is unramified at every \(p\mid R_+\), this intersection lies
in \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_{25})\), and it is exactly \(K_0\) by the
degrees. Chebotarev in the compositum supplies \(q\) outside
the ramified primes. At \(q\) all classes in \(U\) localize to
zero. At \(2,5\) the prescribed multiplier preserves the local
image. At all other primes the only required classes are
unramified or the local cohomology is zero by QN1. This proves
(1), including the case \(R=1\).

For (2), use [Thorner–Zaman, Theorem 1.1](https://msp.org/ant/2017/11-5/ant-v11-n5-p04-s.pdf)
with the abelian subgroup fixing \(B\) in the same compositum.
The desired conjugacy class meets that subgroup by construction.
Its characters have conductor norm \(O(R^8)\): away from
\(2,5\) they ramify only above \(R\), tamely at depth at most
one. At \(2,5\) the local exponent-five characters from \(NB/B\)
have bounded depth, since deep units are fifth powers in a
field of degree at most eight. The supplement from the
cyclotomic condition has bounded depth there as well. The
discriminant of \(B\) is \(O(|d|^4)\) and \([B:\mathbf Q]=8\).
Substitution in the theorem gives \(q\ll |d|^{2776}R^{4168}\)
and hence the stated multiplier. Finally the Kummer evaluations
of rational divisors lie in the trivial \(\tau\)-space by
[Corollary QN5](QUINTIC_NORM_JACOBIAN_SELMER_MOMENTS.md#locally-admissible-classes-outside-the-rational-divisor-image),
while \(\tau=2\) on \(U\).

## Uniformity across local choices and the rank of the dihedral part

**Corollary DS2.** For fixed \(d\) and \(R\) there are at most
\(5^{\omega(R_+)}\) multipliers of the size in (2) covering
all spaces in Theorem DS1 with tame support \(R\). For squarefree
\(d\), the dimension of
\(\mathcal K\cap\operatorname{Sel}_{\phi}(J_{d w^2}/K_0)\)
is \(r_5(k)+O(1+\omega(w))\), with an absolute constant,
where \(r_5(k)=\dim\operatorname{Cl}(k)[5]\).

For the covering count, there are five nonvertical lines in
the two-dimensional Kummer space at \(p\mid R_+\) when the
valuation is the nonzero coordinate. At the other primes of
\(R\) the invariant line is unique. For each pattern take the
maximal subspace of \(\mathcal K\) with these local lines,
with the indicated conditions at \(2,5\), and with unramified
Kummer extension elsewhere. It is finite by class field
theory for \(k\), since the conductors are bounded in terms of
\(d,R\) and the local degree-five characters have bounded
depth at the fixed bad primes. For a pattern realized with
support exactly \(R\), the maximal space also has that support.
It thus has its own multiplier by Theorem DS1, which works
simultaneously for all its subspaces.

For the rank estimate, an unramified elementary class
extension of \(k\) has generalized dihedral group over
\(\mathbf Q\), since conjugation acts by inversion on the
class group. Its Kummer space gives \(r_5(k)\) classes before
imposing the Selmer conditions. In the local Kummer space the
unramified line has dimension at most one at each place of \(B\).
For each rational prime dividing \(10w\), its localization at
one prime determines the other localizations up to character
scalars. Imposing zero there therefore costs at most one
condition per rational prime. At all other places the classes
satisfy QN1. Conversely the \(\mathcal K\)-part of the Selmer
group gives elementary abelian extensions over \(k\). Outside
\(2,5\) they can ramify only at rational primes dividing \(w\).
The tame additive contribution above each such prime is at
most two to the ray class rank, by the residue unit exact
sequence, and the contribution at \(2,5\) is bounded
absolutely by the local degree and exponent. The abelian ray
class quotient adds at most these dimensions to \(r_5(k)\).
This proves the estimate uniformly.

The covering bound costs a subpower when \(d,R\) vary over
polynomial ranges, for each fixed support \(R\). It gives
no saving for the sum over the supports themselves. For example
when \(R\le X^D\), any fixed power moment of the dihedral
part, summed over squarefree \(d\) of size at most \(X\) and the
covering multipliers for that one \(R\), is bounded by the
corresponding moment of \(h_5(k)\) times \(O_{D,\epsilon}(X^\epsilon)\).
This follows from the dimension estimate and
\(5^{O(\omega(Rq))}\ll_{D,\epsilon}X^\epsilon\) for the
multipliers in (2). The rank estimate concerns this character
space and the comparison does not estimate the rank of the
rational Jacobian from below.

## Genuine compatible and incompatible families

**Corollary DS3.** In \(k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\) there are pairs
of dihedral quintic fields with disjoint prime supports of
relative ramification, each individually realizable in (1),
that cannot coexist in the Selmer group of any \(J_{-2w^2}\).
There are also arbitrarily large independent collections of
such fields with disjoint supports that do coexist. In the
latter collections their classes all lie in the order-four
cyclotomic character space and are outside the rational
divisor image.

The ring of integers of \(k\) is norm Euclidean, since rounding
the two coefficients has norm error at most \(3/4\). Its units
are \(\{\pm1\}\). For each rational prime \(p\equiv1\bmod5\) split
in \(k\), the quotient
\[
 (\mathcal O_k/(p))^\times\longrightarrow
       \mathbf F_p^\times/\mathbf F_p^{\times5},\qquad
                         (a,b)\longmapsto[a/b]
 \tag{5}
\]
is a ray class quotient, inverted by \(\operatorname{Gal}(k/\mathbf Q)\).
It gives a dihedral normal closure \(N_p\), ramified over \(k\)
at both primes over \(p\), with no other relative ramification.
At \(2\) the quadratic inertia in \(N_p/\mathbf Q\) is a reflection
and normal in the decomposition group. A reflection in \(D_5\)
has normalizer of order two, so the local degree over \(k\)
is one. At \(5\), which is inert in \(k\), the unramified
Frobenius in \(N_p/\mathbf Q\) has order two, so again
\(N_p/k\) splits locally. Thus the local conditions at \(2,5\)
are automatic, also in composita of these fields.

Fix \(p_0=11\). Chebotarev gives infinitely many \(p\) split
completely in \(B\) whose Frobenius in \(N_{11}/k\) is nontrivial:
the two Galois fields meet in \(k\). At such a prime \(p\), the
field \(N_p\) supplies inertia and \(N_{11}\) supplies an
independent unramified direction. The dimension of \(L_p\)
for the two fields is two. This violates a necessary local
condition regardless of \(w\), including without the
normalization of Theorem DS1. The single field \(N_p\), and
also \(N_{11}\), has one-dimensional ramified localization
and is individually realizable.

For compatible collections, choose successive \(p\) split in
\(B\), in all prior \(N_{p_i}\), and in the Kummer extensions
over \(B\) of all ratios \(\pi_i/\bar\pi_i\), where
\(\pi_i\in\mathcal O_k\) has norm \(p_i\). This complete
splitting can be imposed by Chebotarev on their normal
compositum. The old fields split at the new prime. Conversely
\(N_p\) splits over each old prime \(p_i\), since the residue
ratio of \(\pi_i\) at \(p\) is a fifth power by the Kummer
condition, and (5) computes the Artin class of the principal
prime ideal \((\pi_i)\). Thus every ramified localization of
the full collection has dimension one. Distinct relative
ramification supports give independence by looking at
inertia at their unique primes. Use Theorem DS1 to embed the
full space.

For exact small controls use the norm generators
\(3+\sqrt{-2}\), \(3+4\sqrt{-2}\), \(33+14\sqrt{-2}\) of norms
\(11,41,1481\), respectively. Use the roots \(3,11,315\) of
\(-2\) in the corresponding prime fields. The ratio in (5)
of the norm-\(41\) generator at \(11\) is \(2\), with
\(2^2\equiv4\bmod11\), yielding incompatibility. The ratios
of the norm-\(1481\) generator at \(11\) and the norm-\(11\)
generator at \(1481\) are \(10\) and \(711\), respectively.
Their powers \(10^2\bmod11\) and \(711^{296}\bmod1481\) are
both one, giving a compatible pair.

## Independence of the two directions of relative splitting

**Corollary DS4.** For the fields \(N_p\) in Corollary DS3,
put a directed edge \(p\to q\) when \(q\) has nontrivial
Frobenius in \(N_p/k\). Every finite directed graph without
loops occurs on such primes, even with all primes \(1\bmod25\).
More precisely, fix \(n\) distinct old primes of the family.
Among new primes \(q\) split completely in \(B(\zeta_{25})\),
any prescription for the \(2n\) incoming and outgoing edges
with \(s\) edges present has relative natural density
\(4^s/5^{2n}\). In particular both local conditions for
coexistence with each of the \(n\) old fields have joint
relative density \(5^{-2n}\).

To see this, for each old prime \(p_i\) use the two degree-five
extensions of \(B\)
\[
   H_i=N_{p_i}B,\qquad K_i=B\big((\pi_i/\bar\pi_i)^{1/5}\big).
 \tag{6}
\]
The absence of \(p_i\to q\) is trivial Frobenius in \(H_i/B\).
The absence of \(q\to p_i\) is trivial Frobenius in \(K_i/B\)
by (5). In the first collection, the Kummer classes with
\(\tau=2\) are independent by their distinct ramification
supports. In the second, the classes with \(\tau=1\) are
independent by their valuations at the corresponding
\(p_i\). The two collections together are independent since
they belong to different character spaces. On their
compositum over \(B\) the involution \(\iota\) inverts the
full Galois group, while on \(B(\zeta_{25})/B\) it acts
trivially. Their intersection over \(B\) is thus trivial.
The conditional Frobenius group for a prime split in
\(B(\zeta_{25})\) is \(\mathbf F_5^{2n}\). The stated pattern is
a conjugacy-invariant subset with \(4^s\) elements, giving
the density and the graph construction by Chebotarev. In
the terminology of
[Stevenhagen, §§2 and 5](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/pmb.a-56.pdf),
one direction is tested by the ray class field and the other
by a fixed Kummer field that tests the extension structure as
the ray prime varies. Here the different characters give
independence of these two tests.

For a one-direction control use \(p=11,q=251\), where
\(\pi_q=3+11\sqrt{-2}\). At \(11\) its residue ratio is \(1\).
At \(251\), using the square root \(91\) of \(-2\),
the ratio for \(3+\sqrt{-2}\) is \(210\) with
\(210^{50}\equiv149\bmod251\). Thus only the direction
\(251\to11\) is present. The density statement fixes the old
primes before averaging over the new prime. A simultaneous
asymptotic when all primes are in a common growing interval
would require additional estimates.
