# True support occurrence for minimal repair

Let \(a+b=c\) be primitive and positive, and write
\(R_a,R_b,R_c\) for its pairwise coprime squarefree radicals.
For fixed \(M\), let \(H_M(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}p^{\max(M,v_p(n))}\).
The statements below distinguish three-support occurrence from
local compatibility or occurrence of the repaired target alone.

## Exact reduction to occurring support unions

Fix the three colored supports \(S_a,S_b,S_c\). The rational pair
\((a/c,b/c)\) is a solution to \(x+y=1\) in the group generated by
\[
 (p,1)\ (p\in S_a),\qquad
 (1,p)\ (p\in S_b),\qquad
 (p,p)\ (p\in S_c).
\]
The group has rank at most \(|S_a|+|S_b|+|S_c|\), with just one
generator per prime. The primitive triple is recovered uniquely from
the rational pair. The Beukers–Schlickewei theorem bounds the number
of triples, independently of their heights, by
\(2^{8(\omega(R)+1)}\), where \(R=R_aR_bR_c\).
For a fixed uncolored \(R\), summing over the \(3^{\omega(R)}\)
colorings still gives \(\exp(O(\omega(R)))\).
[Beukers–Schlickewei, Theorem 1.1](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~beuke106/s-units.pdf)

In the balanced minimally repaired sector
\(c^{a_-\lambda}\le R_i\le c^{a_+\lambda}\) for every letter,
with \(a_->0\), \(H_M(c)\le Y\) implies
\[
               R=R_aR_bR_c\le Y^{3a_+/(Ma_-)}.
\]
Hence the per-support multiplicity is \(Y^{o(1)}\) uniformly,
at fixed \(M,\lambda,a_\pm\). The count exponent of minimally
repaired quotients is precisely the count exponent of distinct
uncolored squarefree support unions which have **at least one**
height-admissible true occurrence with the balanced colors.
Equivalently, for each union take the smallest admissible
\(H_M(c)\) over the finite set of its actual solutions, with
value \(+\infty\) for no such solution. Count the finite minima
up to \(Y\). Fixing the target radical alone does not give the
height-free subpower multiplicity, since the other supports
would then vary.

This reduction is invariant under permuting the three supports
and using the corresponding signed additive relation. For a
cutoff in repair height, let \(Q_{\rm any}(Y)\) count primitive
quotients with some letter's repair at most \(Y\), and let
\(Q_{\rm all}(Y)\) require this of every letter, always with
the three radical balances relative to the same primitive
maximum \(c\). Then
\[
 Q_{\rm all}(Y)\le Q_{\rm any}(Y)\le
 Q_{\rm all}\!\left(Y^{(a_++1/(M\lambda))/a_-}\right).
\]
Indeed \(R_i^M\le H_M(a_i)\le R_i^M c\), with \(a_i\) any
letter. Each fixed choice of repaired letter gives a count
between these two. Thus taking the best repaired orientation
changes the height exponent by only the displayed factor.
All three individual radical balances are used here. A bound
on the sum of two radical weights does not allow this
orientation comparison by itself.

## An average finite-field test equivalent to true occurrence

Here is another exact reformulation, retaining all three caps
and the original height condition. Fix parameters \(X,N\) with
\(N=X^\gamma\) for fixed \(\gamma>0\). A support record means
three pairwise coprime squarefree integers \(R_i\le N\).
For each record attach arbitrary subsets of the complete
positive integers at most \(X\) on those exact supports.
One may additionally impose height-bin, balance, or repair
cutoff constraints on each candidate triple, independent of
the test prime.

Call the record true if some candidate tuple satisfies
\(a+b=c\). For a prime \(q\), call it admitted if some
candidate tuple satisfies \(a+b\equiv c\bmod q\).
The candidate representatives can vary with \(q\).
For \(Q=N^\beta\) with fixed \(\beta>0\), sample \(k\)
primes uniformly and independently in \([Q,2Q]\), with
\(k\) fixed. If \(S_k\) is the number of records admitted by
all tests and \(S_{\rm true}\) is the true occurrence count,
then
\[
       0\le\mathbb E S_k-S_{\rm true}
                 \ll \frac{N^3 X^{o(1)}}{Q^k}.             \tag{SO1}
\]
The fixed-support Rankin estimate gives \(X^{o(1)}\)
candidate representatives for each letter uniformly.
For a false record, every candidate difference \(a+b-c\)
is nonzero of magnitude at most \(2X\). It has at most
\(\log(2X)/\log Q=O_{\gamma,\beta}(1)\) prime divisors
in \([Q,2Q]\). The union over all candidate tuples thus
has cardinality \(X^{o(1)}\). The probability of passing
one test is at most \(X^{o(1)}/Q\), using the prime number
theorem. There are at most \(N^3\) records, and true
records pass every test, proving (SO1).
The same argument applies if a record is counted with its
admissible labels, as long as the number of possible
labels per support tuple is \(X^{o(1)}\).

Taking \(k\beta>2\) makes the error a power below \(N\).
Thus a bound \(O(N^{1-\sigma})\) for the average simultaneous
modular support incidence, for any such \(k,\beta\), is
equivalent up to a possibly smaller positive power margin
to a saving for true support occurrence. In a balanced abc
height bin one takes \(N=X^{(1/3+O(\xi))\lambda}\) and leaves
strict margins for that tolerance. If \(k\beta>3\), there
even exists one common list of \(k\) prime tests rejecting
every false record, since the expected number remaining
tends to zero. These equivalences do not count the modular
incidences themselves.

## Two local feasibility obstructions

### Arbitrary depth without the height condition

Let \(R_a=2p_a,\ R_b=p_b,\ R_c=p_c\), where \(p_a\) is an
odd prime near \(N/2\), \(p_b\equiv7\bmod8\) is near \(N\),
and \(p_c\equiv3\bmod8\) is near \(N\). Take the ranges or
classes so the supports are disjoint.
For every prescribed collection of positive exponents
\(d_p\) on the primes in these supports, there are positive
pairwise coprime integers on these exact supports satisfying
\[
      a+b-c\equiv0\pmod{\prod_{p\mid R_aR_bR_c}p^{d_p}} .
\]
Indeed at \(p_b\) the order of \(2\) modulo \(p_b^{d_{p_b}}\)
is odd, and at \(p_c\) it has precisely one power of two.
This follows from the symbol \((2/p)\), from
\(p_b,p_c\equiv3\bmod4\), and from reduction modulo the
underlying odd prime, whose kernel has odd order.
Take the exponent on \(2\) to be a large odd common multiple
of the odd order at \(p_b\) and the odd part of the order
at \(p_c\). This makes \(2^{v_2(a)}\) equal to \(+1\) modulo
\(p_b^{d_{p_b}}\) and to \(-1\) modulo \(p_c^{d_{p_c}}\).
Choose each other prime exponent a sufficiently large common
multiple of the Euler orders of the prescribed prime powers
of the other letters. Thus at a prime of \(R_a\) the last
two terms are both one and the first zero, at \(R_b\) the
first and third are one and the second zero, and at \(R_c\)
the first is minus one, the second one, and the third zero.
The same argument allows products of primes in the indicated
classes on \(R_b,R_c\).

The prime number theorem in progressions allows
\(N^{1-o(1)}\) possible target radicals \(R_c\) with sizes
of all three supports comparable to \(N\). This local test
therefore does not save in the number of support records or
of target radicals. The witnesses use unrestricted exponents;
they do not satisfy the repair-height and original-height
requirements in the counting problem.

### Correct heights and repair, but only the first prime layer

For every large \(N\) there are \(\gg N^2\) pairs
\(u,v\in[N,2N]\) for which \(u,v,w=u+v\) are squarefree
and pairwise coprime. For completeness impose
\(u\equiv1,v\equiv2\bmod4\). At any odd prime the excluded
squarefree and common-divisor classes have total density
\(O(p^{-2})\), and there is an allowed class at each prime
(for example \(u=v=1\) modulo \(p^2\)). The finite-prime
CRT density is bounded below uniformly in the cutoff.
For primes above a fixed cutoff \(P\), the square-divisor
loss is \(O(N^2/P+N^{3/2})\): for any one of the three
linear forms \(L\) the condition \(L(u,v)\equiv0\bmod p^2\)
costs \(O(N^2/p^2+N)\) pairs with \(p\ll\sqrt N\).
The common-prime loss is \(O(N^2/P+N\log N+N)\) from
\(\sum_{P<p\le2N}(N/p+1)^2\).
Take \(P\) large, then \(N\) large.
As each \(w\) has \(O(N)\) preimages, there are \(\gg N\)
distinct such squarefree \(w\).

Fix any large odd integer \(e\), set \(\lambda=3/e\), and
take
\[
                 A=u^e,\qquad B=v^e,\qquad C=w^e.
\]
These have genuine pairwise coprimality, comparable
original heights, and all three radicals \(\asymp N\),
so the normalized radical masses converge to \(1/3\).
For \(M\ge e\) the actual one-target minimum is
\(H_M(C)=w^M\), of size \(\asymp N^M\).
Since \(e\) is odd and \(w=u+v\),
\[
        A+B-C\equiv0\pmod{u v w} ,
\]
as is seen separately modulo each coprime factor.
Nevertheless \(A+B<C\). In particular with all three
support caps, correct primitive height and correct
minimal-repair height, exact simultaneous congruences
modulo the first powers of **all** support primes allow
\(\gg Y^{1/M}\) distinct target radicals at cutoff \(Y\),
up to an \(M\)-dependent constant.
The radical caps may be imposed with arbitrarily small
fixed logarithmic tolerances.

The construction also saturates the relaxed weighted test.
Since there are \(\gg N^2\) pairs and at most \(O(N)\) of
them for any target root, \(\gg N\) target radicals have
\(\gg N\) false representatives at their corresponding
fixed target \(C=w^e\). The relaxed sum of weights is
therefore \(\gg Y^{2/M}\).

For any support prime \(p\nmid e\) that is odd, this example
has valuation exactly one in the additive error. At \(p\mid u\),
the first-order term in \((u+v)^e-v^e\) is
\(e v^{e-1}u\), with exact valuation one, while the other terms
and \(u^e\) have valuation at least two. At \(p\mid v\)
interchange the roles; at \(p\mid w\) use \(v=w-u\) and oddness
in the same expansion. Thus the example pinpoints why
height-admissible congruences at all support primes need the
original high-power depths, or a genuine aggregate substitute
like (SO1), before they can imply occurrence.

### Correct heights and repair through the second support layer

Let \(u\in[3H,4H]\) be odd, \(v\in[H,2H]\) satisfy \(v\equiv2\bmod4\), and \(\gcd(u,v)=1\). Put
\[
       \ell=u^2-v^2,\qquad m=2uv,\qquad n=u^2+v^2 .
\]
These are comparable to \(H^2\), pairwise coprime original Pythagorean parameters, and \(\ell^2+m^2=n^2\).

#### Squarefree root parameters

A fixed positive proportion of these pairs can be required to have \(u,v,u-v,u+v,n\) free of odd square factors. At each odd prime \(p\), the CRT conditions remove \(O(p^{-2})\) of residue pairs modulo \(p^2\): this is immediate for the four linear forms and common divisibility, and for \(u^2+v^2\) after assuming not both coordinates vanish modulo \(p\), reduction to at most two classes of \(u/v\bmod p^2\) gives the same bound. There is an allowed class at every odd prime, for example \(u\equiv1,v\equiv p\bmod p^2\). The product of local densities at a finite cutoff \(P\) is uniformly bounded below.

For primes \(p>P\), each linear-form square condition costs at most \(O(H^2/p^2+H)\) pairs with \(p\ll\sqrt H\), hence total \(O(H^2/P+H^{3/2})\). Common prime divisibility costs \(O(H^2/P+H\log H+H)\). For the quadratic form, after excluding common prime division, \(p^2\mid n\) makes both coordinates units. For each fixed \(v\), at most two lifted classes of \(u\bmod p^2\) occur. Thus the cost at such \(p\) is \(O(H^2/p^2+H)\); summing over primes \(P<p\ll H\) gives \(O(H^2/P+H^2/\log H)\) by the elementary upper bound for the number of primes. Take \(P\) large and then \(H\) large. This yields \(\gg H^2\) pairs as claimed.

The odd coprime factors \(u-v,u+v\) give \(\operatorname{rad}(\ell)=\ell\). For \(m\), the 2-adic valuation is two and every odd positive valuation is one, so \(\operatorname{rad}(m)=m/2\). Also \(n\) is odd and squarefree. Each \(n\) has at most \(H^{o(1)}\) preimages by the fixed positive definite quadratic norm bound (or \(r_2(n)\le4\tau(n)\)). Thus there are \(H^{2-o(1)}\) distinct target radicals \(n\).

#### Simultaneous second-layer divisibility

Fix any odd integer \(e\ge3\), and put
\[
              A=\ell^{2e},\quad B=m^{2e},\quad C=n^{2e}.
\]
These are positive pairwise coprime integers of comparable height with
\[
                 \operatorname{rad}(A),\operatorname{rad}(B),
                 \operatorname{rad}(C)\asymp C^{\lambda/3},
                 \qquad \lambda=\frac{3}{2e}.
\]
Indeed \(\operatorname{rad}(ABC)=\ell mn/2<n^3=C^\lambda\),
so the exact combined budget holds.
For fixed \(M\ge2e\), the true minimum repaired target is \(H_M(C)=n^M\). Yet \(A+B<C\), by strict convexity of the \(e\)-th power on positive arguments.

Write \(U=\ell^2,V=m^2,W=n^2\), so \(U+V=W\). Reducing \(U^e+V^e-W^e\) modulo \(U,V,W\) separately shows it is divisible by their product, since they are pairwise coprime and \(e\) is odd. In particular it is divisible by
\[
       (\operatorname{rad}(A)\operatorname{rad}(B)\operatorname{rad}(C))^2.
\]
For any odd prime of one of the three supports not dividing \(e\), its valuation in the additive error is exactly two. For instance at \(p\mid\ell\), expand \((U+V)^e-V^e\): the first-order term \(e V^{e-1}U\) has valuation two and all later terms, and \(U^e\), have larger valuation. The other two supports use the analogous expansion, with oddness giving the cancellation at \(W\).

Consequently, any fixed logarithmic tolerances in all three radical caps eventually admit this false family. At a repaired cutoff \(Y\asymp (H^2)^M\) the simultaneous tests at the first two prime layers allow \(Y^{1/M-o(1)}\) distinct target radicals, despite the absence of true occurrences among these prescribed representatives. This is an obstruction to this local test only. The full original high valuations, which are generally \(2e\) on the supports here, cannot be replaced by their truncation at two.

## Exact weighted form on the repaired target radical alone

For fixed squarefree \(R\), every possible repaired target of
radical \(R\) is of the form \(H=R^M W\), with \(W\) supported
on \(R\). Under \(H\le Y\) there are only \(Y^{o(1)}\) choices
of \(W\), uniformly in \(R\le Y^{1/M}\), by the fixed-support
Rankin estimate. For each \(H\), the corresponding primitive
quotients are in exact bijection with positive integers \(u<H\)
satisfying the following conditions. Put \(g=\gcd(u,H)\).
For every \(p\mid R\), require
\[
 \begin{cases}
   v_p(u)<M,& v_p(H)=M,\\
   v_p(u)=0,& v_p(H)>M .
 \end{cases}
\]
The quotient is \((u/g,(H-u)/g,H/g)\).
The displayed conditions ensure exactly that the quotient
target has radical \(R\) and that its least repaired target
is \(H\): at exponent \(M\), a positive quotient valuation
is permitted anywhere up to \(M\), while at an exponent
above \(M\) the original exponent must already equal it.
The radical of the first quotient summand is exactly the
product of primes of \(u\) outside \(R\), and the second
uses the primes of \(H-u\) outside \(R\). Apply the desired
three radical caps and the primitive height condition to
these quantities and to \(H/g\).

Equivalently write \(F(c)\) for the number of original
primitive representations \(a+b=c\) satisfying the sector,
and
\[
    m_Y(R)=
      \max_{\substack{\operatorname{rad}(c)=R\\H_M(c)\le Y}} F(c)
\]
with an empty maximum counted as zero. The possible original
targets \(c\le Y\) on such a fixed support also number
\(Y^{o(1)}\). Thus the minimally repaired quotient count has
the same exponent as the weighted sum
\[
                \sum_{R\le Y^{1/M}} m_Y(R).
\]
Dyadic or fixed exponent-bin summation shows that a power
saving with exponent \(s\) for this sum is equivalent,
up to arbitrary exponent losses, to the uniform bounds
\(\#\{R:m_Y(R)\ge Y^u\}\ll Y^{s-u}\) over the relevant
\(u\ge0\). In particular counting only the target radicals
which occur covers the \(u=0\) part. It does not bound
larger representation fibres without an additional result.
This formulation retains both free radical restrictions
as a genuine correlation on one repaired denominator.

## Marginal complexity forced by the fixed-shift gap

The uniform fixed-shift theorem yields one quantitative
restriction on the weights \(m_Y(R)\), though not a
subpower bound. Refine the balanced sector enough that the
two residual free radical caps for a fixed complete \(c\)
are in a common small neighborhood of \(1/3\), normalized
by \(L=\lambda+o(\lambda)\). The complete shift \(c\)
already has numerical weight one and radical at most
\(c^{a_+\lambda}\), so fulfills the shift alternative of
that theorem. Its high full divisor can be obtained directly
by omitting degree positions below a sufficiently small
fixed multiple of \(1/\lambda\). Those omitted positions
carry less than half its numerical weight. Sum the
uniform fixed-shift bound over the root-height bins,
with subpower cost. Thus for some \(s_0>0\), for all
sufficiently small fixed \(\lambda\) and small tolerance,
\[
                 F(c)\ll c^{(1/3-s_0)\lambda+\varepsilon}
\]
uniformly in the complete shift. The same argument for either
other fixed letter works on a family where all three numerical
heights are bounded below by a fixed positive constant,
using the signed orientation and the same balance.
[A uniform gap for comparable residual root caps](ABC_AFFORDABLE_FIBRE_AFFINE_RANK.md#a-uniform-gap-for-comparable-residual-root-caps)

Since \(c\le Y^{1/(M a_-\lambda)}\) in the repair sector,
this gives
\[
       m_Y(R)\ll Y^{(1/3-s_0)/(M a_-)+\varepsilon}.
\]
In particular a minimally repaired family at exponent \(1/M\)
must have its occurring target radicals grow at exponent at
least
\[
       \frac{1-(1/3-s_0)/a_-}{M}>0
\]
for \(a_-\) sufficiently close to \(1/3\). Equivalently
a sufficiently small marginal support exponent gives a
strict saving by this argument, even when those support
values have many complete realizations. This does not
show that the occurring target radical marginal has
exponent near \(1/M\), nor control the high fibres in the
weighted sum when the marginal is larger.
