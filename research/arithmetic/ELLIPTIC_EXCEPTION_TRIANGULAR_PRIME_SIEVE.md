# A triangular prime sieve in the actual rational closure

Arithmetic refinements accompany the hazard formula below.
[EKX1--EKX3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_KUMMER_BRANCH_EXCLUSION.md)
identify positive-density eligible primes with no supported finite
zero branch; only the pole contributes there, and every feasible
smaller-prime prefix survives. [EFO2--EFO3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_FINITE_OBSTRUCTION_CERTIFICATES.md)
show that a large terminal finite obstruction must instead be
supported entirely on fatal zero branches, with formal depth at
least two. [ECM1--ECM3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_COMMON_QUOTIENT_MOMENTS.md)
give exact common-quotient correlations and a sufficient conditional
second-moment route to divergent hazard. The required asymptotic
moment estimates remain open.

[ESR1--ESR2](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_SMALL_REDUCTION_IMAGES.md) bound
small rational reduction images by a height product, giving absolute
coefficient loss `O((log p)^A/sqrt(p))` on a set of prime density one
for every fixed `A>0`. This marginal estimate does not bound the
conditional returns or correlations needed for divergent hazard.

For sufficiently large good primes, the new formal \(p\)-coordinate
is independent of every earlier local test. The reduction modulo
\(p\) retains its correlations with those tests. This gives an exact
conditional hazard at every formal depth and an exponential bound
for paths with large accumulated hazard. Intrinsically almost every
everywhere locally soluble vector has finite total hazard. The
remaining arithmetic question is whether weighted divergence occurs
almost everywhere; no such distribution assertion is proved here.

## 1. The prime-primary filtration

Use the existing elliptic curve and function
\[
 E_0:\ y^2=x^3-10656x^2+27091730x,\qquad
 F(x)=128x-25865,\quad d=51730.
\]
Let \(\Gamma=E_0(\mathbb Q)\), of rank two and rational torsion
\(C_2\), with four-class map \(\alpha\) as before.
Choose any integral free basis only to identify its profinite
completion as
\[
 \widehat\Gamma\simeq C_2\times\prod_{\ell}
                               \mathbb Z_\ell^2.   \tag{1.1}
\]
No displayed rational points are claimed to be that basis.
Give this group normalized product Haar measure \(\mathbb P\).
The rational map extends to a surjective homomorphism
\(\widehat\Gamma\to H_\infty\) of
[EFA3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_FINITE_LOCAL_ASYMPTOTICS.md). Its pushforward measure is
the intrinsic normalized Haar measure \(\nu_\infty\).
Indeed every finite continuous quotient of the rational closure
pulls back to a finite quotient of \(\Gamma\), and the extended
compact image contains the dense rational image.

Let \(\mathcal F_{<p}\) be the sigma-algebra generated by \(C_2\)
and the factors \(\mathbb Z_\ell^2\) with \(\ell<p\).
Write \(\mathcal F_{\le p}\) for the same sigma-algebra with the
\(p\)-factor included. The class \(\alpha\) is measurable already
in the two-primary data.

For each good prime let \(N_p=\#E_0(\mathbb F_p)\).
The finitely many bad primes have finite quotients
\(E_0(\mathbb Q_q)/E_1(q)\). Choose a fixed integer \(p_*\ge11\)
larger than all bad primes, all prime divisors of the orders of
those finite quotients, and the remaining small exceptional primes.
Only existence of this finite cutoff is used.

**Lemma ETP1 (lower-prime reduction and fresh formal coordinate).**
For every good prime \(q\ge7\), every prime divisor of \(N_q\) is
strictly smaller than \(q\). Consequently, for every prime \(p>p_*\):

1. every local test at \(q<p\), and every fixed global class
   condition, is \(\mathcal F_{<p}\)-measurable;
2. reduction modulo \(p\) is \(\mathcal F_{<p}\)-measurable;
3. the formal coordinate at \(p\) is independent of
   \(\mathcal F_{<p}\), and is uniformly distributed in
   \(E_{e_p}(p)\), with \(e_p\) as in EHD3.

**Proof.** The rational two-torsion point gives \(2\mid N_q\).
Hasse's bound gives \(N_q<2q\) for \(q\ge7\). If a prime
\(\ell\ge q\) divided \(N_q\), its complementary factor would
be at least two because \(N_q\) is even and \(\ell\) is odd.
This would imply \(N_q\ge2\ell\ge2q\), a contradiction.
This also reproves \(q\nmid N_q\).

The good local group splits into its finite prime-to-\(q\) torsion
part and its formal pro-\(q\) part, by EHD3. The finite part of
the map from (1.1) uses only factors whose primes divide \(N_q\),
and the formal part uses only \(\mathbb Z_q^2\).
Hence the entire local image at \(q\) uses only prime factors
at most \(q\). For a bad prime, its finite quotient uses only
the finitely many prime divisors accounted for in \(p_*\), and
its sufficiently deep formal group uses its own prime.
The two-primary global class is also accounted for. This proves
assertion 1.

At the new prime \(p\), its reduction uses only prime divisors of
\(N_p\), all smaller than \(p\), proving assertion 2. Its formal
projection is a continuous homomorphism from \(\mathbb Z_p^2\)
onto \(E_{e_p}(p)\). Haar measure pushes forward to Haar measure.
The independent prime factors of (1.1) then prove assertion 3.
These are statements on the actual rational closure via its
surjective Haar pushforward, not an ambient product assumption.
\(\square\)

The exact good-reduction and formal decomposition is
[EHD3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_AMBIENT_HAAR_DIAGNOSTIC.md). Its Hasse
input is Milne, *Elliptic Curves* (2006), Chapter IV, Theorem 9.4,
printed pp.149--150, and its local filtration input is Chapter II,
Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 5.9, pp.62--63 and 67:
[primary book](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/ectext6.pdf),
[retained copy](sources/milne-elliptic-curves-2006.pdf).
All finite prime-to-\(p\) entanglements remain in the
\(\mathcal F_{<p}\)-measurable reduction.

## 2. The exact loss at a prime of formal depth one

For \(p>p_*\), \(p\equiv2\pmod3\), let \(\mathcal B_p\) be the
following subset of \(E_0(\mathbb F_p)\):
\[
 \mathcal B_p=\{O\}\cup
       \{\overline{R_+},\overline{R_-}\text{ when }(d/p)=1\},
                                                               \tag{2.1}
\]
where \(R_\pm\) are the two zeros of \(F\). As in EHD1 they
have distinct unit \(y\)-coordinates when present.
Let \(\mathscr R_p\) be the return event that the new reduction
lies in \(\mathcal B_p\). It is \(\mathcal F_{<p}\)-measurable.
Let \(\mathscr G_p\) be the event of local solubility at \(p\);
changes at finitely many inadmissible local points have measure zero.

Fix the certified quadratic class \(a_0=10q_0\), and put
\[
 C_{<p}=\{\alpha=a_0\}\cap\bigcap_{q<p}\mathscr G_q,
 \qquad
 \lambda_p=1-\frac{\mathbb P(C_{<p}\cap\mathscr G_p)}
                         {\mathbb P(C_{<p})}.       \tag{2.2}
\]
Every denominator in (2.2) is positive by the certified seed and
EFA2. The normalization here is the same whole-group normalization
used in EFA4; the quotient is a conditional probability.

**Theorem ETP2 (actual conditional branch formula).** If \(e_p=1\),
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb P(\mathscr G_p\mid\mathcal F_{<p})
 =1-\left(1-\frac1{p^2+p+1}\right)
                              \mathbf1_{\mathscr R_p}}
                    \quad\text{almost surely},     \tag{2.3}
\]
and therefore
\[
 \boxed{\lambda_p=
 \left(1-\frac1{p^2+p+1}\right)
                 \mathbb P(\mathscr R_p\mid C_{<p}).} \tag{2.4}
\]
For arbitrary \(e_p\), let
\(\delta_p=3\lceil e_p/3\rceil-e_p\). One still has the pole bound
\[
 \boxed{\lambda_p\ge
 \left(1-\frac{p^{2-\delta_p}}{p^2+p+1}\right)
       \mathbb P(\overline P_p=O\mid C_{<p}).}        \tag{2.5}
\]

**Proof.** Given the reduction, the formal coordinate is fresh and
uniform by ETP1. If \(e_p=1\), it fills the whole residue disc.
Outside the branch residues, \(F\) is a unit and the point is
locally soluble. In every branch residue, the valuation test is
divisibility by three of the formal depth from the center;
the pole has valuation \(-2n\), which gives the same condition.
The conditional soluble mass is exactly \(1/(p^2+p+1)\), as
calculated in EHD1. This proves (2.3). Integrating it over the
past-measurable event \(C_{<p}\) gives (2.4).

For general \(e_p\), the pole center is always in the formal
subgroup. Conditional on reduction \(O\), its soluble mass is
exactly the expression in EHD3, equation (4.4). Discarding any
additional obstruction from the other residue discs gives
(2.5). \(\square\)

The return probability in (2.4) need not be \( |\mathcal B_p|/N_p\),
or even positive. It involves the actual reduction image and all
earlier conditions. The class-specific automatic primes of
[EPE2](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_PRIME_ELIGIBILITY.md) have zero loss.
No independence is asserted for these return events.

## 3. Infinite returns are incompatible with all-primes solubility

Let
\[
 \mathcal Q=\{p>p_*:p\equiv2\pmod3,\ e_p=1\}.
\]
This is a deterministic set associated to the fixed curve and
rational group. No infinitude or density of \(\mathcal Q\) is
assumed.

**Theorem ETP3 (predictable-return exclusion).** In intrinsic Haar
measure on \(H_\infty\),
\[
 \boxed{\nu_\infty\left(
      \{\text{locally soluble at every prime}\}
        \cap\{\mathscr R_p\text{ occurs infinitely often for }
                                  p\in\mathcal Q\}\right)=0.}  \tag{3.1}
\]
More quantitatively, the probability of passing the tests at the
first \(k\) finite return primes in \(\mathcal Q\) is at most
\(133^{-k}\), when the event also requires those \(k\) returns
to exist.

**Proof.** Expose the factors of (1.1) in increasing prime order.
The occurrence of a return at \(p\) is known before the \(p\)-factor
is exposed. If it occurs, (2.3) says that passing its new test has
conditional probability
\[
 \frac1{p^2+p+1}\le\frac1{133}.
\]
Let \(\tau_k\) be the \(k\)-th return prime in \(\mathcal Q\),
with value infinity if it does not exist. For any fixed \(p\),
the event that \(\tau_k=p\), together with success at the preceding
return primes, belongs to \(\mathcal F_{<p}\).
Decompose according to this value of \(\tau_k\) and condition on
\(\mathcal F_{<p}\). The bound above gives
\[
 \mathbb P(\tau_k<\infty,\text{ success at the first }k\text{ returns})
 \le\frac1{133}
 \mathbb P(\tau_k<\infty,\text{ success at the first }k-1
                                      \text{ returns}).
\]
The last event is contained in the corresponding \((k-1)\)-return
success event. Induction gives \(133^{-k}\). All-primes soluble
vectors with infinitely many returns belong to every such event,
so their probability is zero. Push forward from (1.1) to the
actual rational closure to obtain (3.1). \(\square\)

In particular, positive intrinsic all-primes measure in the certified
class would require a positive-measure set of vectors with only
finitely many returns to \(\mathcal B_p\) for \(p\in\mathcal Q\).
A recurrence theorem saying that almost every vector in that class
has infinitely many such returns would therefore prove its
all-primes measure zero. No such recurrence theorem is established
here. The weaker assertion that \(\mathcal Q\) is infinite would
not suffice.

A further sufficient condition follows directly from (2.4) and
[EFA4](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_FINITE_LOCAL_ASYMPTOTICS.md#6-conditional-prime-losses-and-the-limiting-measure). If, on a positive-natural-density set of primes in
\(\mathcal Q\),
\[
 \mathbb P(\mathscr R_p\mid C_{<p})\ge c/p
                   \quad\text{for a fixed }c>0,
\]
then the actual losses have divergent sum, the fixed-class
all-primes measure is zero, and
\(A_{K_0}(B)=o(\log B)\). This is an explicitly unproved reduction
to return probabilities, not a marginal-product argument.

## 4. The exact hazard at every formal depth

The depth-one restriction can be removed by retaining the local
offset of each zero branch. For an active prime \(p>p_*\), let
\(\tau_p(r)\) be the canonical prime-to-\(p\) torsion lift of
\(r\in E_0(\mathbb F_p)\). When \((d/p)=1\), put
\[
 \beta_p=R_+-\tau_p(\overline{R_+})\in E_1(p),\qquad
 k_p=v_p(t(\beta_p)).
\]
Use \(k_p=\infty\) if \(\beta_p=O\). Equivalently
\[
 k_p=v_p(t([N_p]R_+)),                              \tag{4.1}
\]
because \(N_p\) is a \(p\)-adic unit and kills the torsion lift.
The negative branch has the same depth. This is fixed local
arithmetic data, not a random variable. If \((d/p)=-1\), \(k_p\)
is unused.

Retain
\[
 \delta_p=3\lceil e_p/3\rceil-e_p,\qquad
 b_p=1-\frac{p^{2-\delta_p}}{p^2+p+1}.
\]
Define a function on residue points by
\[
 w_p(r)=
 \begin{cases}
 b_p,&r=O,\\
 b_p,&r=\overline{R_\pm}\text{ and }k_p\ge e_p,\\
 \mathbf1_{3\nmid k_p},
       &r=\overline{R_\pm}\text{ and }k_p<e_p,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{4.2}
\]
The zero-branch rows are present only when \((d/p)=1\).

**Theorem ETP4 (all-depth conditional hazard).** For every active
prime \(p>p_*\), put \(h_p=w_p(\overline P_p)\). Then \(h_p\) is
\(\mathcal F_{<p}\)-measurable and
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb P(\mathscr G_p\mid\mathcal F_{<p})=1-h_p}
                      \quad\text{almost surely}.   \tag{4.3}
\]
In particular
\[
 \lambda_p=\mathbb E(h_p\mid C_{<p}),\qquad
 h_p\ge b_p\,\mathbf1_{\{\overline P_p=O\}}.          \tag{4.4}
\]
At an automatic prime \(p\equiv1\pmod3\), set \(h_p=0\).

**Proof.** Reduction is known from the lower-prime components,
and the independent formal coordinate \(U\) is uniform in
\(E_{e_p}(p)\), by ETP1. At the pole, its soluble fraction is
\(1-b_p\), as in EHD3. Outside the branch residues the function
\(F\) is a unit, so the hazard is zero.

In a zero branch residue the local point is
\(\tau_p(\overline{R_+})+U\), and its displacement from \(R_+\)
is \(U-\beta_p\). If \(k_p\ge e_p\), translation by \(\beta_p\)
preserves Haar measure on \(E_{e_p}(p)\). The soluble fraction is
again \(1-b_p\). If \(k_p<e_p\), every displacement \(U-\beta_p\)
has exact formal depth \(k_p\), by the nonarchimedean valuation
law. The simple zero of \(F\) makes its valuation exactly that
depth. ELV1 then gives the third row of (4.2). The negative branch
is identical, since its offset is \(-\beta_p\). This proves the
whole residue table and (4.3). Integrate over the past event
\(C_{<p}\) for (4.4). \(\square\)

The exact kernel (4.2) retains both the actual rational formal
depth and the branch offset. In particular a deeper rational image
can make an entire zero-residue component either soluble or
insoluble; replacing every branch by its full ambient disc would
lose this distinction.

## 5. A weighted pathwise sieve and finite count bounds

Fix the prefix
\[
 C_0=\{\alpha=a_0\}\cap\bigcap_{q\le p_*}\mathscr G_q,\qquad
                    \mu_0=\mathbb P(C_0)>0.
\]
For \(Y>p_*\), let \(C_Y\) impose the remaining local tests up to
\(Y\), and put
\[
 H_Y=\sum_{p_*<p\le Y}h_p,\qquad
 C_Y=C_0\cap\bigcap_{p_*<p\le Y}\mathscr G_p,\qquad
                     \mu_Y=\mathbb P(C_Y).          \tag{5.1}
\]
The sum includes all primes; automatic primes have zero hazard.
This lets us expose the primary factors in their complete increasing
order, so every new \(h_p\) is measurable immediately before its
test.

**Theorem ETP5 (weighted predictable hazards).** For every fixed
\(Y>p_*\) and \(u>0\),
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb P(C_Y\cap\{H_Y\ge u\})
                         \le\mu_0e^{-u},}           \tag{5.2}
\]
and hence
\[
 \boxed{\mu_Y\le
       \mathbb P(C_0\cap\{H_Y<u\})+\mu_0e^{-u}.}      \tag{5.3}
\]
In intrinsic Haar measure, for all four classes together,
\[
 \boxed{\nu_\infty\left(
       \{\text{locally soluble at every prime}\}
          \cap\left\{\sum_{p>p_*}h_p=\infty\right\}\right)=0.}   \tag{5.4}
\]
This uses every formal depth \(e_p\), without a depth-one
distribution assumption.

For the fixed certified class, (5.3) gives the arithmetic bound
\[
 \boxed{A_{K_0}(B)\le
 \left\{\mathbb P(C_0\cap\{H_Y<u\})+\mu_0e^{-u}\right\}
     C_E\log B+O_{E,Y}(\sqrt{\log B}).}              \tag{5.5}
\]
Here \(Y,u\) are fixed; no bound uniform in a growing prime set
is asserted.

**Proof.** Enumerate all primes between \(p_*\) and \(Y\).
At a partial stage write \(C_j,H_j\) for the current survival
event and accumulated hazard, and set
\[
 Z_j=\mathbf1_{C_j}\exp(H_j),\qquad Z_0=\mathbf1_{C_0}.
\]
Immediately before the next test, both the preceding \(Z\) and
the new \(h_p\) are known. Equation (4.3) gives
\[
 \mathbb E(Z_j\mid\mathcal F_{<p})
   =Z_{j-1}e^{h_p}(1-h_p)\le Z_{j-1},              \tag{5.6}
\]
because \(e^h(1-h)\le1\) for \(0\le h\le1\).
Taking expectations inductively yields
\(\mathbb E Z_j\le\mu_0\). On the event in (5.2),
\(Z_j\ge e^u\), proving (5.2), and partitioning \(C_Y\) at
that threshold proves (5.3). The same elementary induction works
for the process stopped when its accumulated hazard first reaches
\(u\); no independent-test or optional-stopping theorem is required.

For (5.4), the calculation is valid for any initial
\(\mathcal F_{\le p_*}\)-measurable prefix. Choose the prefix
of all small-prime soluble tuples without restricting the global
class. On the all-primes soluble set, the events \(H_Y\ge u\)
increase as \(Y\) grows. Their measures are at most the initial
prefix measure times \(e^{-u}\) by (5.2). First let \(Y\) grow,
and then let \(u\to\infty\). This proves (5.4), after Haar
pushforward to \(H_\infty\).

Finally the sharp finite-place theorem
[ESQ3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_SHARP_FINITE_LOCAL_ERROR.md) gives
\(N_{S_Y,a_0}(B)=\mu_YC_E\log B+O_{E,Y}(\sqrt{\log B})\).
Every all-places target passes those finite tests. Combining
this with (5.3) proves (5.5). \(\square\)

The coefficient in (5.3) is an actual rational-closure quantity.
The event \(H_Y<u\) depends on finitely many reduction coordinates
and the fixed local numbers \(e_p,k_p\). Given certified finite
local-image quotients and these data, its intersection with \(C_0\)
has an exact rational Haar measure by
[EQA2](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_QUANTITATIVE_FINITE_LOCAL_ASYMPTOTICS.md) and
[the partial-saturation procedure](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_FINITE_QUOTIENT_SATURATION.md).
For a rational threshold \(u\), this is a finite weighted-group
calculation. This observation inherits that procedure's supplied-input
scope; it does not construct all such certificates uniformly in \(Y\).
The exponential term is the explicit analytic bound in (5.2).

A useful consequence of (5.4) is the pole-only criterion
\[
 \nu_\infty\left(
   \{\text{locally soluble everywhere}\}\cap
   \left\{\sum_{\substack{p>p_*\\p\equiv2\ (3)}}
          b_p\,\mathbf1_{\{\overline P_p=O\}}=\infty\right\}\right)=0.
                                                               \tag{5.7}
\]
Indeed its sum is bounded above by \(\sum h_p\).
When \(3\nmid e_p\), the coefficient \(b_p\) is bounded below
by \(1/2\). When \(3\mid e_p\),
\[
 \frac1{p+1}<b_p=\frac{p+1}{p^2+p+1}<\frac1p.
\]
Thus almost every everywhere locally soluble vector has only
finitely many pole returns at primes with \(3\nmid e_p\), and
its pole returns at the remaining primes have a convergent
reciprocal-prime sum. These are necessary pathwise conditions,
not assertions that typical vectors violate them.

ETP3 is recovered when \(e_p=1\): every branch return then has
hazard \(1-1/(p^2+p+1)\), so infinitely many returns give divergent
accumulated hazard. More generally, proving weighted divergence
for almost every vector in the certified class would prove
\(\mu_\infty=0\), and hence \(A_{K_0}(B)=o(\log B)\) by EFA3.
Neither that divergence nor the weaker depth-one recurrence
condition is proved here.

## 6. A conditional comparison with elliptic Wieferich equidistribution

Choose a full free Mordell--Weil basis \(P_1,P_2\), only for this
comparison. At a good prime \(p\ge7\), define
\[
 \kappa_i(p)=t([N_p]P_i)/p\pmod p,\qquad
 w_i(p)=\widetilde\kappa_i(p)/p\pmod{\mathbb Z},
\]
where \(0\le\widetilde\kappa_i(p)<p\) is the integer representative.
These use the fixed short model and \(t=-X/y\).

**Proposition ETP6 (conditional depth-one density).**
Assume that the pairs \((w_1(p),w_2(p))\), indexed by good primes,
are equidistributed for normalized Haar measure on
\((\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^2\). Then the primes with \(e_p>1\)
have natural density zero, and the primes with \(e_p=1\) have
natural density one.

**Proof.** The rational torsion is killed by \(N_p\).
The definition of \(e_p\), applied to a full free basis, gives
\[
 e_p>1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                  \kappa_1(p)=\kappa_2(p)=0.
\]
Every such pair lies in each small box about the origin of the
two-dimensional torus. Equidistribution bounds its upper density
by the Haar measure of that box. Letting the box shrink proves
density zero. Finitely many bad and small primes do not affect
the conclusion. \(\square\)

The source for this conjectural context is N. M. Katz,
*Wieferich Past and Future*, section 4, printed pp.3--4, for the
Lie-algebra definition of the fractions, and Conjecture 7.4,
printed p.8, for simultaneous elliptic Wieferich equidistribution:
[author's primary PDF](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/wieferich38.pdf),
[retained copy](sources/katz-wieferich-past-and-future-author.pdf),
[context metadata](sources/katz-wieferich-past-and-future-author.json).
His coordinate convention differs by a fixed sign from \(t=-X/y\);
that sign preserves equidistribution. Conjecture 7.4 is proposed
for a non-CM curve of rank at least two. ETP6 explicitly assumes
the stated equidistribution and does not establish it.

This conditional density statement gives neither finitely many
exceptions nor a summable exceptional-prime estimate. It also
gives no recurrence of branch returns. It is context for the
depth-one route, not a premise of the unconditional all-depth
hazard kernel or the weighted pathwise theorem ETP4--ETP5.

## 7. Scope and the remaining arithmetic input

The theorem is an arithmetic consequence of the rational two-torsion,
Hasse's bound and the local valuation test. It goes beyond the
ambient calculation: the formal independence is proved inside the
actual rational image, while the correlated finite reductions are
retained as conditional events.

The [fixed branch point](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_BRANCH_TWIST.md) is
nontorsion and independent of the rational group over its quadratic
field. That can be input to future Kummer or division-field work
on the returns, but it supplies no recurrence or density assertion
by itself. The weighted return-divergence condition and its possible
division-field inputs remain unresolved; no separate depth-one
distribution hypothesis is required by ETP5.

The new ingredients are the written prime-factor argument,
prime-primary filtration, conditional integration, the elementary
return-time induction and the exponential expectation bound. They require no finite experiment or new
checker. No value of the intrinsic all-primes measure, Hasse
infinitude, growing-prime error estimate, formal verification or
external review is claimed.
