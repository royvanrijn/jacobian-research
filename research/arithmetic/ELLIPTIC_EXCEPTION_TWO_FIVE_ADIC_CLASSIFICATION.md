# Exact two-adic and five-adic tests on the elliptic exceptional slice

The remaining small-prime tests on the existing elliptic slice have
simple descriptions. At two, the global descent data determine the
entire rational image in every finite local quotient; no Mordell--Weil
basis is needed. At five, integral \(x\)-coordinates always give a
local point. Combining the exact two-adic image with the complete
three-adic classification gives a stronger upper bound for Hasse
failures on this slice.

## 1. Notation and proof inputs

Use the curve, factors and admissible open from
[ELV1](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_EXPLICIT_LOCAL_CRITERION.md):
\[
 E_0:\ y^2=x^3+ax^2+bx,\quad a=-10656,\quad b=27091730,
 \qquad X=x-3552,
\]
\[
 v=\frac{3(x+432)}{6400},\quad
 z=\frac{3(128x-25865)}{819200},\quad
 c=\frac{x-5200}{1920},\qquad
 h(4/5+w)=g(w)=w^3-zw+zc.                            \tag{1.1}
\]
The quadratic discriminant is \(D=-3x/1600\). A full field fibre
means an admissible point with irreducible cubic factor; its entire
fibre is the matching product \(K\times L\).

Write \(E_n(p)\) for the formal subgroup at \(p\), with parameter
\(t=-X/y\) and depth \(n=v_p(t)\). For a point of exact depth \(n\),
\[
 v_p(x)=v_p(X)=-2n,\qquad v_p(y)=-3n.                \tag{1.2}
\]
The identity belongs to every \(E_n(p)\). The subgroup and quotient
statements remain valid at bad reduction: Milne, *Elliptic Curves*
(2006), Chapter II, Theorem 4.1, printed pp.62--63, and Lemma 5.9,
printed p.67, give \(E_n/E_{n+1}\simeq\mathbb F_p\) and
\[
 t(P+Q)=t(P)+t(Q)+O(p^{5n})\quad(P,Q\in E_n(p)).
                                                               \tag{1.3}
\]
The primary source is the
[author-hosted book](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/ectext6.pdf),
with [retained copy](sources/milne-elliptic-curves-2006.pdf).
The short \(X,y\) model is integral at both primes.

The [elliptic arithmetic theorem](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_ARITHMETIC.md)
gives the squareclass images of the two-isogeny descent. Put
\(q_0=2709173\), and let
\[
 E_0':\ y'^2=x'^3+21312x'^2+5183416x'.
\]
With the usual descent maps \(\alpha,\alpha'\), one has
\[
 \operatorname{im}\alpha=\{1,2,5q_0,10q_0\},\qquad
 \operatorname{im}\alpha'=\{1,14,1889,26446\}.        \tag{1.4}
\]
The first image is generated by the classes of
\[
 T_0=(0,0),\qquad
 P=\left(\frac{373248}{49},\frac{59459616}{343}\right)
\]
on \(E_0\). The second is generated by the two-torsion point
\(T_0'\) and
\[
 Q'=\left(\frac{3584}{25},\frac{4301248}{125}\right).
\]
For the dual isogeny \(\psi:E_0'\to E_0\), set
\[
 S=-\psi(Q')=
 \left(\frac{92179201}{6400},
       \frac{553221611649}{512000}\right),\qquad R=P+T_0.
                                                               \tag{1.5}
\]
The sign agrees with the explicit positive \(y\)-coordinate in (1.5).

## 2. Exact rational two-adic image

**Theorem ETF1 (image without a basis assumption).** Let
\(\Gamma=E_0(\mathbb Q)\) and \(H=\langle P,T_0,S\rangle\).
Then \([\Gamma:H]\) is finite and odd. For every \(N\ge5\), the
image of \(\Gamma\) in its two-adic quotient is exactly
\[
 \boxed{\langle T_0\rangle\times E_4(2)/E_N(2).}       \tag{2.1}
\]
Equivalently its closure in \(E_0(\mathbb Q_2)\) is
\(\langle T_0\rangle\times E_4(2)\). In particular every admissible
rational point is either formal of depth \(n\ge4\), or is its
translate by \(T_0\), where \(v_2(x)=1+2n\ge9\).

**Proof of the odd index.** The imported descent identities are
\[
 \ker\alpha=\psi(E_0'(\mathbb Q)),\quad
 \ker\alpha'=\phi(E_0(\mathbb Q)),\quad
 \psi\phi=[2],\quad \psi(T_0')=O.                   \tag{2.2}
\]
They are stated with the explicit maps in Dujella, *Number Theory*
(2021), section 15.5, printed pp.507--508:
[author's book page](https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/~duje/numbertheorybook.html),
[retained section](sources/dujella-number-theory-2021-section15-5.pdf).
Those statements were inspected directly.

For any point of \(\Gamma\), subtract a combination of \(P,T_0\) to
kill its \(\alpha\)-class. Write the result as \(\psi(U')\).
Subtract a combination of \(Q',T_0'\) from \(U'\) to kill its
\(\alpha'\)-class, using (1.4). Equation (2.2) now expresses the
original point as an element of \(H+2\Gamma\). Thus
\(\Gamma/H=2(\Gamma/H)\). The Mordell--Weil group is finitely
generated, so this quotient is a finite group of odd order.
This proves only an odd-index statement, not that the displayed
points form a basis.

**Proof of the local image.** The four classes in the first image
(1.4) become only \(1,2\) in \(\mathbb Q_2^*/\mathbb Q_2^{*2}\),
since \(5q_0\equiv1\pmod8\).

A rational point with negative \(v_2(x)\) is in \(E_1(2)\).
If \(x\) is a unit, the equation gives \(y^2\equiv3x\pmod8\), so
\(x\equiv3\pmod8\), a forbidden local \(\alpha\)-class.
If \(v_2(x)=1\), write \(x=2u\); after division by four,
\(y^2/4\equiv3u\pmod8\), forcing \(u\equiv3\pmod8\), again
a forbidden class. For \(v_2(x)=k\ge2\), the term \(bx\) has
strictly smallest valuation, so \(k\) is odd and at least three.
Translation by \(T_0\) sends
\[
 (x,y)\longmapsto\left(\frac bx,-\frac{by}{x^2}\right).           \tag{2.3}
\]
Its new \(x\)-valuation is \(1-k\), negative and even. Therefore
every rational point lies in
\(G=\langle T_0\rangle+E_1(2)\), including \(O,T_0\).

Exact arithmetic gives
\[
 R=\left(\frac{663747385}{186624},
          -\frac{6525490184045}{80621568}\right),
 \qquad v_2(x(R))=v_2(x(S))=-8.                     \tag{2.4}
\]
Thus \(R,S\) have formal depth four and \(H\subset
\langle T_0\rangle+E_4(2)\). By (1.3), multiplication by two raises
every formal depth \(n\ge1\) by exactly one. A point of depth four
therefore generates the cyclic quotient \(E_4(2)/E_N(2)\) of order
\(2^{N-4}\).

The group \(G/E_N(2)\) is a finite two-group. Since \(H\) has odd
index in \(\Gamma\), their images in this quotient agree. The
points \(T_0,R\) already generate the right side of (2.1), proving
the finite-image assertion and then the closure assertion.
The final valuation description follows from (2.3). \(\square\)

## 3. The exact test at two and its local counting proportion

**Theorem ETF2 (two-adic solubility).** At an admissible rational point:

- every point in \(T_0+E_4(2)\) has a local source point;
- a point of formal depth \(n\ge4\) has a local source point if and
  only if \(3\mid n\).

For formal points the quadratic is the nonsplit field
\(\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt{-3})\), so the second assertion is an exact
criterion for the cubic root as well.

**Proof.** At formal depth \(n\ge4\), (1.1) gives
\[
 v_2(z)=-8-2n,\qquad v_2(c)=-7-2n.
\]
The coefficient valuations of \(g\), in ascending degree, are
\[
 (-15-4n,\ -8-2n,\ \infty,\ 0).
\]
Its Newton polygon is one edge, so every root has valuation
\(-5-4n/3\). If \(3\nmid n\), no root is in \(\mathbb Q_2\).
For a formal point, \(1+a/x+b/x^2\) is one modulo eight;
factoring \(y^2=x^3(1+a/x+b/x^2)\) therefore shows that \(x\)
is a local square and \(D=-3x/1600\) is nonsquare.
If \(n=3m\), substitute \(w=2^{-5-4m}U\) and divide by the
leading scaling factor. The resulting integral polynomial reduces
to \(U^3+1\) modulo two, with simple root one.

For a point in \(T_0+E_4(2)\), one has \(v_2(x)\ge9\), so
\(v_2(z)=-15,\ v_2(c)=-3\). The polynomial
\(2^{18}g(U/8)\) has integral coefficients and reduces to
a nonzero multiple of \(U+1\) modulo two. Its simple root lifts.
These arguments use the Newton and Hensel inputs retained in ELV1.
\(\square\)

Let \(M_2(B)\) count admissible full field-fibre targets on this slice
which are locally soluble at two, in the actual target height of
EEC1. With its matching-target coefficient \(C_E\), one has
\[
 \boxed{M_2(B)=\frac47 C_E\log B+o(\log B).}           \tag{3.1}
\]
Indeed (2.1) gives equal mass to the two components. The translated
component is entirely soluble. Within \(E_4(2)\), exact depth \(n\)
has mass \(2^{-(n-3)}\). The soluble formal mass is
\[
 a_2=\sum_{j\ge2}2^{-(3j-3)}=\frac17,
 \qquad \frac12(1+a_2)=\frac47.                     \tag{3.2}
\]
Finite cosets are equidistributed by the canonical-height lattice
argument of [EEC1](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_COUNT.md). Truncate the formal
annuli at depth \(N\), use that finite quotient, and then let
\(N\to\infty\); the tail has mass tending to zero. This proves (3.1).
No error uniform in \(N\) is asserted by this direct approximation.
The separate logarithm argument of
[ESQ3](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_SHARP_FINITE_LOCAL_ERROR.md) gives an
\(O_E(\sqrt{\log B})\) remainder with coefficient \(4/7\), and for
the other fixed finite-place counts here. Negation preserves the conditions,
so dividing points by the two generic preimages of a target
preserves these proportions.

## 4. The exact test at five

**Theorem ETF3 (five-adic solubility).** At every admissible rational
point the full fibre has a \(\mathbb Q_5\)-point if and only if
\[
 \boxed{x\in\mathbb Z_5
       \quad\text{or}\quad
       v_5(x)<0\ \text{and}\ 3\mid v_5(x).}           \tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof for integral \(x\).** If \(x\) is a unit, the elliptic equation
modulo five permits only \(x\equiv1,2\). At \(x\equiv2\), the unit
part of \(D=-3x/1600\) is a square, so the quadratic splits.
At \(x\equiv1\), direct substitution in (1.1) gives
\[
 125g(U/5)\equiv U^3+2U+2\pmod5,
\]
which has the simple root \(U=3\).

If \(v_5(x)=k\ge2\), the elliptic equation forces \(k\) odd and at
least three. Then \(v_5(z)=-1,\ v_5(c)=1\), and \(5g(w)\) reduces
to a nonzero linear polynomial, with simple root zero.

It remains to treat \(v_5(x)=1\). Write \(F=128x-25865\).
Here \(v_5(F)\) is one or two. To prove the upper bound, if it
were at least three, put \(x_0=25865/128\). Then
\(v_5(x-x_0)\ge3\), while
\[
 f_E(x_0)=x_0(639735/128)^2
\]
has valuation three and \(v_5(f_E'(x_0))\ge1\). Taylor expansion
would give \(v_5(f_E(x)-f_E(x_0))\ge4\), making \(y^2=f_E(x)\)
have odd valuation three, impossible.
Also \(v_5(c)=0\). If \(v_5(F)=1\), then \(v_5(z)=-1\);
the polynomial \(5g(w)\) has a nonzero linear reduction and lifts
a root. If \(v_5(F)=2\), then \(z\) is a unit. The integral
depressed cubic cannot have a triple residue root, since its
linear coefficient is a unit. A reducible or singular residue
cubic has a simple root; otherwise it is irreducible and its
square unit discriminant makes the matching quadratic split.
This is exactly the unit case proved in ELV1, and gives a local
point here as well.

**Proof for nonintegral \(x\).** Put \(k=v_5(x)<0\); it is even.
One has \(v_5(z)=k-2,\ v_5(c)=k-1\). The Newton polygon of \(g\)
has a single edge and all roots have valuation
\[
 (2k-3)/3.
\]
If \(3\nmid k\), the cubic has no local root. The elliptic equation,
after factoring \(x^3\), shows that \(x\) is a local square:
the remaining factor is one modulo five and therefore a square.
Consequently \(D\) has nonsquare class \(-3\), so the quadratic
has no local root either. If \(k=3l<0\), set \(w=5^{2l-1}U\)
and divide by its leading scaling factor. The resulting reduction
is \(U^3+\text{unit}\). Cubing is a bijection in \(\mathbb F_5^*\),
so it has a nonzero simple root and Hensel lifts it.
This proves (4.1). \(\square\)

## 5. Joint two-adic and three-adic counts

Use the complete pointwise tests and local proportions of
[the three-adic classification](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_COMPLETE_THREE_ADIC_CLASSIFICATION.md).
Label its reduced nonidentity points by
\[
 A=(X,y)=(0,0)=\overline{T_0},\qquad
 B=(1,0)=\overline S,\qquad A+B=(2,0).
\]
Thus \(\overline P=A\) and \(\overline R=O\). The closure of the
rational image at three is \(E[2](\mathbb Q_3)\times E_2(3)\).
The complete three-adic theorem determines this image using a finite
three-saturation certificate at the good primes 41 and 53; it does
not assume a Mordell--Weil basis. In the displayed basis its
conditional soluble fractions in the four residue discs are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
   &O&A&B&A+B\\ \hline
   &1/13&0&1/3&1 .
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{5.1}
\]
These fractions and the exact closure are imported from the complete
three-adic theorem, not inferred from a sampled point set.

**Lemma ETF4 (the joint finite image).** For \(N\ge5\), \(M\ge3\),
the two-primary joint image at two and three is
\[
 \left\{\big((c,u),\,cA+dB\big):
       c,d\in\mathbb F_2,\ u\in E_4(2)/E_N(2)\right\}.            \tag{5.2}
\]
The three-primary formal image \(E_2(3)/E_M(3)\) is independent of
(5.2) in this finite image.

**Proof.** The odd-index subgroup \(H\) computes every two-primary
image. Its generators \(T_0,R,S\) map respectively to
\[
 ((1,0),A),\quad ((0,\bar R),O),\quad ((0,\bar S),B).
\]
The point \(\bar R\) generates the cyclic formal quotient.
Using it to cancel the formal coordinate of \(\bar S\) leaves
\(((0,0),B)\), proving (5.2). In particular the two-adic component
\(c\) is exactly the \(A\)-coordinate of the three-adic reduction;
it is not independent of that reduction.

A subgroup of a finite abelian product of a two-group and a
three-group is the product of its primary projections: suitable
integer multiples project onto either primary part. This separates
the three-primary formal image from (5.2), without assuming any
strong-approximation property or a Mordell--Weil basis. \(\square\)

**Theorem ETF5 (joint local proportion and a finite upper bound).**
Let \(M_{2,3}(B)\) count admissible full field-fibre targets soluble
at both two and three. Then
\[
 M_{2,3}(B)=\kappa_{2,3}C_E\log B+o(\log B),\qquad
 \boxed{\kappa_{2,3}=289/1092.}                       \tag{5.3}
\]
For the Hasse-failure count \(A_E(B)\), this implies
\[
 \boxed{\limsup_{B\to\infty}
       \frac{A_E(B)}{C_E\log B}\le\frac{289}{1092}.}     \tag{5.4}
\]
A fixed finite quotient already gives
\[
 \boxed{A_E(B)\le\frac7{24}C_E\log B
                         +O_E(\sqrt{\log B}).}        \tag{5.5}
\]

**Proof.** In (5.2), all four pairs \(c,d\) have equal mass.
The two-adic translated component \(c=1\) is entirely soluble;
its three-adic disc \(A\) is forbidden, while \(A+B\) has the
fraction from (5.1). The formal component \(c=0\) has two-adic
fraction \(a_2=1/7\), independent of its \(B\)-coordinate and the
three-primary formal variable. Hence
\[
 \kappa_{2,3}
 =\frac14\left(d_{A+B}+\frac17(d_O+d_B)\right),
\]
which gives (5.3). Approximation by finite quotients, followed by
the tail limit, gives the asserted local-count asymptotic just as
in (3.1). Everywhere local solubility implies both tests, proving
(5.4).

For (5.5), truncate at \(E_6(2)\) and \(E_3(3)\). Within \(E_4(2)\)
there is no permitted depth below six, so retain the untested
\(E_6(2)\)-tail of fraction \(1/4\). The three-adic discs \(O,B\) each retain at most \(1/3\),
\(A\) none, and \(A+B\) all. The retained fraction is
\[
 \frac14\{1+\tfrac14(\tfrac13+\tfrac13)\}=\frac7{24}.
\]
These are fixed unions of finite-index Mordell--Weil cosets.
EEC1's lattice count supplies their \(O_E(\sqrt{\log B})\)
errors. The finite inadmissible and reducible exceptions and
the generic two-to-one target map preserve the stated bounds.
\(\square\)

## 6. Replay and the remaining all-primes problem

Run

```sh
python3 research/arithmetic/verify_elliptic_exception_two_five_adic_classification.py --write-receipt
```

The [checker](verify_elliptic_exception_two_five_adic_classification.py)
verifies the isogeny images, rational points, translation, depths,
finite descent-group generation, bounded Newton and Hensel controls,
the joint two-primary image and the exact rational counting factors.
The two-adic controls use depths 4 through 30 and odd units 1 through
15, together with their two-torsion translates. The five-adic controls
use unit coordinates 1 through 49; valuations 1, 3, 5, 7 with units
1 through 125 and the exact local elliptic-square test; and formal
depths 1 through 12 with units 1 through 10. The forbidden integral
two-adic classes are enumerated completely modulo 32. Joint cyclic
models cover \(5\le N\le12\), and the fixed upper count uses exactly
\(E_6(2),E_3(3)\). These are declared finite controls, not proofs by
extrapolation.
The [receipt](elliptic_exception_two_five_adic_classification_checks.json)
states the finite coverage and pins the proof inputs. The written
descent, local-field and lattice arguments establish the unrestricted
claims; the finite controls do not replace them.

The Hasse subset must also pass five and the infinitely many
valuation tests of ELV1. The local-only proportions above do not
prove that infinitely many targets pass all places. No uniform
tail error, formal verification, external review, or saturated
Mordell--Weil basis is claimed.
