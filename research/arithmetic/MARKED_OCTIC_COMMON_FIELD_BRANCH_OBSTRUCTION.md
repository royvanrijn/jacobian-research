# Branch fields obstruct rational pairings of marked octic covers

Use the marked octic \(F\) of
[MO1](QUADRATIC_MIRROR_CLASS_RANK_FOUR_FROM_MARKED_OCTIC.md).
For the two inequivalent scales \(49,147\) in
[BS3–BS4](MARKED_OCTIC_BETA_CUBIC_SCALE_CLASSIFICATION.md),
a rational family common to their quadratic fields would give
nonconstant maps \(u,v\in\mathbb Q(x)\) with
\(F(49u^3)F(147v^3)\) square. Each parameter map then has degree
at least \(12\), and if one has degree \(12\), the other has
degree \(14\) with a specified ramification pattern. A composition
of quadratic rational maps is also excluded at arbitrary degree.
The obstruction comes from the simultaneous cubic fields of
the branch points.

## The cubic fields of individual branches

**Proposition BF1.** Put
\[
  G(Z)=49Z^4+750Z^3-2403Z^2+27000Z+63504,\qquad F(t)=G(t^2).
\]
Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_4\) be the roots of \(G\) and let
\(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3,\sqrt{a_1},\ldots,\sqrt{a_4})\). For
\(c\in\mathbb Q^\times\) write \(B_c\) for the roots of
\(G(c^2 Z^6)\). Suppose
\[
     v_2(c)\equiv v_2(d)\equiv0\pmod3,\qquad
     c/d\notin\mathbb Q^{\times3}.
\]
The polynomials defining \(B_c,B_d\) are irreducible over
\(\mathbb Q\). For every \(\alpha\in B_c\), \(\beta\in B_d\)
the fields \(E(\alpha),E(\beta)\) are distinct cyclic cubic
fields over \(E\), and \([E(\alpha,\beta):E]=9\).
The full branch fields satisfy
\[
 [E(B_c):E]=[E(B_d):E]=27,\qquad
 [E(B_c,B_d):E]=81 .
\]

Over \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{11})\), the quartic \(G\)
has two reciprocal pairs \((a_1,a_2)\) and \((a_3,a_4)\),
each with product \(36\). Their sums are
\[
 s_\pm=\frac{-375\pm198\sqrt{11}}{49}.
\]
In fact \(G(Z)/Z^2=49s^2+750s-5931\) for \(s=Z+36/Z\).
The two discriminants \(s_\pm^2-144\) have product
\(-11390625/343\). The norm of each discriminant in \(K_0\)
is negative, as is their product in both real embeddings.
This proves independence of the two square classes over \(K_0\).
The splitting field of \(G\) consequently admits independent
swaps of the two reciprocal pairs over \(K_0\). That field and
\(E\) both have degree a power of two over \(\mathbb Q\).

The \(2\)-adic valuations of the coefficients of \(G\),
in ascending degree, are \(4,3,0,1,0\). The Newton polygon
gives root valuations \(2,2,0,0\) in the normalization
\(v_2(2)=1\). The product in each pair is \(36\), so that each
pair contributes exactly one root with valuation \(2\)
at any chosen place. By the independent swaps, all four
choices of one root from each pair occur among the places of
\(E\) over \(2\).

In \(E^\times/E^{\times3}\) let \(U_c\) be the space of
classes generated by \(a_i/c^2\). If a product of these
generators with exponents \(e_i\in\mathbb F_3\) has all
valuations over \(2\) equal to zero modulo three, the four
places just described force \(e_i+e_j=0\) whenever \(i,j\)
belong to opposite pairs. Here clearing the valuation
denominator is valid because the ramification index has
order a power of two. The exponents are thus \((e,e,-e,-e)\)
and the product is trivial by the reciprocal pair products.
It follows that \(U_c\) has dimension three and meets the
subspace with all valuations over \(2\) zero modulo three
only in zero. This also holds for \(U_d\).

Let \(r\) be the class of \((c/d)^2\) in \(E^\times/E^{\times3}\).
A noncube rational remains a noncube in the field \(E\) of
degree a power of two. Thus \(r\ne0\), and its valuations
above \(2\) are zero modulo three. Each generator for \(U_d\)
is the corresponding generator for \(U_c\) plus \(r\).
In particular \(U_c+U_d\) has dimension four. A generator for
\(U_c\) and a generator for \(U_d\) always give independent
lines. The cubic fields of the branch points are precisely
the Kummer fields for these lines, since \(\alpha^6=a_i/c^2\)
and \(\alpha^3\in E\). These facts give the field degrees.

For irreducibility, \(a_i\) determines its pair sum and
\([\mathbb Q(a_i):\mathbb Q]=4\). If \(\sqrt{a_i}\) belonged
to \(\mathbb Q(a_i)\), its trace to \(K_0\) would have square
\(s_\pm+12\) or \(s_\pm-12\), since its norm would be \(6\) or
\(-6\). The two norms of \(s_\pm+12\) and \(s_\pm-12\) are
\(-1125/7\) and \(10125/49\), respectively, both nonsquares
over \(\mathbb Q\). Thus \(\sqrt{a_i}\) has degree eight.
Since \(\alpha^3\) is a rational multiple of \(\sqrt{a_i}\)
and \(Z^3-\alpha^3\) is irreducible over \(E\), the degree
of \(\alpha\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(24\). This applies at
both scales.

## Simultaneous pullbacks and parameter degree

**Proposition BF2.** Under the hypotheses of BF1, suppose
nonconstant \(u,v\in\mathbb Q(x)\) satisfy
\[
                      F(cu^3)F(dv^3)\in\mathbb Q(x)^{\times2}.
                      \tag{1}
\]
Every branch point of either cover has an odd-index preimage.
The arithmetic monodromy order of each parameter map is
divisible by three. In particular neither map is a
composition of rational maps of degrees one and two over
\(\mathbb Q\).

For a map of degree \(e\), the odd pullback set consists of
the points over its \(24\) branch targets with odd
ramification index. The pole of \(F(cZ^3)\) has even order
\(24\) and contributes no points. Equation (1) says that the
two odd pullback sets are the same set \(S\). If one branch
target has only even-index preimages, all its conjugates do.
Each of these \(24\) fibers contributes at least \(e/2\)
to the ramification sum. This contradicts the total
\(2e-2\) in Riemann–Hurwitz.

Let \(H\) be the field generated over \(E\) by the coordinates
of \(S\). Surjectivity onto both branch sets shows that it
contains \(E(B_c,B_d)\). By BF1, \(H/E(B_c)\) and \(H/E(B_d)\)
have degrees divisible by three. The field of all preimages
of the \(c\)-branches over \(E(B_c)\) contains \(H\); its
degree has prime factors only from the arithmetic monodromy
order of \(u\). Indeed take the Galois closure over the
rational target field, extend the constants to \(E(B_c)\),
and take the compositum of the residue splitting fields
at the \(24\) branch values. At each value the residue
group is a quotient of a decomposition subgroup. All
specialized preimages are obtained this way, including
at ramified fibers, by specializing the homogeneous fiber
polynomial. The same argument applies to \(v\).
For a tower of quadratic maps the same exclusion follows
directly by solving the successive quadratic equations
above each branch point.

**Theorem BF3.** Under the hypotheses of BF1, any pair
satisfying (1) has \(\deg u,\deg v\ge12\). If one map has
degree \(12\), the other has degree \(14\). The degree-\(12\)
map is unramified above its \(24\) branch targets. Above
each of the \(24\) branch targets of the degree-\(14\) map
there are twelve simple preimages and one of index two.
The latter map has total ramification contribution two
elsewhere. In particular the two marked scales \(49,147\)
satisfy these exclusions.

Fix a branch point \(\alpha\in B_c\).
The odd preimages of \(\alpha\) are in \(S\), and \(v(x)^3\)
for a point \(x\) in this fiber lies in \(E\).
For each fixed value of \(v(x)^3\), its three cube
roots occur equally often among the odd preimages,
also with equal multiplicities for any fixed index of \(u\).
Indeed \(v(x)\) is a branch point in \(B_d\), and BF1 makes
its cubic field over \(E(\alpha)\) nontrivial. Galois over
\(E(\alpha)\) therefore permutes the three values transitively.
It follows that both the number of odd preimages and the
sum of their indices are divisible by three. The same holds
after switching \(u,v\). The odd-index sum has the parity
of the degree. Degree \(13\) is impossible for either
map, since its odd-index sum is at most \(9\) at every
branch target. The remaining even-index sum is at least
four, giving total ramification at least \(48\) rather
than at most \(24\).

A map of degree \(e\le12\) is unramified above all branch
targets. Any one ramified branch fiber would give \(24\)
by irreducibility, exceeding \(2e-2\). Hence \(3\mid e\)
and \(|S|=24e\). For the other map of degree \(f\),
\[
                            20f+4\ \le\ |S|\ \le\ 24f.
                            \tag{2}
\]
In fact at an index \(i\) in a branch fiber the loss in odd
preimages from the \(i\) sheets is \(i-1\) for \(i\) odd
and \(i\) for \(i\) even. Either loss is at most \(2(i-1)\).
Summing gives a loss of at most \(4f-4\).
For \(e=3,6,9\), (2) forces \(f\le12\), so both maps
are unramified on the branch fibers and \(f=e\).
For \(e=12\), it forces \(12\le f\le14\), with \(f\ne13\).

To exclude equal degrees \(3,6,9,12\), first reduce to
\(\mathbb Q(u,v)=\mathbb Q(x)\). By Lüroth's theorem
\(\mathbb Q(u,v)=\mathbb Q(y)\) for a rational function \(y\).
The square in (1) descends to \(\mathbb Q(y)\). If its
radicand had an odd divisor point on the \(y\)-line, the
point would map to a branch target of one of the two covers
and would have at least \(24\) conjugates. Since it becomes
a square on the \(x\)-line, each of these fibers of \(y\)
would be fully even, contradicting Riemann–Hurwitz as in BF2.
An even divisor on the rational \(y\)-line makes the
radicand a rational constant times a square. The constant is
a square because \(\mathbb Q\) is algebraically closed in
\(\mathbb Q(x)\). Thus reduction divides both degrees by the
same factor and preserves (1).

For a primitive pair, work over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
Off the diagonal \(x=x'\), the equation \(u(x)=u(x')\)
is cut out by a bihomogeneous equation of bidegree
\((e-1,e-1)\) after dividing out the diagonal.
Fix a primitive cube root \(\zeta\). The equation
\(v(x)=\zeta v(x')\) has bidegree \((f,f)\).
For every \(\alpha\in B_c\) and each packet with fixed
\(v(x)^3\), the populations of the three values of \(v(x)\)
among odd preimages are \((k,k,k)\). They contribute
\(3k^2\ge3k\) distinct ordered off-diagonal intersections.
There are consequently at least \(|S|\) intersections.
If \(|S|>2(e-1)f\), the bidegree intersection bound
forces a common component. It dominates the \(x\)-line
because \(u\) is nonconstant and its fibers are finite.

On the resulting component the pairs have identical
\(u\) and identical \(v^3\), with different \(v\) at the
generic point. Therefore
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}(u,v^3)\) is a proper subfield of
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}(x)\). By primitivity, adjoining
\(v\) gives \(\overline{\mathbb Q}(x)\), so the extension is
cyclic cubic and has a generator fixing \(u\) and sending
\(v\) to \(\zeta v\). If also \(|S|>2(f-1)e\), the reverse
argument gives a generator fixing \(v\) and sending \(u\)
to \(\zeta u\). The two automorphisms commute and generate
\((\mathbb Z/3)^2\) because their actions on \(u,v\) are
independent. This group cannot act faithfully on the
projective line in characteristic zero. After diagonalizing
one of its order-three elements, a commuting transformation
must fix its two fixed points individually, so the
centralizer is a group of scalings.

In each of the equal-degree cases \(3,6,9,12\), both strict
inequalities hold, since \(|S|=24e>2(e-1)e\). If the pair
were imprimitive, its descended degrees would be an equal
smaller pair and the preceding constraints would still apply.
This proves the exclusions. Finally, for \((e,f)=(12,14)\)
each branch fiber of the second map contains at most twelve
odd preimages, since their number is divisible by three
and even. Their total is \(288\), so every fiber contains
twelve. Their index sum must be divisible by three, hence
is also twelve, and the remaining two sheets form one
index-two point. The ramification count elsewhere is
\(26-24=2\).

## Scope and evidence

The comparison applies to the common-field route suggested
by BS4. It leaves open higher-degree rational pairings
whose arithmetic monodromy orders are divisible by three,
and solutions in rational parameter pairs on a thin set.
Even an infinite supply of common fields would still require
checking the independence of an additional class and the
local conditions on that class. MO3's slice, which ramifies
at \(5,11\), is disjoint from the simultaneously split BS4
slices.

Fried and MacRae's Theorem 2.3 characterizes divisibility
between two separated-variable polynomial differences by a
common outer polynomial. Fried's later paper treats rational-map fiber
products and their low-genus components, especially §1 and
the hypotheses of its main theorem in §4.4. Neither statement
is used as a proof of this simultaneous squareclass condition.
The argument here uses the distinct arithmetic cubic
fields of individual branch points to force intersections
on a fiber-product equation. [Fried–MacRae, *On Curves with
Separated Variables*](https://www.math.uci.edu/~mfried/paplist-cov/SepVarbsCurves69.pdf)
and [Fried, *Taming Genus 0 (or 1) Components on Variables
Separated Equations*](https://www.math.uci.edu/~mfried/paplist-ff/ReducibilityHypothesis-VarSep.pdf).
