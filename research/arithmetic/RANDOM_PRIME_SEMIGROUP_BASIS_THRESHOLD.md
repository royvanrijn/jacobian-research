# Additive basis thresholds for random prime semigroups

Admit \(2\) and select each odd prime \(q\) independently with
probability \(q^{-\gamma}\), where \(\gamma>0\). Write \(C\) for
the multiplicative semigroup generated by these primes, including 1,
and \(N_\omega(x)=|C\cap[1,x]|\). The sumset \(kC\) allows repeated
summands. This extends the [binary random semigroup theorem](RANDOM_SPLIT_PRIME_SUMSETS.md).

**Theorem.** For \(0<\gamma<1\) put \(\alpha=1-\gamma\).
Almost surely, \(F_\omega(s)=\sum_{a\in C}a^{-s}\) converges absolutely
for \(\Re s>\alpha\) and factors there as
\[
 F_\omega(s)=\zeta(s+\gamma)G_\omega(s),
\]
with \(G_\omega\) holomorphic and nonvanishing on \(\Re s>\alpha/2\).
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{N_\omega(x)}{x^\alpha}\ \longrightarrow\
 A_\omega:=\frac{G_\omega(\alpha)}{\alpha}\in(0,\infty)
\]
almost surely and in every finite \(L^m\), \(m\ge1\).

**Theorem.** Fix \(k\ge2\) and put \(\gamma_k=1-1/k\).
If \(0<\gamma<\gamma_k\), then for some \(c,\delta>0\) depending only
on \(k,\gamma\),
\[
              \Pr(n\notin kC)\ll_{k,\gamma}\exp(-c n^\delta).
\]
In particular \(kC\) is almost surely cofinite. If \(\gamma>\gamma_k\),
then \(kC\) almost surely has natural density zero. At the critical value
\(\gamma=\gamma_k\), almost surely
\[
 \overline d(\mathbb N\setminus kC)
 \ \ge\ \exp\left(-\frac{\Gamma(1+1/k)^k}{k!}\,A_\omega^k\right)>0.
 \tag{1}
\]
Consequently for each fixed \(0<\gamma<1\) the least asymptotic basis
order is almost surely \(\lfloor (1-\gamma)^{-1}\rfloor+1\).

## Almost-sure counting and moments

Let \(p_q=q^{-\gamma}\) for odd \(q\) and \(p_2=1\), and let \(X_q\)
indicate selection. Absolute convergence on \(\Re s>\alpha\) follows
from \(\mathbb E\sum_{q\ge3}X_q q^{-\sigma}<\infty\) for each
\(\sigma>\alpha\). Intersect the resulting probability-one events over
a countable sequence of \(\sigma\) decreasing to \(\alpha\).

For \(u\ge2\), the variance of
\(S(u)=\sum_{3\le q\le u}(X_q-p_q)\) is \(O_\gamma(u^\alpha)\).
Kolmogorov's maximal inequality for the prime-indexed independent sums
gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left(\max_{u\le 2^j}|S(u)|>2^{j(\alpha/2+\varepsilon)}\right)
 \ll_\gamma 2^{-2j\varepsilon}.
\]
Borel–Cantelli and a countable choice of positive \(\varepsilon\)
give \(S(u)=O_{\omega,\varepsilon}(u^{\alpha/2+\varepsilon})\)
for every \(\varepsilon>0\). Partial summation then gives locally
uniform convergence of \(\sum_{q\ge3}(X_q-p_q)q^{-s}\) on
\(\Re s>\alpha/2\). On that half-plane the sum of prime-power terms
with exponent at least two in
\[
 \log G_\omega(s)=
 \log\frac{1-2^{-(s+\gamma)}}{1-2^{-s}}
 +\sum_{q\ge3}(X_q-p_q)q^{-s}
 +\sum_{q\ge3}\sum_{j\ge2}
      \frac{X_q q^{-js}-q^{-j(s+\gamma)}}{j}
\]
converges absolutely and locally uniformly almost surely. In fact, on
a compact set with \(\Re s\ge\sigma>\alpha/2\), its random absolute
majorant has finite expectation by \(\gamma+2\sigma>1\) and
\(2(\sigma+\gamma)>1\). The dyadic logarithm uses the power-series
branches on \(\Re s>0\). Exponentiation gives the factorization and
nonvanishing. The logarithm is real at \(s=\alpha\), so
\(0<G_\omega(\alpha)<\infty\).

The function \(H(t)=N_\omega(e^{t/\alpha})\) for \(t\ge0\), extended
by zero for \(t<0\), is nondecreasing and right-continuous. Its
Laplace–Stieltjes transform at \(z\) is \(F_\omega(\alpha z)\),
initially on \(\Re z>1\). After subtraction of \(A_\omega/(z-1)\)
it is holomorphic across the boundary line \(\Re z=1\), because
\(\zeta(s+\gamma)\) has its sole pole there at \(s=\alpha\), with
residue one in the \(s\) variable. Wiener–Ikehara gives
\(H(t)e^{-t}\to A_\omega\).
[Korevaar, Theorem 5.1](sources/korevaar-wiener-ikehara-2006.pdf)

For integer \(m\ge1\), consider
\(\mathbb E\prod_{i=1}^m\mathbf 1_{\{a_i\in C\}}\) as a function of
\((a_1,\ldots,a_m)\), multiplicative over primes. Its local generating
function in formal variables \(t_i\) is
\(1+p_q(\prod_i(1-t_i)^{-1}-1)\), since all exponent vectors except
the zero vector require the same single selection at \(q\).
Factor out the \(m\) local series \((1-q^{-\gamma}t_i)^{-1}\).
The resulting factor is
\[
 H_q(\mathbf t)=
 \left(1+p_q\left[\prod_{i=1}^m(1-t_i)^{-1}-1\right]\right)
 \prod_{i=1}^m(1-q^{-\gamma}t_i).
\]
At an odd prime the total-degree-one coefficients cancel. In the
norm obtained by summing absolute coefficients weighted by
\(q^{-\alpha}\) per degree, the nonconstant part has norm
\(O_{m,\gamma}(q^{-\gamma-2\alpha})\). To see this, the degree-two
and higher part of \(\prod_i(1-t_i)^{-1}\) has weighted norm
\(O_{m,\alpha}(q^{-2\alpha})\), and each term from the finite
product with positive degree brings a factor \(q^{-\gamma}\).
The dyadic factor has finite norm. Since \(\gamma+2\alpha=1+\alpha\),
the multivariate multiplicative coefficient function \(h\) of
the factors \(H_q\) satisfies
\(\sum_{\mathbf d}|h(\mathbf d)|\prod_i d_i^{-\alpha}<\infty\).

Equivalently, in each coordinate the expectation is the convolution
of \(h\) with \(a_i\mapsto a_i^{-\gamma}\). Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E[N_\omega(x)^m]
 =\sum_{\mathbf d} h(\mathbf d)\prod_{i=1}^m
                          \sum_{b\le x/d_i} b^{-\gamma}
 \ll_{m,\gamma} x^{m\alpha}.
\]
The higher-moment bounds give uniform integrability of every fixed
power of \(N_\omega(x)/x^\alpha\), so the almost-sure limit also holds
in each finite \(L^m\), including real \(m\ge1\).
Using \(\sum_{b\le u}b^{-\gamma}\sim u^\alpha/\alpha\) and dominated
convergence also gives the convergent moment product
\[
 \mathbb E[A_\omega^m]=\alpha^{-m}\prod_q
 (1-q^{-1})^m\left(1+p_q\left[(1-q^{-\alpha})^{-m}-1\right]\right)
 \qquad(m\in\mathbb N).
 \tag{2}
\]

## Rough squarefree tuples below the threshold

Fix \(k\ge2\) and put \(j=k-1\). For a given large \(n\), let the
first \(j\) coordinates \(a_i\) range in
\([n/k,n/k+n/(4k^2)]\), and put \(a_k=n-\sum_{i=1}^j a_i\).
All \(a_i\) have size comparable to \(n\), with constants depending
only on \(k\). Impose congruences modulo 4 so that the first \(j\)
coordinates are odd and \(a_k\) is odd if \(n\equiv k\bmod2\), and
is \(2\bmod4\) otherwise. In the latter case, fix the first \(j-1\)
odd coordinates modulo 4 and choose the remaining odd residue to
make \(a_k\equiv2\bmod4\).

For each odd prime \(q\le2k\), choose nonzero residues for the \(k\)
coordinates that sum to \(n\bmod q\). This is possible because any
element of \(\mathbb F_q\) is the sum of two nonzero elements.
The Chinese remainder theorem combines these conditions on the first
\(j\) coordinates into a box of progressions of fixed step. Let
\(N\asymp_k n^j\) be its size. Sieve the product \(a_1\cdots a_k\)
over primes \(q>2k\). There are at most \(kq^{j-1}\) roots of the
product modulo \(q\), by the union bound on the \(k\) affine forms.
Writing \(g(q)\) for the root proportion gives \(0\le g(q)\le k/q<1\).
For squarefree \(d\le n^{1/2}\) supported on these primes, the root
count is \(g(d)d^j\le k^{\omega(d)}d^{j-1}\) by the CRT.
Each residue vector contributes
\(N/d^j+O_k((n/d)^{j-1})\) box points. The divisibility count is
thus \(Ng(d)+O_k(n^{j-1}k^{\omega(d)})\).

Mertens' product estimates and
\((1-k/q)^{-1}=(1-1/q)^{-k}(1+O_k(q^{-2}))\) give the dimension
bound \(\prod_{w\le q\le v}(1-g(q))^{-1}\ll_k(\log v/\log w)^k\)
and \(V(z)=\prod_{q<z}(1-g(q))\gg_k(\log z)^{-k}\), with
products restricted to \(q>2k\).
The lower-bound fundamental lemma at level \(D=n^{1/2}\) applies
to \(z=n^\theta\) for a fixed sufficiently small \(\theta=\theta_k>0\).
The summed error through \(D\) is
\(O_k(n^{j-1}D(1+\log D)^{k-1})\), by
\(k^{\omega(d)}=\tau_k(d)\) on squarefree \(d\) and elementary
factorization counting. It is \(o(n^j/(\log n)^k)\).
This leaves \(\gg_k n^j/(\log n)^k\) tuples.
[Cao–Huang–Zhang, Theorem 3.2](sources/cao-huang-zhang-2509.23320v1.pdf)
[Rosser–Schoenfeld, Theorems 7–8](sources/rosser_schoenfeld_1962.pdf)

Delete tuples with \(q^2\mid a_i\) or \(q\mid(a_i,a_l)\) for some odd
prime \(q\) and distinct \(i,l\). All such \(q\) are at least \(z\).
For a square divisor, a variable occurring with coefficient \(1\)
or \(-1\) has \(O_k(n/q^2+1)\) choices after the others are fixed.
Summing for \(z\le q\le\sqrt n\) costs
\(O_k(n^j/z+n^{j-1/2})\). For \(k\ge3\), each pair of forms has
a two-by-two minor of determinant \(1\) or \(-1\). In the two
corresponding box coordinates each root pair modulo \(q\) therefore
fixes a single residue vector given the remaining coordinates.
The bound \(O_k(n^{j-2}(n/q+1)^2)\) summed over \(z\le q\le n\)
is \(O_k(n^j/z)\). For \(k=2\), a shared prime must divide \(n\)
and at most \(1/\theta\) distinct primes \(q\ge z\) do so. They
cost \(O_\theta(n/z)\) choices in total.
All deletion counts are \(o(n^j/(\log n)^k)\).

Let \(\mathcal D_n\) be the resulting family of \(m\gg_k
n^{k-1}/(\log n)^k\) ordered tuples. For \(u\in\mathcal D_n\), let
\(R_u\) be the odd part of the product of its coordinates. Then
\(R_u\asymp_k n^k\), \(R_u\) is squarefree, and it has at most
\(J=\lceil k/\theta\rceil\) prime factors, all at least \(z\).
Its event of success has probability \(R_u^{-\gamma}\). If \(W\)
counts successes, \(\mu=\mathbb E W\asymp_{k,\gamma}m n^{-k\gamma}\).
For overlapping tuples \(u,v\), the joint probability is
\((R_uR_v)^{-\gamma}(R_u,R_v)^\gamma\).

Put \(D(d)=|\{u:d\mid R_u\}|\) for squarefree \(d>1\). For such a
tuple distribute the primes of \(d\) into the pairwise coprime
divisors \(d_i\mid a_i\). The number of assignments is at most
\(k^J\) whenever \(D(d)>0\). For each feasible assignment eliminate a
coordinate with the smallest \(d_i\) by the sum constraint.
All \(d_i\le n\), so a remaining coordinate has at most
\(O(n/d_i)\) choices. Dropping any constraints on the eliminated
coordinate, whose divisor is at most \(d^{1/k}\), gives
\[
 D(d)\ll_{k,\theta} n^{k-1}d^{-1+1/k}.
\]
Also \(\sum_{d>1}D(d)\le2^Jm\), by counting divisors of each \(R_u\).
Writing \(a=1-1/k\), the ordered distinct-pair overlap sum in
Janson's inequality is bounded by
\[
 \Delta
 \ll_{k,\gamma}n^{-2k\gamma}\sum_{d>1}d^\gamma D(d)^2
 \ll_{k,\gamma,\theta} n^{-2k\gamma}n^{k-1}
                       z^{-(a-\gamma)}m
       \qquad(0<\gamma<a).
\]
The first inequality bounds the gcd gain by the sum over its
nontrivial divisors. For the second, use the bound for one factor
\(D(d)\), \(d\ge z\) whenever \(D(d)>0\), and sum the other factor.
In particular \(\Delta/\mu^2\ll_{k,\gamma}(\log n)^k
z^{-(a-\gamma)}\) after fixing \(\theta\).
Janson gives \(\Pr(W=0)\le\exp(-\mu^2/(2(\mu+\Delta)))\).
Any fixed \(0<\delta<\min(k-1-k\gamma,\theta(a-\gamma))\)
works in the asserted stretched-exponential bound. Borel–Cantelli
gives cofiniteness.
[Janson–Warnke, introductory equation (2)](sources/janson-warnke-1406.1248v1.pdf)

## Critical and supercritical sumsets

If \(\gamma_k<\gamma<1\), the counting theorem gives
\(|kC\cap[1,x]|\le N_\omega(x)^k=O_\omega(x^{k(1-\gamma)})=o(x)\).
If \(\gamma\ge1\), for any fixed \(s>0\) the Euler product for
\(\mathbb E\sum_{a\in C}a^{-s}\) converges because its odd-prime
terms differ from 1 by \(O_s(q^{-\gamma-s})\). Thus almost surely
\(N_\omega(x)=O_\omega(x^s)\) for any fixed \(0<s<1/k\), giving
density zero also in this range.

At \(\gamma=\gamma_k\) put \(\alpha=1/k\). Let \(T_\omega(x)\) count
unordered representations with sum at most \(x\), allowing repeated
summands. With probability one the scaled counting measure
\(x^{-\alpha}\sum_{a\in C,\,a\le x}\delta_{a/x}\) on \([0,1]\)
converges weakly to \(A_\omega\alpha t^{\alpha-1}\,dt\).
This follows by testing intervals \([0,t]\) with the counting
asymptotic. The product measures converge on the simplex
\(\sum_i t_i\le1\), whose boundary has zero limiting measure.
The normalized ordered count tends to
\[
 A_\omega^k\int_{\sum_i t_i\le1}
             \prod_i\alpha t_i^{\alpha-1}\,d\mathbf t
 = A_\omega^k\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha)^k}{\Gamma(1+k\alpha)}.
\]
The integral is over \(t_i\ge0\) and follows by successive
beta integrals. Tuples with a repeated coordinate contribute
at most \(\binom{k}{2}N_\omega(x)^{k-1}=o(x)\).
Every tuple with distinct coordinates is counted \(k!\) times
in the ordered count. With \(\kappa_k=\Gamma(1+1/k)^k/k!\),
\[
             T_\omega(x)/x\ \longrightarrow\ \kappa_k A_\omega^k
                  \quad\text{almost surely and in }L^1.       \tag{3}
\]
For the \(L^1\) claim, \(T_\omega(x)\le N_\omega(x)^k\) and
the \(2k\)-th counting moment give a uniform \(L^2\) bound.

Expose the choices at odd primes up to \(y\ge2\), with sigma field
\(\mathcal F_y\). For a fixed exposed configuration there are only
\(O_y((1+\log x)^{O_y(1)})\) integers up to \(x\) represented by
tuples using only deterministic selected primes. Indeed each
exponent of each exposed prime or of 2 is \(O(\log x)\).
Discard these targets. Every remaining candidate representation
has conditional success probability \(w\le y^{-\gamma}\) because
it requires an unexposed prime, or \(w=0\) if it includes a
known excluded prime. Failure events are decreasing in the remaining
independent bits. Harris' inequality and
\(-\log(1-w)\le w/(1-y^{-\gamma})\) give
\[
 \Pr(n\notin kC\mid\mathcal F_y)
 \ge \exp\!\left(-\frac{\mathbb E[R_k(n)\mid\mathcal F_y]}
                        {1-y^{-\gamma}}\right),
\]
where \(R_k(n)\) is the random unordered representation count.
[Janson–Warnke, introductory equation (3)](sources/janson-warnke-1406.1248v1.pdf)

Average over the retained \(x-o_y(x)\) targets through the integer
\(x\) using Jensen's inequality. The sum of their conditional
representation expectations is at most
\(\mathbb E[T_\omega(x)\mid\mathcal F_y]\). By (3), after division
by \(x\) this tends to \(\kappa_k\mathbb E[A_\omega^k\mid\mathcal F_y]\)
on every atom of the finite sigma field. Writing \(D^*=
\overline d(\mathbb N\setminus kC)\), conditional reverse Fatou
for the bounded gap density gives
\[
 \mathbb E[D^*\mid\mathcal F_y]\ge
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{\kappa_k\mathbb E[A_\omega^k\mid\mathcal F_y]}
                        {1-y^{-\gamma}}\right).
\]
The fields \(\mathcal F_y\) increase to the sigma field of the random
model. Martingale convergence for \(D^*\) and \(A_\omega^k\) now
gives (1). Log-convexity of \(\Gamma\) on \([1,2]\) gives
\(0<\kappa_k<1\). For the least-order assertion, intersect the
probability-one conclusions over the countable set of \(k\ge2\),
and use \(N_\omega(x)=o(x)\) for \(k=1\).
