# Full holonomy of the coalesced attachment interface

Let \(D\) denote a double transposition and \(T\) a 3-cycle.
The explicit marked polynomial bases of
[Corollary 6 of the cycle-folding theorem](ALTERNATING_HURWITZ_CYCLE_FOLDING.md)
have more attachment symmetry than transitivity alone:
their full attachment groups are symmetric in type \(D^r\), and alternating
in type \(TD^{r-1}\) except for a Klein-four degree-seven case.
These are statements about stabilizers of specified bases, not a
connectivity theorem for all polynomial tuples.

## The interface and the literal action

Put \(N=2r+1\), \(r\geq3\). Permutations compose from right to left.
A positive Hurwitz move acts by
\[
 Q_i(\ldots,a,b,\ldots)=(\ldots,b,b^{-1}ab,\ldots).
\]
The first factor \(\delta\) is marked. We use the subgroup generated by
\(Q_1^2,Q_2,\ldots,Q_{r-1}\). A compressed word writes \(1\) for
\(Q_1^2\), \(j\geq2\) for \(Q_j\), and \(-j\) for the corresponding
inverse; words act from left to right.

Let \(O=\{a:\delta(a)=a\}\). Write \(\delta=pq\), with \(p,q\)
transpositions, and add one new symbol \(\infty\). For \(a\in O\),
the coalesced expansion is
\[
 \bigl((\infty\,a)p,\;(\infty\,a)q,\;g_2,\ldots,g_r\bigr).       \tag{1}
\]
Both new factors are double transpositions. There are two ordered
splittings \(pq=\delta\) for type \(D\), and three for type \(T\).

The cable of code \(1\) is \((2,1,1,2)\) in ordinary generators
on the expanded tuple, the cable of \(-1\) is its inverse, and
the cable of code \(j\geq2\) is \(j+1\). The local calculation
from the cited cycle-folding proof gives the following transport rule.
At a positive marked move on \((\delta,\gamma)\), the attachment,
the splitting, and the marked factor are conjugated or transported
by \(\gamma\); at a negative marked move the transport is
\(\delta\gamma\delta^{-1}\). Unmarked moves have identity transport.
Here every unmarked factor \(\gamma\) is a double transposition.
Accumulating these permutations gives \(h(w)\), with
\[
 (\delta,p,q,a)\longmapsto
 \bigl(h\delta h^{-1},hph^{-1},hqh^{-1},h(a)\bigr).              \tag{2}
\]
All transports are even.

A **literal loop** returns each factor of the polynomial tuple
exactly, with no final common conjugation. Such a loop has
\(h\delta h^{-1}=\delta\), so \(h\) restricts to a permutation of \(O\).
Let \(\mathcal H(t)\leq S(O)\) be the image of all literal loops,
and let \(\mathcal H_{\rm pure}(t;p,q)\) be the image of literal
loops which are also pure braids on the ordered branch positions
and hold the ordered splitting \((p,q)\) fixed in (2).
The latter is a subgroup of the former.

These definitions concern the one-new-sheet expansion (1). Adding
a double transposition on two new leaf sheets below is a construction
of chosen polynomial bases; it is not an identification of two different
extension interfaces.

## Theorem 1. Full attachment groups

Start with the following degree-seven bases, whose first factor is marked:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 t_D(3)&=((01)(34),(12)(45),(23)(56)),\\
 t_T(3)&=((012),(23)(45),(34)(56)).
 \end{aligned}                                               \tag{3}
\]
For \(r>3\), embed (3) in \(S_{2r+1}\) and append, in increasing
order of the new labels, the factors
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 (2\,x)(5\,x+1),&t_D(r),\\
 (3\,x)(4\,x+1),&t_T(r),
 \end{array}
 \qquad x=7,9,\ldots,2r-1.                                  \tag{4}
\]
These are full-\(A_N\) polynomial tuples with \(N\)-cycle product,
as proved in the cited Corollary 6.

For every ordered splitting \(pq=\delta\),
\[
 \mathcal H_{\rm pure}(t;p,q)=\mathcal H(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 S_{2r-3},&t=t_D(r),\ r\geq3,\\
 V_4\text{ in its regular action on four symbols},&t=t_T(3),\\
 A_{2r-2},&t=t_T(r),\ r\geq4.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{5}
\]
The pure loops realizing the mixed-case lower bounds can be chosen
to fix every sheet in the support of \(\delta=(012)\) pointwise.
In particular, the exceptional mixed group at \(r=3\) is smaller
than \(A_4\).

### Proof: upper bounds

For type \(D\), \(S(O)\) is the ambient upper bound.
For type \(T\), \(h\) centralizes \((012)\). On its three-symbol
support the restriction lies in \(\langle(012)\rangle\) and is even.
Since \(h\) is even on all sheets, its restriction to \(O\) is even.
Thus \(\mathcal H(t_T(r))\leq A(O)\).

The sharper degree-seven upper bound is a finite Schreier calculation,
described below with its complete domain and replay algorithm.
It proves \(\mathcal H(t_T(3))\leq V_4\) for all literal marked loops,
without assuming they are pure or preserve the splitting.

### Proof: double-transposition seed

The following two pure literal loops of \(t_D(3)\) have the displayed
full transports:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 (2,1,1,1,-2)&(34)(56)\\
 (1,2,2,-1,-1,-2,-2,-1)&(01)(25).
 \end{array}                                                \tag{6}
\]
These are the exact identities in the cited Corollary 6. Their
restrictions to \(O=\{2,5,6\}\) are \((56)\) and \((25)\),
which generate \(S_3\). Both preserve the two transpositions
\((01),(34)\) separately, and hence preserve both ordered splittings.

### Proof: the mixed seeds

For \(t_T(3)\), use the pure literal loops
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 (2,1,-2)&(34)(56)\\
 (1,1,2,-1,-2,1,-2,1,2)&(012)(36)(45).
 \end{array}                                                \tag{7}
\]
Cubing the second loop makes its transport \((36)(45)\).
Together with the first this generates the regular \(V_4\) on
\(\{3,4,5,6\}\), fixing \(0,1,2\) pointwise. This proves the
lower bound with every splitting fixed.

For \(t_T(4)\), whose fourth factor is \((37)(48)\), three pure
literal loops have transports
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 (2,-1,-2)&(34)(56)\\
 (-3,2,-1,-2,3)&(38)(47)\\
 (-3,-3,-2,-1,2,3,3)&(46)(78).
 \end{array}                                                \tag{8}
\]
All three fix \(0,1,2\) pointwise. Exact closure of their restrictions
to \(\{3,4,5,6,7,8\}\) has order \(360\): start at the identity,
left multiply by each of the three displayed involutions, and retain
each previously unseen permutation until the queue is empty.
The checker performs this finite calculation with literal arrays;
all resulting permutations are even and all \(360\) distinct.
Consequently these restrictions generate \(A_6\).
No higher-degree census is used here.

### Proof: the uniform extension

Let the old outside set be \(O\), and append
\(\eta=(a\,u)(b\,v)\), where \(a,b\in O\) are distinct and \(u,v\)
are new. Every old pure literal loop remains valid, with its old
transport extended to fix \(u,v\).

There is a pure literal loop with transport exactly \(\eta\).
If its factor has zero-based index \(j\geq3\), use
\[
                  (j,j-1,\ldots,2,\;1,\;-2,\ldots,-j).       \tag{9}
\]
Positive unmarked moves bring \(\eta\) unchanged to the second
position. It commutes with \(\delta\), so \(Q_1^2\) fixes this pair
and transports by \(\eta\); undoing the unmarked moves restores
the entire tuple. This loop fixes the marked support pointwise.

For type \(D\), assume the old image contains \(S(O)\), where
\(|O|\geq3\). Choose \(c\in O\setminus\{a,b\}\).
Conjugation by \(\eta\) turns \((a\,c)\) into \((u\,c)\)
and \((b\,c)\) into \((v\,c)\). The old transpositions together
with these two connect all the new sheets in a transposition graph,
and hence generate \(S(O\cup\{u,v\})\).
For completeness, edge transpositions of a connected graph generate
all transpositions: along a path, successively conjugating an endpoint
edge produces a transposition between its first vertex and each
subsequent vertex. Star transpositions then generate the full
symmetric group. All seed transports preserve \((01)\) and \((34)\)
separately, so every inductive word still preserves the splitting.

For type \(T\), begin with the \(A_6\) seed (8). Assume the old
image contains \(A(O)\), \(|O|\geq6\), with marked support fixed.
Choose distinct \(c,d\in O\setminus\{a,b\}\). Conjugation by
\(\eta\) produces \((u\,c\,d)\) and \((v\,c\,d)\)
from the old 3-cycles \((a\,c\,d)\) and \((b\,c\,d)\).
The group \(A(O)\) is 2-transitive: any map on two ordered distinct
letters can be made even by interchanging two other old letters.
Conjugation therefore supplies every 3-cycle \((u\,i\,j)\)
and \((v\,i\,j)\) with \(i,j\in O\) distinct. Conjugating
\((u\,i\,j)\) by \((v\,i\,j)\) also supplies \((u\,j\,v)\).
Thus all types of 3-cycles on \(O\cup\{u,v\}\) are present.
They generate the alternating group: a product of two transpositions
is either a 3-cycle, a product of two 3-cycles when disjoint,
or the identity, and every even permutation is a product of such
pairs. The upper parity bound now gives equality.

Conjugation and concatenation of the pure literal loops used above
are still pure literal loops. In the mixed family they fix the marked
support pointwise, so they preserve all ordered splittings.
This proves every equality in (5), subject only to the finite
degree-seven upper calculation completed next. \(\square\)

## The complete degree-seven Schreier upper bound

This is the only exceptional-case exhaustion in the proof.
Fix the literal product \(P=\prod t_T(3)\). There are \(70\)
3-cycles and \(105\) double transpositions on seven symbols.
For every such pair \((\delta,\gamma)\), the required last factor
is forced:
\[
                 \theta=(\delta\gamma)^{-1}P.
\]
Keeping exactly those pairs for which \(\theta\) is a double
transposition yields \(196\) literal tuples.

Independently, breadth-first exploration from \(t_T(3)\) under
the four signed codes \(1,-1,2,-2\) reaches exactly these \(196\)
tuples and checks all \(784\) directed edges. Store one transport
\(T_x\) along a chosen path from the seed to each reached vertex \(x\).
For an edge \(x\xrightarrow{c}y\) with step transport \(h_c\),
its fundamental closed-loop transport is
\[
                         T_y^{-1}h_cT_x.                    \tag{10}
\]
The checker verifies that every restriction of (10) to
\(\{3,4,5,6\}\) belongs to
\[
 V_4=\{1,(34)(56),(35)(46),(36)(45)\}.                         \tag{11}
\]
This accounts for every literal closed word. Indeed, along an arbitrary
closed path, substituting
\(h_c=T_y(T_y^{-1}h_cT_x)T_x^{-1}\)
makes all intermediate \(T_x\)'s cancel, expressing its transport
as a product of the fundamental transports (10).
All such transports centralize the initial marked 3-cycle, so their
restrictions compose on the same outside set. The lower bound (7)
then gives exact \(V_4\).

The [checker](verify_full_coalesced_attachment_holonomy.py) derives
both the forced-last-factor domain and the entire closed move graph.
No selected-loop list alone is used for this upper bound.

## Corollary 2. Several ordered attachment choices

Consider the diagonal action of the same literal stabilizer on ordered
\(k\)-tuples of distinct choices in \(O\). Even when the ordered splitting
in (1) is held fixed, its orbit counts are:

* Type \(D\): one orbit for every \(0\leq k\leq2r-3\).
* Type \(T\), \(r\geq4\), with \(m=2r-2\): one orbit for
  \(0\leq k\leq m-2\), and two orbits for \(k=m-1,m\).
* Type \(T\), \(r=3\): one, three, six, six orbits for \(k=1,2,3,4\),
  respectively; \(k=0\) has one orbit.

For \(A_m\), an ordered matching of at most \(m-2\) letters extends
to an even permutation by correcting parity on two unused letters.
For \(m-1\) or \(m\) specified letters there is no parity correction,
giving precisely two orbits. The regular \(V_4\) has trivial stabilizer
as soon as one letter is chosen, so for \(k\geq1\) the count is
\(4!/(4-k)!/4\).

These are multiple choices in the same coalesced attachment action.
They assert neither a simultaneous multiple-cover construction nor
transitivity of the separate two-new-leaf extension interface.

arbitrary-tree normalization theorem. The unclassified all-degree
