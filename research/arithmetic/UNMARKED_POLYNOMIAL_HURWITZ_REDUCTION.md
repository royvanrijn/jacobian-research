# Removing branch markings from the polynomial Hurwitz problem

The two marked polynomial connectivity questions arising from the
doubled-edge reduction can both be replaced by unmarked questions.
The double-transposition case has a uniform explicit generating tuple
and a literal braid stabilizer that cycles every finite branch label.
The case with one 3-cycle has a distinguished branch type already.

This reduces the remaining uniform connectivity problem for the
original even-degree passport to two unmarked polynomial-cactus
connectivity problems. It does not establish either remaining
connectivity assertion.

## The spaces and the exact marked subgroup

Let r>=4, N=2r+1, and fix P=(0 1 ... N-1). Write D for the DT class
and T for the 3-cycle class in A_N. Put

\[
 X_D=\{(g_1,\ldots,g_r)\in D^r:g_1\cdots g_r=P\},
\]

and let X_T consist of the fixed-product tuples with one entry in T
and all other entries in D, with the T entry allowed in any position.
Let Y_T be the subset with its T entry first.

These are the unmarked fixed-product sets; the first entry is marked
when the action is restricted to

\[
                 H=\langle Q_2,\ldots,Q_{r-1},Q_1^2\rangle.
\]

All tuple products in this note use ordinary permutation composition,
with the rightmost permutation applied first. A displayed sequence of
Hurwitz moves is applied from left to right, using

\[
               Q_i:(a,b)\longmapsto(b,b^{-1}ab).
\]

The following subgroup identification is part of the proof; enlarging
the allowed marked subgroup is unnecessary.

## Lemma 1. The allowed generators give the full strand stabilizer

For the standard strand permutation map pi:B_r->S_r,

\[
                     H=\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{Stab}(1)).
\]

**Proof.** Each displayed generator lies in the right side.
For the reverse inclusion consider configurations consisting of one
marked point z and an unordered set S of r-1 other points, with z
different from every point of S. The forgetful map that removes the
mark has degree r onto the unordered r-point configuration space.
Its fundamental group is pi^-1(Stab(1)).

Instead forget z and retain S. This is a locally trivial bundle with
fiber C minus S: in a small neighborhood, disjoint disks around the
points and small isotopies identify the punctured planes. Loops of
the other r-1 points lift while keeping z fixed; their standard
generators are Q_2,...,Q_(r-1).

A loop whose projected braid of S is null-homotopic can, by lifting
a null-homotopy through these local identifications, be represented
by moving z alone in C minus S. This punctured plane has fundamental
group generated by meridians around its individual punctures.
Those meridians are represented by

\[
 (Q_{j-1}\cdots Q_2)\,Q_1^2\,
                 (Q_2^{-1}\cdots Q_{j-1}^{-1}),
                         \qquad 2\le j\le r,
\]

with an empty outer word for j=2, after choosing the usual paths to
the punctures. They all belong to H. The projected loop and the
kernel loop therefore both lie in H, proving the reverse inclusion.
This uses only the local bundle identifications, path lifting and
the elementary meridian generators of a punctured plane. ∎

## Theorem 2. A uniform cyclic DT tuple and a literal stabilizer

Let c=P^-2, d=(0 2)(3 4), and define

\[
                  g_i=c^{-(i-1)}d\,c^{i-1},
                                  \qquad 1\le i\le r.
\tag{1}
\]

Then the tuple generates A_N and has product P. Let rho denote the
cycling braid consisting of Q_1,Q_2,...,Q_(r-1), in that order.
The braid

\[
                              \boxed{\rho^N}
\tag{2}
\]

fixes the literal tuple (1). Its permutation of the r branch labels
is an r-cycle.

**Proof.** Write C=P^2=(a_0 a_1 ... a_(2r)), with a_j=2j modulo N.
The two edges of d are

\[
                         (a_0\,a_1),\quad(a_2\,a_{r+2}).
\]

Left multiplication of C by the first transposition isolates a_0
and leaves a 2r-cycle. The second cuts that cycle at two points
distance r apart. Thus

\[
                             dP^2\text{ has type }(r,r,1).
\tag{3}
\]

Since c^r=P and c^-1=P^2, telescoping gives

\[
       g_1\cdots g_r=(dc^{-1})^r c^r=(dP^2)^rP=P.
\tag{4}
\]

The product P makes the generated group transitive. It is primitive:
a nontrivial block size in this odd degree is at least three.
A DT moving a block would move at least two such blocks, hence at
least six letters. Each generator therefore fixes every block
setwise, which would contradict transitivity.

The full alternating group follows already from the first three
generators, without a classification of primitive DT groups.
They are

\[
 d_1=(02)(34),\quad d_2=(24)(56),\quad d_3=(46)(78).
\]

The element

\[
                 e=d_1(d_1d_2)^2=(04)(23)
\]

belongs to the group, and

\[
                              (ed_3)^2=(0\,6\,4)
\tag{5}
\]

is a 3-cycle. Jordan's prime-cycle theorem applies because N>=9
and the 3-cycle fixes at least three letters. All generators are
even, so the group is exactly A_N. The precise imported formulation
is Jones, Theorem1.1, already retained in the
[primary-source ledger](alternating_hurwitz_source_ledger.json).

For a tuple with product P, the cycling braid has the exact formula

\[
 \rho(g_1,\ldots,g_r)
        =(g_2,\ldots,g_r,P^{-1}g_1P).
\tag{6}
\]

For (1), this is simultaneous conjugation by c^-1=P^2: the final
entry agrees because c^r=P. Applying the same formula N times is
simultaneous conjugation by P^(2N), the identity. This proves the
literal fixed-tuple assertion (2).

Each Q_i permutes adjacent branch labels, so rho induces an
r-cycle. Raising it to N=2r+1 leaves an r-cycle, since N is one
modulo r. ∎

The product and stabilizer formulas also hold for r=2,3; the full
A_N assertion here is restricted to r>=4. The displayed first three
generators use the literal label 8; the 3-cycle argument is applied
only for N>=9.

## Lemma 3. A transitive stabilizer removes a marking

Let a group B act transitively on a set X, let pi:B->S_r be a
homomorphism, and put H=pi^-1(Stab(1)). If the stabilizer K of one
point of X is transitive on the r labels through pi, then H acts
transitively on X.

**Proof.** Use left actions. Write X=B/K. For any beta in B,
transitivity of pi(K) supplies k in K such that
pi(k)(1)=pi(beta)^-1(1). Then beta k lies in H, and

\[
                           \beta K=(\beta k)K.
\]

Thus every H-orbit meets the base point's H-orbit, so there is only
one. Equivalently H\B/K is the set of K-orbits on the r possible
markings. The conclusion also applies to right-action conventions
by passing to the inverse left action; the stabilizers and the
transitivity assertions are unchanged. ∎

## Theorem 4. Unmarked connectivity suffices for both marked spaces

For X_D, transitivity under B_r is equivalent to transitivity under
the exact subgroup H of Lemma1. For X_T, transitivity under B_r is
equivalent to transitivity of Y_T under H.

**Proof.** For X_D, the literal tuple (1) lies in the space and its
stabilizer contains rho^N. Theorem2 makes its strand action transitive.
Lemma3 proves the implication from B_r transitivity to H transitivity.
The converse follows from H being a subgroup of B_r.

For X_T, any braid carrying a tuple whose T entry is first to another
such tuple must return that distinguished entry to the first position.
Its strand permutation fixes 1, so Lemma1 places it in H.
Consequently a transitive B_r action gives a transitive H action on
Y_T. Conversely any tuple in X_T can be moved to Y_T by moving its
unique T entry to the first position. Transitivity on Y_T then
connects every pair of tuples in X_T. ∎

Both formulations concern the literal fixed-product sets. If instead
one takes the usual absolute quotient by the centralizer of P,
the central full twist acts as simultaneous conjugation by P^-1.
Every centralizer element is a power of P, and this full twist lies
in H. Hence the same orbit-connectivity statements hold in that
absolute quotient.

## Corollary 5. The remaining uniform problem is unmarked

Let n=2r+2>=10. If the two unmarked polynomial spaces X_D and X_T
are braid transitive, the original degree-n full-alternating passport

\[
                       D^{r+1},\quad(n-1,1)
\]

is braid transitive.

**Proof.** Theorem4 gives the two marked polynomial connectivity
hypotheses. Corollary6 of the
[cycle-folding note](ALTERNATING_HURWITZ_CYCLE_FOLDING.md)
supplies attachment transitivity uniformly. Its Corollary5 lifts
the marked paths, and Corollary4 connects the two coalescence
types. ∎

This is a sufficient criterion. The original tuple space can connect
different coalesced polynomial types, so a converse has not been
established. Degree fourteen, with polynomial degree thirteen and
r=6, remains the first unresolved complex case in the sequence.

## Exact evidence and scope

The [checker](verify_unmarked_polynomial_hurwitz_reduction.py)
replays (1)--(6), the literal braid (2), its branch-label action, the
short 3-cycle certificate, and the central full-twist formula.
The [receipt](unmarked_polynomial_hurwitz_reduction_verification.json)
records deterministic controls through r=50 and finite symmetric-group
controls for Lemma3. These supplement the symbolic all-degree proof.

The subgroup equality in Lemma1 is proved by the configuration-space
argument, rather than inferred from finite permutation tests.
No complete census in polynomial degree thirteen or higher is
claimed. No rational-point theorem or uniform unmarked connectivity
theorem follows from these stabilizers.

The [source ledger](unmarked_polynomial_hurwitz_reduction_source_ledger.json)
pins the existing proof inputs and the new exact evidence. No
priority claim, formal verification or external human review is made.
