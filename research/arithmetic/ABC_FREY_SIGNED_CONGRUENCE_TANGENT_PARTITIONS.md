# First-order directions and the signed Frey congruence index

Let \(f\) be the rational weight-two newform of the fixed-level Frey
family, and write \(R=\operatorname{rad}(abc)\). Use the exact
\(R\)-new subspace with all the Atkin–Lehner signs of \(f\).
The congruence index \(r_f\) from its integral Hecke order satisfies
the [full-abc equivalence](ABC_FREY_COEFFICIENT_PRIME_OLD_ANNIHILATOR.md#one-classical-signed-space-and-one-signed-toric-lattice).
At a coefficient prime the individual congruences give an upper
bound by the sum of their depths. A stronger upper bound groups
the competing forms by linear independence of their first
Hecke differences. Each independent group costs only its largest
depth. The grouping works at different depths and at every
coefficient prime.

## Local depths and independent groups

Use the full Hecke order on the signed space and extend coefficients
from \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) to the ring of integers \(\mathcal O\) of
a finite splitting field, with uniformizer \(\pi\), residue field
\(k\) and ramification index \(e\). On this order \(T\) there are
integral eigencharacters \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_s\),
with \(\lambda_0\) corresponding to \(f\). Write \(e_0\) for the
rational idempotent of \(f\), and set \(v=e\,v_\ell(r_f)\).
The least exponent making \(\pi^v e_0\in T\) is indeed \(v\), by
flat base change of the kernel of projection to the other rational
factors and then evaluation at \(f\).

Let \(t_1,\ldots,t_b\) be algebra generators of \(T\) over
\(\mathcal O\). For instance, the good prime Hecke operators
through \(B_{\mathrm{sign}}\) suffice by
[(OA8)](ABC_FREY_COEFFICIENT_PRIME_OLD_ANNIHILATOR.md#shorter-coefficient-vectors-for-a-fixed-sign-character),
the Hecke recurrences and the scalar action at bad primes.
Put \(x_j=t_j-\lambda_0(t_j)\) and, for every \(i\ne0\), define
\[
 m_i=\min_j v_\pi(\lambda_i(x_j)),\qquad
 b_i=\big(\pi^{-m_i}\lambda_i(x_j)\bmod\pi\big)_{1\le j\le b}.
 \tag{TD1}
\]
A depth-zero competitor can be removed at unit cost, using
\(\lambda_i(x_j)-x_j\) for any \(j\) with unit evaluation.
Write \(I\) for the remaining indices. Each \(b_i\) for
\(i\in I\) is nonzero. For these indices the normalized
difference of \(\lambda_i\) and \(\lambda_0\) is a derivation
into \(k\) at their common residual character. Products of two
differences are zero after division by \(\pi^{m_i}\) and
reduction, since \(m_i\ge1\). Consequently linear ranks of
collections of \(b_i\) can be computed in any algebra generators.

If \(S\subseteq I\) has linearly independent \(b_i\), set
\(M_S=\max_{i\in S}m_i\). The row matrix
\((\pi^{-m_i}\lambda_i(x_j))_{i\in S,j}\) is surjective over
\(\mathcal O\) onto the coordinates of \(S\), since its
reduction has full row rank. Solve exactly for \(u_j\) with
\[
   \sum_j u_j\pi^{-m_i}\lambda_i(x_j)=\pi^{M_S-m_i}
                     \quad (i\in S).
\]
Then \(\pi^{M_S}-\sum_j u_jx_j\) vanishes on all of \(S\)
and has value \(\pi^{M_S}\) at \(f\). Multiplying over any
partition \(\mathcal P\) of \(I\) into independent groups gives
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
   \max_{i}m_i\ \le\ e\,v_\ell(r_f)\
     \le\ U_\ell:=\min_{\mathcal P}\sum_{S\in\mathcal P}\max_{i\in S}m_i\
     \le\ \sum_i m_i .}
 \tag{TD2}
\]
Empty maxima and sums are zero. The lower bound follows by
evaluating \(\pi^v e_0\) at each pair of characters. The singleton
partition recovers the pairwise sum bound. If all directions
are independent, the largest pairwise depth gives the exact
index, even with different depths. In that case the local
order itself on \(f\) and its residual competitors is
\[
 \{(a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_{|I|})\in\mathcal O^{1+|I|}:
                  a_i\equiv a_0\pmod{\pi^{m_i}}\text{ for each }i\}.
\]
In fact the same surjective row matrix shows that the image
of the augmentation ideal is exactly
\(\prod_{i\in I}\pi^{m_i}\mathcal O\). Conversely,
if the local order is this coordinatewise congruence order,
the normalized reduction of its augmentation ideal surjects
onto \(k^I\). Its reduction is the span of the columns from
(TD1), since the ideal is generated by the \(x_j\) and all its
multipliers have a common residual value. Thus independence
is equivalent to this exact description of the order.

The normalized bound \(U_\ell/e\) is independent of the
choice of splitting field. In a common extension all the
\(m_i\) and \(e\) scale by the ramification index, and the
directions undergo residue-field extension and unit row
rescalings, preserving the matroid.

For equal positive depths \(m_i=d\), the upper bound is
\(d\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq I}
       \lceil |S|/\dim_k\langle b_i:i\in S\rangle\rceil\)
by the matroid partition theorem. For prescribed differing group
capacities \(M_1,\ldots,M_a\), a partition into independent groups
at those capacities exists exactly if
\[
       |S|\le \sum_{j=1}^a
             \dim_k\langle b_i:i\in S,\ m_i\le M_j\rangle
                         \qquad(S\subseteq I).
 \tag{TD3}
\]
Indeed the \(j\)-th matroid is the vector matroid with the
ineligible elements made loops. This is the matroid union
criterion. Minimizing \(\sum_j M_j\) over admissible capacities
gives \(U_\ell\). Any explicit partition already certifies an
upper bound and only requires finitely many row-rank checks.
[Edmonds, Theorem 1](sources/edmonds-matroid-partition-1965.pdf).
[Edmonds–Fulkerson, Theorem 1c](sources/abc-edmonds-fulkerson-matroid-partition-1965.pdf).

There is also a polynomial separator for distinct directions
at a common depth \(d\). Let \(h\) be a degree for which
polynomial evaluation through degree \(h\) is surjective at
the points \(0\) and \(b_i\) of a group. Solve over \(\mathcal O\)
for a polynomial \(F\) through degree \(h\) with values \(1\)
at zero and \(0\) at the exact points
\((\pi^{-d}\lambda_i(x_j))_j\). Then
\(\pi^{dh}F((\pi^{-d}x_j)_j)\in T\) separates the whole group
at cost \(dh\). This follows by the same lifting of a full-row-rank
matrix. For \(a\) distinct nonzero points the degree can always
be at most \(a\), by multiplying pair separators for each point.
Over \(k\) of size \(q\), it can also be at most \(b(q-1)\)
by the coordinate indicator polynomials on \(k^b\).

## Aggregate pairwise depth without splitting fields

Let \(W\) be the rational complement of \(f\) in the signed space.
Take the same integral generators before localization and write
\[
   Q_f(\mathbf X)=
      \det\nolimits_W\!\left(\sum_j X_j(t_j-\lambda_0(t_j))\right),
   \qquad C_f=\operatorname{cont}_{\mathbb Z} Q_f>0 .
 \tag{TD4}
\]
The determinant has integer coefficients because the integral
cusp forms in \(W\) give an invariant lattice. Over a splitting
field it factors into the nonzero linear polynomials whose
coefficient vectors are the eigenvalue differences. Gauss's
lemma over each coefficient DVR gives the exact formula
\[
             v_\ell(C_f)=\frac{1}{e}\sum_i m_i .
 \tag{TD5}
\]
In particular the total pairwise depth is a globally computable
integer content. It has the same prime support as \(r_f\), by
(TD2), and \(r_f\mid C_f\). The depth sum inequality is the
split rank-one case of the comparison of Berger–Klosin–Kramer.
The independent-group bound in (TD2) uses the directions as
additional data.
[Berger–Klosin–Kramer, Proposition 4.3 and Example 5.3](sources/berger-klosin-kramer-higher-congruences-2014.pdf).

One can isolate the excess of \(C_f\) by normalization. Over
\(\mathcal O\), use \(A\) for the image of \(T\) on the
competing factors and \(J\subset A\) for the image of
\(\ker\lambda_0\). Then \(A/J\simeq\mathcal O/(\pi^v)\).
The normalization \(\widetilde A=\mathcal O^s\) has
\(J\widetilde A=\prod_i\pi^{m_i}\mathcal O\). The inclusion
\(A/J\to\widetilde A/J\widetilde A\) has kernel
\((A\cap J\widetilde A)/J\) and cokernel
\(\widetilde A/(A+J\widetilde A)\). Thus
\[
 \begin{split}
 e\,v_\ell(C_f/r_f)
   &=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}\bigl(\widetilde A/(A+J\widetilde A)\bigr)
     -\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}\bigl((A\cap J\widetilde A)/J\bigr),\\
 0 &\le e\,v_\ell(C_f/r_f)
                 \le \operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}(\widetilde A/A).
 \end{split}
 \tag{TD6}
\]
The length on the right can be restricted to the residual
factor of \(f\). All other factors have unit image of \(J\).
These formulas specify the extra information needed to turn
large pairwise depth into a lower bound for the actual index.

## Adjoint interpretation at the large-image primes

For a Frey form and \(\ell\ge5\), the completed signed good
Hecke order at the residual character of \(f\) carries the
single Galois representation in the
[joint division-field argument](ABC_FREY_JOINT_DIVISION_FIELDS.md#the-image-over-the-hecke-order).
At these primes that order is the full signed order by the
[Katz comparison](ABC_FREY_JOINT_DIVISION_FIELDS.md#comparing-the-intersection-depth-to-the-frey-degree).
After scalar extension one can specialize the same representation
to \(\rho_i\) and \(\rho_0\). For \(i\in I\),
\[
 c_i(g)=\pi^{-m_i}(\rho_i(g)-\rho_0(g))\bar\rho(g)^{-1}\bmod\pi .
 \tag{TD7}
\]
This is a trace-zero adjoint cocycle because the determinants are
the same. Its class has the same linear dependence relations
over \(k\) as the derivation in (TD1).

Indeed the trace of \(c_i(g)\bar\rho(g)\) is the normalized
Hecke trace difference. An inner cocycle has zero trace
difference. Conversely suppose a linear combination of the
trace differences is zero on all group elements. Its matrix
derivation \(d\) on the group algebra satisfies
\(\operatorname{tr}d(x)=0\) for every \(x\). If
\(\bar\rho(x)=0\), the derivation rule gives
\(0=\operatorname{tr}d(xy)=\operatorname{tr}(d(x)\bar\rho(y))\)
for every \(y\). Absolute irreducibility and the nondegenerate
matrix trace pairing give \(d(x)=0\). Then \(d\) descends to a
derivation of \(M_2(k)\) and is inner. Its inner matrix can be
made trace-zero by subtracting a scalar since \(2\) is invertible.
Finally the traces generate the completed good Hecke order, so
zero on all trace differences is equivalent to zero on (TD1).
Thus adjoint classes or their restrictions can certify the
independent groups in (TD2).

In a Galois splitting field, inertia conjugate coefficient
embeddings of a single competitor always give parallel
directions. Indeed an inertia element \(\sigma\) fixes \(k\)
and sends \(b_i\) to
\((\sigma(\pi)/\pi\bmod\pi)^{m_i}b_i\).
So the rank test retains the multiplicities contributed by
ramified conjugates.

## Limits of pairwise depth and the remaining estimate

Even the whole pairwise depth matrix can fail to determine
\(v\). Over a complete DVR with at least \(n+1\) residue
elements take \(a>0\) and distinct nonzero residual units
\(u_1,\ldots,u_n\). For any \(a\le h\le na\) set
\[
 x=(0,\pi^a u_1,\ldots,\pi^a u_n),\qquad
 T_h=\mathcal O[x]+\pi^h\mathcal O^{n+1}\subset\mathcal O^{n+1}.
\]
Every distinct pair of coordinates has depth exactly \(a\),
using \(\pi^h\mathcal O^{n+1}\) and the entries of \(x\).
The idempotent denominator at the first coordinate is exactly
\(\pi^h\). For the lower bound, if a polynomial \(P(x)\) has
all competing values in \(\pi^h\mathcal O\), divide \(P(X)\)
by \(\prod_{i=1}^n(X-\pi^a u_i)\). The remainder has degree
less than \(n\). Vandermonde interpolation at the \(u_i\)
shows that its \(j\)-th coefficient times \(\pi^{aj}\) lies
in \(\pi^h\mathcal O\). In particular the constant term does,
and the divisor polynomial has constant term divisible by
\(\pi^h\) too. Thus \(P(0)\in\pi^h\mathcal O\).
The upper bound follows from the included \(\pi^h\mathcal O^{n+1}\).
This is a family of abstract orders, refining the endpoint
examples for pairwise depths. It gives no modular realization
of the extreme values.
[Related sharp endpoint for the trace-order bound (DF5)](ABC_FREY_JOINT_DIVISION_FIELDS.md).

For the fixed Frey family, a uniform estimate
\[
        \sum_\ell (U_\ell/e)\log\ell
               \le (2+\varepsilon)\log R+O_\varepsilon(1)
 \tag{TD8}
\]
suffices for abc. The sum is finite by (TD5). The new local
gain applies whenever leading adjoint or Hecke directions
can be grouped independently. A global bound still requires
control on the number and depths of those groups. For a
disproof, the aggregate pairwise depth must be paired with
a bound on the local deficits in (TD6), or with a direct
lower bound on \(r_f\).

## A signed modular packet at level 442

The space new at level \(442=2\cdot13\cdot17\) with
\((U_2,U_{13},U_{17})=(1,-1,-1)\) has three rational forms.
From PARI the rows below are their first 15 coefficients,
the bound (OA8). Take the first row as the distinguished form.
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&2&1&4&2&-4&1&1&4&-2&2&-1&-4&8\\
 1&1&2&1&-2&2&2&1&1&-2&4&2&-1&2&-4\\
 1&1&0&1&2&0&4&1&-3&2&-2&0&-1&4&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The two pairwise moduli are \(6\) and \(2\), so \(C_f=12\).
At \(2\) the two depths are \(1,1\) and their directions
are independent from just the \(T_3,T_5\) columns, namely
\((0,1),(1,1)\). Equation (TD2) gives \(v_2(r_f)=1\).
At \(3\) there is one competitor of depth one, and other
primes contribute nothing. Thus \(r_f=6\), also verified by
saturation and Smith normal form of the coefficient matrix.
This example concerns a signed modular space, outside the
fixed-level Frey input family.

To reproduce the packet and coefficient vectors in PARI/GP,
the form parameters in the selected packet have linear coefficient
field polynomials.

```gp
{
  N = 442;
  space = mfinit([N,2],0);
  eigensystems = mfeigenbasis(space);
  for(i=1,#eigensystems,
    F = eigensystems[i];
    if(vector(3,j,lift(mfcoef(F,[2,13,17][j]))) == [1,-1,-1],
      print([poldegree(mfparams(F)[4]),mfcoefs(F,15)[2..16]]);
    );
  );
}
```
