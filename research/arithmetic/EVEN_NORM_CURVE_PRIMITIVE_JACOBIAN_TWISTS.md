# Positive rank on the successive factors of an even norm Jacobian

The independent prime-ratio marks on the even norm
curve give infinite-order points at every
\(3\)-power level. A dyadic torsion calculation
makes the distinction between a point coming from
a lower level and a genuinely new point exact.
Each level gives two simple abelian varieties
whose twists have the same conductor.

Use the fixed coefficient primes of
[TP5–TP6](TWO_MOVING_PRIME_CLASSES_FROM_EVEN_NORM_CURVES.md),
with \(m=3^n,\ A=a^m=(p-q)/4\), and \(B=-(p+q)/2\).
For \(0\le k\le n\) put \(r_k=3^k\) and
\[
        C_k:R^2=A^2u^{2r_k}+Bu^{r_k}+1,\qquad
        J_k=\operatorname{Jac}(C_k).
\]
Here \(J_0=0\). For \(k\ge1\), write
\(\pi_k:C_k\to C_{k-1}\) for \((u,R)\mapsto(u^3,R)\).
With \(c_k=a^{m/r_k}\), normalize \(w=c_k u\).
The reciprocal involution \(\eta_k\) sends
\((w,R)\) to \((w^{-1},R/w^{r_k})\). It induces
compatible involutions on the Jacobians and on
the successive quotient
\[
                    N_k=J_k/\pi_k^*J_{k-1}.
\]
Let \(B_{k,+}\) and \(B_{k,-}\) be the images of
\(1+\eta_k\) and \(1-\eta_k\) on \(N_k\).
A quadratic twist of a Jacobian here uses its
hyperelliptic involution, which acts as negation.

## The two simple factors at each level

**Theorem ENJ1.** Each \(B_{k,\pm}\) is simple over
\(\mathbb Q\), of dimension \(d_k=3^{k-1}\).
The dyadic inertia characteristic polynomial on
its rational \(3\)-adic Tate module is
\(\Phi_{3^k}(t)\) for a tame generator.
Every dyadic inertia-fixed three-primary torsion
point of \(N_k\) is rational. This fixed group is
\(C_3^2\), the image of the two boundary directions
of \(J_k\). If a quadratic twist \(D\) is inert
at two, then \(N_k^{(D)}(\mathbb Q_2)[3]=0\).

First, \(\pi_k^*:J_{k-1}\to J_k\) is injective.
Its kernel is killed by three by the norm
identity. A nonzero order-three line bundle in
the kernel would give an étale cyclic cover of
degree three over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\)
through which \(\pi_k\) factors. To see the
factorization, pull back the associated cubic
torsor to \(C_k\). Its underlying line bundle
is trivial, so over the algebraically closed
constant field the pulled-back torsor is
trivial. Both covers would have the same degree,
but \(\pi_k\) ramifies at the four boundary
points. This gives the required contradiction.

The reciprocal quotients can be seen in
\(s=w+w^{-1}\) and
\(v_\pm=R(w\pm1)/w^{(r_k+1)/2}\). They have
equations
\[
       v_\pm^2=(s\pm2)\bigl(D_{r_k}(s)+B/A\bigr),
       \qquad
       D_r(w+w^{-1})=w^r+w^{-r}.
\]
The \(r_k\) roots of the Dickson factor are
distinct since the \(2r_k\) inverse-paired roots
upstairs are distinct and avoid \(w=\pm1\).
The extra root \(s=\mp2\) is distinct as well.
Both quotient genera are \((r_k-1)/2\).
Norm and pullback give the two eigenspaces of
\(\eta_k\) on the rational Tate module.
After subtracting the preceding level, both
dimensions are \(d_k=(r_k-r_{k-1})/2\).

Use the compact-type dyadic model of
[TP4](TWO_MOVING_PRIME_CLASSES_FROM_EVEN_NORM_CURVES.md).
The hypothesis on \(v_2(A)\) holds at every level
since \(r_k\mid v_2(A)\). Each inertia generator
acts on a component \(U^{r_k}=x(1-x)\) by a
primitive \(r_k\)-th root on \(U\). On its
Jacobian, the subgroup generated by the \(3^j\)-th
power of the generator has fixed Tate space of
dimension \(3^j-1\) for \(0\le j\le k\).
Indeed the quotient curve is
\(V^{3^j}=x(1-x)\), and pullback and norm
identify its rational Tate module with the
invariants. The two components give dimensions
\(2(3^j-1)\) on \(J_k\). Since every
\(\Phi_{3^j}\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q_3\),
the characteristic polynomial for the
successive quotient is
\(\Phi_{3^k}(t)^2\). Each of the two factors of
dimension \(d_k\) must have one copy. Any
positive-dimensional rational subvariety of
either factor would have a Tate module of
dimension at least \(\deg\Phi_{3^k}=2d_k\).
This proves simplicity.

On the three-primary torsion the fixed subgroup
of this generator on \(N_k\) has order
\(\Phi_{3^k}(1)^2=9\). This follows by taking
the index of \(1-\tau\) on its \(3\)-adic Tate
lattice, where \(\tau\) is the generator, and
using the torsion module obtained by inverting
three in the lattice and then taking the
quotient by the lattice. On \(J_k\), the
dyadic inertia-fixed group has order \(r_k^2\)
by the component argument of TP4. Its two
rational boundary directions already have
that order. The intersection with the
embedded preceding Jacobian is its full
inertia-fixed group of order \(r_{k-1}^2\).
Thus the image has order nine and equals
the group on \(N_k\). In fact each direction
has order three in the quotient since
pulling back the boundary divisors makes
the boundary classes triple. Frobenius acts
trivially on this group. On an inert
unramified quadratic twist, inertia stays
the same and Frobenius acts as negation.
This gives zero dyadic rational three-torsion.

## Both marked points have infinite order in the new factors

**Theorem ENJ2.** For either real sign of the
genuine TP6 ratios with \(2\) inert, every
\(B_{k,+}^{(D)}\) and \(B_{k,-}^{(D)}\) has
positive rank over \(\mathbb Q\) on the same
quadratic discriminants \(D\). In particular
\(J_n^{(D)}\) has rank at least \(2n\).
The number of such \(D\) of either sign and
\(|D|\le X\) is \(\gg_m X^{1/m}/(\log X)^{C_m}\).
The higher mirror quotient and the two actual
moving prime classes are those of TP6.

Let \(u_0=P/Q\) be the genuine ratio and let
\(P_k=(u_0^{m/r_k},R)\in C_k(K)\), where
\(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\) and \(R^2=F(u_0)\)
at level \(n\). Conjugation takes \(P_k\)
to \(\iota P_k\), where \(\iota\) is the
hyperelliptic involution. Thus
\(\delta_k=[P_k-\iota P_k]\) represents a
rational point of \(J_k^{(D)}\).

For \(f_{\pm,k}=Au^{r_k}\pm1+R\), its divisor
is \(r_k\) times a boundary divisor.
Evaluation on a degree-zero divisor away
from its support defines a homomorphism
\(J_k(K)\to K^\times/K^{\times3}\) by
Weil reciprocity. The curve has rational
boundary points, so every \(K\)-rational
divisor class admits a \(K\)-rational
representative, which may be moved away
from this support. Under \(\pi_k\), the
function is the pullback of \(f_{\pm,k-1}\).
Evaluating on a pulled-back divisor
therefore gives a cube, even at \(k=1\)
where the preceding Jacobian is zero.

At \(P_k\) the two evaluations of \(\delta_k\)
are precisely the original \(\gamma_+\) and
\(\gamma_-\). The reciprocal map fixes
\(\gamma_+\) and inverts \(\gamma_-\):
it carries \(f_{+,k}\) to
\(f_{+,k}/(Au^{r_k})\), and \(f_{-,k}\)
to \(-\bar f_{-,k}/(Au^{r_k})\).
Consequently the plus evaluation of
\((1+\eta_k)\delta_k\) and the minus evaluation
of \((1-\eta_k)\delta_k\) are \(\gamma_+^2\)
and \(\gamma_-^2\), respectively. Both
are noncubes by the basis prescription at
the two moving primes.

If either of the two images in \(N_k^{(D)}\)
were torsion, it would have order prime to
three by ENJ1. Multiplying by that order
would take the corresponding divisor class
into the embedded preceding Jacobian over
\(K\). The same multiple of its noncube
evaluation would then be a cube, a
contradiction. The images are therefore
infinite-order points on the claimed
factors. Rank adds in the rational isogeny
decompositions at the successive levels.
The fixed-coefficient prime-ratio count in
TP6 gives the claimed range on both signs.

## Exact common conductors

**Theorem ENJ3.** For the two factors at each
level in ENJ1, write \(d_k=3^{k-1}\).
The two underlying conductors are
\[
 N(B_{k,\pm})=
       2^{2d_k}3^{(2k-1)d_k+1}(pq)^{d_k}.
\]
For the genuine inert dyadic slices of ENJ2
they become
\[
 N(B_{k,\pm}^{(D)})=
       2^{2d_k}3^{(2k-1)d_k+1}|D|^{2d_k}.
 \tag{1}
\]
For each fixed \(m,k\) and either sign, the
number of these attained common conductors
up to \(Z\) is
\(\gg_m Z^{1/(2m d_k)}/(\log Z)^{C_m}\).
At \(k=1\) the formula is \(36D^2\).

At two the whole \(J_k\) has tame conductor
exponent \(2(r_k-1)\) by TP4 since inertia has
no fixed Tate subspace. At \(p,q\) the smooth
projective model has two rational components
meeting at \(r_k\) ordinary nodes over the
geometric residue field. In fact at either
prime \(\ell=p,q\) the equation is
\[
            R^2=(Au^{r_k}\pm1)^2-\ell u^{r_k},
\]
and the \(r_k\) distinct roots of the
binomial are units. The two factors of
the difference of squares at each node
have product \(-\ell\) times a unit, giving
a regular semistable chart. The dual graph
has first Betti number \(r_k-1\), and the
reduction is totally toric. The conductor
exponent at each of \(p,q\) is \(r_k-1\).

Away from \(2,3,p,q\) and the primes dividing
\(A\), the branch polynomial has distinct
residue roots and yields good reduction.
At an odd \(\ell\mid A\), \(\ell\ne3\), the
quadratic polynomial in \(u^{r_k}\) has a
unit root \(\alpha\) and the root
\(\beta=1/(A^2\alpha)\). The branch roots
lie in an unramified extension since
\(\ell\nmid r_k\) and \(A=c_k^{r_k}\).
There are two proper clusters of size
\(r_k\), with depths \(0\) and
\(-2v_\ell(c_k)\), and no nested proper
subclusters. The leading-coefficient
valuation is even, as are every root
distance appearing in \(\nu_{\mathfrak s}\).
The good-reduction criterion for the
Jacobian over odd residue characteristic
therefore gives good reduction here.
[Dokchitser–Dokchitser–Maistret–Morgan, Theorem 10.3](sources/dokchitser-dokchitser-maistret-morgan-local-hyperelliptic.pdf).

At three the unit root satisfies
\(\alpha\equiv1\bmod3^{2n+2}\), since
\(B\equiv-1\bmod3^{2n+2}\) and \(A\) has
higher valuation. Both root scales
\(\alpha^{1/r_k}\) and
\(\beta^{1/r_k}=c_k^{-2}/\alpha^{1/r_k}\)
can be chosen rational over \(\mathbb Q_3\).
All branch roots are in the cyclotomic
field \(L=\mathbb Q_3(\zeta_{r_k})\), with
two blocks of size \(r_k\) given by these
scales times \(\mu_{r_k}\). Inside either
block, the positive-genus proper clusters
are cosets of the subgroups of sizes
\(3^j,\ 1\le j\le k\). Each such cluster has
three odd children. In the normalized
valuation of \(L\), the depths in the first
block are \(3^{k-j}\), and in the second
these depths have the common even shift
\(-2v_L(c_k)\). Distances across blocks
have valuation \(-2v_L(c_k)\). Thus for
any principal cluster \(\mathfrak s\),
the sum \(\nu_{\mathfrak s}\) of the
leading-coefficient valuation and the
depths of the joins with the roots is odd:
the \(r_k\) terms within its block are odd
and the other terms are even. The top
cluster is nonprincipal with two odd
children.

After a further ramified quadratic
extension of \(L\) the cluster
semistability criterion holds. One may
include an unramified quadratic extension
first to make the final extension Galois
over \(\mathbb Q_3\): the conjugate ratios
of a uniformizer of \(L\) are then squares
by Hensel's lemma. The special fiber is a
tree with an elliptic component for every
principal cluster. The nonidentity
inertia element of the last quadratic
extension fixes the centers and the
scaled horizontal coordinates. It
negates the scaled ordinate on each
component, because the ordinate is
divided by the square root of a
uniformizer of \(L\) to the odd power
\(\nu_{\mathfrak s}\). It acts as
negation on the whole Tate module, so
the tame term of the conductor is
\(2(r_k-1)\).
[Dokchitser–Dokchitser–Maistret–Morgan, Theorems 8.5 and 10.1](sources/dokchitser-dokchitser-maistret-morgan-local-hyperelliptic.pdf).

The wild term is a sum over the root
orbits, by their Theorem 11.3. In
each block, the orbit at level \(j\)
has cyclotomic root field of degree
\(2\cdot3^{j-1}\), discriminant exponent
\((2j-1)3^{j-1}\) and residue degree one.
The singleton orbit contributes zero.
The sum over the two blocks is
\[
              2\sum_{j=1}^k
                 \bigl((2j-3)3^{j-1}+1\bigr)
               =2\bigl((k-2)3^k+k+2\bigr).
\]
The total exponent at three is
\(2((k-1)3^k+k+1)\).

The plus and minus Tate representations
have equal conductor exponents at every
prime. Away from three this follows from
the map \(\rho-\rho^{-1}\), where
\(\rho(w)=\zeta_{r_k} w\). It interchanges
the two eigenspaces of \(\eta_k\). It is
invertible on the rational Tate module:
the only possible zero eigenvalue comes
from the order-one eigenvalue of \(\rho\),
and the quotient by \(\rho\) has genus
zero. The map is defined over the
cyclotomic field, unramified at the
primes in question. At three the two
blocks give two invariant direct summands
of the component Jacobian. The reciprocal
map exchanges the blocks and commutes
with inertia, so each of its two
eigenspaces is isomorphic as an inertia
representation to one block. The
conductor exponents at level \(k\) for
the two full eigenspaces are therefore
half the exponents for \(J_k\). Subtract
the corresponding exponents at \(k-1\),
taking zero at level zero. This gives
the first formula of ENJ3.

On the twisted slices the local quadratic
character is unramified at two and
trivial at three. At other primes dividing
\(D\), twisting a good or totally toric
representation by a ramified quadratic
character in odd residue characteristic
removes all inertia invariants with no
wild term. At \(p,q\) every \(D\) in the
slice ramifies, so the two original
toric exponents are replaced by twice
the dimensions. This proves (1).
The lower count follows by substituting
the conductor bound for the discriminant
bound in ENJ2. All constants are
for fixed \(m\) and the corresponding
fixed coefficient pair.

The two factors in ENJ1 are separately
simple. The positive-rank and conductor
claims do not require them to be
nonisogenous over \(\mathbb Q\) for all
\(k>1\). For the level-two coefficient
fixture, their reductions at \(17\) have
primitive Frobenius traces \(3,-3\), which
proves nonisogeny for that pair and for
its simultaneous twists. The
[exact checker](verify_even_norm_curve_primitive_jacobian_twists.py)
replays the quotient and prime residue
identities, cyclotomic contributions and
the two Frobenius traces.

## Simultaneous rational nonisogeny at every level

**Theorem ENJ4.** For every fixed \(m=3^n\) one
can choose \(a=2\cdot3^{2n+3}\) and two fixed
coprime squarefree integers \(p>q>1\) with
\(p-q=4a^m\) and \(p,q\equiv1-3^{2n+2}\bmod
3^{2n+3}\), such that the two factors at every
level \(k\) are nonisogenous over \(\mathbb Q\).
The conclusions of ENJ1–ENJ3 and TP6 apply to
these coefficients on the same genuine ratios.
In TP3 the two fixed ideal classes are those of
the products of the ramified primes over
\(p\) and over \(q\). In particular the twisted
Jacobian is isogenous to a product of \(2n\)
pairwise nonisogenous simple factors over
\(\mathbb Q\), each of positive rank, with the
common conductor formula (1) within each level.

Fix \(r=3^k\) and a rational prime
\(\ell>4r+3\) with \(\ell\equiv-1\bmod r\).
For \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_\ell\setminus\{\pm2\}\)
write \(t^+_j(\lambda),t^-_j(\lambda)\) for the
Frobenius traces of the two full reciprocal
quotients with exponent \(j=r\) or \(r/3\).
When \(j=1\) the resulting curves have genus
zero and trace zero. Write \(\chi\) for the
quadratic character of \(\mathbb F_\ell\)
extended by zero. The difference of the two
trace formulas is
\[
 t^+_j(\lambda)-t^-_j(\lambda)=
 \sum_{s\in\mathbb F_\ell}
   \bigl(\chi(s-2)-\chi(s+2)\bigr)
               \chi(D_j(s)+\lambda).
 \tag{2}
\]
Use the right hand side also at \(\lambda=\pm2\).

Let \(U\) be the norm-one subgroup of
\(\mathbb F_{\ell^2}^\times\), with quadratic
character \(\psi\). If \(s=w+w^{-1}\) with
\(w\in\mathbb F_\ell^\times\) and \(w\ne\pm1\),
the difference of characters in (2) is zero.
For \(w\in U\setminus\{\pm1\}\), choose
\(t^2=w\) in \(\mathbb F_{\ell^2}\).
Then \(t^\ell=\psi(w)t^{-1}\), so
\(s+2=(t+t^{-1})^2\) has quadratic character
\(\psi(w)\). The two characters at \(s\pm2\)
are opposite since \(s^2-4\) is nonsquare.
Each such \(s\) has the two inverse preimages
in \(U\). The endpoints \(s=\pm2\) contribute
\(-\psi(1),-\psi(-1)\) in the character
difference. Hence (2) is
\[
             -j\sum_{v\in U^j}
                         \psi(v)\chi(v+v^{-1}+\lambda),
 \tag{3}
\]
using \(w\mapsto w^j\), whose kernel has
odd order \(j\).

Subtract (3) for \(j=r/3\) from the formula
for \(j=r\). The resulting primitive trace
difference is \(H(\lambda)=\sum_s
 b(s)\chi(s+\lambda)\), where
\[
 b(s)=-r\!\!\sum_{\substack{v\in U^r\\v+v^{-1}=s}}\!\!\psi(v)
       +\frac r3\!\!\sum_{\substack{v\in U^{r/3}\\v+v^{-1}=s}}\!\!\psi(v).
\]
The coefficients sum to zero since \(\psi\)
is nontrivial on both odd-index subgroups.
Orthogonality gives
\(\ell b(s)=\sum_\lambda H(\lambda)\chi(s+\lambda)\).
If \(H\) were supported on \(\lambda=\pm2\),
a split \(s\ne\pm2\) would force
\(H(2)+H(-2)=0\), because \(b(s)=0\) and
the two character values are equal and
nonzero. Then \(b(s)\) would be proportional
to \(\chi(s+2)-\chi(s-2)\).
For interior points with \(v\in U^r\) and
with \(v\in U^{r/3}\setminus U^r\), the
ratios to that character difference are
\(-2r/3\) and \(r/3\), respectively. Both
types exist since \((\ell+1)/r>4\).
The proportionality is impossible. Thus
some good \(\lambda\) gives distinct
primitive traces at \(\ell\).

Choose a different such prime \(\ell_k\)
and a good \(\lambda_k\) for each level.
With \(A=a^m\) as in the statement, impose
\[
       q\equiv-A(\lambda_k+2)\pmod{\ell_k},
                   \qquad p=q+4A .
\]
This gives \(B/A\equiv\lambda_k\) at \(\ell_k\)
and ensures neither coefficient is zero
there. Impose also \(q\) odd and the
specified \(3\)-adic progression. The
Chinese remainder theorem supplies one
progression of \(q\). Both \(q\) and \(q+4A\)
are coprime to its modulus. For every
other prime \(\ell\), at most two classes
modulo \(\ell^2\) destroy squarefreeness.
The product of the allowed local
proportions is positive. To justify the
density, first impose the exclusions at
primes up to a fixed \(Y\) by the Chinese
remainder theorem and then bound the
remaining exclusions by
\(O(X/Y+\sqrt{X+4A})\) for \(q\le X\).
Take \(Y\) large with the progression
fixed. This gives a positive-density set
of squarefree pairs in the progression.
Their gcd is one since any common prime
divides \(4A\), which is supported at two
and three. Fix any such pair with both
coefficients larger than one. The
distinct good-prime traces prove
nonisogeny, which is preserved by
simultaneous twisting. The dimensions
distinguish factors at different levels.

The coefficient arguments of TP5 extend
to these squarefree coefficients as
follows. At every prime over \(p\) or
\(q\) impose the same respective residue
\(u\equiv-1/a\) or \(1/a\). These primes
ramify with valuation one by the same
norm identities. For the two Kummer
frames, use a prime dividing \(p\) and
a prime dividing \(q\); the valuation
matrices remain unimodular. The rest of
the local, Chebotarev and field-count
arguments of TP1–TP2 are unchanged.
In (6) of TP3 the fixed ramified ideal
products have squarefree norms \(p,q\)
and are independent by the same imaginary
norm bound when \(|D|>4pq\).
The branch irreducibility proof of TP5
uses that \(q\) has no nontrivial square
factor in \((T+c)^2(T-2c)\).
The local model and conductor calculation
at \(p,q\) in TP5 and ENJ3 is applied to
each distinct prime dividing either
coefficient. All local conditions in
TP6 at two and three and the vanishing
at the ramified odd places stay the
same. ENJ1–ENJ3 then apply with these
fixed coefficients and the same genuine
moving prime prescription.

The reciprocal quotient construction is
classical. Tautz–Top–Verberkmoes give the
odd-degree quotient formula in §2.2 and
use its cyclic correspondences for real
multiplication. The finite-field
trace separation here supplies simultaneous
rational nonisogeny in the squarefree
arithmetic slice.
[Tautz–Top–Verberkmoes, §§2.2 and 3.1](https://research.rug.nl/files/1054394337/explicit-hyperelliptic-curves-with-real-multiplication-and-permutation-polynomials.pdf).
