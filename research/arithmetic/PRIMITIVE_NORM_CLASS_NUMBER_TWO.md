# Primitive x²+5y² sums at the logarithmic counting scale

Put
\[
 P=\{x^2+5y^2>0:(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2,\ \gcd(x,y)=1\},
 \qquad R_P(N)=\#\{(a,b)\in P^2:a+b=N\}.
\]
Pairs are ordered and values are counted without coordinate multiplicity.
Define
\[
 J_{-20}(N)=\prod_{\substack{p\mid N\\p\equiv11,13,17,19\ (20)}}
                                      \frac{p-1}{p-2}.
\]

**Theorem CN2.** There are constants \(c_1,c_2>0\) and \(N_0\) such
that every \(N\ge N_0\) with \(N\bmod8\in\{2,3,4,6,7\}\) satisfies
\[
 c_1\frac{N J_{-20}(N)}{\log N}
       \le R_P(N)\le c_2\frac{N J_{-20}(N)}{\log N}.        \tag{CN2.1}
\]
For all other residue classes the count is exactly zero. Both inequalities
are uniform in every target-prime valuation, including those at 2 and 5.
No numerical \(N_0\), leading constant or complete global exception list
is supplied.

**Proposition CN2Finite.** For every \(2\le N\le2^{24}=16777216\),
\[
 N\in P+P\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 N\bmod8\in\{2,3,4,6,7\}\ \text{ and }\ N\notin\{3,4\}.  \tag{CN2.2}
\]
The numbers 3 and 4 are actual exceptions: the only primitive value at
most 4 is 1. Completeness of this exception list is asserted only through
the stated bound. The unrestricted equality with the same two exceptions
remains open here.

The arithmetic class-number-two criterion below is classical. The new
counting deduction is a finite-period extension of the existing asymmetric
proof: its stated coefficient hypotheses do not literally include 2 or 5,
nor do they state the genus filters used here. We supply the filter,
normalization and restoration argument explicitly. It uses the same
analytic estimates, without a class-field or nonabelian distribution
input. This is internal mathematical review and exact replay, without
formal verification, external review or an established priority claim.

## The class constraint is a genus character

Let \(\chi=\chi_{-20}\), \(\psi=\chi_{-4}\), and let \(C\) contain 1
and integers supported on primes \(1,3,7,9\pmod{20}\). Define
\(S_\alpha=\{m\in C:\psi(m)=\alpha\}\), \(\alpha=\pm1\). Then
\[
                  P=S_+\ \dot\cup\ 5S_+
                       \ \dot\cup\ 2S_-\ \dot\cup\ 10S_-.
                                                               \tag{CN2.3}
\]
The four parts are disjoint by their valuations at 2 and 5.

For clarity, the complete class argument is PL4 of the
[local arithmetic source](PRIMITIVE_NORM_LOCAL_STRUCTURE.md). Minkowski's
bound gives ideal class group \(C_2\), generated by the nonprincipal
ramified prime above 2. The prime above 5 is principal. For split odd
primes, the principal class is \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), the other class is
\(p\equiv3\pmod4\). Since inverse classes coincide, orienting a split
prime cannot change its ideal class. A primitive norm therefore has no
inert factor, has valuations at most one at 2 and 5, and its class product
is principal exactly when its odd core has \(\psi=+1\) without a factor
2, and \(\psi=-1\) with a factor 2. This proves (CN2.3).

This does not restrict each split prime to the principal class. For
example, \(3\) is split but not a principal norm, whereas
\(6=1^2+5\cdot1^2\) is primitive. Also multiplication by an arbitrary
member of \(C\) need not preserve \(P\): \(1\in P\) but \(3\notin P\).
The genus signs must travel with every cofactor in the counting proof.

For \(L=40\), write
\[
 H=\ker\chi=\{1,3,7,9,21,23,27,29\}\subset(\mathbb Z/40)^*,
\]
\[
 A_+=\{1,9,21,29\},\qquad A_-=\{3,7,23,27\}.
\]
The primitive residue set modulo 40 is
\(\{1,5,6,9,14,21,25,29,30\}\). Its sums give exactly
\(N\bmod8\in\{2,3,4,6,7\}\). The local analysis proves sufficiency
over every \(\mathbb Z_p\) as well: there is no additional obstruction
at 5 or any good prime. The finite table below gives explicit positive
counting strata, including targets with arbitrarily large 5-adic valuation.

## A fixed-period extension of the weighted theorem

Use \(V(n)=\tau(n)\mathbf1_C(n)\) and normalized shifts
\[
 F_B(m)=V(Bm)/V(B),\qquad G_D(l)=V(Dl)/V(D).
\]
For fixed \(t\in\{1,2,5,10\}\), put \(\ell=tL\) and
\(H_\ell=\ker\chi\subset(\mathbb Z/\ell)^*\). Inflation deletes no
new prime, and \(|H_\ell|=t|H|\).

**Lemma CN2Filter.** There are fixed \(\eta,\theta>0\), with \(\theta\)
chosen after and sufficiently smaller than \(\eta\), such that uniformly
for \(B,D,u,v\in C\), \(\max(B,D,u,v)\le N^\theta\), both genus signs,
all sufficiently large targets and all coefficient gcds,
\[
 \sum_{\substack{um+tvl=N\\\psi(m)=\alpha,\ \psi(l)=\beta}}
                F_B(m)G_D(l)
 =\frac{N}{utv}\,\Gamma_{t;u,v;\alpha,\beta}(N)
       \rho_{F_B}\rho_{G_D}
       \prod_{p\nmid L}\frac{D_p}{M_{A,p}M_{B,p}}
                  +O(N^{1-\eta}).                         \tag{CN2.4}
\]
The summation variables are positive. Radial factors and residues have
exactly the definitions (H3)--(H4) of the
[subgroup theorem](ASYMMETRIC_SUBGROUP_SPLITTING.md), with local line
\(ux+tvy=N\). The fixed factor is the actual finite count
\[
 \Gamma_{t;u,v;\alpha,\beta}(N)
 =\frac{\ell}{|H_\ell|^2}
  \#\{(a,b)\in H_\ell^2:ua+tvb=N\bmod\ell,
                         \psi(a)=\alpha,\psi(b)=\beta\}.    \tag{CN2.5}
\]
If this factor is zero, the count is exactly zero. A positive factor
forces \((N,t)=1\). Other coefficient/good-prime obstructions are
retained in \(D_p\), rather than presumed positive. Exact divisibility
and valuation predicates are included after decreasing the family exponent.

**Proof.** This is a transfer of the proof (H9)--(H18), with the following
explicit changes. Work first in the coefficient/target-unit core reached
by the gcd substitutions described below. On the original first variable,
insert the filter
\[
 w(m)=\mathbf1_{\psi(m)=\alpha}\,
       \mathbf1_{t\mid N-um}\,
       \mathbf1_{(z,L)=1,\ \chi(z)=1,\ \psi(z)=\psi(v)\beta},
               \qquad z=(N-um)/t.                         \tag{CN2.6}
\]
It is periodic modulo \(tL\): changing \(m\) by \(tL\) changes
\(z\) by \(-uL\). It is precisely the fixed-place condition on \(l\),
since \(z=vl\). No inverse of \(t\) modulo \(L\) is used.

Expand \(w\) into the finitely many Dirichlet characters \(\xi\bmod\ell\)
on the first unit variable. Each Fourier coefficient has modulus at most
one. If \(\xi\in H_\ell^\perp=\{1,\chi\}\), then
\(F_B\xi=F_B\) pointwise. Otherwise both first character factors
\(\xi,\xi\chi\) are nonprincipal, and the fixed-twist bounds (H9)
apply at modulus \(\ell\). The shifted correction kernels keep their
same absolute norm and divisor bounds. Kernel indices need only be
\(\ell\)-units with \(\chi=1\); they need not belong to \(C\), since
inert prime squares occur. Every smooth-part phase \(\xi(jc_0)\) is
retained; neither \(c_0\in C\) nor a trivial phase is assumed.

The filter ensures \(\chi(l)=1\). The second kernel index \(k\) also
has \(\chi(k)=1\), so the original exact symmetric divisor pairing of
\(l/k\) remains valid. Its divisibility congruence is
\[
 um\equiv N\pmod{t v k a}.
\]
The filter already enforces its \(t\)-part; \((vka,tL)=1\).
Consequently the varying congruence is exactly
\[
                   um\equiv N\pmod{vka}.                  \tag{CN2.7}
\]
Only the fixed periodic filter carries the bad-prime coefficient.
The true hyperbola boundary is \(um+t v k a^2=N\).

Thus the existing
[FixedPeriodBoxesGeneral](ASYMMETRIC_FIXED_MODULUS_THEORY.md) is used
at period \(\ell\), with good modulus \(vka\). In the medium-variable
case BGLM has the same parameters
\(a_1=N,a_2=ujc_0,a_3=1,d=vka_{\rm aux}\), now \(c=\ell\).
All varying and auxiliary moduli are \(\ell\)-units. The target is
not required to be an \(\ell\)-unit. The reduced fixed progression
class and all inverse multipliers meet exactly the original hypotheses.
The fixed scalar phase is independent of the varying modulus.
No bad-prime auxiliary-modulus estimate is invoked.

All kernel truncations, gcd and small-summand tails, smooth-part losses,
box boundaries and nonprincipal models have the same exponents as
(H17)--(H18). The coefficient \(t\) only changes fixed real endpoints;
\(\ell\le400\), the signs and all residue-dependent Fourier coefficients
form finite bounded families. The same parameter order and a smaller
positive \(\eta,\theta\) work simultaneously. This verifies the analytic
error claim using precisely the retained estimates.

The principal factor requires its own normalization. Put
\(\mu=|H_\ell|^{-1}\sum_{a\in H_\ell}w(a)\). Orthogonality gives
\(\sum_{\xi\in H_\ell^\perp}\widehat w(\xi)=\mu\), as in (H13).
For every surviving \(a\), the equation in (CN2.5) has exactly \(t\)
choices of \(b\bmod\ell\), all lifting the same residue modulo \(L\).
Hence
\[
 \Gamma=\ell t\mu/|H_\ell|=2tL\mu/\varphi(L).             \tag{CN2.8}
\]
Because (CN2.7) has no factor \(t\), divisor completion first gives
real factor \(N/(uv)\) and raw fixed-g coefficient
\(2(\varphi(L)/L)\mu\), exactly as in (H18). Reassembling the
principal first sequence uses \(F_B\xi=F_B\) pointwise, not any
unsupported assertion about individual expanded kernel terms. Since
\(\varphi(\ell)/\ell=\varphi(L)/L\), (H10) converts this to
\[
 \frac{N}{uv}\frac{2L\mu}{\varphi(L)}\rho_{F_B}\rho_{G_D}
 =\frac{N}{utv}\Gamma\rho_{F_B}\rho_{G_D},
\]
with the unchanged good-prime radial product. At good primes \(t\) is
an invertible multiplier and can be absorbed into the second local
variable without changing its valuation. This proves the main.

For completeness, the exact reductions preserve the genus filters:

* Divide \(r=(u,v)\) out of coefficients and target when \(r\mid N\).
  It is a unit modulo \(\ell\), so (CN2.5) is unchanged; otherwise the
  original count is zero.
* For coprime coefficients put \(d=(u,N)\), \(e=(v,N)\). In a
  nonzero stratum \((N,t)=1\), and the equation forces \(e\mid m\),
  \(d\mid l\). Substituting \(m=em'\), \(l=dl'\) gives shifts
  \(Be,Dd\), coefficients \(u/d,t(v/e)\), target \(N/(de)\),
  exact prefactor \(F_B(e)G_D(d)\), and signs
  \(\alpha\psi(e),\beta\psi(d)\).
* In the target-unit core, \(g=(m,N)\) also divides \(l\).
  Division gives shifts \(Bg,Dg\), target \(N/g\), prefactor
  \(F_B(g)G_D(g)\), and signs \(\alpha\psi(g),\beta\psi(g)\).

All removed divisors are actual members of \(C\), so are units at
\(\ell\). Multiplication of the finite variables by \(e,d\), or by
\(g,g\), bijects their transformed pairs with the original pairs.
Scaling the corresponding equation by these units proves exact
invariance of \(\Gamma\). Divisor-bounded prefactors preserve a
smaller saving, as in (H18). More general small divisibility predicates
\(m=bx,l=cy\) carry signs \(\alpha\psi(b),\beta\psi(c)\), shifts
\(Bb,Dc\), and coefficients \(ub,tvc\); the same bijection applies.
Finite differences give exact valuations within the smaller power budget.
This proves the lemma and its predicate interface. \(\square\)

## Unweighting and the full local target set

For \(u=v=1\) and positive \(\Gamma\), the same positive finite-block
sieve and restoration in section 6 of the subgroup theorem give
\[
 \#\{m+t l=N:m,l\in C,\psi(m)=\alpha,\psi(l)=\beta\}
                       \asymp N J_{-20}(N)/\log N.         \tag{CN2.9}
\]
Here is why the fixed filter preserves that argument. At good unit-target
primes the rough weighted bad density is \(4/p+O(p^{-2})\) on split
primes and zero on inert primes. Split-prime density is \(1/2\), so the
rough dimension is two. The normalized first- and second-side restoration
directives each have ratio \(a_p=(p-1)/(p(p-2))\) on the split class.
They restore exponent \(2(1/2)=1\). At split target primes the old
common-cofactor compensation applies; at inert target primes the factor
\((p-1)/(p-2)\) remains. This gives the logarithmic exponent one.

The indispensable finite factor is preserved at every step. If the
actual values are \(du x,dv y\), residual signs are
\(\alpha\psi(du),\beta\psi(dv)\), on the line
\(ux+tvy=N/d\). Multiplication by \(du,dv\) bijects its finite
solutions with the original ones. Thus the same positive \(\Gamma\)
factors out of every directive, divisibility and common-cofactor main.
Primes with \(\psi(p)=-1\) are not discarded; discarding them would
incorrectly reduce restoration to the principal-prime density \(1/4\).
The uniform error budget and positive cofactor size bound are precisely
those of the existing section 6. Recoverable patterns are unchanged;
genus filtering neither increases residual weights nor duplicates a
recovered profile. This proves the lower bound in (CN2.9).

For the upper bound drop the genus filters. Every good inert prime
excludes two residue classes on the linear equation, merging into one
exactly when it divides the target. The same fixed-power upper block
sieve therefore has dimension one and target product \(J_{-20}\).
It also applies to any fixed line \(r m+s l=N\),
\(r,s\in\{1,2,5,10\}\), after dividing their common factor when possible:
all their prime factors divide \(L\), while all sieved primes are good.
Fixed bad-prime congruences affect only the constants.

The actual positive finite densities now complete the application. Put
\[
 q_{t;\alpha,\beta}(M)=
 \#\{b\in A_\beta:M-tb\in A_\alpha\pmod{40}\}.
\]
Counting lifts to \(tL\) in (CN2.5) gives
\(\Gamma=(5/8)q\). A sufficient choice for every admissible target is:

| Original \(N\bmod40\) | Primitive summand stratum | Reduced line and signs | \(\Gamma\) |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 2,18,22,38 | \(S_++S_+\) | \(m+l=N;++\) | 5/4 |
| 10,30 | \(S_++S_+\) | \(m+l=N;++\) | 5/2 |
| 6,14,26,34 | \(S_++5S_+\) | \(m+5l=N;++\) | 5/2 |
| 3,7,23,27 | \(S_++2S_-\) | \(m+2l=N;+-\) | 5/4 |
| 15,35 | \(S_++2S_-\) | \(m+2l=N;+-\) | 5/2 |
| 11,19,31,39 | \(S_++10S_-\) | \(m+10l=N;+-\) | 5/2 |
| 12,28 | \(2S_-+2S_-\) | \(m+l=N/2;--\) | 5/4 |
| 20 | \(2S_-+2S_-\) | \(m+l=N/2;--\) | 5/2 |
| 4,36 | \(2S_-+10S_-\) | \(m+5l=N/2;--\) | 5/2 |

In the divided rows, both lifts of \(N/2\pmod{40}\) are covered.
These 25 target classes modulo 40 are exactly the five allowed classes
modulo 8. Every target divisible by 5 uses coefficients prime to 5,
so the argument covers arbitrary 5-adic valuations. Each selected
stratum supplies the lower bound (CN2.1). For the upper bound sum the
fixed-line bounds over the sixteen disjoint ordered valuation strata
in (CN2.3). Removing fixed common factors 2 or 5 does not change
\(J_{-20}\), so these finite upper bounds have the required scale.
This proves Theorem CN2. \(\square\)

the flags by deleting all inert factors, valuations greater than one at

every index, not merely on samples. A third trial-factor class-character
implementation agrees through 10000.

An exact packed-bit union using the 672 primitive values at most 5000
covers every locally admissible target except 3 and 4. Every remaining
candidate is checked against every possible positive smaller summand,
so absence is exhaustive and no short-summand assumption is used. The
full represented mask has zero forbidden positions and exactly
10485758 represented targets among 10485760 locally admissible targets.
This proves (CN2.2). Corrupted membership and target masks are rejected.
