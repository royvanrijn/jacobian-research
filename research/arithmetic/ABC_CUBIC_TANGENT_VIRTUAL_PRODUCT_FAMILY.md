# A virtual product from two locally obstructed cubic norm classes

The coefficient-prime obstruction in the doubled cubic family
can be different for a class and for its product with the
other class. For an explicit odd coefficient pair, both
individual directions fail the virtual-unit test throughout
the family. Their product is a nonzero virtual unit on a
fixed local set of parameters. This gives infinitely many
imaginary quadratic fields carrying a genuine order-three
ideal class and three positive-rank quadratic twists.

## The product and its dyadic condition

**Theorem VP1.** In the doubled family of
[DB1](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_DOUBLED_BRANCH_FAMILY.md#classification-of-the-doubled-branch-model),
the inverse product of the two norm classes is represented by
\[
 \gamma=U+2X-1,\qquad X=\frac{w+1}{w-1},\qquad
 U=\frac{Y}{(w-1)^2}.
\]
In any nonsplit nondegenerate quadratic specialization it is a
virtual unit exactly when \(w\) is a \(2\)-adic unit.
If some odd coefficient prime \(q\) has \(3\nmid v_q(b)\)
and \(2\) is not a cube modulo \(q\), then \(\gamma\) is a
noncube in every such specialization.

The cube relation and the equation \(U^2=X^3+X^2-X\) are
[DB6](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_DOUBLED_BRANCH_FAMILY.md#the-third-elliptic-quotient).
Writing \(h=X-1=2/(w-1)\) gives
\[
 U^2=h^3+(2h+1)^2,\qquad
 \operatorname N(\gamma)=-h^3.
\]
At a place where \(h\) has negative valuation,
\(U^2\) is dominated by \(h^3\), so the valuation of
\(\gamma\) is a multiple of three. Where \(h\) is integral,
both norm conjugates are integral. If \(h\) is a unit
their norm is a unit. If \(h\) has positive valuation at
a prime of odd residue characteristic, the sum of the
conjugates is \(2(2h+1)\), a unit, so the norm makes their
valuations multiples of three. This proves the virtual-unit
test at every odd prime.

If \(v_2(w)=0\), then \(v_2(h)\le0\) and the preceding
unit and pole arguments apply also at \(2\). If \(v_2(w)\ne0\),
then \(v_2(h)>0\) and
\(U^2/(2h+1)^2=1+h^3/(2h+1)^2\) is a local square congruent
to one modulo eight. Choose the root \(U/(2h+1)\equiv1\bmod4\).
Then \(\gamma\) has valuation one at that split prime, and
its other valuation is nonzero modulo three by the norm.
This proves the exact dyadic condition.

At the odd coefficient prime in the theorem, \(v_q(w)\ne0\)
and the quadratic field is split by DB2.
If \(v_q(w)>0\), on the branch \(Y\equiv1\bmod q\)
one has \(U\equiv1,\ X\equiv-1\), hence
\(\gamma\equiv-2\bmod q\).
If \(v_q(w)<0\), use the branch \(Y/w^2\equiv1\bmod q\).
Then \(U\equiv1,\ X\equiv1\) and \(\gamma\equiv2\bmod q\).
These residues are noncubes since \(-1\) is a cube.
A unit that is a cube in the local field must have cubic
residue, which proves the noncube assertion.

## An odd inverse pair with genuine class-group torsion

**Theorem VP2.** There are at least
\[
 X^{1/6}\exp\!\left(-O\!\left(\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}\right)\right)
\]
distinct imaginary quadratic fields of absolute discriminant
at most \(X\) in the doubled genus-five family with \(b=35\).
They have both positive weighted directions for
\(k_1=-135/7,\ k_2=-240737/204800\).
Their cubic norm classes span a two-dimensional space,
with a virtual-unit line generated by the product.
This line maps injectively into the ideal class group.

To find the profiles, the rational branch parametrization of
[GT1](ABC_RECIPROCAL_GENUS_TWO_TANGENT_OBSTRUCTION.md#which-weighted-profiles-have-a-rational-branch-point)
gives the coefficient class
\[
 4k(k+1)\equiv -4(2r+1)(r-1)(r+2)
                  \pmod{\mathbb Q^{\times3}} .
\]
The identity uses
\(r^4+2r^3-2r-1=(r-1)(r+1)^3\).
The two choices \(r=1/3,16/17\) give the indicated profiles.
Their exact coefficients are \(35(24/7)^3\) and
\(1225(561/6400)^3\), respectively.
The two rational halves on \(E_{35},E_{1225}\) are
\((-10,-15)\) and \((-49,-1176)\).

Take \(s\in(-3/20,-1/8)\) with \(s\) a \(2\)-adic unit.
Then \(w=1225s^3\) is a unit at \(2\) and lies in
\((-2-\sqrt5,-1)\), so the radicand is negative.
The real abscissa argument in DB3 makes both directions
positive. At \(7\), the coefficient \(b=35\) has valuation
one and \(2\) is not a cube. Thus the product class is
a noncube and a virtual unit by VP1. Each individual class
fails the valuation test at \(7\) by DB2. These facts make
the two classes independent and their virtual-unit subspace
one-dimensional. The field has \(5\) split by DB2 and so
differs from \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\). Its units are cubes.
The exact sequence from virtual units to ideal-class
\(3\)-torsion is consequently injective.

To count fields, retain the \(2\)-adic unit condition in
the argument of
[DM2](MORDELL_CUBIC_DOUBLED_SIMULTANEOUS_TWISTS.md#counting-distinct-simultaneous-twists).
The interval still gives \(\gg H^2\) primitive rational
parameters of height at most \(H\). The fixed cubic algebra
for two-descent is now \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{-1225})\).
The height and rank bounds there apply with this fixed cubic
and the fixed curve \(E_{35}\), since only the fixed bad
primes and primes dividing \(d\) contribute extra valuation
coordinates. This gives the same uniform per-field
multiplicity \(\exp(O(\log H/\log\log H))\), and
\(|d|=O(H^{12})\). The displayed lower bound follows.

At \(s=-1/7\) the field is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-31})\).
For the choice \(\sqrt{-31}=a\), the virtual class is
represented by \((8+3a)/64\), of norm \((7/16)^3\).
Its ideal is the cube of a fractional ideal in the
nonprincipal class of one prime over \(7\).
The two actual profile parameters are
\(720/77\) and \(15421329/78438400\),
both in their positive direction cones.
The [checker](verify_cubic_tangent_virtual_product_family.py)
replays the two scaled halves, exact specialization,
valuations, and class group computation.

## Three marked twists with a class-group direction

**Corollary VP3.** The lower count of VP2 also holds with the
following properties. The negative \(d\) is prime to \(2,3,5,7\),
is split at \(2,5,7\), and is nonsplit unramified at \(3\).
The \(d\)-twists of \(E_{35},E_{1225},H_3\) all have
positive rank. Their conductors are \(44100d^2,44100d^2,20d^2\),
and their finite \(3\)-Selmer dimensions are even, even, and
odd, respectively.

In the same interval impose
\[
 v_2(s+1/7)\ge11,\qquad v_3(s+1/7)\ge3 .
\]
These fixed congruences retain \(\gg H^2\) primitive
rational parameters and imply the \(2\)-adic unit condition.
The radicand at \(s=-1/7\) is \(-71424/2401\), with
valuations eight and two at \(2,3\). The polynomial
for the radicand is integral at both places, so the
two congruences preserve its squareclasses there.
The classes there are those of \(-31\), giving the
stated split and unramified places. The primes \(5,7\)
split by DB2.

The positive-rank argument of DM1 uses the two marked
noncube evaluations, and applies to \(E_{35},E_{1225}\)
because \(2b\) is noncubic for both.
For \(H_3\), the point argument of
[DJ3](MORDELL_DOUBLED_JACOBIAN_COMPLEMENT.md#three-simultaneous-marked-quadratic-twists)
uses its nontrivial \(3\)-isogeny evaluation, now supplied
by VP1. The base conductors of the first two curves are
both \(44100=2^2 3^2 5^2 7^2\), as checked exactly.
At their fixed bad primes the twists are locally isomorphic
or unramified quadratic twists, which preserve the local
conductor. At every other prime dividing \(d\) the exponent
is two by the same good reduction twist computation as DM1.
This gives all three conductors.

For \(E_{35}\) and \(E_{1225}\), the formulas of
[Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman, Propositions 34, 39 and 49](sources/bhargava-elkies-shnidman-mordell-isogeny-selmer.pdf)
give local ratios \(1/3\) at \(2,5\), and one at \(3,7\)
and at infinity. At \(3\) the coefficient is a unit
congruent to \(d^3\equiv-1\bmod9\), so the exceptional
unit conditions do not apply. Away from the fixed primes,
the only relevant coefficient valuation is three at
\(q\mid d\), giving ratio one. Both global ratios are \(1/9\),
so their Selmer dimensions are even with no rational
three-torsion. For \(H_3\), the local computation from DJ3
is unchanged at \(2,5\) and at infinity. At \(3\) the twist
is unramified and both isogenous curves have good reduction,
giving ratio one. The global ratio is \(1/3\) and its
Selmer dimension is odd. The same height multiplicity
bound as in VP2 completes the count.
