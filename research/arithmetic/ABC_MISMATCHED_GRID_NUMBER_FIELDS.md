# Fixed-number-field reduction for mismatched valuation orders

Fix integers \(P,Q,R\ge2\) with \(1/P+1/Q+1/R<1\).
For squarefree \(S\ge1\), let \(\mathcal E_{P,Q,R}(S)\) consist
of primitive positive triples \(a+b=c\) with no height bound, for which
\[
  P\mid v_q(a),\qquad Q\mid v_q(b),\qquad R\mid v_q(c)
  \qquad(q\nmid S).
\]
There is a constant \(C_{P,Q,R}>0\) such that
\[
                 \#\mathcal E_{P,Q,R}(S)
                          \ll_{P,Q,R} S^{C_{P,Q,R}} .
\]
Under the discriminant multiplicity conjecture in all relevant
bounded degrees, the exponent \(C_{P,Q,R}\) can be replaced
by an arbitrary positive \(\varepsilon\), with the constant
then depending on \(\varepsilon\). This applies to arbitrary
fixed hyperbolic signatures, including pairwise coprime ones.

## The specialization fields

Choose a fixed connected regular cover \(\pi:Y\to\mathbb P^1\)
of signature \((P,Q,R)\) above \(0,1,\infty\), with genus
\(g(Y)\ge2\), over a fixed number field \(K_0\) over which
all deck transformations are defined. The existence and genus
come from Darmon–Granville Proposition 3.1. For
\(t=a/c\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0,1\}\), the field \(L_t/K_0\)
generated by its geometric fibre has degree at most
\(\deg(\pi)\). At every place above a prime \(q\nmid S\),
away from a fixed finite bad set, its intersection orders
with the branch sections are divisible by \(P,Q,R\)
respectively. Indeed \(a,b,c\) are pairwise coprime and
\((t,1-t)=(a/c,b/c)\). Ramification indices over \(q\) in
the base field only multiply the intersection orders.
Beckmann's specialization criterion as recorded in
Darmon–Granville Proposition 3.2 now gives that \(L_t\)
is unramified outside the places over \(S\) and the fixed bad set.
[Darmon–Granville, §3 and Propositions 3.1–3.2](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/darmon/pub/Articles/Research/12.Granville/pub12.pdf)

Let \(E_0/K_0\) be a fixed finite extension containing
\(J[2]\) for the Jacobian \(J\) of \(Y\), and a basepoint
of \(Y\). For each \(t\), put \(E_t=L_t E_0\).
Both \([E_t:\mathbb Q]\) and its ramification set, up to the
primes over \(S\), are bounded by fixed data. In fixed degree
the local discriminant exponents are uniformly bounded:
tame exponents are bounded by the degree, and only the
finitely many primes at most that degree can be wild,
with bounded local degree at each. Thus
\[
                       |\operatorname{disc}(E_t)|
                                        \ll S^{C_1}
\]
for a constant \(C_1\) depending on the fixed cover and
extension. A bounded-degree number field of discriminant
in this range has an integral primitive generator of
polynomial height in \(S\). For example the successive
minima of its Minkowski lattice have product bounded by a
constant times its covolume. The first minimum is bounded
away from zero by the norm of a nonzero algebraic integer,
so a full set of linearly independent integers has all
Minkowski coordinates at most a fixed power of the
discriminant. A bounded integral combination of those
integers separates the embeddings and generates the field.
Counting the resulting integer minimal polynomials proves
that the number of possible \(E_t\), as subfields of a
fixed algebraic closure, is \(O(S^{C_2})\). The number
of embeddings of any one of the fields is bounded.

## A weighted number-field estimate for the rational points

Over \(E_t\) the Kummer injection for \([2]\) lands in
\((E_t^\times/E_t^{\times2})^{2g}\).
Outside the places over \(S\), \(2\), and the fixed bad
reduction support of \(J\), the image is unramified:
\([2]\) is finite étale on the good abelian scheme, so
the inverse image of an integral point is finite étale.
The unramified classes have even valuations and their number
is bounded using the \(S\)-unit group modulo squares and
the square torsion of the class group. Consequently
\[
 \operatorname{rank}J(E_t)
       \ll_{Y,E_0} 1+\omega(S)+\log|\operatorname{Cl}(E_t)[2]|.
\]
Uniform Mordell–Lang bounds \(\#Y(E_t)\) exponentially in
this rank. Minkowski's bound on the class number makes it
\(O(S^{C_3})\) with another fixed constant. Each
\(t=a/c\) determines the primitive triple, and at least
one \(Y(E_t)\)-point lies above it. Summing over the
fields gives the stated polynomial bound. More precisely,
the reduction bounds the count by
\[
       C^{1+\omega(S)}
       \sum_{E}|\operatorname{Cl}(E)[2]|^{C'},
\]
where the sum runs over fields of bounded degree with
ramified primes among \(S\) and the fixed exceptional set.
The cover, field degree and implied constants are fixed before
the height limit.
[Gao–Ge–Kühne, Theorem 1.1′](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.15085v4)

The bounded discriminant exponents give at most
\(C_4^{1+\omega(S)}\) distinct discriminants even as \(S\)
varies. If for each fixed degree the number of number
fields of exact absolute discriminant \(D\) is
\(O_\varepsilon(D^\varepsilon)\), this gives
\(O_\varepsilon(S^\varepsilon)\) possible fields after
resizing the exponent. The same conjecture also
bounds \(|\operatorname{Cl}(E)[2]|\) by an arbitrary
small power of the discriminant. Nontrivial quadratic
unramified class extensions \(E'/E\) have
\(|\operatorname{disc}(E')|=|\operatorname{disc}(E)|^2\)
in degree \(2[E:\mathbb Q]\), and distinct such extensions
have bounded multiplicity on forgetting the embedded \(E\).
This supplies both subpower estimates in the weighted
number-field bound from a single conjecture.
[Pierce–Turnage-Butterbaugh–Wood, discriminant multiplicity and torsion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02008)

If the product of the primes with defective valuation
divisibility is at most \(X^{f\lambda}\), sum the
unconditional bound over the squarefree \(S\) up to
that limit. This gives
\(O_{P,Q,R}(X^{(1+C_{P,Q,R})f\lambda})\).
In particular it gives a strict small-budget saving
on fixed mismatched grids for a signature-dependent
small positive defect cap. Under the stated conjecture
the exponent becomes \(f\lambda+\varepsilon\).
Neither conclusion exchanges the fixed-signature
height limit with a growing-signature limit.

## A support-only bound from a single prime in the three orders

Fix a prime \(\ell\). Write \(m_i=\ell^{v_\ell(P_i)}\) for
\((P_1,P_2,P_3)=(P,Q,R)\), allowing \(m_i=1\). If
\[
                          \sum_{i=1}^3 1/m_i<1,
 \tag{MF-ell}
\]
then the height-free bound improves unconditionally to
\[
          \#\mathcal E_{P,Q,R}(S)
                    \le \exp O_\ell(1+\omega(S)).
 \tag{MF-support}
\]
The constant is uniform in the orders satisfying (MF-ell).
In particular, it applies to the separate grids
\((2,4,8)\), \((4,4,4)\) and \((3,3,9)\), and to all
coordinatewise multiples and permutations of each of these
signatures.

### An \(\ell\)-group covering with controlled inertia

Condition (MF-ell) is necessary and sufficient for a
geometrically connected Galois cover of genus at least two
with \(\ell\)-group monodromy, branch points among
\(0,1,\infty\), and the indicated upper bounds on inertia
orders in the sense of divisibility. Necessity is immediate
from Riemann–Hurwitz: its local indices \(e_i\) divide \(m_i\)
and \(2g-2=\deg(\pi)(1-\sum_i1/e_i)\).

For sufficiency sort the three \(m_i\). For \(\ell\ge5\)
the minimum permitted signature is \((\ell,\ell,\ell)\).
Use the cyclic cover \(z^\ell=t(1-t)\), whose three
indices are \(\ell,\ell,\ell\) and whose genus is
\((\ell-1)/2\ge2\).

For \(\ell=2\) the minimal signatures are \((2,4,8)\)
and \((4,4,4)\), under coordinatewise divisibility.
First take the genus-one cyclic cover \(z^4=t^2(1-t)\),
of signature \((2,4,4)\). For \((2,4,8)\), the sole
point above infinity can still ramify to order two.
Take an unramified cyclic double cover of the genus-one
curve. It has two points above that point.
For \((4,4,4)\), use the two points over zero on the
original genus-one curve, each with remaining allowed
ramification two. In either case now take a cyclic
double cover of the genus-one curve branched just at
these two points, with resulting genus two.

For \(\ell=3\) the minimum signature is \((3,3,9)\).
Start with the genus-one cyclic cover \(z^3=t(1-t)\).
Take an unramified cyclic triple cover of this genus-one
curve and choose two of the three points over the
original point above infinity. A cyclic triple cover
branched at just those two points has genus three and
uses up no more than the allowed inertia nine at infinity.

All the genus-one constructions can be made over a
fixed number field. After choosing an algebraic point
as origin, an unramified cyclic cover of degree \(\ell\)
is given by an isogeny, for example the dual of the
quotient by a cyclic subgroup of order \(\ell\).
For a cyclic cover branched at two points \(P_0,Q_0\),
choose a degree-zero divisor \(D_0\) on the genus-one
curve over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) with
\(\ell[D_0]=-[P_0-Q_0]\), using divisibility in
\(\operatorname{Pic}^0\). A rational function of divisor
\(P_0-Q_0+\ell D_0\) defines the required Kummer cover.
Its indices are \(\ell\) just at the two points.
All fields of definition here are fixed for the minimal
signature and hence for \(\ell\).

Taking the Galois closure of the tower of cyclic
\(\ell\)-steps gives an \(\ell\)-group. This follows
inductively by adjoining over the preceding Galois
closure all conjugates of the last cyclic step.
The inertia order in the normal closure is the least
common multiple of the local indices, as is seen from
the cycle lengths in the geometric monodromy action.
It still divides the permitted order at each branch.
The normal closure has genus at least two by
Riemann–Hurwitz. Enlarge the fixed base number field so
that all its deck transformations are defined there.
This completes the construction.

### Counting the specialization fields of the tower

Apply the specialization criterion above to this fixed
regular cover. The fibre field \(L_t/K_0\) is Galois with
group a subgroup of the fixed deck group. Indeed its
geometric fibre is a torsor for the constant deck group.
Thus it has \(\ell\)-power degree and ramification only
above \(S\) and fixed primes. Now choose \(E_0\) to
contain \(J[\ell]\), the \(\ell\)-th roots of unity and
a basepoint of the fixed curve. Then \(E_t=E_0L_t\) is
an \(\ell\)-extension of fixed bounded degree over \(E_0\).

Every such field can be reached by a bounded number
of cyclic degree-\(\ell\) steps. All ramification in
each step lies above \(S\) and the fixed exceptional
primes. The invariant-class bound in a cyclic step
\(F'/F\) gives
\[
       \log |\operatorname{Cl}(F')[\ell]|
            \le \ell\log|\operatorname{Cl}(F)[\ell]|
                         +O_\ell(1+\omega(S)),
\]
since the field degrees are bounded in terms of \(\ell\).
The bound iterates from the fixed class group of \(E_0\).
It also bounds the number of choices for the next
cyclic extension: over a field containing
\(\zeta_\ell\), its Kummer class has valuations divisible
by \(\ell\) outside the allowed primes. The \(S\)-unit
rank and the same class torsion give at most
\(\exp O_\ell(1+\omega(S))\) such classes at each step.
Thus this also bounds the number of possible fields
\(E_t\).
[Cyclic-step class-group estimate](ABC_PRIMITIVE_NORM_RAY_PACKING.md#prime-power-towers-remove-the-class-number-cost)

For each field, repeat the unramified Kummer argument
with \([\ell]\) instead of \([2]\). The bounds on
\(\ell\)-torsion and the number of allowed places
give \(\operatorname{rank}J(E_t)\ll_\ell 1+\omega(S)\).
Uniform Mordell–Lang on this fixed curve now gives
at most \(\exp O_\ell(1+\omega(S))\) points in each
field. Every solution determines one distinct
ratio \(a/c\), so summing over the fields proves
(MF-support).

In an anatomic box, let \(V_i(m_i)\) be the radical
mass at valuation degrees divisible by \(m_i\), in units
of \(\log X\), and \(L\) be the total radical mass in
those units. The defect support has logarithmic cap
\(L-\sum_i V_i(m_i)\). Equation (MF-support) therefore
gives the count exponent inequality
\[
                          D\le L-\sum_i V_i(m_i).
 \tag{MF-row}
\]
The same bound permits retaining just a visible subsum
in each \(V_i\). This extends the earlier two-equal-grid
row to the three prime-primary signatures above. For
example, the conditions \(2\mid v_q(a)\),
\(4\mid v_q(b)\), \(8\mid v_q(c)\) off a joint support
of radical \(X^{f\lambda}\) give \(X^{f\lambda+\varepsilon}\)
with no distinguished short height.
[Earlier two-equal-grid descent](ABC_PRIME_POWER_FERMAT_DESCENT.md#a-three-cap-coefficient-defect-inequality)

## Why a solvable cover cannot handle pairwise coprime orders

The geometric triangle group
\[
   \Delta(P,Q,R)=
   \langle u,v,w\mid u^P=v^Q=w^R=uvw=1\rangle
\]
has abelianization
\[
 (\mathbb Z/P\oplus\mathbb Z/Q\oplus\mathbb Z/R)/
                            \langle(1,1,1)\rangle .
\]
When the three orders are pairwise coprime this is trivial.
Every nontrivial quotient of the perfect triangle group
is therefore nonsolvable. In particular there is no
connected nontrivial three-point covering with solvable
geometric monodromy whose respective inertia orders divide
\(P,Q,R\). The conclusion also applies to a nonregular
cover, by passing to its Galois closure. A
prime-primary subgroup chain cannot replace the field
estimate in this three-point route.

For three distinct prime orders, such a nontrivial regular
cover must use all three full inertia orders. If one inertia
is trivial, the other two branch generators are inverse
and their common order divides two distinct primes. Hence
the covering degree is divisible by \(PQR\), and its
genus is at least \(1+\tfrac12PQR(1-1/P-1/Q-1/R)\).
A fixed-degree number-field argument necessarily loses
uniformity as the mismatched prime orders grow.

There is also a direct obstruction if one drops primitivity.
For pairwise coprime \(P,Q,R\), every rational \(0<t<1\)
is the ratio \(Ax^P/(Cz^R)\) of some positive integer
solution \(Ax^P+By^Q=Cz^R\) with fixed positive coefficients.
Choose \(u\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) such that \(ut/A\),
\(u(1-t)/B\) and \(u/C\) are respectively \(P\)-th,
\(Q\)-th and \(R\)-th powers in \(\mathbb Q\). The valuation
of \(u\) at each prime satisfies three compatible
congruences by the Chinese remainder theorem, with only
finitely many nonzero choices. Multiplication of \(u\)
by a suitable \(\operatorname{lcm}(P,Q,R)\)-th power
makes each root integral. These lifts need not have
coprime roots. For \((P,Q,R)=(5,7,11)\), \(A=B=C=1\),
and \(t=1/2\), the minimal \(2\)-adic valuation of \(u\)
giving integral roots is \(176\), which gives
\(x=2^{35},y=2^{25},z=2^{16}\). Every positive integer
lift of this ratio shares the prime \(2\).
Thus counting the weighted ratio before the primitive
local conditions would count all rational ratios
instead of the desired exceptional set.

## A counting corollary with all orders variable

Let \(\mathcal F(X,Y)\) count primitive positive triples \(a+b=c\le X\)
that admit some hyperbolic signature \((P,Q,R)\) of integers at
least two, with the product of the primes defective for this
signature at most \(Y\ge1\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\) there is
a constant \(B_\varepsilon\) such that
\[
              \mathcal F(X,Y)\ll_\varepsilon
                                 X^\varepsilon Y^{B_\varepsilon}.
\]
In particular the primitive exact-power triples of Fermat–Catalan
up to height \(X\), with all three exponents variable, have count
\(O_\varepsilon(X^\varepsilon)\). This is a counting statement,
and leaves global finiteness open. Under the discriminant
multiplicity hypothesis one can take \(B_\varepsilon=1+\varepsilon\).

Use the canonical decompositions \(a=A x^P\), \(b=B y^Q\),
\(c=C z^R\) with the residues of valuations allocated to
\(A,B,C\). Their joint squarefree support has size at most
\(Y\). One may restrict orders to \(O(\log(2X))\): on a
nonunit root the order is at most \(\log_2 X\). An order
larger than this has unit root; replace it by
\(\lceil\log_2 X\rceil+7\). This preserves the canonical
defect split and hyperbolicity. If multiple orders are
replaced, their reciprocal sum is at most \(2/7\)
in addition to the third reciprocal, which is at most
\(1/2\); if only one changes, the other two cannot both
be two in a hyperbolic signature, so their sum is
at most \(5/6\).
Fixing the orders and the joint defect support gives
\(X^{o(1)}\) coefficient choices uniformly: each prime may
be assigned one of three letters and a residue exponent
in \([1,O(\log X)]\). To make the last assertion uniformly
subpower even though the orders grow, use the bound for
\(X^{o(1)}\) polynomial-height integers on a prescribed
polynomial-height support, from the Rankin argument.
The potential support is a divisor of a radical of size
at most \(X^3\) in a nonempty record. Thus each coefficient
costs subpower independently of the size of the orders.

Take a fixed \(K_0\) sufficiently large in terms of
\(\varepsilon\). If two orders exceed \(K_0\), freeze the
joint defect support at cost \(O(Y)\), then those two
coefficients at subpower cost. Enumerating their roots
costs \(O(X^{2/K_0})\) and determines the third actual value.
The choices of orders cost \(O((\log X)^3)\).

If at most one order exceeds \(K_0\), the two smaller
ones are a fixed pair after summing a finite list.
Put \(C_*=\max C_{P,Q,7}\) over pairs \(P,Q\) in that list
with \(1/P+1/Q<1\), ignoring permutations. Choose a
second fixed threshold \(K_1\) large compared with
\((1+C_*)/\varepsilon\). If the largest order \(L>K_1\),
enumerate the complete value \(M=D u^L\) on that letter
at cost \(X^{1/L}\) after fixing the coefficient \(D\).
For applying the fixed-signature result, relabel the two
other letters by \(P,Q\) and give this letter order \(7\)
on the corresponding branch section. Its order can be treated as \(7\)
with all abnormalities contained in the primes of \(M\).
Thus the new signature has joint defect support bounded
by \(Y\operatorname{rad}(u)\le YX^{1/L}\), and it is
hyperbolic since \(P,Q\) cannot both be two. The fixed
signature bound now gives \(Y^{C_*}X^{C_*/L}\), which
fits the asserted height loss. If the largest order is
at most \(K_1\), all signatures form a fixed finite list,
and summing the fixed-signature bound over supports up
to \(Y\) proves the result. Under the conditional
support-subpower version take the support exponent
in each application as small as needed, giving the
stated \(Y^{1+\varepsilon}\).


## Transfer to a powerful third letter

Fix \(P,Q\ge2\) with \(1/P+1/Q<1\). Require two
letters to be exact \(P\)-th and \(Q\)-th powers,
respectively, and the remaining letter to be \(r\)-full.
The last radical is at most \(X^{1/r}\). Include its
primes in \(S\), and use the fixed signature having
order \(7\) on that letter. This is hyperbolic, since
the largest reciprocal sum of the other two allowed
orders is \(5/6\). The number of primitive triples
is at most
\[
                         O_{P,Q}(X^{C_{P,Q}/r})
\]
for some \(C_{P,Q}\) independent of \(r\), without any
exponent-divisibility condition on the \(r\)-full letter.
Under discriminant multiplicity the exponent becomes
\(1/r+\varepsilon\). The height cut here is on complete
values, as in the powerful-triple count.


## Conductor limits beyond fixed valuation grids

At a good tame specialization prime the inertia order
is exactly the geometric branch order divided by its
gcd with the actual valuation. This yields a weighted
conductor calculation even for profiles that cannot
be captured by three exact power orders. On balanced
prime-degree profiles with microscopic masses,
the good-prime root-discriminant charge is at least
\((85/126-o(1))L\). For balanced profiles uniform
over long intervals of consecutive degrees it is at
least \((4/9-o(1))L\), with an exact weighted formula
in the imbalanced case. These bounds hold uniformly
in the branch orders at good primes. A moving twist
that produces an unramified fibre point at such a
prime must carry the same inertia order itself.
