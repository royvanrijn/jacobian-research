# A cubic norm product giving three-torsion in both mirror fields

The doubled family's virtual product can be made a local cube
at \(3\) while staying a noncube globally. It then supplies
an order-three ideal class in its imaginary quadratic field
and a cyclic unramified cubic extension of the real mirror field.
The construction keeps the two weighted directions positive.

## The local cube and the real cyclic cubic extension

**Theorem MC1.** Use the doubled family (1) of
[DB1](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_DOUBLED_BRANCH_FAMILY.md#classification-of-the-doubled-branch-model)
with an imaginary squareclass \(d\) and \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\).
Suppose \(w\) is a \(2\)-adic unit, \(1/w\equiv3\bmod9\),
and the virtual product class \(\gamma\) of VP1 is noncubic.
Assume \(K\ne\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\).
Then both \(K\) and \(K'=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3d})\) have
nontrivial \(3\)-torsion in their ideal class groups.
The class \(\gamma\) gives an unramified cyclic cubic extension
of \(K'\), split at the primes above \(3\).
The associated rational cubic field is totally real, has
discriminant equal to the fundamental discriminant of \(K'\),
and is generated by a root of
\[
 T^3+\frac{6}{w-1}T-\frac{2(w+3)}{w-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]

Write \(z=1/w\) at \(3\). The radicand divided by \(w^4\)
is \((1-z^2)(1+4z-z^2)\equiv1+4z\bmod9\)
and is a local square. On the branch with
\(Y/w^2\equiv1+2z\bmod9\), one has
\[
 U\equiv1+4z,\qquad X\equiv1+2z,\qquad
 \gamma=U+2X-1\equiv2+8z\equiv8\pmod9.
\]
Thus \(\gamma\) is a cube at this prime over \(3\).
The cube map from \(1+3\mathbb Z_3\) onto \(1+9\mathbb Z_3\)
can be checked by expanding \((1+3r)^3\)
and applying Hensel's lemma in \(r\).
The conjugate value is also a cube by the cube norm.
Since \(w\) is a dyadic unit, VP1 gives the virtual-unit
condition at every finite prime.
In the imaginary field other than \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\),
units are cubes, so this supplies the nontrivial ideal class.

For the mirror use \(F=K(\zeta_3)=K'(\zeta_3)\).
The cube class remains nontrivial in \(F\), by the degree-two
norm. The extension \(F(\sqrt[3]\gamma)/F\) is unramified
at every finite prime: the valuations are divisible by three
away from \(3\), and the class is locally a cube at \(3\).
The involution on \(F\) fixing \(K'\) conjugates both
\(K\) and \(\zeta_3\). It acts trivially on the cubic
Kummer Galois group, because these two conjugations
invert the radical and the root of unity, respectively.
The order-six group over \(K'\) is thus cyclic and has a
unique cubic subextension. That cubic extension is unramified
over \(K'\), since a ramification index three could not disappear
after the quadratic base change to \(F\). It is also split
above \(3\): the local extension over \(F\) is trivial and
an unramified cubic local factor could not become trivial
after a quadratic base change.

For (1), take the Cardano sum
\(\sqrt[3]\gamma+n/\sqrt[3]\gamma\), with
\(n=-(X-1)\) and \(n^3=\operatorname N\gamma\).
Its three conjugates are cyclically permuted over \(F\)
because \(\gamma\) is not a cube. Thus (1) is irreducible.
Its discriminant has squareclass \(-3d>0\), so its
Galois closure is the \(S_3\) extension with quadratic
subfield \(K'\) just constructed.
The cubic field has fundamental discriminant
\(\operatorname{disc}(K')\) because the cyclic extension
of the resolvent is unramified. This is the same Kummer
reflection mechanism as in
[RF1 and RT1](ABC_COHERENT_REFLECTION_AT_THREE.md#the-residue-test).
For the cubic conductor relation with the quadratic resolvent
see [Cohen–Morra, Section 1.1](sources/cohen-morra-2010-fixed-quadratic-resolvent-v1.pdf).

## Fixed weighted profiles

**Corollary MC2.** For the two profiles of VI1 at \(t=2\),
with coefficients scaled to \(b=15,\ b^2=225\), there are at least
\[
 X^{1/6}\exp\!\left(-O\!\left(\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}\right)\right)
\]
distinct imaginary fields of absolute discriminant at most \(X\)
satisfying MC1 and carrying both positive directions.
The real mirror fields and the reflected cubic fields are
distinct, with discriminant size at most \(3X\).

Use \(s\in(-1/4,-1/5)\) with \(s\) a \(2\)-adic unit,
\(3s\equiv1\bmod3\), and \(v_7(s)\ne0\).
Then \(-2-\sqrt5<w=225s^3<-1\), so both directions
are positive by the argument of DB3.
At \(3\) one has \(v_3(w)=-1\) and
\(3w=(b/3)^2(3s)^3\equiv1\bmod3\), giving
\(1/w\equiv3\bmod9\).
At \(7\), \(w\) has nonzero valuation. On a split branch
\(\gamma\equiv-2\) if \(v_7(w)>0\), or
\(\gamma\equiv2\) if \(v_7(w)<0\), by VP1.
Both residues are noncubes modulo seven.
The field splits at \(5\) by DB2, so it differs from
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\). Thus MC1 applies.
It suffices for the count to take \(v_7(s)>0\), a fixed
local congruence together with the conditions at \(2,3\).
The fixed-curve counting argument of DM2 gives the result.
The map from imaginary fields to their mirrors is injective,
and the cubic field's quadratic resolvent recovers the mirror.
Since \(3\) splits in \(K\), the mirror discriminant
is exactly three times the absolute imaginary discriminant.

For example \(s=-5/21\) gives
\(d=-565358149646\) and \(w=-3125/1029\). A monic integral
model of (1) is
\(Z^3-6411699Z-163929302\), with field discriminant
\(6784297795752\), the mirror's fundamental discriminant.
The imaginary and real mirror class-group invariants are
\(66690,2,2,2,2\) and \(6,2,2,2,2\), respectively,
so both have \(3\)-rank exactly one in this instance.
The [checker](verify_cubic_tangent_mirror_class_family.py)
verifies the local conditions, polynomial discriminant and
proof-enabled class group computations.

## Varying profiles and distinct mirror pairs

**Corollary MC3.** There are at least
\[
 X^{1/36}\exp\!\left(-O\!\left(\frac{\log X}{\log\log X}\right)\right)
\]
distinct imaginary quadratic fields of absolute discriminant
at most \(X\) satisfying MC1, with pairwise distinct unordered
inverse coefficient classes as in VI2. The real mirror fields
and fundamental reflected cubic fields are also distinct.

In VI1, instead of its particular \(s(t)\), take
\[
 s=\frac{70}{35t^4+22},
\]
with \(t\) even at \(2\), integral at \(5,7\), and
\(t\equiv1\bmod9\). For \(t\ge2\) the new value of \(w\)
still lies between \(1/3\) and \(1/2\), by the same
positive expression as in VI1 with denominator factor
\((1+22/(35t^4))^3\).
It is a dyadic unit and is divisible by \(5,7\).
The coefficient \(b=(t^6-4)/4\) has valuation one at
\(3\), and the denominator of \(s\) is \(3\bmod9\);
hence \(v_3(s)=-1,\ 3s\equiv1\bmod3\).
This gives \(1/w\equiv3\bmod9\).
The noncube and positive-direction arguments of VI1 apply
throughout the restricted family.

In the skew box of VI2 impose \(c\) prime to \(3\) and
\(a\equiv c\bmod9\), for \(t=a/c\).
The primitive-pair count remains \(\gg H^{4/3}\), by
counting the coprime numerators in their progression modulo
eighteen. The coefficient separation argument is identical.
The rational function \(w(t)\) still has degree at most
twelve, so the same discriminant bound and uniform field
multiplicity through the fixed \(H_3\) apply. This proves
the lower bound with profiles varying. These counts concern
ideal classes and fundamental cubic discriminants. They
do not require individual prime values of the two profile
directions.
