# The index of a plane through a coherent prime direction

A full congruence lattice gives an exact index on any rational plane through its prime direction. The index factors assign common divisors to pairs of scaled normal coordinates. This lowers the count of occupied rank-one records with a large index, with a small scaled coordinate relative to that index, or with two nearly cancelling cofactor terms.

## Exact index and divisibility

Use the [coherent norm triples](ABC_COHERENT_NORM_PENCILS.md#exact-reconstruction-from-three-signed-coefficients) with signed normalized coefficients \(x,y,z\). Put \(q_1=|x|,\ q_2=|y|,\ q_3=|z|\) and \(Q=q_1q_2q_3\). Use the [exact-modulus lattice](ABC_COHERENT_NORM_COFACTOR_CUBIC.md#exact-cubic-and-quadratic-forms) \(\Lambda\) containing the prime vector \(p=(p_1,p_2,p_3)\). Its three unit ratio relations are \(w_1=c_1w_2\bmod q_1,\ w_3=c_2w_2\bmod q_2,\ w_1=c_3w_3\bmod q_3\). The respective cubes of the ratios are \(q_3^2/q_2^2,\ q_3^2/q_1^2,\ q_1^2/q_2^2\) in the indicated rings, by the signed reconstruction congruences. The exact moduli refine the original full-modulus lattice by a bounded power of two.

Let \(\Pi\) be a rational plane through \(p\). Let \(\nu\) be its primitive integer normal and \(n\) the cross product of a basis of \(\Pi\cap\Lambda\), with the two signs chosen compatibly. Write \(n=g\nu\) and put \((\nu_1^*,\nu_2^*,\nu_3^*)=(\nu_3,\nu_1,\nu_2)\), with the same reordering for \(n^*\). Thus coordinate \(i\) of the reordered normal is opposite the two coordinates entering the relation at \(q_i\).

**Proposition (Plane index and primitive scaled normal).** Define \(d_i=\gcd(q_i,\nu_i^*)\), \(t_i=q_i/d_i\) and \(b_i=\nu_i^*/d_i\). Then
\[
   g=t_1t_2t_3,\qquad n_i^*=q_i a_i,\qquad a_i=(g/t_i)b_i,\qquad
   \gcd(a_1,a_2,a_3)=1,\qquad t_i=\gcd(q_i,a_j,a_k),                 \tag{1}
\]
where \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\). The cofactor satisfies
\[
           S=a_1^3q_1+a_2^3q_2+a_3^3q_3=mQ,\qquad m\in\mathbb Z.     \tag{2}
\]
With \(D_*=d_1d_2d_3\) it follows that
\[
          mD_* \equiv b_i^3 d_i(g/t_i)^2\pmod {t_i^2}.               \tag{3}
\]

For the index, restrict each congruence functional to the integer plane \(\Pi\cap\mathbb Z^3\). Modulo \(q_i\), the ideal generated by its values is generated by the two-row minors of the matrix with rows \(\nu\) and the congruence functional. Indeed a unimodular basis adapted to the primitive row \(\nu\) reduces those minors to the functional's first two coordinates, which generate the values on the plane. At \(q_1\), the minors generate the ideal of \(\nu_3\) and \(c_1\nu_1+\nu_2\). Since \(p\in\Pi\cap\Lambda\), substitution gives
\[
             (c_1\nu_1+\nu_2)p_2\equiv-\nu_3p_3\pmod{q_1}.
\]
The incident prime \(p_2\) is a unit modulo \(q_1\). Thus the ideal is generated by \(\nu_3\) alone, and the image of the congruence functional has order \(t_1\). The other two images have orders \(t_2,t_3\) by the same argument on the incident coordinates. These orders are pairwise coprime, so the combined image has order \(t_1t_2t_3\). This is the plane index \([\Pi\cap\mathbb Z^3:\Pi\cap\Lambda]\), which is also \(g\).

Now \(q_i=d_i t_i\) and \(n_i^*=g d_i b_i\) give the formula for \(a_i\). If a prime divides \(g\), it divides only one \(t_i\); then \(b_i\) and \(g/t_i\) are both units at that prime. A prime outside \(g\) dividing every \(a_i\) would divide every \(\nu_i^*\). Hence \(a\) is primitive. For the final gcd in (1), work at a prime of \(q_i\). Both other \(a\)-coordinates are divisible to the depth of \(t_i\). If that depth is less than the depth of \(q_i\), then \(\nu_i^*\) is nonunit, and at least one of the other primitive normal coordinates is a unit. Its corresponding \(b\)-coordinate is also a unit, since the other two \(d\)'s divide coprime moduli. This establishes the stated gcd.

For (2), the cross product of two vectors satisfying the ratio modulo \(q_1\) gives \(n_2=-c_1n_1\bmod q_1\). Cube this relation, substitute the cube of \(c_1\) and cancel the unit \(q_2^2q_3^2\). The resulting congruence is \(q_1\mid a_2^3q_2+a_3^3q_3\). Repeat at the other two moduli and use their coprimality. Substituting (1) into (2) and dividing by \(g\) gives the exact equation \(mD_*=\sum_i b_i^3d_i(g/t_i)^2\). Modulo \(t_i^2\) only term \(i\) survives, proving (3). For example, if \(q_i\) is squarefree, then \(m(D_*/d_i)\equiv b_i^3(g/t_i)^2\bmod t_i^2\).

For an exact example take \(p=(19,23,29)\), \((x,y,z)=(2,3,5)\) and \(d=860107,\ \delta=2\). The traces \(17890,34000,24148\) give the three norms and the coherent product with middle factor \(23^3\). The normal \(\nu=(-29,29,-4)\) is primitive, and \(p,(1,1,0)\) form an integral basis of its plane. Direct evaluation of the three congruence functionals on this basis gives local indices \(1,3,5\). Equations (1)–(3) then give \(g=15\), \(a=(-30,-145,87)\), and \(m=-196912\). This is a plane-index example without a rank restriction.

## Counting with the index and a pair of coordinates

Choose \(\Pi\) through the first two successive-minimum directions of the exact-modulus lattice, for a rank-one record occupied by a coherent prime path. Write \(P=\|n\|\asymp\lambda_1\lambda_2\). Here \(\lambda_1\asymp Z\) because the prime direction is primitive and is the only line in the original rank-one ball. Since \(\lambda_2\le\lambda_3\) and \(\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3\asymp Q\), this gives \(P^2\ll ZQ\). The exact lattice still records the actual prime triple, and the finite refinement at two causes no increase in the number of original records represented by a chosen prime triple and signed coefficients.

**Proposition (Rank-one sectors from the plane index).** Let the prime interval be \([Z,2Z)\) and the normalized coefficients be \(O(V)\), with \(Z\ge2,\ V\ge1\). Up to an \(O_\varepsilon((ZV)^\varepsilon)\) factor, the sectors with a zero cofactor coordinate, a vanishing pair of nonzero terms of \(S\), or \(m=0\) contribute respectively
\[
                 V^2/Z,\qquad V^{5/3},\qquad
                          Z^{3/2}V^{3/2}+V^2.                       \tag{4}
\]
On the other records fix \(G\ge1\), and restrict to \(g\ge G\). The counts below also hold, with the same subpower factor.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{additional condition} & \text{upper bound}\ \hline
\text{none} & ZV^3/G\
\min_i|a_i|\le A & A Z^{1/2}V^{5/2}/G\
|a_i^3q_i+a_j^3q_j|\le\lambda\max(|a_i^3q_i|,|a_j^3q_j|)
 \text{ for some } i\ne j & ZV+\lambda ZV^3/G
\end{array}                                                        \tag{5}
\]
Here \(A\ge1\) and \(0<\lambda\le1/2\). The bounds apply to subfamilies satisfying any additional field-window or witness selection condition.

If the close pair in the last row contains a coordinate with \(|a_i|\le A\), the joint bound is
\[
                   A Z^{1/2}V+\lambda A Z^{1/2}V^{5/2}/G.          \tag{6}
\]

If one normal coordinate is zero, the two moduli whose defining relations involve that coordinate divide both other coordinates. The relation \(n\cdot p=0\) then writes the two remaining coordinates as a scalar multiple of the two corresponding distinct primes in reversed order and with opposite signs. Their coprimality forces the scalar to be divisible by both moduli. Thus \(P\gg Z q_jq_k\) if coordinate \(i\) of \(n^*\) vanishes. From \(P^2\ll ZQ\) we have \(q_jq_k\ll q_i/Z\). Counting the two factors of integers up to \(q_i/Z\), then summing \(q_i\ll V\), gives the first estimate in (4). If two nonzero terms of \(S\) cancel, their moduli are both perfect cubes, by pairwise coprimality and comparison of valuations in \(a_i^3q_i=-a_j^3q_j\). This gives the second estimate.

For the final estimate of (4), fix dyadic scales \(Q_i\asymp q_i\), \(Q_*=Q_1Q_2Q_3\), \(w=\max_i Q_i\), and a dyadic scale for \(P\). Use the exact linear equation \(S=0\) and the primitive \(a\) of (1). There are \(O(P^3/Q_*)\) choices of the three nonzero \(a_i\). Scaling the \(q_i\) coordinates by \(Q_i^{-1}\) gives a plane lattice of covolume \(\|(a_1^3Q_1,a_2^3Q_2,a_3^3Q_3)\|/Q_*\gg P^3/(Q_*w^2)\). If the candidates span a line, their primitivity leaves at most two. In rank two the scaled lattice count is \(O(Q_*w^2/P^3)\) by successive minima. This is the same scaled-plane argument as in the [rank-two count](ABC_RANK_TWO_NORM_PLANE_DIVISORS.md#counting-the-rank-two-congruences), with no need to divide the already primitive \(a_i\) by a common factor. Summing the resulting \(O_\varepsilon((ZV)^\varepsilon(P^3/Q_*+w^2))\) over the boxes and using \(P^2\ll ZQ_*\) proves (4).

For (5), fix the dyadic scales and \(g\asymp G'\). For each split \(g=t_1t_2t_3\), choose \((a_i,q_i)\) and \((a_j,q_j)\). For either pair, the moduli are multiples of \(t_i\) and the scaled coordinates are multiples of \(g/t_i\), by (1). This gives \(O((Q_i/t_i)(P t_i/(Q_i g)))=O(P/g)\) choices, with the count zero if a nonzero multiple cannot fit. The third modulus divides the nonzero sum \(a_i^3q_i+a_j^3q_j\), so has subpower many choices by the divisor bound. If \(|a_i|\le A\) is prescribed, the first pair instead costs \(O(AQ_i/g)\). Each resulting modulus triple leaves subpower many root classes and at most one primitive positive prime direction per record. There are \(O_\varepsilon(G'^{1+\varepsilon})\) choices of \(g\) and its split in a dyadic range, so the first two bounds follow from \(P^2\ll ZQ_*\), then dyadic summation for \(G'\ge G\).

For the close-pair condition in (5), first choose \(a_i,a_j\). At fixed \(g,t_i,t_j,t_k\), the cost is \(O(P^2/(Q_iQ_j g t_k))\). The reduced fractions \((q_i/t_i)/(q_j/t_j)\) are spaced by \(\gg(t_j/Q_j)^2\), because they have denominator \(O(Q_j/t_j)\). The close-pair condition restricts that ratio to an interval of length \(O(\lambda(Q_i/t_i)/(Q_j/t_j))\). It therefore leaves \(O(1+\lambda Q_iQ_j/(t_it_j))\) choices for the two moduli. Again choose the third by divisibility. Multiplying and summing in a dyadic range \(g\asymp G'\) gives
\[
     O_\varepsilon\left((ZV)^\varepsilon
                      (P^2/(Q_iQ_j)+\lambda P^2/G')\right).
\]
Since \(P^2/(Q_iQ_j)\ll ZQ_k\), summing proves the third bound in (5). For the joint condition, replace the cost of the first coordinate by \(O(A t_i/g)\) before the same fraction count. The two terms become \(O_\varepsilon((ZV)^\varepsilon(AP/Q_j+\lambda A P Q_i/G'))\), and \(P/Q_j\ll Z^{1/2}V\) by \(P^2\ll ZQ_*\). This gives (6).

## Consequence at the original crossover

At \(V=Z^{5/4}\) all sectors in (4) save a power over \(V^3\). The union of the following additional sectors has size \(O_\varepsilon(V^{59/20+\varepsilon})\):
\[
 \begin{gathered}
       g\ge V^{17/20},\qquad
       \min_i |a_i|\le g V^{1/20},\
 |a_i^3q_i+a_j^3q_j|
    \le \min(1/2,g V^{-17/20})\max(|a_i^3q_i|,|a_j^3q_j|)
    \quad\text{for some } i\ne j .
 \end{gathered}
\]
For the last two cases take \(A=O(G' V^{1/20})\) and \(\lambda\le O(G' V^{-17/20})\) in each dyadic index box before summing. This leaves the small-index records whose scaled coordinates are all larger, and whose cofactor terms avoid the given pairwise cancellation. These restrictions concern the auxiliary cofactor, independently of the sizes of the lifted witness congruences or the original Heron terms.

The same saving also holds jointly if a close pair contains \(|a_i|\le V^{1/2}\) and its relative cancellation is at most \(\min(1/2,g V^{-9/20})\). In (6) the first term is \(O(V^{19/10})\), and the second is \(O(V^{59/20})\) in each index box.
