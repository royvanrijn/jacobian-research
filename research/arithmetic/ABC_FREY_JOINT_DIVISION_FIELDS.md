# Joint division fields for full-level Frey congruences

Let \(E\) be the Frey curve of a primitive positive triple \(a+b=c\),
with \(R=\operatorname{rad}(abc)\), at least two odd primes in \(R\),
and conductor \(N\). Write \(f=f_E\), and let \(\delta_1\) be the
classical optimal modular degree. Use the normalized weight-two
newforms of exact level \(N\), trivial character, and the full
Atkin–Lehner signs of \(f\). At a coefficient prime \(\ell\ge5\)
retain the places of the competing newforms whose residual
eigensystem agrees with \(f\) away from \(N\ell\).

Let \(F_\ell=\mathbb Q(\mu_{\ell^\infty})\). Let \(K'_\ell\) be the
compositum of \(F_\ell\) and the fields cut out by the \(\ell\)-adic
representations at all the retained places of all competing
newforms. Put \(K_\ell=\mathbb Q(E[\ell^\infty])\) and
\(J_\ell=K_\ell\cap K'_\ell\). We prove that
\[
 J_\ell=F_\ell(E[\ell^{e_\ell}]),
 \qquad
 \ell^{e_\ell}=\text{the Frey idempotent denominator in the
 localized signed new Hecke algebra}.
 \tag{DF1}
\]
Here \(e_\ell=0\) and \(J_\ell=F_\ell\) if there is no retained
competitor. The formula uses the joint field of the competitors.
It includes coefficient primes dividing the conductor.

Over \(F=\mathbb Q(\mu_\infty)\), let \(J\) be the compositum of
the \(FJ_\ell\) for \(\ell\ge5\), and write \((\delta_1)_{\{2,3\}}\)
for the part of \(\delta_1\) at \(2\) and \(3\). The sharp
bound
\[
             (\delta_1)_{\{2,3\}}[J:F]^{1/3}
                         \ll_\varepsilon R^{2+\varepsilon}
                 \quad(\varepsilon>0)
 \tag{DF2}
\]
is equivalent to full abc. The degree \([J:F]\) is finite.

## The image over the Hecke order

Let \(\mathcal T_\ell\) be the \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-algebra of tuples generated
by \(T_s\) for primes \(s\nmid N\ell\) on the exact-level signed
space described above. It is finite over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\).
Let \(A\) be its complete local factor at \(f\bmod\ell\), with
residue field \(\mathbb F_\ell\). By strong multiplicity one
and the splitting of a finite algebra over the complete base,
its characteristic-zero factors are \(f\) and the retained
coefficient places. As with the trace construction in
[(HN2)](ABC_FREY_FULL_LEVEL_CONGRUENCES.md#trace-differences-at-a-prime-exactly-dividing-the-level),
Chebotarev and closedness give an \(A\)-valued trace for every
Galois element. The traces generate \(A\) as a closed
\(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-algebra.

The Frey curve has full residual image at every \(\ell\ge5\), as
follows. A rational cyclic \(\ell\)-isogeny on \(E\), together
with its full rational \(2\)-torsion, would give a rational
cyclic \(4\ell\)-isogeny on an isogenous curve. In fact take
distinct rational order-two subgroups \(C_1,C_2\) of \(E\).
The composition \(E/C_1\to E\to E/C_2\), using the dual
isogeny in the first step, is cyclic of degree four. The
image of \((E/C_1)[2]\) under the dual is \(C_1\), disjoint
from \(C_2\), so the order-four kernel has just one nonzero
order-two point. Transport the \(\ell\)-subgroup to \(E/C_1\)
and add it to this cyclic kernel. The Mazur–Kenku list excludes
\(4\ell\) for \(\ell\ge5\), proving residual irreducibility.

Pasten's Lemma 6.12 supplies an odd multiplicative prime \(r\)
with \(\ell\nmid v_r(\Delta_E)\). After adjoining \(\mu_\ell\)
and the unramified extension splitting the reduction, the
Tate parameter at \(r\) still has valuation prime to \(\ell\),
since the ramification degree of the extension is prime to
\(\ell\). Kummer theory gives a nontrivial unipotent in the
residual image, also when \(r=\ell\). An irreducible subgroup
containing such a unipotent contains the two root groups
generated by it and a conjugate with a different fixed line.
These generate \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)\).
The cyclotomic determinant gives full
\(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)\).
[Mazur–Kenku classification and proof](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82233672.pdf).
[Pasten, Lemma 6.12](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09251).
[Lemos, Theorem 1.1, the general cyclic-isogeny case at larger primes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01985).

The characteristic-zero traces together form a
two-dimensional pseudorepresentation of \(G_{\mathbb Q}\) with
cyclotomic determinant and absolutely irreducible residual trace.
The Carayol–Nyssen theorem realizes it as
\(\rho:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\operatorname{GL}_2(A)\), with the
stated specializations. This representation is continuous. For
example choose four image matrices whose reductions span the
matrix ring and express all entries by the trace pairing with
those matrices. The determinant is the cyclotomic character
since it has that value on every characteristic-zero factor.
[Carayol, Theorems 1 and 2](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kyodo/kokyuroku/contents/pdf/0884-03.pdf).

In fact the image of \(\rho\) is
\[
       G=\{g\in\operatorname{GL}_2(A):\det g\in\mathbb Z_\ell^\times\}.
 \tag{DF3}
\]
Here are details valid for all \(\ell\ge5\). Let \(G_0\) be
the image, \(H=G_0\cap\operatorname{SL}_2(A)\), and \(\mathfrak m\)
the maximal ideal of \(A\). The residual image of \(H\) is
\(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)\), since commutators already
surject there. Write \(V=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\). The first
congruence layer of \(H\) is
\(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)\otimes W\) for a subspace \(W\subset V\).
Indeed the adjoint for the residual full general linear group is
absolutely simple, and its isotypic direct sum has just these
submodules. This first layer contains the \(\ell\)-direction.
For a lift \(g\in H\) of the unipotent \(u=1+e_{12}\), compute
\(g^\ell\equiv 1+\ell e_{12}\bmod\mathfrak m^2\).
The terms linear in \(g-u\) give the sum of the conjugates by
\(u^j\) modulo \(\ell\). Their entries are polynomials of degree
at most two in \(j\), so the sum vanishes for \(\ell\ge5\).

Pass to \(A'=A/(\mathfrak m^2+\widetilde W)\), where
\(\widetilde W\subset\mathfrak m\) is the preimage of \(W\).
This ring has characteristic \(\ell\) and square-zero
maximal ideal \(V/W\). The image of \(G_0\) in
\(\operatorname{GL}_2(A')\) injects on reduction. Indeed, an
element reducing to the identity commutes with the residual
group to first order, since its commutators belong to the first
layer of \(H\) that we killed. Its first-order part is thus
scalar, and the determinant, which is constant and reduces to one
in \(A'\), makes this part zero.

The resulting section of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)\)
over \(A'\) has constant determinant. Its conjugacy to the
constant section follows from
\[
 H^1(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell),
                  \mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_\ell))=0.
\]
For completeness, restrict to the upper unipotent Sylow
subgroup \(U=\langle u\rangle\). This restriction is injective
by transfer since the index is prime to \(\ell\).
The cyclic cohomology quotient \(H^1(U,\mathfrak{sl}_2)\)
is the coinvariant line represented by \(e_{21}\). The norm
operator is zero, since \((\operatorname{Ad}u-1)^3=0\) and
\(\ell-1\ge4\). The matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(t,1)\) acts on
the coinvariant by \(t^{-1}\) and on evaluation of a cocycle
at \(u\) by another \(t^{-1}\), since it conjugates \(u\) to
\(u^t\). The resulting character \(t^{-2}\) has zero invariants.
This proves the vanishing, which also holds with coefficients
tensored by \(V/W\).

Thus every image trace in \(A'\) is constant. Trace generation
forces \(V/W=0\). At higher levels the commutator of \(1+x_i\)
and \(1+x_j\) represents \([x_i,x_j]\) in degree \(i+j\).
Since the Lie brackets span \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) and \(V\)
generates the associated graded ring in positive degrees,
induction gives the full determinant-one layer at every degree.
Completeness gives \(\operatorname{SL}_2(A)\) in the image.
The cyclotomic determinant is surjective onto
\(\mathbb Z_\ell^\times\), proving (DF3). The use of the full
general linear residual group in the first layer includes
\(\ell=5\).
[Related subgroup classifications, especially the cohomology distinction at \(5\) in Remark 3.42 of Böckle–Arias-de-Reyna](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03114).

## Recovering the joint intersection from the denominator

Let \(e_f=(1,0,\ldots,0)\) in the characteristic-zero product,
and define \(e_\ell=\min\{m\ge0:\ell^m e_f\in A\}\).
The kernel of projection of \(A\) onto the product of the
competitor factors is exactly
\(\ell^{e_\ell}\mathbb Z_\ell e_f\). This follows since
evaluation on \(f\) takes values in \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) and
includes the constant copy of \(\mathbb Z_\ell\). If there
are competitors then \(e_\ell\ge1\) by locality. By (DF3),
the image on \(E\) of the elements acting identically on every
competitor is exactly
\[
       \{u\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z_\ell):
                          u\equiv 1\bmod\ell^{e_\ell}\}.
 \tag{DF4}
\]
For the inclusion, their determinant is one and their difference
from the identity is supported at \(f\). Conversely for any such
\(u\) the tuple \((u,1,\ldots,1)\) has entries in \(A\) and
determinant one, hence is in the image. The integral specialization
on \(f\) is the Tate module of \(E\) up to integral equivalence,
by absolute residual irreducibility. The subgroup in (DF4)
cuts out the composite of the cyclotomic field and the division
field at exponent \(e_\ell\), proving (DF1). The empty case
follows from the convention for \(K'_\ell\).
Thus \(e_\ell\) is also the largest \(n\) such that
\(\mathbb Q(E[\ell^n])\subseteq K'_\ell\).

There is a basic restriction on replacing the joint intersection
by pairwise intersections. Let \(b_j\) be the denominator exponent
in the image of \(A\) on just \(f\) and the \(j\)-th competitor.
Projection gives \(b_j\le e_\ell\). Multiplying lifts that have
value \(\ell^{b_j}\) on \(f\) and vanish at the \(j\)-th competitor
gives
\[
                       \max_j b_j\le e_\ell\le\sum_j b_j.
 \tag{DF5}
\]
The sum is sharp for abstract trace orders with full image.
Take the order
\(\mathbb Z_\ell[t]/(t\prod_{j=1}^s(t-\ell^a u_j))\), where
\(a>0\) and the \(u_j\) are distinct units. At the root zero the
idempotent is the monic numerator \(\prod_j(t-\ell^a u_j)\)
divided by its value at zero. The integral power basis gives
denominator exponent \(sa\), while each pair projection has
exponent \(a\). The subgroup of \(\operatorname{GL}_2\) of this
order with determinant in \(\mathbb Z_\ell^\times\) has full
residual image and contains matrices of determinant one with
any prescribed trace. Thus the image condition alone gives no
bound by the largest pairwise depth. The example is an abstract
order and group.

## Comparing the intersection depth to the Frey degree

The operator algebra \(\mathcal T_\ell\) acts integrally on every signed
full-level toric lattice used to define \(B\) in (HN1). The
matching newforms transfer by rational Jacquet–Langlands and
the same tuple of good operators is obtained in each lattice.
Putting \(\ell^{e_\ell}e_f\) equal to zero on the other local
factors gives
\[
                        v_\ell(B)\le e_\ell .
 \tag{DF6}
\]

For the other bound, \(\mathcal T_\ell\) is the image of the full classical
Hecke algebra over \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) on the exact-level signed
space. Here is why deleting \(T_\ell\) at the coefficient prime
and the other bad operators in the definition of the away algebra
does not change this image. On the signed newspace every
\(U_q\) for \(q\mid N\) is scalar, namely the signed eigenvalue
when \(q\parallel N\) and zero when \(q^2\mid N\). In the
integral \(q\)-expansion lattice of this space, the pairing with
the full Hecke algebra is \(T,g\mapsto a_1(Tg)\). It is a perfect
pairing: \(a_1(T_ng)=a_n(g)\), the coefficient functionals span the
dual by saturation of the integral lattice, and the full
coefficient sequence detects any nonzero operator under the
pairing. It suffices by Nakayama to detect a nonzero form modulo
\(\ell\) by its coefficients of index prime to \(N\ell\).
The scalar bad-prime recurrences then recover all coefficients
of index prime to \(\ell\). When \(\ell\mid N\) they recover all
the coefficients outright, including at multiples of \(\ell\).
When \(\ell\nmid N\), vanishing of the coefficients prime to
\(\ell\) puts the reduction in the kernel of Katz's theta on
weight two at level prime to \(\ell\). The kernel there is zero
for \(\ell\ge5\). One may pull back the form to prime-to-\(\ell\)
full level at least three, using the \(q\)-expansion principle
for reduction, and apply Katz's injectivity for
\(1\le k\le\ell-2\). Hence the good coefficient functionals
span the dual in both cases.
[Katz, §II, Corollary 3](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/modformcharp.pdf).

The classical full-space \(f\)-projector denominator in the
full Hecke algebra has valuation \(v_\ell(\delta_1)\) when
\(\ell^2\nmid N\), by Agashe–Ribet–Stein. This hypothesis holds
here at every odd \(\ell\). Restricting the signed new Hecke
algebra therefore gives
\[
                          e_\ell\le v_\ell(\delta_1).
 \tag{DF7}
\]
[Agashe–Ribet–Stein, Theorem 3.6(b) and Lemma 4.2](https://www.math.fsu.edu/~agashe/math/moddeg3.pdf).

## One finite extension over the cyclotomic field

By (DF1) and (DF4), for \(e_\ell>0\),
\[
 [J_\ell:F_\ell]=|\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/\ell^{e_\ell})|
                 =\ell^{3e_\ell}(1-\ell^{-2}).
\]
This group is perfect: elementary unipotents generate it and
each is a commutator with a diagonal matrix whose conjugation
multiplier differs from one by a unit. Hence base change to
the abelian extension \(F/F_\ell\) preserves the degree and
group. For different \(\ell\ge5\), the groups have distinct
unique nonabelian simple composition factors
\(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)\). Their associated
extensions \(FJ_\ell/F\) are linearly disjoint. Indeed a
nontrivial common quotient would be perfect and have a
nonabelian simple composition factor from both sides. The
same argument gives disjointness from the compositum of any
finite set of the other extensions. By (DF7) there are only
finitely many nontrivial factors. Consequently
\[
    [J:F]=\prod_{\ell\ge5:\,e_\ell>0}\ell^{3e_\ell}(1-\ell^{-2}),
    \qquad
    [J:F]^{1/3}\asymp\prod_{\ell\ge5}\ell^{e_\ell}
 \tag{DF8}
\]
with absolute comparison constants.

Write \(C=(\delta_1)_{\{2,3\}}\prod_{\ell\ge5}\ell^{e_\ell}\).
By (DF7), \(C\mid\delta_1\). In the other direction (DF6)
controls the large-prime part of \(B\), and \(B\mid\delta_{D,M}\)
by (HN1). The Shimura degree comparison (SP9) and its exact
transfer factor give
\[
 \big(\delta_{D,M}\big)_{\{2,3\}}
        \le (\delta_1)_{\{2,3\}}\exp(O(1+\omega(D))).
\]
In this inequality the product of the transferred Tate
exponents can only decrease the left side relative to the
classical degree. Hence \(B\le C\exp(O(1+\omega(D)))\).
Since \(\log\delta_1\) and \(\log B\) both equal
\(\log c+\log R+o(\log c)\) uniformly, the same is true
of \(\log C\). This proves the equivalence (DF2) by (DF8),
together with the elementary cases of at most one odd prime.
[Exact degree transfer and low-support reduction](ABC_CUBIC_ADJOINT_SHIMURA_PROJECTORS.md).
[Full-level signed projector comparison](ABC_FREY_FULL_LEVEL_CONGRUENCES.md).

The remaining estimate is the size of the joint division-field
overlaps across coefficient primes, together with the finitely
supported part of the modular degree. In particular, (DF5) shows
why bounding only the deepest individual congruence leaves a
possible accumulation of congruences in a single sign packet.
