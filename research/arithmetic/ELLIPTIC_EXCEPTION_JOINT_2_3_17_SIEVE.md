# A joint two-, three-, and seventeen-adic sieve below one quarter

On the existing elliptic slice, the local tests at two, three and
seventeen retain an exact proportion `5321/23946` of matching field
targets. A finite congruence sieve already gives a Hasse upper
coefficient `1687/6936<1/4`, with a square-root logarithmic error.
The joint image is proved from the known rational points and descent
information; independence of the local tests is not presumed.

## 1. Inputs and coordinate conventions

Use the translated elliptic curve and parameter

\[
 E_0:\ y^2=x^3-10656x^2+27091730x,\quad X=x-3552,
 \qquad v=\frac{3(x+432)}{6400},\quad F(x)=128x-25865.
                                                               \tag{1.1}
\]

Write `G=E_0(Q)`, and use the exact points

\[
 P=\left(\frac{373248}{49},\frac{59459616}{343}\right),
 \quad T=(0,0),\quad
 S=\left(\frac{92179201}{6400},
          \frac{553221611649}{512000}\right),\quad R=P+T.
                                                               \tag{1.2}
\]

Let `E_n(p)` be the formal subgroup with parameter `t=-X/y` of
valuation at least `n`. The existing inputs are:

- [ETF1 and ETF4](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_TWO_FIVE_ADIC_CLASSIFICATION.md):
  `<P,T,S>` has odd index in `G`, its two-adic closure is
  `<T> times E_4(2)`, and the joint image with reduction at three
  is as described below;
- [ETC3a](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_COMPLETE_THREE_ADIC_CLASSIFICATION.md):
  finite reduction tests at `41,53` prove the needed three-saturation,
  so the actual three-adic closure is `E(F_3) times E_2(3)`;
- [ETC1 and ETC5](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_COMPLETE_THREE_ADIC_CLASSIFICATION.md):
  in the four residue discs below, exact conditional three-adic
  soluble masses are `1/13,0,1/3,1`;
- [EEC1](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_COUNT.md): matching targets have coefficient
  `C_E=pi/(3 sqrt(R_x))`, with finite-index cosets counted by the
  canonical-height lattice argument;
- [ELV1](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_EXPLICIT_LOCAL_CRITERION.md): at seventeen,
  local solubility is equivalent to `v_17(F(x)) in 3Z`.

Fix the residue basis

\[
 A=\overline T=(0,0),\qquad B=\overline S=(1,0)
                  \quad\text{in }E_0(\mathbb F_3).
\]

Thus `A+B=(2,0)`. In particular `B` is the reduction of `S`, not
the reduction of the earlier second point `P_2`. For two-primary
coordinates write `mR+nS+cT`, with `c in {0,1}`, and put `d=n mod2`.
Its residue at three is `cA+dB`. At two its component is `c`, and
its normalized formal coordinate has parity `m+n` whenever `c=0`.
More precisely, choose an additive coordinate on `E_4(2)`; the
coefficients of both `R,S` are odd because both have exact depth four.
ETF4 makes that formal coordinate independent of `d` in finite
quotients, with both `c,d` uniform.

## 2. The exact joint image at seventeen

**Lemma J2171 (joint image and seventeen-adic branch discs).** One has

\[
 E_0(\mathbb F_{17})\simeq C_{18},\quad
 \overline P=\overline S=g=(2,14),\quad
 \operatorname{ord}(g)=9,\quad
 \overline T=(0,0),\quad\operatorname{ord}(\overline T)=2.
                                                               \tag{2.1}
\]

Its three branch residues for the valuation test `F` are

\[
 O,\quad(16,4),\quad(16,13).
                                                               \tag{2.2}
\]

They have `C_2` coordinate zero and `C_9` coordinates `0,3,6` in
the decomposition generated by `T,g`. The joint rational image has
the following properties:

1. the `C_2` coordinate at seventeen is `m+c mod2`;
2. the `C_9` coordinate is uniform and independent of the two-primary
   data and of the three-adic formal coordinate;
3. the formal seventeen-adic image is the whole `E_1(17)` and is
   independent of those finite data and of the other formal coordinates.

Within each branch residue disc in (2.2), the exact soluble Haar
fraction is `1/307`. In the finite quotient by `E_3(17)`, excluding
depths one and two leaves one of its `17^2=289` classes. Outside the
three branch residue discs, every point is locally soluble at seventeen.

**Proof.** The discriminant of the elliptic equation is a seventeen-adic
unit. Finite-field enumeration and addition verify (2.1)--(2.2).
The points `P,T` already generate the entire reduction group. Exact
rational addition gives

\[
 v_{17}(t([9]P))=1,\qquad
    t([9]P)/17\equiv10\pmod{17}.                  \tag{2.3}
\]

Equivalently `[18]R=2[9]P` has formal depth one. The formal-group
structure therefore gives the full formal seventeen-adic image.

The two-primary assertions use the odd-index statement ETF1, not a
basis claim for `G`. Any finite two-group image of `G` is computed
by `<R,S,T>`. Since `R=P+T`, its `C_2` coordinate at seventeen is
one, while that of `S` is zero; hence the coordinate is `m+c`.
Together with the normalized two-adic formal parity `m+n`, this
gives all two-primary correlations used below.

The only possible remaining entanglement is between `C_9` and the
three-adic formal group. It is resolved by exact depths:

\[
 v_3(t(R))=3,\qquad v_3(t([2]S))=2.                \tag{2.4}
\]

Subtract the local two-torsion part from `S` at three, and choose
an additive coordinate `E_2(3) ~= Z_3` in which this formal part
is a unit. The coordinate of `R` is divisible by three. Both
`R,S` have `C_9` coordinate one in (2.1). Thus the two rows giving
formal and `C_9` coordinates have the shape

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
             \qquad a\in3\mathbb Z_3,\quad b\in\mathbb Z_3^*.
                                                               \tag{2.5}
\]

Their determinant is a unit. This proves surjectivity onto
`E_2(3)/E_N(3) times C_9` at every finite level, and then onto
`E_2(3) times C_9`. ETC3a caps the actual formal image at `E_2(3)`.
At each finite level, `<R,S,T>` therefore gives the entire allowed
three-primary image; its odd index gives the entire two-primary
image, and (2.3) gives the full seventeen-primary formal image.
Integer Chinese remainder approximation in the coefficients of
`R,S,T` realizes the product of these primary images. The actual
group `G` cannot enlarge any of them. Thus the independence statements
follow from the proved joint image, rather than from the individual
local images.

For the local test, `F=0` has `x=25865/128`, reducing to `16`.
The equation gives precisely the two points in (2.2), with unit
`y` coordinates. In these discs `v_17(F)` equals the depth from
the corresponding local branch point. At `O`, if the formal depth
is `j`, then `v_17(x)=-2j`, and `v_17(F)=-2j`. Outside these
discs `F` is a unit. ELV1 therefore gives solubility exactly for
depth divisible by three in each branch disc. Its conditional mass is

\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}(1-17^{-1})17^{1-3k}
                =\frac1{17^2+17+1}=\frac1{307}.    \tag{2.6}
\]

The quotient by `E_3(17)` has `289` formal classes in each residue
disc; only the branch-centered class can have depth at least three.
This proves the finite necessary condition as well. \(\square\)

The formal subgroup and depth inputs here are those already retained
from Milne, *Elliptic Curves* (2006), Chapter II, Theorem 4.1 and
Lemma 5.9, pp. 62--63 and 67:
[primary book](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/ectext6.pdf),
[retained copy](sources/milne-elliptic-curves-2006.pdf).

## 3. A finite congruence bound strictly below one quarter

**Theorem J2172 (finite Hasse upper bound).** The Hasse-target count
on this existing elliptic slice satisfies

\[
 \boxed{A_E(B)\le\frac{1687}{6936}C_E\log B
                         +O_E(\sqrt{\log B})},
 \qquad \frac{1687}{6936}<\frac14.                 \tag{3.1}
\]

**Proof.** Use the two-adic quotient by `E_6(2)`, the three-adic
quotient by `E_3(3)`, and the seventeen-adic quotient by `E_3(17)`.
On the formal two-adic component, local solubility requires depth at
least six, leaving one of four formal classes. On the three-adic
residue discs `O,A,B,A+B`, finite necessary soluble fractions are
`1/3,0,1/3,1`, respectively, by ETC1. Without seventeen these give
the finite upper fraction `7/24` from ETF5.

Let `u mod4` be the normalized formal coordinate at two. Its parity
is `m+n`, so the `C_2` coordinate at seventeen is
`u+d+c mod2`. Subject to the finite local tests at two and three,
the seventeen-adic branch fraction is as follows.

| Residue at three | Conditions at two | Seventeen branch fraction | Joint mass before its formal test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `O` (`c=d=0`) | `u=0 mod4` | `1/3` | `1/144` |
| `A` (`c=1,d=0`) | irrelevant | irrelevant | `0` |
| `B` (`c=0,d=1`) | `u=0 mod4` | `0` | `0` |
| `A+B` (`c=d=1`) | automatic | `(1/2)(1/3)` | `1/24` |

For `B`, the seventeen-adic `C_2` coordinate is one, so no branch
is possible. For `A+B`, the parity of `u` is uniform and only one
parity gives `C_2=0`; the independent `C_9` then lands in three of
nine branch values. The total branch mass is `7/144`.
Lemma J2171 excludes `288/289` of those classes at the chosen finite
seventeen-adic level. The retained fraction is consequently at most

\[
 \frac7{24}-\frac7{144}\frac{288}{289}
        =\frac{1687}{6936}.                        \tag{3.2}
\]

Every condition is a union of cosets in one fixed finite quotient
of `G`. EEC1's lattice argument gives its main coefficient and
`O_E(sqrt(log B))` error. The sets are invariant under negation,
and the two generic point preimages of a target are `P,-P`, so the
same fraction applies to targets. Finite inadmissible or reducible
cubic exceptions change only `O_E(1)`. Every Hasse target passes
these necessary tests, proving (3.1). \(\square\)

## 4. The exact local-only proportion and the remaining problem

**Theorem J2173 (exact local-only density).** Let `M_{2,3,17}(B)`
count admissible full field-fibre targets locally soluble at these
three primes, without imposing the other finite places. Then

\[
 \boxed{M_{2,3,17}(B)
      =\frac{5321}{23946}C_E\log B+o(\log B).}       \tag{4.1}
\]

In particular

\[
 \limsup_{B\to\infty}\frac{A_E(B)}{C_E\log B}
               \le\frac{5321}{23946}\approx0.2222083.           \tag{4.2}
\]

**Proof.** The exact formal soluble mass at two is `a_2=1/7`.
At three the conditional masses in `O,A,B,A+B` are
`1/13,0,1/3,1`. Thus the exact two-and-three retained fraction is
`289/1092` by ETF5. Its portion in seventeen-adic branch residues
is

\[
 \frac1{24}+\frac{a_2}{12\cdot13}
              =\frac1{24}+\frac1{1092}.
\]

The first term comes from `A+B`, the second from `O`. The `B`
branch remains impossible because every soluble formal two-adic
point has even normalized coordinate. By (2.6), the fraction
`306/307` of this branch mass is locally insoluble at seventeen.
Therefore the exact local-only proportion is

\[
 \frac{289}{1092}
    -\left(\frac1{24}+\frac1{1092}\right)\frac{306}{307}
              =\frac{5321}{23946}.                \tag{4.3}
\]

To pass from finite quotients to this local-only count, truncate all
formal annuli and the branch-centred neighbourhoods at fixed depths.
The omitted Haar masses tend to zero. At each fixed truncation use
the proved finite joint image and the lattice count, first letting
`B` tend to infinity and then the truncation depth tend to infinity.
This proves (4.1). It does not give an error uniform in the truncation
depth, and no `O_E(sqrt(log B))` error with the coefficient in (4.1)
is asserted. \(\square\)

**Corollary J2174 (four exact fixed-field coefficients).** In the
count of Theorem J2173, the four quadratic fields have the following
separate coefficients, always relative to the whole-slice constant
`C_E`. Write `q_0=2709173` and let `delta` denote the squareclass
map of the elliptic arithmetic note.

| `delta` | Quadratic field | Coefficient `mu_K` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1` | `Q(sqrt(-3))` | `205/111748` |
| `5q_0` | `Q(sqrt(-40637595))` | `1/84` |
| `2` | `Q(sqrt(-6))` | `205/2456` |
| `10q_0` | `Q(sqrt(-81275190))` | `1/8` |

For each row, its full field targets locally soluble at `2,3,17`
satisfy

\[
 M_{2,3,17;K}(B)=\mu_K C_E\log B+o(\log B).
                                                               \tag{4.4}
\]

The four positive coefficients sum to `5321/23946`. In particular
the coefficient for the certified Hasse seed's field is exactly
`1/8`. Positivity here concerns this fixed set of three primes; it
does not assert positivity for every larger finite set in the other
three quadratic classes.

**Proof.** The global squareclasses are

\[
 \delta(R)=5q_0,\quad\delta(S)=1,\quad\delta(T)=10q_0.
\]

Thus in the coordinates of section 1,
`delta=(5q_0)^{m mod2}(10q_0)^c`. For `c=0`, soluble formal
two-adic points have even normalized coordinate, so `m mod2=d`.
The `O` and `B` residue discs consequently give squareclasses
`1` and `5q_0`, with two-and-three masses respectively
`(1/4)(1/7)(1/13)=1/364` and
`(1/4)(1/7)(1/3)=1/84`. For `c=1`, only `d=1` survives at
three. The two equally frequent parities of `m` give classes `2`
and `10q_0`, each of mass `1/8`.

The seventeen-adic `C_2` coordinate `m+c` is zero exactly for
classes `1,2`. In each of them the independent `C_9` coordinate
lands in the branch set with probability `1/3`. Their retained
factor is therefore

\[
 1-\frac13\frac{306}{307}=\frac{205}{307}.
\]

For classes `5q_0,10q_0` the `C_2` coordinate is one, so no
seventeen-adic branch disc occurs and every target passes that test.
Multiplying the four masses gives the displayed table. The same
finite-quotient approximation used in Theorem J2173 proves (4.4)
in each clopen squareclass. \(\square\)

Consequently the Hasse count with each fixed quadratic field satisfies
`limsup A_{E,K}(B)/(C_E log B)<=mu_K`. Since that field has matching
coefficient `C_E/4`, the corresponding limiting upper proportions
within its matching targets are, in the table order,
`205/27937, 1/21, 205/614, 1/2`. These are upper bounds, not Hasse
asymptotics or an assertion of Hasse infinitude in every class.

The certified Hasse point supplies `A_E(B)>=1` for sufficiently large
`B`. Neither upper bound proves Hasse infinitude or solubility at all
remaining primes. The still-open problem is the all-primes valuation
sieve in [ELV1](ELLIPTIC_EXCEPTION_EXPLICIT_LOCAL_CRITERION.md) on
this same elliptic slice.

## 5. Exact controls and scope

Run `.venv/bin/python research/arithmetic/verify_elliptic_exception_joint_2_3_17.py`.
The [checker](verify_elliptic_exception_joint_2_3_17.py) enumerates the
eighteen points over `F_17`, verifies the displayed point orders and
branch positions, computes the rational formal-depth witnesses,
and exhausts the finite parity/weight table giving (3.2). It also
checks all rational constants in (4.3), the descent classes of the
displayed generators, and the four fixed-field coefficients. The
[receipt](elliptic_exception_joint_2_3_17_checks.json) pins these
controls. The global saturation, local root classifications and
canonical-height argument retain their separate written and imported
scopes. No full Mordell--Weil basis, formal verification, external
review or all-primes Hasse-density theorem is claimed.
