# Exact cubic ray Selmer groups in an inert-prime Mordell family

Let \(k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-2})\) and let \(\mathcal P\) be the
set of rational primes \(p\nmid6\) with
\(p\equiv2\bmod3\) and \(\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)=-1\).
For a squarefree product \(R\) of primes in \(\mathcal P\), put
\[
 A=-2R^2,\quad E=E_A:y^2=x^3+A,\quad E'=E_{-27A},\quad
 \epsilon_R=\mathbf 1_{\{R\equiv\pm1\bmod9\}}.
\]
Write \(\psi:E\to E'\) for the rational \(3\)-isogeny with
kernel generated by \((0,\sqrt A)\), and \(\hat\psi\) for
its dual. The kernel fields are \(k\) and
\(\mathbf Q(\sqrt6)\), respectively. The local Selmer ratios
and Kummer evaluations used below are in
[Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman, Remark 25 and Propositions 33–36](https://math.huji.ac.il/~shnidman/j%3D0fixedtypos.pdf).
Their isogeny Selmer averages over acceptable sets of
coefficients concern a different ordering and family. Here
the local calculations determine the entire Selmer group
for every member of a family ordered by elliptic conductor.

## The cubic ray space and the two isogenies

**Theorem MS1.** For every such \(R\), there are identifications
\[
 \operatorname{Sel}_{\psi}(E/\mathbf Q)=
   \operatorname{Hom}\big(\operatorname{Cl}_{(3^{2\epsilon_R}R)}(k),
                          \mathbf F_3\big)^-,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Sel}_{\hat\psi}(E'/\mathbf Q)=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Sel}_3(E/\mathbf Q)\cong
                           \operatorname{Sel}_{\psi}(E/\mathbf Q).
 \tag{1}
\]
The superscript minus requires conjugation of \(k\) to act
by negation on the character. In particular the
\(3\)-Selmer dimension is exactly \(\omega(R)+\epsilon_R\).

The module \(E[\psi]\) has the sign action of \(k\).
Restriction to \(k\) identifies its rational cohomology
with the anti-invariant continuous cubic characters of the
absolute Galois group of \(k\), because \([k:\mathbf Q]=2\)
is prime to \(3\). The dual Kummer description is
\[
  H^1(\mathbf Q,E'[\hat\psi])
  \cong (k^\times/k^{\times3})_{N=1}.
  \tag{2}
\]
In (2) evaluation on \(E(\mathbf Q)\), or on the local
curve over a completion, uses \(y-R\sqrt{-2}\).

At primes away from \(6R\) the local Kummer images are
unramified by good reduction, and the real cohomology is
zero for modules of odd order. At \(2\), the curves on both
sides have type II and Tamagawa number one, from valuation
one of the constant coefficient. Neither kernel is split
there. Both local images are therefore zero. An unramified
cubic character of the ramified quadratic field
\(k\otimes\mathbf Q_2\) is conjugation-invariant, so the
condition on the forward anti-invariant character is
equivalently unramified there.

At \(p\mid R\), both curves have type IV, and the Tamagawa
numbers of \(E,E'\) are \(1,3\), respectively: the unit
part after dividing the constant coefficient by \(p^2\)
is nonsquare for \(E\) and square for \(E'\). This follows
from \(\left(\frac{-2}{p}\right)=-1\),
\(\left(\frac{-3}{p}\right)=-1\), and
\(\left(\frac{6}{p}\right)=1\).
The local Selmer ratios of the isogenies are \(3,1/3\),
with kernel orders \(1,3\), respectively. Thus the dual
Kummer image is zero. The forward image has order three
and is all of the local cohomology: the local character
space over the unramified quadratic field \(k_p\) has an
invariant valuation coordinate, with just one anti-invariant
coordinate from the residue units \(\mathbf F_{p^2}^\times\),
on which Frobenius acts as inversion modulo cubes.
Equivalently all tame anti-invariant ray characters at that
prime are allowed.

At \(3\), the field \(k\) splits. Choose \(s=\sqrt{-2}\)
in \(\mathbf Q_3\) with \(s\equiv4\bmod9\).
Here \(E'[\hat\psi](\mathbf Q_3)=0\), since the coefficient
\(-27A\) has odd \(3\)-adic valuation. Applying
Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman Proposition 34 to \(E'\),
whose coefficient is \(27(2R^2)\), gives
\(c_3(\hat\psi)=1\) when \(R\equiv\pm1\bmod9\)
and \(3\) otherwise. In the latter case evaluation at
\((0,-Rs)\in E(\mathbf Q_3)\) gives the class of
\(-2Rs\), which is congruent to \(R\bmod9\). This is a
noncube unit and hence generates the entire dual image
of order three. Thus the dual local image consists
only of units in both cases. By local Tate duality
the forward image is its exact annihilator. It is all
of \(H^1(\mathbf Q_3,\mathbf F_3)\) when
\(R\equiv\pm1\bmod9\), and the unramified line otherwise:
local reciprocity pairs the unit subgroup with the
restriction of a character to units.
[Fisher, Lemma 2.4](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~taf1000/papers/ctp.pdf)

This gives exactly the ray modulus in (1). At each of the
two split primes over \(3\), a cubic character has conductor
exponent at most two since \(1+9\mathbf Z_3\) consists of
cubes. The field \(k\) is norm Euclidean by rounding two
coefficients, which gives norm error at most \(3/4\), and
its only units are \(\pm1\). Thus the ray exact sequence
gives one anti-invariant dimension for every \(p\mid R\)
and one further when the modulus includes \((9)\):
the order-three quotients of the two local unit groups at
\(3\) are interchanged, giving one minus direction.
For the dual Selmer group, every class in (2) has valuations
divisible by three at every prime, by the local descriptions.
The class number one and the fact that the units are cubes
force the class itself to be zero. Finally
\(E'(\mathbf Q)[\hat\psi]=0\) and the dual Selmer group is zero,
so the composed-isogeny Selmer exact sequence gives the
last identification in (1).
[Bhargava–Elkies–Shnidman, equation (22)](https://math.huji.ac.il/~shnidman/j%3D0fixedtypos.pdf)

**Corollary MS2.** Nonzero lines of \(\operatorname{Sel}_3(E/\mathbf Q)\)
correspond to isomorphism classes of noncyclic cubic fields
with quadratic resolvent \(k\) whose exact discriminant has
the form
\[
     -8\big(3^{2e}m\big)^2,\qquad m\mid R,\quad
     0\le e\le\epsilon_R,\quad \omega(m)+e>0 .
 \tag{3}
\]
For each such \(m,e\) the number of fields is
\(2^{\omega(m)+e-1}\).

In fact a nonzero line gives a cyclic cubic field over \(k\),
with conjugation acting by inversion, and its normal closure
over \(\mathbf Q\) has Galois group \(S_3\). Its relative
conductor over \(k\) is the rational ideal \((3^{2e}m)\).
Each nonzero local coordinate at \(p\mid R\) has conductor
one at the inert prime. At \(3\) a nonzero anti-invariant
ramified coordinate has conductor two at both primes
because \(\mathbf F_3^\times\) has order two.
The relative conductor-discriminant relation gives
\(\operatorname{Disc}(F)=\operatorname{Disc}(k)(3^{2e}m)^2\)
for its cubic field \(F\).
[Cohen–Thorne, §1 and §3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.3563)
There are \(2^{\omega(m)+e}\) characters with exactly these
nonzero coordinates, and each nonzero line consists of two
characters. As exact controls, the lone cubic field for
\(R=1\) is generated by a root of \(x^3-3x-10\), of
discriminant \(-648\); for \(R=5\) it is generated by a root
of \(x^3-x^2+2x+2\), of discriminant \(-200\).

## Conductor-ordered moments

Let \(N(E)\) denote the elliptic conductor. The three
classes of \(R\) modulo \(9\) up to sign give
\[
  N(E)=N_jR^2,\qquad
  N_1=N_4=1728,\quad N_2=576\quad
  (R\equiv\pm j\bmod9,\ j\in\{1,2,4\}).
  \tag{4}
\]
At \(2\), type II and \(v_2(\Delta)=6\) give conductor
exponent six. At each \(p\mid R\), type IV and
\(v_p(\Delta)=4\) give conductor exponent two. At \(3\)
translate \(x=X-1\) to put the singularity at the
origin. Then \(a_2=-3,a_4=3,a_6=A-1\) and
\(b_8=-12(A-1)-9\). For \(A\equiv4,7\bmod9\), Tate's
type II test applies with \(v_3(a_6)=1\), giving exponent
three. For \(A\equiv1\bmod9\), \(v_3(b_8)=2\) gives type
III and exponent two. The latter case is exactly
\(R\equiv\pm2\bmod9\). These are the minimal models
at the primes in question, as their discriminant
valuations are below twelve. The formula was also checked
on \(R=1,5,29,71\) by exact local reduction in PARI/GP.
[Tate, §7 steps 3–5](https://wstein.org/Tables/antwerp/tate/tate.pdf)

**Corollary MS3.** For any fixed real \(\nu\ge0\), set
\(z=3^\nu\) and
\[
 G_z=\lim_{s\to1^+}(s-1)^{z/4}
                  \prod_{p\in\mathcal P}(1+z p^{-s}) .
\]
This is positive and finite. Summing over the isomorphism
classes of the family with \(N(E)\le Y\) gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{R\ {\rm in\ the\ family}\\N(E)\le Y}}
      |\operatorname{Sel}_3(E/\mathbf Q)|^\nu
 \sim
 \frac{G_z}{3\Gamma(z/4)}
       \left(\frac{z+1}{\sqrt{1728}}+\frac1{\sqrt{576}}\right)
       \sqrt Y\,\bigl(\tfrac12\log Y\bigr)^{z/4-1}.
 \tag{5}
\]
For \(\nu=0\) this counts the family. For example the
mean size of its \(3\)-Selmer group has order
\((\log Y)^{1/2}\).

Here distinct squarefree \(R\) give distinct rational
isomorphism classes because the ratio of two corresponding
coefficients can be a rational sixth power only when the
two squarefree \(R\) are equal. The set \(\mathcal P\) is
a union of six reduced residues modulo \(72\) and has prime
density \(1/4\); its primes have equal prime densities
\(1/12\) at each of \(2,5,8\bmod9\).
For a character \(\chi\) of
\((\mathbf Z/9\mathbf Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\), consider the
squarefree-supported multiplicative function with prime
value \(z\chi(p)\) on \(\mathcal P\) and zero elsewhere.
Its mean on primes is \(z/4\) for the trivial character
and zero for the other two. The fixed-modulus prime
number theorem gives the weighted prime-sum hypothesis
of
[de la Bretèche–Tenenbaum, Theorem 1.2 and equation (1.10)](https://tenenb.perso.math.cnrs.fr/PPP/On-SD.pdf)
with arbitrarily large logarithmic saving. Their prime-power
summability hypotheses hold because the prime values
are bounded and all higher prime-power values are zero.
For the trivial character the leading constant is
\(G_z/\Gamma(z/4)\); \(G_z>0\) follows from the
convergent prime-sum difference \(\sum_p
(z\mathbf 1_{\mathcal P}(p)-z/4)/p\) and absolute
convergence of higher prime powers. For the two
zero-mean characters all expansion coefficients vanish
by the reciprocal gamma factors. Orthogonality therefore
gives the same leading sum
\(G_z X(\log X)^{z/4-1}/(3\Gamma(z/4))\)
in each class up to sign modulo \(9\). In that sum set
\(X=(Y/N_j)^{1/2}\) and multiply the \(j=1\) term by \(z\),
as dictated by (1). This yields (5) without replacing a
fixed-support estimate by an uncontrolled sum over \(R\).
The moment formula addresses Selmer sizes in this conductor
family; the distribution of their rationally soluble
classes remains separate.
