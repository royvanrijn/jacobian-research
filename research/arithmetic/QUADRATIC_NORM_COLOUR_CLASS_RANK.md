# Independent ideal classes carried by one norm coefficient

A fixed coefficient in a coherent prime path records a Mordell–Weil
constraint as well as a rational point count. The differences of ideal
classes carried by its edges span a space of dimension at most the
Jacobian rank. A binary sextic shows that one fixed coefficient can
carry two independent classes on a single Mordell curve for
\(\gg X^{1/3}\) distinct imaginary quadratic fields of discriminant
at most \(X\).

## From one colour to one class subspace

**Proposition CR1.** Fix an odd prime \(\ell\) and a squarefree signed
integer \(D\) defining a quadratic field \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\) with
\(K\not\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\). Fix \(w\in\mathbb Q^\times\).
Suppose a collection of nonzero elements
\(\alpha=(u+v\sqrt D)/2\) and fractional ideals \(\mathfrak a\) satisfy
\[
 (\alpha)=\mathfrak a^\ell,\qquad N(\alpha)=n^\ell,\qquad
 v=w b^\ell,\quad b\in\mathbb Q^\times,\ n\in\mathbb Q^\times .
\]
Write \(J_w\) for the Jacobian of \(C_w:y^2=x^\ell+Dw^2/4\). The ideal
classes \([\mathfrak a]\) span a subspace of \(\mathrm{Cl}(K)[\ell]\)
of dimension at most \(\operatorname{rank}J_w(\mathbb Q)\). If a set of
\(r\) classes is independent, the corresponding \(r\) points
\[
              P=\left(\frac{n}{b^2},\frac{u}{2b^\ell}\right)
\]
give independent divisor classes \([P-\infty]\) modulo torsion.

Put \(W=(0,w\sqrt D/2)\). The function \(f=y-w\sqrt D/2\) has
divisor \(\ell(W-\infty)\). Evaluation on degree-zero divisors induces
a homomorphism \(J_w(K)\to K^\times/K^{\times\ell}\) by Weil
reciprocity. For a divisor through \(\infty\), use the leading
coefficient of \(f\) there in the parameter
\(x^{(\ell-1)/2}/y\). This coefficient is \(1\). Equivalently, in the
moving-divisor proof, reciprocity uses the leading coefficients at
common points of the two divisors. The sign from the coefficient at
infinity is itself an \(\ell\)-th power. The image of \([P-\infty]\)
is therefore
\[
                 [f(P)]=[\bar\alpha/b^\ell]=[\bar\alpha].
\]
On the subgroup generated by these divisors, all evaluations are virtual
units, meaning their valuations are multiples of \(\ell\). Mapping
\([\beta]\) in the virtual units to the class of the fractional ideal
whose \(\ell\)-th power is \((\beta)\) gives a homomorphism to
\(\mathrm{Cl}(K)[\ell]\). On the indicated generators its images are
\(-[\mathfrak a]\).

There is no rational \(\ell\)-primary torsion in \(J_w\). Over
\(\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\), let \(\tau\) be the automorphism from
\(x\mapsto\zeta_\ell x\) and let \(\phi=1-\tau\). The differentials
\(x^j\,dx/y\), \(0\le j<(\ell-1)/2\), have eigenvalues
\(\zeta_\ell^{j+1}\), and their complex conjugates supply the other
nontrivial eigenvalues on first homology. Thus
\(\deg\phi=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(1-\zeta_\ell^i)=\ell\), and
\([\ell]\) is a unit in \(\mathbb Z[\tau]\) times \(\phi^{\ell-1}\).
The nonzero class \([W-\infty]\) generates \(\ker\phi\), since it
is fixed by \(\tau\) and a function with just one simple pole on
\(C_w\) would force genus zero. The nontrivial automorphism of
\(K(\zeta_\ell)/\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\) sends \([W-\infty]\)
to its negative, using the divisor of \(x\). If a nonzero
\(\ell\)-torsion point were rational over \(\mathbb Q\), its last
nonzero iterate under \(\phi\) would be a nonzero kernel point
rational over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\), a contradiction.

The class map on the subgroup generated by the \([P-\infty]\) thus
kills its torsion, which is prime to \(\ell\). The dimension bound
follows from its free rank. An integral dependence modulo torsion of
\(r\) selected points can be taken with primitive coefficient vector:
from a relation with torsion sum, divide all the coefficients by their
gcd, and the new sum is still torsion. Independence of the images
rules out this primitive relation.

For \(\ell=3\), [Elkies, Section 2.1 and Lemma 1](https://antsmath.org/ANTSXVI/papers/Elkies.pdf)
give the same evaluation on the coefficient-one minimal model and
show that its entire rational image lies in the virtual units under
their squarefree discriminant hypotheses. Proposition CR1 uses the
virtual-unit condition on the supplied witnesses for each \(w\).
The [quintic Jacobian comparison](QUINTIC_NORM_JACOBIAN_SELMER_MOMENTS.md)
bounds the rank from above by class ranks and local terms.

## The class labels in a coherent prime graph

**Corollary CR2.** In a coherent prime graph of exponent \(\ell\) as in
the [signed prime-path construction](ABC_COHERENT_PRIME_PATH_HIGHER_ODD_TORSION.md#coherent-witnesses-with-a-bounded-unit-logarithm),
assume the field satisfies CR1. Label the vertex of \(\mathfrak p_i\)
by its class relative to one base vertex in the common
\(\ell\)-torsion coset. An edge has colour \(w\) when \(v_{ij}\) has
class \(w\) modulo \(\ell\)-th powers in \(\mathbb Q^\times\).
For every collection \(S\) of colours, each connected component of the
subgraph with colours in \(S\) has at most
\[
             \ell^{\sum_{w\in S}\operatorname{rank}J_w(\mathbb Q)}
\]
distinct labels. For a complete graph on \(s\) distinct labels,
\[
        s-1\ \le\ \sum_w\bigl(\ell^{\operatorname{rank}J_w(\mathbb Q)}-1\bigr),
\]
summing over the colours at any one vertex.

In fact \((\alpha_{ij})=(\overline{\mathfrak p}_i\mathfrak p_j)^\ell\),
so for each colour its edge differences lie in the class subspace of
CR1. Paths with colours in \(S\) have endpoint differences in the sum
of those subspaces. At one vertex, the nonzero differences of a given
colour are distinct when the labels are distinct. A rank-zero colour
can only join equal labels. Primes with a repeated label can still give
genuine edges of that colour. The bound measures the rank needed for
different ideal classes to coexist in one colour, while the
[fixed-colour graph count](ABC_COHERENT_CUBIC_GRAPH_MORDELL_LANG.md)
bounds the number of prime realizations. Summing either over the colours
that actually occur at prime-path scales still requires a bound on their
support or distribution.

## A squarefree sextic with two independent classes and points

**Theorem CR3.** There are \(\gg X^{1/3}\) distinct positive squarefree
\(d\le X\), with \(d\equiv19\bmod24\), for which there exist coprime odd
integers \(m,n\asymp d^{1/3}\) and positive integers \(y_1,y_2\) with
\[
       m^3-y_1^2=n^3-y_2^2=d,\qquad
       \gcd(m,y_1)=\gcd(n,y_2)=1.
\]
In \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\), the cube roots of the principal ideals
\((y_1+\sqrt{-d})\) and \((y_2+\sqrt{-d})\) define independent classes
of order three. The integral points \((m,y_1),(n,y_2)\) on
\(E_d:y^2=x^3-d\) are independent modulo torsion. In conductor order
this supplies \(\gg Y^{1/6}\) distinct \(E_d\) at conductors at most
\(Y\). The norms \(m,n\) in this theorem are unrestricted by primality.

For parameters \(a,u>0\) set \(b=4u\) and
\[
 \begin{split}
 m &= 3a^2-2b^2,\qquad n=3a^2+b^2,\qquad
 F_\pm=3a^2\pm3ab+b^2,\\
 y_1 &= (b/4)(12F_+-3F_-)
       =u(27a^2+180au+144u^2),\\
 y_2 &= (b/4)(12F_++3F_-)
       =u(45a^2+108au+240u^2).
 \end{split}
\]
Here \(n^3-m^3=9b^2F_+F_-=y_2^2-y_1^2\). Their common radicand is
\[
 \begin{split}
 F(a,u)&=m^3-y_1^2\\
       &=27a^6-1593a^4u^2-9720a^3u^3-30960a^2u^4
           -51840au^5-53504u^6 .
 \end{split}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
The choice of how to factor the square difference is important. For
example, the symmetric choice \(y_i'=(b/2)(3F_+\mp3F_-)\) gives the
same difference of squares, but \(y_2'=3bn\) and the common radicand
becomes \(n^2(n-9b^2)\).

Work in the rectangle \(200L\le a\le201L\), \(L\le u\le2L\) for
large \(L\). Then \(a\ge100u\), \(m,n\asymp L^2\) and \(F\asymp L^6\).
For the last estimate the sum of the absolute values of the terms
after \(27a^6\), divided by \(a^6\), is less than \(1\) on this
rectangle. Require \(a\) odd, \(4\mid u\), \(3\nmid u\),
\(23\mid u\), and \(a\equiv1\bmod23\). These give \(F\equiv3\bmod8\)
and \(F\equiv1\bmod3\). Squarefree values have \(\gcd(a,u)=1\) by
homogeneity. Then \(m,n\) are odd, prime to \(3u\), and coprime to
each other since \(n-m=48u^2\). Modulo \(m,n\) respectively,
\[
       y_1/u\equiv36u(12u+5a)\pmod m,\qquad
       y_2/u\equiv108au\pmod n .
\]
A prime shared by \(y_1\) and \(m\) would divide \(12u+5a\) and
\(25m\equiv-368u^2\) modulo that prime. This forces the prime \(23\),
which is excluded by the parameter congruences. A prime shared by
\(y_2\) and \(n\) would divide \(a\), then also \(u\).

Both norm factors above every prime dividing \(m\) or \(n\) are
coprime. Indeed a common prime ideal divisor of either element
and its conjugate would divide \(2y_i\), and the rational primes
in the norm are odd and coprime to \(y_i\). It follows that every
supporting rational prime is split and occurs on one side only.
The factors are ideal cubes with cube-root ideals \(I,J\) of norms
\(m,n\). Any principal ideal among \(I,J,IJ,I\bar J\) would have an
integral generator of norm \(m,n\) or \(mn\). Their one-sided
factorizations on disjoint prime supports exclude a rational
generator. The norm of a nonrational integer in the imaginary
quadratic field is at least \(d/4\), whereas \(mn\ll L^4<d/4\).
This proves independence of \([I],[J]\), since any nonzero
relation in their three-torsion span would make one of these four
ideals principal up to inversion. Apply CR1 with \(\ell=3,D=-d,w=2\)
to the two integral points.

The discriminant of \(F(x,1)\) is
\(2^{38}3^{36}5^2\cdot7\cdot23^3\), so the binary form has no repeated
factor. This identity can be checked by the resultant with its
derivative. For the squarefree sieve, take a finite set of primes
containing \(2,3,5,7,23\). At each of these primes other than \(3\),
choose \((a,u)\equiv(1,0)\) modulo its square, giving \(F\equiv27\).
At \(3\), choose \((a,u)\equiv(1,1)\bmod9\), giving \(3\nmid F\).
The same local choices at the remaining primes show positive local
factors. Outside the finite set the defect in the squarefree
local density is \(O(p^{-2})\), by the simple projective roots and
the pairs with both coordinates divisible by \(p\).
[Manduchi, Proposition 1.1 and Remark 1.1](https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1995__99_1_33_0.pdf)
apply since every factor has degree at most six. Subtracting the
four rectangle asymptotics gives \(\gg L^2\) pairs in the indicated
subrectangle, squarefree outside the finite set. Inside the set
the imposed congruences already make \(F\) a unit.

To count distinct \(d\), \(F\) has a real linear factor since
\(F(0,1)<0\) and the leading coefficient in \(a\) is positive.
[Stewart–Xiao, Lemma 2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.03427)
then bounds the parameter pairs with \(0<|F|\le Z\) whose values
are represented outside their rational automorphism orbit by
\(O_\varepsilon(Z^{3/(4\sqrt6+3)+\varepsilon})\).
With \(Z\asymp L^6\) this is \(o(L^2)\). The rational automorphism
group of \(F\) is finite since it permutes six distinct projective
roots with finite scalar kernel. Each remaining value accounts for
only a bounded number of pairs. Scaling \(L\) as a small constant
times \(X^{1/6}\) gives the lower bound.

At every prime \(p\ge5\) dividing \(d\), the discriminant of the
displayed Mordell model has valuation \(2\) and the model is
minimal with tame additive reduction, of conductor exponent \(2\).
At primes \(p\ge5\) away from \(d\) it has good reduction. At \(2,3\)
there is a uniform bound on the conductor exponent, also seen
directly from the finitely many classes of the unit \(-d\) modulo
sixth powers in \(\mathbb Q_p^\times\). Thus \(N(E_d)\ll d^2\)
and its set of prime factors outside \(2,3\) recovers \(d\).
This proves the conductor statement.

For example, \((a,u)=(9201,92)\) gives
\[
 (m,n)=(253704355,254110627),\quad
 (y_1,y_2)=(224421506676,359083409772),\quad
 d=11\cdot241475561\cdot6128812975362169 .
\]
The identities, primality of these three factors and the
discriminant of (1) can be checked in PARI/GP using
`isprime` and `poldisc`. The norm argument gives independence
for this example without computing a class group or rank.

[Craig's irregular discriminants construction](https://www.i-repository.net/contents/osakacu/sugaku/111F0000002-01402-7.pdf)
uses several coordinated representations by square-minus-cube
forms to construct quadratic fields of high three-rank.
[Stewart–Top, Section 11](https://www.math.rug.nl/~top/StewartTop.pdf)
gives rank lower bounds for families of sixth-power-free Mordell
twists. The count here imposes squarefreeness of the displayed
coefficient and exhibits the two independent ideal classes and
points together. The prime-path applications would additionally
need appropriate prime norms or prime products in prescribed
windows.
