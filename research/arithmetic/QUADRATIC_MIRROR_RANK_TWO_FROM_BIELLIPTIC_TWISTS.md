# Rank two in both quadratic mirrors from a bielliptic sextic

A genus-two curve with two degree-two projections onto a
fixed elliptic curve gives two independent cubic norm
classes. On a single set of explicit local conditions both
classes are virtual units, both are cubes at three, and the
two associated twist points are independent. The degree-six
field parameter permits a squarefree-value count with no
logarithmic loss.

## Independent classes on the genus-two curve

**Theorem BT1.** Put \(A=61,\ B=19\) and
\[
 E:\ y^2=x^3+(8x+9)^2,\qquad
 G(t)=-A(t^2+1)\bigl(B(t^2+1)^2-A^2t^2\bigr).
\]
For \(t\ne0\) on \(C:Y^2=G(t)\), set
\[
 X_1=-\frac{B(t^2+1)}{A},\quad X_2=\frac{X_1}{t^2},
 \quad x_i=X_i-1,\quad y_1=\frac{BY}{A^2},\quad y_2=\frac{y_1}{t^3}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The two maps \((x_i,y_i):C\longrightarrow E\) have degree
two. The corresponding twist over \(\mathbb Q(t)\) has
rank exactly two, and the two norm functions
\[
 \alpha_i=y_i+8x_i+9=y_i+1+8X_i,\qquad
 \operatorname N(\alpha_i)=-x_i^3
 \tag{2}
\]
give independent cubic classes over the algebraic closure
of the constant field.

In the coordinate \(X=x+1\), the cubic on \(E\) is
\(F(X)=X(X^2+AX+B)\). It has a rational point of order
two at \(X=0\), and its marked point of order three
has \(X=1\). Substituting (1) gives
\[
 F(X_1)=\frac{B^2}{A^4}G(t),\qquad
 F(X_2)=\frac{F(X_1)}{t^6}.
\]
These are the two-abscissa formulas of
[Rubin–Silverberg, Corollary 3.3](sources/rubin-silverberg-rank-frequencies-quadratic-twists.pdf),
with the parameter scaled by \(B\). The two factors of
\(G\) have disjoint simple roots. For the quartic factor
this follows by treating it as a quadratic in \(t^2\),
whose constant term is \(B\) and discriminant is
\(A^2(A^2-4B)\). Thus \(C\) is smooth of genus two.
Both abscissa maps have degree two. The involution
\(t\mapsto-t,\ Y\mapsto Y\) fixes the first elliptic
point and negates the second, so their pushforwards from
\(\operatorname{Jac}C\) occupy opposite eigenspaces.
Each is surjective and the joint map to \(E^2\) is an
isogeny. This gives the exact rank since
\(\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q} E=\mathbb Z\) and
anti-invariant rational maps modulo constants inject into
the rational Hom group from \(\operatorname{Jac}C\) to \(E\).

The function \(y+8x+9\) has divisor \(3((0,-9)-O)\).
Pullback along either degree-two map preserves the
nonzero divided divisor class by pushforward. The two
pulled-back classes lie in opposite involution eigenspaces
of the \(3\)-torsion, proving independence.

This choice of degree-two maps matters for marked cubic
classes. In the neighboring construction of
[Rubin–Silverberg, Corollary 3.5 and Remark 3.6](sources/rubin-silverberg-rank-frequencies-quadratic-twists.pdf),
the second projection to \(E\) factors through the dual of
the isogeny with kernel generated by the mark on \(E\).
Pullback along that dual kills the marked divided divisor,
because its map on \(\operatorname{Pic}^0\) is the
original isogeny. A pulled-back norm function with trivial
divided-divisor class is a constant times a cube. The
rational constant is a cube as well, since its square is
a cube by the quadratic norm identity. Thus the marked
pair in that construction can have two independent
Mordell–Weil points and supplies at most one cubic class.
Earlier genus-two constructions already give rational
Jacobian \(3\)-torsion of rank two, for example
[Levin–Yan–Wiljanen, proof of Theorem 9](sources/levin-yan-wiljanen-large-three-rank.pdf).
The conditions below concern simultaneous mirror classes
and rank on one fixed quadratic twist.

## Local conditions, mirror classes, and elliptic conductors

**Theorem BT2.** Let \(0<t<1/20\) be rational and impose
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 t&\equiv1\pmod{8},& t&\equiv1\pmod{3^8},&
 t&\equiv1\pmod7,\\
 t&\equiv3\pmod{13},&t&\equiv0\pmod5,&t&\equiv2\pmod{19}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3}
\]
Write \(d\) for the signed squarefree part of \(G(t)\) and
\(D\) for the discriminant of \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)\).
Then \(K\) is imaginary, \(2\mid d\), and \(3,5,19\)
split in \(K\). Both \(K\) and
\(K'=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3d})\) have \(3\)-rank at least two.
The real mirror \(K'\) has at least four associated
totally real cubic fields of fundamental discriminant
\(3|D|\), whose normal closures are split over \(3\)
as extensions of \(K'\). The primes above \(7,13\) have
independent classes modulo cubes in the mirror class group.
The \(d\)-twist of \(E\) has rank at least two and
conductor \(285D^2\).

The sign condition follows from \(A^2t^2<B\).
At \(2\) both \(X_i\) have valuation one and the \(x_i\)
are odd, so the cube norms in (2) are units.
In addition \(G(t)/2\equiv1\bmod8\), using
\(t^2\equiv1\bmod16\), which gives the local squareclass
of \(2\). At an odd prime other than \(3\), a pole in
\(x_i\) gives \(2v(y_i)=3v(x_i)\) and \(y_i\) dominates
\(8x_i+9\). Thus its valuation is divisible by three.
If \(x_i\) is integral, positive valuation of \(\alpha_i\)
forces \(x_i\equiv0\) at the prime by its cube norm.
Then \(8x_i+9\) is a unit and at most one norm conjugate
has positive valuation, necessarily a multiple of three.

At \(3\), at the point \(t=1\), both linear terms
\(1+8X_i\) equal \(-243/61\), and
\[
 \frac{y_1^2}{3^6}=\frac{3610}{226981}\equiv1\pmod9 .
\]
The rational functions \(y_i^2\) and \(1+8X_i\) have
denominators prime to \(3\) near \(1\), so the congruence
modulo \(3^8\) preserves the residue of \(y_i^2/3^6\)
modulo nine and gives \(1+8X_i\equiv0\bmod3^5\).
The field splits at \(3\) and \(\alpha_i/27\equiv\pm1\bmod9\)
at both primes. Such a unit is a cube in \(\mathbb Q_3\).
Hence both elements are cubes there and virtual units
everywhere.

The two remaining split evaluations are independent.
In the indicated branches, raising the residues in (2)
to \((q-1)/3\) gives
\[
 \begin{array}{c|r|r|c}
 q & t\bmod q & y_1\bmod q &
 (\alpha_1^{(q-1)/3},\alpha_2^{(q-1)/3})\bmod q\\ \hline
 7  &1&3&(4,4)\\
 13 &3&2&(9,1).
 \end{array}
\]
The cube norms mean that changing the split branch
inverts the row. Thus both virtual units are independent
in \(K^\times/K^{\times3}\) uniformly on (3).
The class-group and mirror descent now follow from the
argument of
[MT2](QUADRATIC_MIRROR_RANK_TWO_FROM_MARKED_MESTRE_TWISTS.md#the-common-local-conditions-and-the-mirror-classes).
It applies to any imaginary quadratic field other than
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\) and an independent virtual norm
pair with both directions local cubes at \(3\). The four
cyclic cubic subextensions of the mirror's unramified
degree-nine extension are split above \(3\).
Both \(7,13\) split completely in \(K(\zeta_3)\).
Their Frobenius on the two radicals is given by the cube
residue rows above. The Frobenius elements therefore
generate the unramified degree-nine quotient of
\(\operatorname{Cl}(K')\), proving independence of the
two split prime classes modulo cubes. In the four cubic
fields the combinations \((i,j)\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_3)\)
are split at \(7\) exactly when \(i+j=0\) and split at
\(13\) exactly when \(i=0\). All other cases at these
primes are inert.

The division polynomial
\(x(3x^3+256x^2+864x+972)\) has irreducible cubic factor.
Thus there is no nontrivial anti-invariant \(3\)-torsion
in \(E(K)\), and the independent cubic evaluations imply
rank at least two on the twist, by the same
[Cohen–Pazuki evaluation](sources/cohen-pazuki-2009-three-isogeny.pdf)
as in MT2. In the coordinates
\(x'=4dx,\ y'=8d\sqrt d\,y\) the fundamental parameter
is \(D=4d\) and the linear term is \(8x'+36d\).
The evaluation at the sign of the mark in (2) is
\((2\sqrt d)^3\alpha_i\).
The base conductor is \(1140=2^2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot19\).
The residues of \(G\) at \(5,19\) are \(1,7\), respectively,
and are nonzero squares. At \(3,5,19\) the twist is therefore
locally isomorphic to \(E\). At \(2\) it is isomorphic to
\(E^{(2)}\), whose conductor has \(2\)-part \(2^6\).
All other primes dividing \(d\) are odd good primes of \(E\)
and give conductor exponent two on twisting. Thus the
conductor is \(4560d^2=285D^2\), as \(D=4d\).
The [exact checker](verify_bielliptic_mirror_rank_two.py)
replays the two maps, the division polynomial and local
computations, including the fixed conductors.

## Counting distinct mirror pairs and twists

**Corollary BT3.** For all sufficiently large \(X\), at
least \(cX^{1/3}\) distinct imaginary quadratic fields of
absolute discriminant at most \(X\) satisfy BT2, with \(c>0\)
fixed. Their mirrors are distinct of discriminant at most
\(3X\). The number of the corresponding twists of conductor
at most \(Z\) is \(\gg Z^{1/6}\).

Homogenize the sextic as \(H(U,V)=V^6G(U/V)\).
Let \(M=8\cdot3^8\cdot7\cdot13\cdot5\cdot19\) and
let \(t_0\bmod M\) be the simultaneous residue (3).
Use \(U=a+b,\ V=21(a+b)+b\), so for positive \(a,b\)
one has \(0<U/V<1/20\). Impose
\[
 b\equiv1-21t_0\pmod M,\qquad
 a\equiv t_0-b\pmod M.
\]
Then \(V\equiv1\bmod M\) and \(U/V\equiv t_0\bmod M\).
The transformed binary form has degree six, nonzero
discriminant, and irreducible factors of degree at most
four. Apply
[Stewart–Top, Theorem 1 and its proof, Section 6](sources/stewart-top-ranks-of-twists.pdf)
with \(k=2\). It gives \(\gg X^{1/3}\) distinct
squarefree integers of absolute size at most \(X\)
represented by the form after dividing by its fixed
square divisor in the stated congruences. Their proof
takes \(a,b>0\), so all values here are negative.
These are distinct squareclasses of \(G(U/V)\), since
\(V^6\) and the divisor removed are squares. Apply
BT2 with the harmless scaling of the bound by four
to pass from \(|d|\) to \(|D|\). The discriminant and
conductor conclusions follow from BT2.

## Fixed doubled profiles with no subpower counting loss

**Corollary BT4.** The distinct-field lower counts in
[DM2](MORDELL_CUBIC_DOUBLED_SIMULTANEOUS_TWISTS.md#counting-distinct-simultaneous-twists),
[DJ3](MORDELL_DOUBLED_JACOBIAN_COMPLEMENT.md#three-simultaneous-marked-quadratic-twists),
[VP2–VP3](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_VIRTUAL_PRODUCT_FAMILY.md),
and [MC2](ABC_CUBIC_TANGENT_MIRROR_CLASS_FAMILY.md#fixed-weighted-profiles)
are each \(\gg X^{1/6}\) on their existing scopes.
Their corresponding counts ordered by the largest of the
specified elliptic conductors, where applicable, are
\(\gg Z^{1/12}\).

For each fixed \(m=b^2=36,1225,225\) the equation for the
field is
\[
 G_m(s)=(ms^3-1)(ms^3+1)(m^2s^6+4ms^3-1).
\]
The four roots of \((w^2-1)(w^2+4w-1)\) are distinct
and nonzero. Thus \(G_m\) is squarefree of degree
twelve, with factors of degree at most six. This is
the \(k=2,\ m=6\) allowance in Stewart–Top, Theorem 1.
Each existing family specifies an open real interval and
finitely many nonempty local parameter conditions,
including the conditions in DJ3, VP3 and MC2 as written.
Choose coprime integers \(r,v\) with \(r/v\) in the
required interval and \(v>0\), and a second fraction
\(r'/v'\) in the interval with \(rv'-r'v=1,\ v'>0\).
The latter follows by taking one Bézout solution and
adding a large multiple of \((r,v)\). For positive \(a,b\),
\[
 s=\frac{ra+r'b}{va+v'b}
\]
lies in that interval. Choose a rational point satisfying
the finitely many local conditions by weak approximation,
and represent it by a primitive numerator and denominator.
Congruences modulo a sufficiently divisible fixed \(M\)
on this pair preserve each local condition, including any
denominator valuation. The integral change of variables
has determinant one, so compatible congruences on \(a,b\)
give exactly this numerator and denominator congruence.
Theorem 1 now yields distinct negative squarefree values
up to the fixed square divisor, as in BT3, with exponent
\(2/12\). The conductors on the stated families are fixed
multiples of \(d^2\). This argument concerns fixed
profiles. The varying-profile count MC3 additionally
requires joint separation of fields and coefficient
classes.
