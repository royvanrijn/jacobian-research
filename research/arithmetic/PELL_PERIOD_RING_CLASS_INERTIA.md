# Pell periods and divisibility of ring class inertia

The Pell period for consecutive powerful integers contains a local
unit index at each ramified rational prime. The index determines
whether the prime contributes to the relative ring class kernel.
The finite class group also determines how far one must go in
degree to detect that inertia. These are different conditions.
An explicit family below realizes arbitrarily large detection
degrees using an actual moving prime ideal.

The conductor exact sequence used here is
[Klüners–Pauli, Theorem 5.6](sources/kluners-pauli-picard-2005.pdf).
[Pitoun–Varescon, Propositions 2.5–2.6](sources/pitoun-varescon-p-ramified-1302.3099v2.pdf)
give the containing tower of ray groups for the full \(p\)-ramified
abelian module. We use the part on which quadratic conjugation acts
by inversion, and track a specified inertia subgroup and a specified
prime ideal. In the prime-radicand setting the unit coefficient
condition belongs to the Ankeny–Artin–Chowla and Mordell problems.
Here the radicands are allowed to be composite, and the condition
on the class group extension is indispensable.

## The full relative order and the powerful-pair period

**Proposition PI1.** For squarefree \(D>1\), take
\(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\), \(O_0=\mathbb Z[\sqrt D]\), and let
\(\epsilon_D=T_D+U_D\sqrt D>1\) be the fundamental totally
positive unit of \(O_0\). Put
\[
 r=\operatorname{lcm}(4,D),\qquad O_1=\mathbb Z+r\sqrt D\,\mathbb Z,
 \qquad H_D=\gcd(D,U_D).
\]
The relative narrow ring class kernel
\(\ker(\operatorname{Pic}^+(O_1)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}^+(O_0))\)
has order \(r/L_D\), where \(L_D\) is the
[exact powerful-pair period multiplier](ERDOS_365_CONSECUTIVE_POWERFUL_TARGET.md#exact-count-by-pell-periods).
Its odd part is \(\prod_{p\mid H_D,\ p\text{ odd}}C_p\).
Its two-part is the dyadic residue quotient divided by the local
image of \(\epsilon_D\). The dyadic quotient before taking this
image is \(C_4\) if \(D\) is even and \(C_2^2\) if \(D\) is odd.
In particular the weight of this field in the powerful-pair
logarithmic limit is exactly
\[
 \frac1{\tau_D}
  =\frac{|\ker(\operatorname{Pic}^+(O_1)\to\operatorname{Pic}^+(O_0))|}
             {r\log\epsilon_D}.
\]

For an inclusion of two orders, the relative exact sequence follows
by writing both narrow groups as the quotient of the finite ideles
by the completed order units and totally positive principal ideles.
Its kernel is the relative local unit quotient modulo the image of
the larger order's totally positive units. Here it is
\[
 \frac{(O_0/rO_0)^\times}{(\mathbb Z/r)^\times\langle\epsilon_D\rangle}.
\]
At an odd prime \(p\mid D\), the local factor before taking the
unit image is the additive group \(\mathbb F_p\):
\([a+b\sqrt D]\) maps to \(b/a\bmod p\), since \((\sqrt D)^2\)
vanishes modulo \(p\). At two, use \(r_2=4\).
If \(D\) is odd, an \(a+b\sqrt D\) is a unit modulo four exactly
when its coefficients have opposite parity. The four classes modulo
scalar units are generated by the two independent involutions
\([\sqrt D]\) and \([1+2\sqrt D]\). If \(D\) is even then \(a\) is
odd and there are again four classes. The square of \([1+\sqrt D]\)
is \([1+2\sqrt D]\) and hence the quotient is cyclic of order four.

The class of \(\epsilon_D\) has order \(L_D\) in the product
since \(\epsilon_D^j\) is scalar modulo \(r\) precisely when
\(r\mid U_{D,j}\). At an odd prime it has order \(p\) exactly when
\(p\nmid U_D\). At two its order is one or two by the
modulo-four calculation in the exact Pell period. These orders
are pairwise coprime across the local factors, so dividing by the
single unit gives the asserted separate primary kernels.

## Inertia in cyclic quotients and exact discriminants

**Theorem PI2.** Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\) be real quadratic
of discriminant \(\Delta\) with \(D\) squarefree and \(p\mid D\)
odd. Put \(O(p)=\mathbb Z+pO_K\) and
\(G=\operatorname{Pic}(O(p))[p^\infty]\).
Let \(I\) be the local inertia kernel of \(G\to\operatorname{Cl}(K)[p^\infty]\).
For a fundamental unit of \(O_K\) written \(a+b\sqrt D\)
with \(a,b\in\mathbb Z_p\), \(I=C_p\) exactly when \(p\mid b\);
otherwise \(I=0\). In the nonzero case define
\[
 h_p=\max\{j\ge0:I\subseteq p^jG\}.
\]
The least degree of a cyclic \(p\)-power quotient of \(G\)
ramified at \(p\) is \(p^{h_p+1}\).
There is such a degree-\(p\) quotient exactly when the
local map on all virtual \(p\)-classes is zero. If \(v_p(b)=1\),
the \(p\)-primary inverse system of ring class groups of conductor
\(p^k\) stabilizes at \(k=1\). Thus the degree assertion then holds
throughout the abelian anticyclotomic \(p\)-extensions
unramified away from \(p\) over \(K\).

More generally, take any set \(S\) of odd ramified rational primes
and a cyclic odd-degree extension \(W/K\) from the ring class group
of conductor \(\prod_{p\in S}p\), of degree \(m\). Let \(S_W\)
be its set of primes with nontrivial relative inertia. Its
generalized dihedral degree-\(m\) subfield \(L/\mathbb Q\)
is totally real and has
\[
 \operatorname{Disc}(L)
     =\Delta^{(m-1)/2}\prod_{p\in S_W}p^{(p-1)m/p}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Conversely, any totally real dihedral extension with cyclic
rotation group of odd order, quadratic subfield \(K\), and
relative conductor dividing \(\prod_{p\in S}pO_K\), comes from
this ring class group.

At a ramified \(p\), write \(\mathfrak p\) for the unique prime of
\(K\) above it. The local quotient of \(O_K^\times\) in the
conductor exact sequence is
\[
 T_p=O_{K_\mathfrak p}^\times/
       \big(\mathbb Z_p^\times(1+pO_{K_\mathfrak p})\big)
       \simeq \mathbb F_p .
\]
Its map is \(a'+b'\sqrt D\longmapsto b'/a'\bmod p\).
A global unit then kills this line or maps to zero, which
gives \(I\). Conjugation acts by inversion on the ring class
group: the product of an ideal and its conjugate is rational
principal, on ideals prime to the conductor. Narrow and ordinary
groups have identical odd-primary parts. For comparison with PI1,
the ramified prime condition for the fundamental unit of \(O_K\)
agrees with \(p\mid U_D\) whenever \(p\) does not divide the
index of totally positive \(O_0\)-units in \(O_K\)-units.
Only \(p=3\) can divide that index for an odd \(p\): at two,
the first-order residue quotient for the inclusion
\(\mathbb Z[\sqrt D]\subset O_K\) has order at most three.

If \(I\ne0\), any cyclic quotient killed by \(p^j\) with \(j\le h_p\)
kills \(I\). In a direct sum of cyclic primary factors of \(G\),
a generator of \(I\) has in each nonzero coordinate of
order \(p^e\) the form \(u p^{e-1}\) for a unit \(u\).
The smallest such \(e\) is \(h_p+1\), and projection to
that coordinate gives the claimed first degree.
For the virtual-class test, let
\[
 V_p(K)=\{\alpha\in K^\times:(\alpha)=\mathfrak a^p\}/K^{\times p}.
\]
Choose a representative that is a unit at \(\mathfrak p\), using a
\(p\)th power to remove its valuation. Its image in \(T_p\)
is well-defined, since a different choice changes it by a local
unit \(p\)th power. The unit line has zero image when \(I\ne0\).
The connecting map
\[
 \operatorname{Cl}(K)[p]\longrightarrow I
\]
takes a class represented by \(\mathfrak a\) to \(p\) times a
lift in \(G\). By the ideal form of the conductor sequence it
takes the image of \(\alpha\) with \((\alpha)=\mathfrak a^p\)
to its local class, up to the common choice of reciprocity sign.
Its image is \(I\cap pG\), proving the equivalence.

For stabilization, the local scalar unit quotient modulo \(p^k\)
has order \(p^k\). If a generating global unit has \(v_p(b)=1\),
raising it to \(p^j\) makes the coefficient valuation \(1+j\).
The first term of the binomial expansion at each \(p\)th power
has that valuation and all other terms are deeper, since \(p\mid D\)
and \(v_p(b)\ge1\). An exponent prime to \(p\) preserves the
valuation. Thus its image has order \(p^{k-1}\) in the quotient
and the relative kernel has order \(p\) at all \(k\).
The transition maps are surjective and have equal group orders.
Any finite abelian odd-primary extension of \(K\) unramified
outside \(p\), with conjugation acting by inversion, kills the
rational scalar local units under reciprocity. Such units are
fixed by conjugation and have odd-order images. Its finite conductor
therefore places it in one of these ring class quotients.
The same argument proves the converse in (1).

For the discriminant, let \(A\) be the cyclic translation group
of order \(m\). At an odd \(p\in S_W\), relative inertia has order
\(p\). The unramified relative Frobenius at \(\mathfrak p\) is
trivial in \(A/I\) because the unique prime \(\mathfrak p\) is
fixed by conjugation, whereas conjugation inverts \(A/I\).
Thus the local group over \(\mathbb Q_p\) is \(C_p\rtimes C_2\).
Its cyclic part over \(K_\mathfrak p\) has nontrivial character
conductors of exponent two: conductor divides \(pO_{K_\mathfrak p}
=\mathfrak p^2\), and \((O_K/\mathfrak p)^\times\) has order \(p-1\).
By the conductor-discriminant formula and the quadratic
discriminant exponent one, the local normal field of degree
\(2p\) has exponent \(p+2(p-1)=3p-2\).
The permutation representation on its \(p\) reflection
cosets is the trivial character plus each of the
\((p-1)/2\) two-dimensional representations once.
The regular representation has those two-dimensional
representations twice and also the sign character of conductor
one. The degree-\(p\) reflection field consequently has
exponent \(3(p-1)/2\).

On \(A\), the reflection acts by \(-1\) after translating the origin.
On \(A/I\), zero gives one orbit of length \(p\), and the
\((m/p-1)/2\) opposite pairs give orbits of length \(2p\).
The discriminant exponent at \(p\) is therefore
\(((3p-2)m/p-1)/2\), an increase of \((p-1)m/p\)
over the quadratic contribution. At any other rational prime
ramified in \(K\) the inertia is quadratic. In the permutation
representation it acts with \((m-1)/2\) sign directions, which
gives the quadratic discriminant contribution even at two.
Other primes are unramified. Since the relative odd-degree
extension of the real quadratic field has only real embeddings,
\(L\) is totally real. The conductor calculation uses
[Milne, Theorem 3.27](sources/milne-cft403.pdf).

## A simultaneous family with genuine prime classes

**Theorem PI3.** Fix a nonempty finite set \(S\) of odd primes, and
positive integers \(a_p\) for \(p\in S\). Put
\(B=\prod_{p\in S}p\) and \(m=\prod_{p\in S}p^{a_p}\).
There are \(\gg_{S,(a_p)} X^{1/(2m)}/(\log X)^2\)
distinct real quadratic fields of discriminant at most \(X\)
for which the following hold simultaneously.

- \(D\) is even, \(p\mid D\) and \(v_p(U_D)=1\) for every
  \(p\in S\).
- An actual split rational prime \(q\) has an oriented prime
  ideal class of exact order \(m\) in \(\operatorname{Cl}(K)\).
  Its lift by contraction to the order of conductor \(B\)
  has exact order \(Bm\). Its \(m\)th power generates the whole
  relative inertia kernel, which is cyclic of order \(B\).
- For every \(p\in S\), the inertia of order \(p\) in the
  anticyclotomic \(p\)-group unramified away from \(p\) over \(K\)
  lies in \(p^{a_p}G\). Every cyclic quotient ramified at \(p\)
  has degree at least \(p^{a_p+1}\).

Choose an even integer \(e>0\) divisible by every \(p-1\) for \(p\in S\).
By CRT and the prime number theorem in fixed arithmetic
progressions one can choose an odd prime \(s\notin S\) such that
\[
 Bc=2^e s+1,\qquad c\equiv1+2p\bmod p^2 \quad(p\in S)
\]
for a positive integer \(c\). Indeed prescribing \(c\) modulo \(p^2\)
gives \(2^e s+1\) modulo \(p^3\) for every \(p\), and
\(s\equiv-1\bmod p\) because \(2^e\equiv1\bmod p\).
The class for \(s\) is reduced, and \(\gcd(s-1,m)=1\).
Set \(A=2(Bc-1)\).
The integer \(c\) is odd, \(\gcd(c,A)=1\) and
\(\gcd(m,\varphi(8A))=1\).

Use primes \(q\) in one reduced progression such that
\[
 q^m\equiv c^2+2A\bmod 8A,\qquad q\equiv1\bmod B.
 \tag{2}
\]
The first congruence is solvable in units by the coprime exponent.
We now choose one auxiliary prime \(\lambda_p\equiv-1\bmod B\)
for each \(p\), all distinct, sufficiently large and prime to \(A\).
At \(\lambda_p\) the map \(q\mapsto q^m\) is bijective.
The Chebyshev polynomial \(T_p\), characterized by
\(2T_p((u+u^{-1})/2)=u^p+u^{-p}\), omits at least two
values modulo \(\lambda_p\). To count the omitted values, each
argument is a trace divided by two either from the split
multiplicative group or from the norm-one group in
\(\mathbb F_{\lambda_p^2}\). They meet only at \(\pm1\).
The power map is bijective in the split group and has image
of index \(p\) in the norm-one group. This gives
\((\lambda_p+1)(p-1)/(2p)\) omitted arguments in the image.
It follows that we can require a nonzero residue of \(q\)
for which
\[
 1+B^2(q^m-c^2)/A\notin T_p(\mathbb F_{\lambda_p}).
 \tag{3}
\]
Each such condition is compatible with (2).

Put \(n=(q^m-c^2)/A\), and write \(d=n(B^2n+2)=r_0^2 D\)
with \(D\) squarefree. For large \(q\), \(n>0\).
Condition (2) gives \(n\equiv2\bmod8\), hence \(v_2(d)=3\).
At every \(p\in S\), \(q^m\equiv1\bmod p^2\) and
\(c^2\equiv1+4p\bmod p^2\), so \(v_p(n)=v_p(d)=1\).
In particular \(O_K=\mathbb Z[\sqrt D]\) and \(r_0\) is a
unit at every \(p\in S\). Two elements in the same field have norms
\[
 u=1+B^2n+B r_0\sqrt D,\qquad N(u)=1,\qquad
 \alpha=Bn+c+r_0\sqrt D,\qquad N(\alpha)=q^m.
 \tag{4}
\]
Condition (3) shows \(u\) is not a \(p\)th power of a totally
positive integral unit for any \(p\in S\). In fact
\(u=\epsilon_D^j\), and if \(p\mid j\), its half-trace is
\(T_p\) of the integral half-trace of \(\epsilon_D^{j/p}\).
Thus \(p\nmid j\). Comparing coefficients in the binomial
expansion modulo \(p\), and then their valuations, proves
\(v_p(U_D)=v_p(Br_0)=1\).

For \(q\nmid c(Bc-2)A\) we have
\(Bn+c\equiv c(Bc-2)/A\bmod q\ne0\).
Then \(d\) is a nonzero square modulo \(q\), \(q\nmid r_0\),
and \(q\) splits in \(K\). The whole ideal of \(\alpha\) is
\(\mathfrak q^m\) at one oriented prime above \(q\) since
\(N\alpha=q^m\) and only one conjugate vanishes modulo \(q\).
At every \(p\in S\), \(\alpha\) has unit scalar coefficient
\(c\bmod p\) and nonzero radical coefficient \(r_0\bmod p\).
If the class of \(\mathfrak q\) had order dividing \(m/p\),
then \(\alpha\) would be a \(p\)th power times a global unit,
contradicting its nonzero image in \(T_p\).
Every global unit has zero image there by the fundamental
unit condition in PI2. This proves the exact order \(m\).
In the order of conductor \(B\), the \(m\)th power of the lift
has the local classes of \(\alpha\), each a generator of its
factor \(C_p\). The exact order is \(mB\).
Its projection to the \(p\)-primary conductor-\(p\) group
therefore shows \(I\subseteq p^{a_p}G\).
PI2 transfers this condition to all higher conductor powers.

Finally, for primes \(q\le Y\) in the fixed progression,
\(d\ll_{S,(a_p)}Y^{2m}\) and \(1<u\ll_{S,(a_p)}Y^m\).
Distinct primes give distinct half-traces of \(u\).
The number of totally positive units in a fixed real quadratic
field up to this size is \(O_{S,(a_p)}(\log Y)\) uniformly:
the ratio of consecutive units exceeds one and has integral
trace at least three. With \(\gg Y/\log Y\) primes available,
the distinct-field count follows. This requires no squarefree-value
estimate for \(d\).

## An exact first detection degree

**Corollary PI4.** For \(D=5615=5\cdot1123\) and \(p=5\) the local
inertia survives, \(h_5=1\), and the least ramified cyclic
degree over \(K\) is \(25\). Its degree-25 reflection field
has discriminant \(22460^{12}5^{20}\).

The fundamental Pell unit is \(1124+15\sqrt{5615}\).
For \(0<b<15\), the integer \(5615b^2+1\) is strictly
between \((75b-1)^2\) and \((75b)^2\).
At \(b=15\) it equals the lower square.
The element \(542+9\sqrt{5615}\) has norm \(-11^5\) and
is supported on a single prime over 11.
Its local image at five is \(9/542\bmod5\ne0\).
Hence the ordinary prime class has order five, the kernel is
in \(5G\), and the order of \(G\) is at least 25.
For the class-number bound use
\(h(K)\log\epsilon_D=(\sqrt{22460}/2)L(1,\chi_{22460})\).
This is [Dirichlet’s formula in the normalization on p.267](sources/bradley-ozluk-snyder-class-number-p267.pdf).
A norm-minus-one unit is impossible since \(5615\equiv3\bmod4\).
The finite sum over one character period, with
partial summation on the tail, gives
\(L(1,\chi_{22460})<2\). The accompanying checker gives
the stronger rational upper bound \(23196575211/22460000000\).
Also \(\log\epsilon_D>7\) since
\(\epsilon_D>2187>e^7\).
Thus \(h(K)<150/7<25\), so the \(5\)-part of the class
number is exactly five, and \(G=C_{25}\).
Equation (1) gives the discriminant. Stabilization applies
since \(v_5(15)=1\).

## Verification and remaining count

The [exact checker](verify_pell_period_ring_class_inertia.py)
checks the parity and residue groups, the norm and CRT identities,
a genuine simultaneous prime fixture with \(B=15,m=45,s=479,c=511,q=11690712001\), and the exact degree-25
example. The class field and counting conclusions use the written
arguments. [AW3–AW4](PELL_PRIME_PATH_WILD_AFFINE_FIELDS.md)
use the same moving prime to prescribe affine Frobenius
and compute all wild character conductors over the composite
cyclotomic mirror. The period weight in PI1 makes the relative inertia
product exact. PI3 shows that a uniform search solely for
degree-\(p\) extensions would miss such factors even when continued
through a fixed number of higher degrees. The full weighted sum
over \(D\), or a uniform upper bound on the number of kernels
with small \(L_D\log\epsilon_D\), remains open.
