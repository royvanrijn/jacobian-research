# Full-level signed Hecke lattices suffice for the Frey degree bound

For a primitive positive triple \(a+b=c\), put \(R=\operatorname{rad}(abc)\)
and \(h=\log c\). Let \(E\) be the Frey curve and \(N=N_E\). Its conductor
is squarefree away from \(2\). For the triples with at least two odd
primes in \(R\), choose the factorization \(N=DM\) from the
[small-level prescription](ABC_CUBIC_ADJOINT_SHIMURA_PROJECTORS.md#a-small-level-projector-target).
Thus \(D\) is odd, \(M\) contains the full \(2\)-part and two or three odd
primes, and all primes in \(D\) are multiplicative for \(E\).
Let \(J=J_0^D(M)\), \(\delta=\delta_{D,M}\), and \(P\) be the rational
projector onto its optimal elliptic quotient in the isogeny class of \(E\).

Let \(U\) be the set of primes exactly dividing \(M\), including \(2\)
when \(2\parallel N\). For \(p\in U\), let \(X_p\) be the geometric
toric character lattice of \(J\) at \(p\). Let \(Y_p\subset X_p\) be the
intersection of \(X_p\) with the rational space spanned by the
cuspidal eigensystems of **exact conductor \(N\)** with the
Atkin–Lehner signs of \(E\), using the action on \(J\). This is a saturated lattice containing
the Frey line. Write \(d_{Y_p}(P)\) for the least positive integer
making \(P\) integral on \(Y_p\), and put
\[
             B=\operatorname{lcm}_{p\in U}d_{Y_p}(P).
\]
There is an absolute integer \(C\) such that
\[
                 B\mid\delta\mid
                 C\,2^{\omega(N)}B\prod_{r\in U}v_r(\Delta_E).
 \tag{HN1}
\]
Here \(\Delta_E\) is the minimal discriminant. In particular the
sharp bound \(B\ll_\varepsilon R^{2+\varepsilon}\) is equivalent to
abc. Every competing eigensystem in these lattices has the full
conductor and the signs of \(E\), at every coefficient prime,
including \(2\).

## Trace differences at a prime exactly dividing the level

Fix \(p\in U\). Rational Jacquet–Langlands identifies \(X_p\) with the
degree-zero module of the definite quaternion algebra of discriminant
\(Dp\) and Eichler level \(M/p\). In particular all its cuspidal
eigensystems have conductors dividing \(N\) and are Steinberg at
\(Dp\). The identification is integral for geometric characters and
supersingular divisors. It works for \(p=2\) when \(2\parallel M\).
[Kohel, §§3.2 and 4.3, Theorem 4.3](https://www.i2m.univ-amu.fr/perso/david.kohel/pub/hecke.pdf).
[Buzzard, Theorems 4.7 and 4.10](https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~buzzard/maths/research/papers/shimura.pdf).

Fix a coefficient prime \(\ell\) and put \(X_{p,\ell}=X_p\otimes
\mathbb Z_\ell\). Use the \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-algebra of operators on
\(X_{p,\ell}\) generated by \(T_s\) for primes \(s\nmid N\ell\).
It contains the tuple \(t(\gamma)\) acting on each eigensystem \(g\)
by \(\operatorname{tr}\rho_{g,\ell}(\gamma)\), for every
\(\gamma\in G_{\mathbb Q}\). To see this, choose one finite
\(\ell\)-adic extension that contains all the finitely many coefficient
embeddings, and stable lattices for their representations. For each
precision, apply Chebotarev to the joint finite quotient defined by
reduction of those lattices. A good Frobenius conjugate to \(\gamma\)
in that quotient gives \(T_s\) converging to the specified tuple as
precision increases. The Hecke algebra is a finite \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)
submodule of \(\operatorname{End}(X_{p,\ell})\), so its image in the
product of the coefficient fields is closed. This uses the trace
of each characteristic-zero representation, with no irreducibility
hypothesis on a residual representation.
[Carayol, §0.7, Hecke polynomials and the associated local representations](https://numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.1512.pdf).

Let \(r\in U\setminus\{p\}\) with \(r\ne\ell\), and let
\(n_r=v_r(\Delta_E)>0\). For \(\tau\in I_r\) and
\(\sigma\in G_{\mathbb Q}\) the operator
\[
                   t(\tau\sigma)-t(\sigma)
 \tag{HN2}
\]
kills every constituent unramified at \(r\), collectively and in
characteristic zero. These are precisely the constituents whose
conductor omits \(r\). On \(E\), the local Tate basis gives a choice
of \(\tau\in I_r\) with
\(\rho_{E,\ell}(\tau)=1+n_r e_{12}\), since the inertia character of
the unit part of the Tate parameter is trivial for \(\ell\ne r\).
An unramified quadratic twist does not change this formula. Thus
the scalar of (HN2) on the Frey line is
\(n_r(\rho_{E,\ell}(\sigma))_{21}\).
[Tate, Galois-compatible uniformization of the multiplicative curve](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/tate/tate-unif/tate-unif.pdf).
[The inertia matrix for the Frey Tate curve](ABC_CUBIC_ADJOINT_TAMAGAWA_FACTORS.md).

If every such lower-left entry is divisible by \(\ell^b\), the first
basis vector spans a \(G_{\mathbb Q}\)-stable cyclic subgroup of
\(E[\ell^b]\). The Mazur–Kenku bound for rational cyclic isogenies gives
\(\ell^b\le163\). With \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,163)\), one can
therefore take (HN2) with a nonzero scalar on the Frey line of valuation
at most \(v_\ell(L n_r)\). For \(\ell>163\) the cost is exactly
\(v_\ell(n_r)\). The choice of one \(\sigma\) kills the whole old
subspace at \(r\) and the cost is independent of its dimension.
[Kenku, proof and classification of rational cyclic degrees](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82233672.pdf).

## Removing a prime power from the old subspace at bounded cost

At a split local prime \(r\), suppose the Eichler level has exponent
\(a\ge2\). Let
\[
 K_0(r^i)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}u&b\\c&v\end{pmatrix}
                  \in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z_r):c\in r^i\mathbb Z_r\right\},
 \qquad t=\operatorname{diag}(1,r).
\]
The two degeneracy spaces at exponent \(a\) are the fixed spaces
of \(K_0(r^{a-1})\) and \(t K_0(r^{a-1})t^{-1}\). Their sum on
each cuspidal eigensystem is the space of smaller conductor
exponent, by the local newvector dimension formula. Here is an
explicit integral cost for projecting away that sum.

Conjugate every group by \(t^{-1}\). The two groups \(H_1,H_2\)
are now subgroups of \(H=K_0(r^{a-2})\). The defining additional
condition on \(H_1\) is \(b\equiv0\bmod r\), and \(H_2\) requires
\(c\equiv0\bmod r^{a-1}\). Their intersection \(H_0\) is the
conjugated level \(K_0(r^a)\). The two subgroups generate \(H\),
as in the calculations below. For a cuspidal constituent of exponent
\(b\le a-2\) the two fixed spaces each have dimension \(a-b\),
and their intersection has dimension \(a-b-1\). When \(b=a-1\)
the two lines have zero intersection. Thus their sum fills the
level-\(a\) space in both cases. Both fixed spaces are zero when
\(b=a\).

If \(a\ge3\), the groups \(H_1,H_2\) each have index \(r\) in \(H\)
and the two restrictions have joint index \(r^2\). This follows
by triangular decomposition in \(H\), where both diagonal entries
are units. Hence \(H_1H_2=H_2H_1=H\). Denote normalized subgroup
averages by \(e_1,e_2,e_H\). The two averages commute and their
product is \(e_H\), by counting fibers in the product of the
subgroups on a finite quotient. On the \(H_0\)-fixed space the
projector onto the orthogonal complement of the two fixed spaces is
\[
                1-e_1-e_2+e_H.
\]
It has denominator dividing \(r^2\) on any invariant integral
function lattice, because the averages there use at most their
indices over \(H_0\).

If \(a=2\), quotient the common compact group by the principal
congruence subgroup. This gives \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_r)\),
with \(H_1,H_2\) two opposite Borel subgroups and \(H_0\) their
diagonal intersection. On the \(H_0\)-fixed space the projector
onto the sum of the two fixed subspaces is
\[
        e_H+\frac{r^2}{r^2-1}(e_1+e_2-e_1e_2-e_2e_1).
 \tag{HN3}
\]
Indeed the permutation representation on the projective line is
the direct sum of the constants and an irreducible complement,
by double transitivity. These are the only irreducible constituents
with a Borel-fixed vector. In the complement the two Borel-fixed
lines are generated by the centered indicator functions at the
two fixed points. Their normalized inner product is \(-1/r\).
Thus \(e_1+e_2\) has eigenvalues \(1\pm1/r\) on their span, and
\(2(e_1+e_2)-(e_1+e_2)^2\) acts there by \(1-1/r^2\).
On the constants the parenthesis in (HN3) vanishes. The formula
follows on every representation, by complete reducibility.
The indices \([H_i:H_0]=r\) and \([H:H_0]=r(r+1)\) show that
the complementary projector has denominator dividing \(r(r^2-1)\).

These averages act on finite sets of definite quaternion classes.
One may first refine the compact level to a normal open subgroup of
\(H\) contained in \(H_0\), with the other local factors fixed.
Integral functions at level \(H_0\) are the invariant integral
functions on this cover, so the same counting denominators apply.
Degree-zero divisors are the elements of the dual of the integral
function lattice that annihilate the constant function. The
transpose endomorphism acts on them with the same denominator,
because the projector kills constants. The Hecke action away from
\(r\) on functions is the double coset action, whose transpose on
divisors is the sum of the corresponding ideal classes. Thus the
projector has the stated action on the conductor summands of
\(X_p\otimes\mathbb Q\). No equivariance for a bad-prime \(U_r\)
operator is required. In particular, when \(v_2(N)\ge2\), a
projector with denominator dividing \(12\) removes every smaller
\(2\)-part of the conductor on \(X_p\), also at coefficient prime
\(\ell=2\).
[Casselman, Theorem 1 and its fixed-vector dimension formula](https://lesesvre.perso.math.cnrs.fr/newforms-references/casselman.pdf).
[Baruch–Purkait, §5 and Theorem 9 on local averages detecting the newspace](https://www.math.titech.ac.jp/top/~purkait/Paper5_Newspace%28withMoshe%29.pdf).

## Recovering the Frey degree from the smaller lattices

At each \(\ell\) choose \(p=\ell\) if \(\ell\in U\), and any
\(p\in U\) otherwise. The characteristic-zero eigensystems of
\(X_p\) already have the required conductor at \(D\) and \(p\).
For each of the other primes exactly dividing \(M\), the operator
(HN2) is available by this pivot choice. Multiply these operators,
and if \(v_2(N)\ge2\) multiply by the integral multiple of the
\(2\)-new projector from the previous section. This maps
\(X_{p,\ell}\) into the intersection with the rational newspace,
and acts by a scalar on the Frey line. Its valuation there is
bounded above by \(v_\ell(12\prod_{r\in U\setminus\{p\}}L n_r)\).
The factors preserve the needed rational summands by their
characteristic-zero description.

For the sign restriction multiply by
\(\sum_{w\in\mathcal W}\epsilon_E(w)w\), where \(\mathcal W\) is
the group of Atkin–Lehner involutions on \(J\) and \(\epsilon_E\)
is the sign character. This integral operator preserves the
newspace, has image in the sign summand, and acts on the Frey line
by \(|\mathcal W|\), which divides \(2^{\omega(N)}\).
Call the resulting integral operator \(S\) and its scalar on the
Frey line \(\lambda\). It commutes with \(P\), since the Heckes,
the newspace projector and the sign operators preserve the Frey
line and its rational complement. In particular \(PS=\lambda P\),
and the image of \(S\) is in \(Y_p\otimes\mathbb Z_\ell\). Thus
\[
 v_\ell(d_{Y_p}(P))
 \ \le\ v_\ell(d_{X_p}(P))
 \ \le\ v_\ell(d_{Y_p}(P))+v_\ell(\lambda).
 \tag{HN4}
\]
The first inequality uses saturation of \(Y_p\). For the second,
\(d_{Y_p}(P)PS\) is integral on \(X_{p,\ell}\). Equivalently the
projector denominator on a lattice is the exponent of its gluing
quotient, namely the quotient by the sum of its intersections with
the line and the kernel of the projector.

By the [component formula (MN1)](ABC_FREY_MONODROMY_NORMS.md#exact-integral-projector-on-the-torus),
\(d_{X_p}(P)=\delta/j_p\), with \(j_p\mid v_p(\Delta_{E_0})\) for
the optimal quotient \(E_0\). This formula applies at the possible
pivot \(p=2\) as well, since \(J\) is semistable and \(E_0\)
multiplicative there. Pasten's isogeny comparison gives
\(v_p(\Delta_{E_0})\mid L n_p\). Combining (HN4) with the pivot
chosen for each \(\ell\) gives (HN1), for example with
\(C=12 L^4\) because \(|U|\le4\). For an arbitrary admissible \(M\)
the same proof gives \(12L^{|U|}\) in place of \(C\).
[Pasten, Lemma 6.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09251).

Every \(n_r\) in (HN1) is \(O(h+1)\), also when \(r=2\),
by the Frey discriminant formula. Therefore
\[
       \log B=\log\delta+O(\omega(N)+\log(h+2))
             =h+\log R+O\!\left(\frac{h}{\log(h+2)}\right),
\]
using [(SP9)](ABC_CUBIC_ADJOINT_SHIMURA_PROJECTORS.md#uniform-shimura-transfer).
The elementary families with at most one odd prime are treated in
the [degree comparison](ABC_CUBIC_ADJOINT_SHIMURA_PROJECTORS.md#a-small-level-projector-target).
This proves the claimed equivalence. It takes at most four lattices
at a single auxiliary level. At any odd coefficient prime
\(\ell>163\) where the chosen simple level primes have
\(\ell\nmid n_r\), the full and restricted projector denominators
at the selected pivot agree \(\ell\)-adically.

The quantitative level-lowering theorems of Kim–Ota and Moakher
compare congruence ideals when imposing Steinberg conditions.
Their hypotheses include residue-characteristic and residual-image
conditions, and Moakher's Theorem B requires nontrivial Steinberg
anchors. The integral inequalities above concern a projector on
an explicit lattice, including characteristic \(2\) after
completion. They use a direct trace annihilator and local
degeneracy averages. The open estimate concerns congruences among
full-level eigensystems with the same signs, including the possible
accumulation of several such congruences.
[Kim–Ota, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02926).
[Moakher, Theorems A and B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15410).
