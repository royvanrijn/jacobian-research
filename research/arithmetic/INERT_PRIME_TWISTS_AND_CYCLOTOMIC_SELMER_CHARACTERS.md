# Exact Selmer character growth at complex-conjugation primes

Fix an odd prime \(\ell\), let \(K_0=\mathbf Q(\zeta_\ell)\),
and fix a squarefree integer \(d\ne0,1,(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell\).
Write \(k=\mathbf Q(\sqrt d)\), \(B=K_0k\), and
\(J_A=\operatorname{Jac}(y^2=x^\ell+A)\). For \(A=d w^2\),
identify the \(1-\zeta_\ell\) isogeny Selmer group
\(S(w)\) over \(K_0\) with a subspace of the anti-invariant
Kummer space in \(B^\times/B^{\times\ell}\).
Let \(\iota\) generate \(\operatorname{Gal}(B/K_0)\), and let
\(\tau\) fix \(k\) and send \(\zeta_\ell\) to \(\zeta_\ell^g\),
for a generator \(g\) of \(\mathbf F_\ell^\times\).
For \(j\) modulo \(\ell-1\), write \(S(w)_j\) for the
\(\tau=g^j\) eigenspace in this Kummer action. These spaces all
have \(\iota=-1\).

## Local Selmer conditions

The argument of
[Proposition QN1](QUINTIC_NORM_JACOBIAN_SELMER_MOMENTS.md#the-local-terms-and-the-central-class-rank)
works for every odd prime \(\ell\). In fact the sum of the
\(\ell\) deck transformations acts by zero since the quotient
has genus zero. Hence \([\ell]\) is a unit times
\((1-\zeta_\ell)^{\ell-1}\). The Jacobian has dimension
\((\ell-1)/2\), so the isogeny has degree \(\ell\).
The divisor \((0,w\sqrt d)-\infty\) generates its kernel since
\(y-w\sqrt d\) has that divisor times \(\ell\) and the genus
is at least one. The quadratic involution acts by negation.
Restriction to \(B\) gives the anti-invariant Kummer
identification. The dual isogeny is a cyclotomic unit times
\(1-\zeta_\ell\), and evaluation of \(y-w\sqrt d\) gives the
local Kummer map via
[Schaefer, Theorem 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.08325).

At a nonsplit place of \(B/K_0\) away from \(\ell\), the local
anti-invariant cohomology is zero: local Kummer spaces of
the two fields with \(\mu_\ell\) both have dimension two and
restriction is injective. At a split place away from \(2,\ell\),
\(v(A)\) is even. If \(2\ell\mid v(A)\), rescale to a unit
constant term and use good reduction to get the unramified
line. If \(2\ell\nmid v(A)\), the local Kummer image is the
line generated by \(2w\sqrt d\). For the latter claim, the
isogeny gives an automorphism on a stable sufficiently small
pro-residue-characteristic subgroup of \(J_A\), and its
kernel and cokernel on the finite quotient have equal orders.
The local cokernel has order \(\ell\), since the kernel is
split. Evaluating at a point with large positive \(v(x)\)
and \(y\) close to \(w\sqrt d\) gives the inverse class of
\(2w\sqrt d\) modulo \(\ell\)-th powers, using
\((y-w\sqrt d)(y+w\sqrt d)=x^\ell\). This class has nonzero
valuation modulo \(\ell\). Evaluation descends from a
function on the curve defined over \(k\), so the image of
\(J_A(\mathbf Q)\) lies in \(S(w)_0\).

## A single set of primes for arbitrary squarefree multipliers

**Theorem IC1.** For every fixed \(\ell,d\) as above there is
a positive-density Chebotarev set \(\mathcal P\) of rational
primes, each congruent to \(-1\bmod\ell^2\), with the following
property. For every squarefree product \(w\) of primes in
\(\mathcal P\), \(S(1)\subseteq S(w)\). Simultaneously for
all \(j\),
\[
  \dim S(w)_j=\dim S(1)_j+
    \begin{cases}
      \omega(w) & (-1)^j\operatorname{sgn}(d)=1,\\
      0 & (-1)^j\operatorname{sgn}(d)=-1.
    \end{cases}
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular the full Selmer dimension increases by
\((\ell-1)\omega(w)/2\). For negative \(d\), the added classes
are all in nontrivial characters. They give that same lower
bound on the dimension of the quotient by the rational
divisor image.

Let \(L\) be the composite of \(B(\zeta_{\ell^2})\), the
Kummer extension of \(S(1)\) over \(B\), and the ray class
field of \(B\) of modulus \((2\ell)^m\mathcal O_B\) for a
fixed sufficiently large \(m\). The modulus is chosen so
that units congruent to one modulo it at the places above
\(2,\ell\) are local \(\ell\)-th powers. The Selmer space
\(S(1)\) is finite, and stable under \(\operatorname{Gal}(B/\mathbf Q)\)
because cyclotomic conjugation replaces the isogeny by a
unit multiple and preserves its local images. The ray
modulus is Galois-invariant. Thus \(L/\mathbf Q\) is finite
Galois. This construction uses the relatively prime degree
between \(k\) and \(K_0\) and does not assume that \(B\) has
class number one.

Choose \(\mathcal P\) by prescribing the conjugacy class of
complex conjugation in \(L/\mathbf Q\), discarding primes
dividing \(2\ell d\). Chebotarev gives positive natural
density. The restriction of the prescribed automorphism to
\(B\) is
\[
 c_B=\tau^{(\ell-1)/2}\iota^{[d<0]} .
\]
It has order two. Hence every \(P\mid p\) of \(B\) for
\(p\in\mathcal P\) has residue degree two, and has trivial
Frobenius in \(L/B\) because the chosen rational Frobenius
itself has order two. In particular \(P\) is ray principal
and all the classes in \(S(1)\) localize to zero at \(P\).
The prime \(p\) splits in \(B/K_0\) at the corresponding
places, since both \(B\) and \(K_0\) have residue degree two.
The action on \(\zeta_{\ell^2}\) gives \(p\equiv-1\bmod\ell^2\).

At a prime over \(p\in\mathcal P\), the local Kummer space
over \(B_P\) has its valuation line fixed by \(c_B\) and its
residue unit line negated, since the local Frobenius raises
residues to their \(p\)-th powers. For \(p\mid w\) the local
Selmer condition is the invariant line: \(2w\sqrt d\) has
valuation one and is fixed modulo \(\ell\)-th powers by
\(c_B\). Away from the primes dividing \(w\), the local
conditions agree with \(S(1)\); at \(2,\ell\) this follows
because each \(p\in\mathcal P\) is a local \(\ell\)-th power
in the rational completions. It is an odd unit at \(2\),
and \(-p\equiv1\bmod\ell^2\) at \(\ell\). At the primes
dividing \(w\), the old classes localize to zero. This
proves \(S(1)\subseteq S(w)\).

Let \(\eta_j\) denote the character of \(\operatorname{Gal}(B/\mathbf Q)\)
with \(\eta_j(\tau)=g^j,\eta_j(\iota)=-1\). If
\(\eta_j(c_B)=-1\), the new local images in this character
are zero, so \(S(w)_j=S(1)_j\). If \(\eta_j(c_B)=1\), the
localizations are determined by one valuation coordinate
per prime dividing \(w\). The kernel of the resulting map
on \(S(w)_j\) is \(S(1)_j\), because zero valuation on the
new local line means zero localization. To obtain every
valuation coordinate, at one chosen \(P\mid p\) take a
generator \(\pi\) of \(P\) congruent to one at the ray
modulus, and project its Kummer class with
\[
   e_j=\frac1{|\operatorname{Gal}(B/\mathbf Q)|}
       \sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(B/\mathbf Q)}
                     \eta_j(\sigma)^{-1}\sigma .
\]
The valuation of \(e_j[\pi]\) at \(P\) is
\(2/|\operatorname{Gal}(B/\mathbf Q)|\) in \(\mathbf F_\ell\):
the only contributing automorphisms are the decomposition
group \(\{1,c_B\}\), and both have character one. This class
is trivial at \(2,\ell\) by the ray congruence and is
unramified away from \(p,2,\ell\). At any other prime
dividing \(w\) its unramified class is zero, since it has
character one on the decomposition involution whereas
that involution negates the unramified line. At \(p\)
the invariant local condition is automatic. The resulting
classes give a full set of independent valuation
coordinates, which proves (1).

## Dihedral extensions at the inert primes

**Corollary IC2.** For negative \(d\), one may assign to each
prime \(p\) in the same set \(\mathcal P\) a cyclic
degree-\(\ell\) extension of \(k\), whose Galois group over
\(\mathbf Q\) is \(D_\ell\), relatively ramified exactly at
the prime over \(p\). The corresponding Kummer classes of
any finite selection all lie in \(S(w)_1\) for the
squarefree product of that selection. They are independent
and disjoint from the rational divisor image. Every other
field in the selection splits over \(k\) at the relative
ramification prime of a given member.

Use \(e_1[\pi]\) from Theorem IC1. Its paired Galois
character is \(\chi/\eta_1\), with \(\chi\) the cyclotomic
character on \(\mu_\ell\). Thus the \(\tau\) action on
the cyclic extension group is trivial and \(\iota\)
inverts it. A complement exists because the base degree
is prime to \(\ell\), and the subgroup from
\(\operatorname{Gal}(K_0/\mathbf Q)\) is then normal.
Its fixed field is the required \(D_\ell\) extension
over \(k\). Local extension across \(B/k\) has degree prime
to \(\ell\), so the relative conductor support and
splitting claimed over \(k\) follow from the valuations
and localizations over \(B\). Independently, at an inert
prime of \(k\) unramified in another dihedral field, its
rational Frobenius is a reflection and its square is
trivial. This accounts for the automatic independence
of the unramified and ramified local conditions at the
selected inert primes.

Myungjun Yu's
[Theorem 4.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07511)
produces arbitrary isogeny Selmer ranks among twists over
a cyclotomic number field, and includes the irreducible
quadratic polynomial in the superelliptic equation.
Here the prescribed rational twists are products from one
Chebotarev set, and (1) determines each character's change
with a common inclusion of spaces. The construction fixes
\(\ell,d\) before taking the positive-density set and gives
no uniform prime distribution as \(d\) varies.
