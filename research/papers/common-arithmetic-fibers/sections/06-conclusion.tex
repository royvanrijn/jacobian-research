\section{Discussion}

The reverse problem began with a tension.  Keller maps seem rigid, while
finite \'etale algebras carry essentially arbitrary arithmetic.  The
construction shows that these two facts are compatible: the map can be
rigid in its Jacobian and still be flexible enough to contain a prescribed
finite \'etale algebra as one complete fiber.

The point is that the construction preserves the algebra itself, not merely
its rank or its geometric points after base change.  Three identities carry
the proof: the compact inverse equation, the derivative-unit reconstruction,
and the function-field comparison
\[
 K(x,y,z)=K(\Pi,B,C)(S).
\]
The first tells us what the marked root must satisfy.  The second turns that
root back into a source point and explains the Jacobian cancellation.  The
third proves that no hidden inverse sheets remain.  Together they show that
the distinguished literal fiber has rank equal to the actual geometric
degree.

This distinction also keeps the effectivity statement precise.  From a
supplied separable presentation \(K[T]/(P)\) and admissible translation
\(a\), all map coordinates, the target, and the fiber isomorphism are
explicit and commute with scalar extension.  For an abstract finite \'etale
algebra, monogenicity proves existence of such data but does not choose it
canonically.

There is a second, slightly broader layer.  The paper proves the same
explicit formulas over every field of characteristic different from \(2\)
when an admissible translation is supplied.  This broader result is
Proposition~\ref{prop:supplied-polynomial-realization}; it is not formalized
end to end.

The characteristic-zero polynomial-presentation theorem, including the actual
geometric degree, and its passage to abstract finite \'etale algebras are
formalized in Lean.
Appendix~\ref{app:lean-correspondence} records the public endpoint
declarations and the exact remaining formal boundary.  The rank-two exclusion
is deliberately separate: it uses the
classical Campbell--Razar--Wright Galois case rather than the formalized
construction.

What I find most satisfying is that the final theorem is larger than the
formula that led to it.  The starting point was one cubic inverse pattern.
Once the derivative was recognized as both reconstruction unit and
Jacobian factor, the degree ceased to matter.  A single marked root could
carry any separable polynomial, and monogenicity then turned that polynomial
statement into a theorem about every finite \'etale algebra of rank at least
three.

\paragraph{Relation to earlier work.}
To my knowledge, no earlier source states that every finite \'etale
\(K\)-algebra of rank at least three occurs as a full Keller fiber in
\(\A^3_K\).  This priority statement is necessarily qualified: searches can
miss unpublished work or a theorem stated in different language.  Gallagher
gives explicit all-degree Keller families with split rational fibers
\cite{Gallagher}; Migus uses available all-degree constructions in the real
non-dense-image problem \cite{Migus}; and Shaska studies the grading and
quotient geometry of the cubic seed \cite{Shaska}.  These results have a
different quantifier order from the prescribed-algebra theorem here.

\paragraph{Provenance and AI assistance.}
The cubic seed was announced by Alp\"oge, who credited Akhil Mathew for
suggesting the problem and Fable for producing the example \cite{Alpoge}.
Its determinant and collision have an independent Isabelle/HOL formalization
\cite{IsabelleAFP}.  The present paper uses that map only as the cubic seed
of the all-degree construction.
