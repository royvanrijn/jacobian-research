# A one-generator cubic Engel-index-four obstruction in every grade

Let \(K\) be any field of characteristic zero. On \(V=K^4\), in coordinate
order \((x,y,s,t)\), put
\[
w=xt-ys,\qquad
H=(sw,tw,t^3,0).
\tag{I.1}
\]
Let \(\mu:V^3\to V\) be the symmetric cubic polarization normalized by
\(\mu(a,a,a)=H(a)\), and put \(L_a(v)=\mu(a,a,v)\).

**Theorem CubicEngelIndexFourOneGeneratorObstruction.** The algebra
\((V,\mu)\) satisfies \(L_a^4=0\) for every \(a\in V\), and some \(L_a^3\)
is nonzero. It is generated as a \(K\)-algebra by \(g=(0,1,0,1)\).
There is a nonzero word in this one generator at every nonnegative
internal grade, where a generator has grade zero and a ternary product
has grade one plus the sum of its three input grades. Consequently the
universal one-generator symmetric ternary algebra satisfying the
Engel-index-four identity has a nonzero homogeneous component in every
internal grade. In particular it is infinite dimensional and is not
nilpotent.

These statements remain valid after every coefficient-field extension.
They are statements about individual tree words and entire graded
components, not about the orbit-size-weighted inverse-sum vectors.

The map is classical. Umirbaev's *Polarization algebras and their relations*,
MPIM Preprint 2014-60, Section 5, printed page 12
([retained PDF, page 16](sources/umirbaev-polarization-algebras-2014.pdf);
[author primary edition](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/2035/1/preprint_2014_60.pdf)),
records the automorphism \(\mathrm{Id}+H\) in its discussion of
one-generator polarization algebras and attributes the example to
Gorni and Zampieri [19]. That reference is *Yagzhev polynomial mappings:
on the structure of the Taylor expansion of their local inverse*,
Ann. Polon. Math. 64 (1996), 285--290. The exact displayed paragraph
and its bibliography were inspected; the original 1996 paper is not
claimed to have been inspected here. No historical-priority claim is made.
The argument below is direct and imports no theorem from that discussion.

## 1. Exact Engel index

The Jacobian is
\[
JH=
\begin{pmatrix}
st&-s^2&xt-2ys&xs\\
t^2&-st&-yt&2xt-ys\\
0&0&0&3t^2\\
0&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{I.2}
\]
Write it as \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}P&Q\\0&R\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
with two-by-two blocks. Here
\[
P=\binom{s}{t}(t,-s),\qquad
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&3t^2\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
so \(P^2=R^2=0\). Hence the only possible nonzero block in its cube
is \(PQR\). Multiplication gives
\[
(JH)^3=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&3st^3w\\
0&0&0&3t^4w\\
0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad (JH)^4=0.
\tag{I.3}
\]
These are identities in \(K[x,y,s,t]\).

Differentiating \(H(a)=\mu(a,a,a)\) gives \(JH(a)=3L_a\), so
\(L_a^4=0\) for every \(a\). At \(a_0=(1,0,0,1)\),
\[
(JH(a_0))^3e_t=3e_y,\qquad L_{a_0}^3e_t=e_y/9\ne0.
\tag{I.4}
\]
Thus the index-four identity cannot be replaced by the index-three
identity for this algebra. This comparison concerns this explicit
algebra only; it asserts no eventual result for the universal index-three
algebra.

## 2. A cyclic generator and two persistent words

Polarization can be computed without choosing a tensor basis:
\[
\begin{aligned}
6\mu(a,b,c)={}&H(a+b+c)-H(a+b)-H(a+c)-H(b+c)\\
             &+H(a)+H(b)+H(c).
\end{aligned}
\tag{I.5}
\]
For \(g=(0,1,0,1)\), define
\[
b=\mu(g,g,g)=e_s,\qquad
c=\mu(g,g,b)=-e_y/3,\qquad
e=\mu(g,b,b)=-e_x/3.
\tag{I.6}
\]
The standard basis is recovered as
\[
e_x=-3e,\qquad e_y=-3c,\qquad e_s=b,\qquad e_t=g+3c.
\tag{I.7}
\]
Thus the algebra generated by \(g\) is all of \(V\).
Equivalently the determinant of the columns \((g,b,c,e)\) is \(1/9\).

For completeness, the alternative longer word is
\[
d=\mu(b,b,c)=e_x/9=-e/3.
\tag{I.8}
\]
The shorter word \(e\) is useful because its internal grade is three,
whereas \(d\) has internal grade five.

Consider the mixed multiplication operator \(M(v)=\mu(g,b,v)\).
Its exact formula is
\[
6M(v)=(v_x-2v_s,\,-v_y-v_t,\,0,\,0).
\tag{I.9}
\]
In particular
\[
M(c)=-c/6,\qquad M(e)=e/6.
\tag{I.10}
\]
Consequently, for every \(k\ge0\),
\[
M^k(c)=(-1/6)^k c\ne0,\qquad
M^k(e)=(1/6)^k e\ne0.
\tag{I.11}
\]
No limiting or finite-prefix argument is involved: (I.11) follows by
induction from the two exact eigenvector identities.

Each occurrence of \(M\) uses the fixed words \(g\) of grade zero and
\(b\) of grade one, and adds one new ternary vertex. It therefore adds
**two** internal vertices and **four** leaves. The two families have
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{word}&\text{internal grade}&\text{number of leaves}\\ \hline
M^k(c)&2+2k&5+4k\\
M^k(e)&3+2k&7+4k
\end{array}
\tag{I.12}
\]
The words \(g\) and \(b\) cover grades zero and one. Every internal grade
is therefore represented by a nonzero word in the single generator \(g\).

## 3. Passage to the universal graded algebra

Let \(\mathcal F\) be the free symmetric ternary \(K\)-algebra on one
generator \(\gamma\), with the internal-vertex grading just defined.
Let \(I\) be the ideal generated by all substitutions into the full
polarization of \(L_a^4(v)=0\) in its eight copies of \(a\), and write
\[
A_{3,4}=\mathcal F/I.
\tag{I.13}
\]
This is the universal one-generator algebra for that identity, using
the same grading convention as
[the subtree quotient definition](SUBTREE_SHUFFLE_AND_INVERSE_DEGREE.md).
No inverse-degree theorem from that note is a premise here.

The ideal is homogeneous. For homogeneous inputs, every term of the
polarized relation uses four new internal vertices and each of its
nine input words once. All terms have the same total internal grade.
Substitution of arbitrary inputs decomposes into these homogeneous
multilinear substitutions, and further ternary contexts preserve
homogeneity of the ideal.

Evaluation at \(g\) defines a homomorphism \(\mathcal F\to(V,\mu)\).
It kills \(I\), since (I.3) proves the Engel identity and in
characteristic zero its full polarization also vanishes. Thus it factors
through \(A_{3,4}\). Construct in \(\mathcal F\) the words corresponding
to \(b,c,e,M^k(c),M^k(e)\) in (I.6)--(I.12).
Each is homogeneous, and each displayed evaluation is nonzero.
Their classes cannot vanish in the quotient. Therefore
\[
(A_{3,4})_q\ne0\qquad\text{for every integer }q\ge0.
\tag{I.14}
\]
The homomorphism need not preserve a grading on the four-dimensional
target; no such grading is asserted. Nonzero evaluations certify the
individual homogeneous source words.

**Corollary AllHigherCubicEngelPiecesSurvive.** Define \(A_{3,p}\) by the
same universal construction with \(L_a^p(v)=0\). Then
\((A_{3,p})_q\ne0\) for every \(p\ge4\) and every \(q\ge0\).
The same four-dimensional algebra satisfies each of these identities,
so the same nonzero evaluations prove the claim. Here \(p\) is the
identity bound; the explicit witness itself has exact Engel index four.

## 4. Exact inverse-degree separation

Individual word survival does not establish survival of inverse-sum
vectors, which are particular weighted sums of words.
In this example both maps \(F_\varepsilon=\mathrm{Id}+\varepsilon H\),
\(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), have polynomial inverses of degree exactly seven.
For target coordinates \((X,Y,S,T)\), set \(u=S-\varepsilon T^3\).
Then
\[
F_\varepsilon^{-1}(X,Y,S,T)=
\left(
(1-\varepsilon uT)X+\varepsilon u^2Y,
-\varepsilon T^2X+(1+\varepsilon uT)Y,
u,\ T
\right).
\tag{I.15}
\]
Indeed \(s=u,t=T\) are recovered first. The first two coordinates of
\(F_\varepsilon\) multiply \((x,y)^T\) by \(I+\varepsilon P\);
its inverse is \(I-\varepsilon P\) because \(P^2=0\).
The term \(\varepsilon T^6Y\) in the first inverse coordinate is
nonzero and is its only degree-seven monomial.

For example, on the line \(zg\), the usual minus-sign inverse is exactly
\[
F_{-1}^{-1}(zg)=zg+z^3b+3z^5c+3z^7e.
\tag{I.16}
\]
Higher inverse coefficients cancel even though the individual words in
(I.11) stay nonzero.

Thus (I.14) obstructs eventual vanishing of the **whole graded pieces**
at index four. It proves neither nonvanishing of arbitrarily high
inverse sums nor an unbounded inverse-degree family at index four.
No assertion about an exact index-three/index-four boundary depends
on any unfinished calculation here.

## 5. Exact support and assurance

The standard-library checker reconstructs the five literal cubic
monomials, differentiates them, multiplies the Jacobian symbolically,
and checks (I.3). It computes polarization in two independent ways:
the symmetric monomial tensor and inclusion-exclusion (I.5).
It verifies the cyclic basis, both eigenvector identities, exact
word grades and leaves, and both compositions of (I.15) for each sign.
Corrupted controls change a cubic sign, change the polarization
normalization, reverse the eigenvalue sign, and miscount the grade shift.

The unbounded word families and all-grade conclusion are the written
induction and universal-quotient argument. Finite word tests do not
replace either proof. The existing graded index-three computations,
the Hopf orbit results and any inverse-growth claims are not premises.
