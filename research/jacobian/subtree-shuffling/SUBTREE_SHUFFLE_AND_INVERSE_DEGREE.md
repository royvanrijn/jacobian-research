# Exact subtree shuffling and uniform inverse degrees

Bisi, Dyszewski, Gantert, Johnston, Prochno and Schmid formulate the constant-vector problem in Conjecture 1.3 of [Random planar trees and the Jacobian conjecture](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70416). Their quantifier is an eventual threshold \(k_0(d,p)\) for each fixed arity \(d\) and shuffle length \(p\). Here \(k\) counts internal vertices. The shuffle lemma, Lemma 3.3, connects vanishing over length-\(p\) shuffles to \((JH)^p=0\). Their Theorem 1.4 makes the passage to the Jacobian conjecture, and uses only \(d=3\) in the shuffle conjecture. This note gives an exact reduction for their constant-vector problem and tests several finite sizes.

## Passing from planar trees to symmetric products

**Proposition ShuffleOrbitReduction.** The planar constant-vector test is equivalent to membership of the orbit-size-weighted unordered tree sum in the span of projected shuffle classes.

Work over \(\mathbb Q\). Let \(V_k\) have the planar full \(d\)-ary trees with \(k\) internal vertices as basis and let \(R_k\) be the span of the sums of the distinct trees in the length-\(p\) shuffle classes. Let \(W_k\) have the unordered full \(d\)-ary shapes as basis. The map \(q:V_k\to W_k\) forgets the child orders and \(s:W_k\to V_k\) sends a shape to the average of its distinct planar representatives. In particular, \(sq\) fixes the sum of all planar trees and \(q\) sends that sum to \(\sum_U \omega(U)U\), with \(\omega(U)\) the number of planar representatives of \(U\).

We claim that \(sq(R_k)\subseteq R_k\). Fix a marked path of \(p\) edges and number the \((d-1)p\) sibling subtrees before shuffling. Initially the sum over permutations of the numbered copies is a fixed nonzero integer times the class sum. Randomly and independently reorder the children at every internal vertex of the fixed frame, including the path and the bottom subtree, and of each numbered sibling subtree. The random reordering for a numbered subtree is attached to that subtree as it moves between slots.

For each permutation of the numbered subtrees, these random choices are uniform among the planar orientations of its unordered result. Every orientation has the same number of preimages under independent child permutations, by orbit–stabilizer. For each fixed choice of the random orders, the sum over assignments of the numbered subtrees is again a positive integer times a single shuffle class. Indeed, the reordered frame fixes the new path and slot addresses, and the reordered numbered subtrees may be assigned to those slots in all orders. Averaging proves the claim. If \(q\) of the planar total is the projection of a vector of \(R_k\), applying \(s\) puts the planar total in \(R_k\). The forward implication follows by applying \(q\).

For exact computations, identify \(\bigoplus_k W_k\) with the free symmetric \(d\)-ary algebra on one generator \(x\), using grafting at a new root for its \(d\)-linear product \(\mu\). Put \(L_a(b)=\mu(b,a,\ldots,a)\). The subspace \(\bigoplus_k q(R_k)\) is the ideal generated by all substitutions into the polarization of \(L_a^p(b)=0\) in its \((d-1)p\) copies of \(a\). To see this, a shuffle rooted at the top of its marked path becomes this polarized sum with the fixed bottom subtree substituted for \(b\). Polarization counts labelled permutations, so repeated subtrees change the sum only by a positive scalar. A path starting below the root adds a fixed context to a root relation. Conversely, each root relation on unordered trees is the projection of a shuffle with some planar representatives and fixed child slots. Each further grafting of a relation with fixed other factors supplies a path in the larger tree. Denote the quotient algebra by \(A_{d,p}\). The arguments use rational coefficients, so the planar membership result is the same over any characteristic-zero coefficient field.

Let \(U_k\in A_{d,p}\) be the class of the orbit-size-weighted sum. It is the coefficient of \(t^k\) in
\[
G=x+t\mu(G,\ldots,G).
\]
For the recursion at grade \(k\), take products of previously reduced factors of grades summing to \(k-1\). Before imposing the root shuffle relations, the products form the basis of the appropriate graded component of the symmetric tensor power of the lower-grade quotient spaces. This accounts for every relation arising from a path below the root. The remaining root relations are multilinear in the bottom and off-path trees, so substitutions from lower quotient bases suffice. The coefficient recursion uses the multinomial factor for each unordered list of arguments. It therefore preserves the planar orbit sizes without enumerating planar trees.

## Inverse-degree interpretation

**Theorem ShuffleInverseDegreeEquivalence.** For fixed \(d,p\), eventual membership of the planar constant vector is equivalent to a dimension-independent bound on inverse degrees for homogeneous degree-\(d\) maps \(I-H\) with \((JH)^p=0\). A failed test at grade \(k\) gives a triangular example with inverse degree exactly \((d-1)k+1\).

For the upper bound, polarize \(H\) to obtain a symmetric \(d\)-linear product on its coordinate vector space. Then \(JH(a)=dL_a\), and its \(p\)-th power vanishes precisely when the polarized identity holds. Sending \(x\) to an arbitrary vector defines an evaluation of \(A_{d,p}\) in this algebra. The homogeneous term of degree \((d-1)j+1\) of the formal inverse is the evaluation of \(U_j\), by the recursion above. If all \(U_j\) beyond a fixed grade vanish, the formal inverse is a polynomial of the resulting bounded degree. Both formal inverse identities are then polynomial identities.

For the reverse implication, when \(U_k\ne0\), take the finite-dimensional graded algebra
\[
B=A_{d,p}\big/\bigoplus_{j>k}(A_{d,p})_j.
\]
Grafting adds one to the sum of internal grades, so the discarded grades form an ideal. On \(B\), set \(H(y)=\mu(y,\ldots,y)\). In a coordinate basis ordered by grade, \(H\) in a given grade depends only on lower grades. Thus \(F=I-H\) is triangular with identity diagonal. Its Jacobian satisfies \((JH)^p=0\) by the same identity. Products attached to trees of more than \(k\) internal vertices vanish for all inputs. The term of ordinary degree \((d-1)k+1\) is nonzero at the generator since it evaluates to \(U_k\). This proves the asserted inverse degree. If \(k\ge p-1\), the nilpotency index is exactly \(p\), since the \(p-1\) step comb at the generator has internal grade \(p-1\), and the ideal has no relation below grade \(p\). A bound uniform over dimensions therefore excludes failure at every sufficiently large \(k\).

A finite propagation test follows from the same recursion. If \(U_k=0\) at all \(k\) from \(K\) to \(d(K-1)+1\), then \(U_k=0\) for all \(k\ge K\). In the first remaining grade the child grades sum to more than \(d(K-1)\), so at least one child coefficient is already zero. Induction gives the conclusion. The same argument applies to the vanishing of entire graded quotient spaces.

## Exact experiments

**Theorem ShuffleSmallDegreeChecks.** The constant belongs to the binary length-three span exactly at grades \(k\ge6\) among positive internal grades. Cubic length three fails through \(k=14\) and succeeds at \(k=15\). Binary length four fails through \(k=14\).

The [rational checker](verify_subtree_shuffle.py) does sparse linear elimination by grade. It reduces the orbit-size-weighted constant by the resulting pivot rows. For a nonzero remainder it also evaluates a rational dual functional that kills the rows and detects the target. As independent controls, the checker enumerates literal planar slot shuffles at six small \((d,p,k)\) settings and compares the projected rank and membership.

| Arity \(d\) | Path length \(p\) | Exact result |
|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | 3 | The grade-five constant is outside the span. The whole unordered quotient vanishes in grades 6 to 11, so the propagation test proves membership for all \(k\ge6\). |
| 2 | 4 | The constant is outside the span at every grade \(k\le14\). At grade 14 the unordered quotient has dimension 245 out of 4,850 shapes. |
| 3 | 2 | The whole unordered quotient vanishes in grades 2 to 4, and therefore at every grade \(k\ge2\). |
| 3 | 3 | The constant is outside the span at every grade \(k\le14\). At grade 14 the unordered quotient has dimension 35 out of 19,241 shapes. At grade 15 the quotient has dimension 16 and the constant vanishes in it. |

The binary length-three case accords with the degree-six inverse theorem of [Singer](https://doi.org/10.37236/1546). The finite grade-14 failures produce inverse degree \(15\) in dimension \(680\) for a quadratic map of Jacobian nilpotency index \(4\), and inverse degree \(29\) in dimension \(195\) for a cubic map of index \(3\), by the truncated quotient construction. The [cubic cube-zero theorem](CUBIC_CUBE_ZERO_SHUFFLE_EXTENSION.md) proves that the cubic length-three threshold is exactly \(15\), and the uniform inverse-degree bound \(29\) is sharp. Its certificate extends the exact rational calculation through grade \(18\), proves whole-piece vanishing modulo a prime at grades \(19\) through \(28\), and applies the classical quadratic inverse gap. The globally quantified assertion over all cubic path lengths is false.
The [retained eighteen-variable cubic collision](CUBIC_INDEX_SEVENTEEN_SHUFFLE_FAILURE.md)
has exact Jacobian nilpotency index17. Its formal inverse does not terminate,
so eventual shuffle membership fails at length17 and every larger length.
The same proof gives an explicit triangular jet family at fixed index17
with unbounded inverse degrees in growing dimensions. Together with the
cube-zero theorem, the smallest failing cubic path length lies in[4,17].
The index-four uniform inverse-degree question and the remaining small-index
boundary remain open.
