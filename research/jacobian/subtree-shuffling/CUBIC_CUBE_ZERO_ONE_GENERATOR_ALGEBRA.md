# The 235-dimensional universal cubic cube-zero algebra

Let \(K\) be a field of characteristic zero. A symmetric ternary algebra
is a \(K\)-vector space with a symmetric trilinear product \(\mu\), with
no associativity assumption. Put
\[
 L_a(b)=\mu(b,a,a).
\]
Let \(A=A_{3,3}\) be the free such algebra on one generator \(x\), subject
to \(L_a^3(b)=0\) for every \(a,b\), or equivalently to its full
six-variable polarization. Grade counts internal ternary vertices.
A grade-\(k\) tree has \(2k+1\) leaves.

**Theorem UniversalCubeZeroAlgebra.** The graded dimensions of \(A\) are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (\dim A_0,\ldots,\dim A_{18})
   &=(1,1,1,1,2,3,4,6,9,13,19,26,34,40,35,16,13,9,2),\\
 A_k&=0\qquad(k\ge19).
 \end{aligned}
\]
In particular, \(\dim_K A=235\). Every subalgebra generated by one
element in a characteristic-zero symmetric ternary algebra with
\(L_a^3(b)=0\) has dimension at most \(235\), and this bound is attained.

**Corollary SharpRepeatedElementThreshold.** Under the same hypotheses,
every ternary tree monomial with at least \(39\) leaves vanishes when all
leaves are evaluated at the same element. The odd-leaf threshold \(39\)
is optimal: a specified \(37\)-leaf monomial is nonzero in \(A\).

More generally, all leaves may be arbitrary elements of one fixed
subalgebra generated by a single element: expand those elements as linear
combinations of words in that generator. Every resulting tree still has
at least 39 generator leaves, and therefore vanishes.

The claim is about each subalgebra generated by one element. It does
not assert that every mixed-input tree with \(39\) leaves vanishes in
every algebra satisfying the Engel identity.

The [sharp inverse-degree theorem](CUBIC_CUBE_ZERO_SHUFFLE_EXTENSION.md)
remains a different statement. Its weighted inverse coefficients vanish
from internal grade \(15\), giving inverse degree \(29\). Whole graded
pieces survive through grade \(18\); their new sharp vanishing threshold
is internal grade \(19\).

For classical terminology, Umirbaev,
[*Polarization algebras and their relations*, MPIM2014-60](sources/umirbaev-polarization-algebras-2014.pdf),
Lemma7 (printed pp7–8), gives \(JH(a)=3L_a\) for \(H(a)=\mu(a,a,a)\).
Section4 (printed p10) defines nil of bounded index using the free
one-generator algebra. The dimension and sharp threshold here come from
the exact quotient calculation, rather than from that dictionary.

## 1. The finite interval that suffices

In a free ternary algebra every positive-grade tree has a root with
three subtrees. Therefore the grade-\(k\) component is spanned by
products of lower components with grades summing to \(k-1\).

**Lemma WholePiecePropagation.** If \(A_{19},\ldots,A_{55}\) are zero,
then \(A_k=0\) for every \(k\ge19\).

For \(k=56\), a potentially nonzero root product would have all three
child grades at most \(18\), since the intermediate grades are zero.
Its total grade would be at most
\[
 1+18+18+18=55,
\]
a contradiction. The same argument applies inductively to every larger
grade: every child has strictly smaller grade. This proves the lemma.
It uses whole-component zeros, not zeros of a particular weighted sum.

Thus the dimension theorem reduces to the exact dimensions through
grade \(18\) and the finite block of whole zeros from \(19\) to \(55\).

## 2. The exact quotient calculation

The [original certificates](cubic_cube_zero_shuffle_certificates.json.gz)
give the rational quotient bases through grade \(18\), and whole-piece
zeros \(19,\ldots,28\). The separately
[replayed next certificate](CUBIC_CUBE_ZERO_GRADE29.md) proves \(A_{29}=0\).
The [new tail packet](cubic_cube_zero_one_generator_certificates.jsonl.gz)
contains the selected root relations for every grade \(30,\ldots,55\).

At grade \(k\), graft unordered triples of the already established
lower quotient basis elements, with grades summing to \(k-1\).
These triples form the prequotient: all relations below the root
have already been imposed. The remaining root identities are polarized
length-three Engel sums, specified by six off-path basis elements and
one bottom basis element. Their total internal grade plus three is \(k\).

The [verifier](verify_cubic_cube_zero_one_generator_algebra.py)
reconstructs every prequotient and validates every substitution. It
regenerates the actual shuffle relation coefficients and checks that
each selected row increases rank over
\(\mathbb F_{1000000009}\). For each grade, the number of independent
selected rows equals the whole prequotient dimension.

There are \(1,991,709\) selected rows in the new interval \(30,\ldots,55\).
The largest prequotient occurs in grade \(38\), with \(175,309\) columns.
The final grade has four columns and four independent valid relations.
Including the earlier certificates, all \(2,185,424\) selected zero-piece
rows in grades \(19,\ldots,55\) are replayed. The input contains
substitution identifiers, not a claimed elimination matrix or supplied
relation coefficients.

The checker additionally rebuilds every rational root-relation space
through grade \(18\). It validates the exact reduced bases and checks
that their selected pivot blocks remain invertible at the same proof
prime. Thus the initial bases specialize to the actual modular
quotients. Every subsequent lower-piece zero shortcut is gated on a
strictly earlier, already verified zero grade.

### Exact row cleanup

The elimination retains normalized rows whose other columns lie below
their leading pivot. When a stored row is a unit vector \(e_j\), its
coefficient in any other row can be removed by subtracting the
corresponding multiple of \(e_j\). A reverse-support table identifies
the affected rows; a newly created unit row is processed in the same
way. Every operation preserves the row span and each existing leading
pivot. Independence is tested by exact elimination before each new
row is inserted.

This is only an exact elimination optimization. The verifier checks that
every used unit row really is \(\{j:1\}\), that each affected row contains
its indicated coefficient, and that the leading pivot lies above \(j\).
Its controls include dependent-row rejection, an invalid-column rejection,
and a unit block above a missing smaller column, which must not be
mistaken for a complete prefix.

The tail packet is streamed one grade at a time. Removing the tree-table
entries of an already proved zero grade saves memory: every subsequent
use of that lower grade is intercepted by the verified zero shortcut.
The nonzero shuffle cache is restricted to grades at most \(18\).

## 3. The passage to characteristic zero

Use the integral polarized presentation over
\(R=\mathbb Z[1/6!]\) from
[the existing proof](CUBIC_CUBE_ZERO_SHUFFLE_EXTENSION.md), Sections 3–4.
At any fixed grade its unordered-tree module and relation presentation
are finite. The normalized shuffle sums differ from full labelled
polarization values by products of factorials dividing \(6!\), hence by
units in \(R\). Forming this quotient commutes with base change.

The exact initial pivot certificates are checked over both
\(\mathbb Q\) and the same finite field. Each later full-rank step
therefore computes the actual modular quotient component. No rational
membership conclusion is inferred merely from one modular target vector
vanishing.

For a finite relation matrix over \(R\), every minor nonzero modulo the
proof prime is nonzero over \(\mathbb Q\). Consequently its rational
quotient dimension is at most its modular quotient dimension. The
certified modular whole-piece zeros therefore imply
\[
 (A_{\mathbb Q})_k=0\qquad(19\le k\le55).
\]
WholePiecePropagation proves all later zeros. The earlier rational
dimensions sum to \(235\), so this is the exact total dimension.
Scalar extension proves the statement over every characteristic-zero
field.

For any element \(a\) in an algebra satisfying the identity, the universal
homomorphism sending \(x\) to \(a\) maps \(A\) onto its generated
subalgebra. This proves the dimension bound and all repeated-element
vanishing assertions. The universal algebra itself attains the bound.

## 4. A concrete nonzero 37-leaf monomial

In \(A\), define
\[
 a=\mu(x,x,x),\qquad b=\mu(a,a,a),\qquad
 L(y)=\mu(x,x,y),\qquad M(y)=\mu(x,a,y).
\]
Then
\[
                         T=L^2(ML)^4b\ne0.                 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(ML\) means first apply \(L\), then \(M\). The initial \(b\) has
internal grade \(4\); \(L\) adds one and \(M\) adds two. Thus \(T\) has
grade \(4+4(1+2)+2=18\), or \(37\) leaves.

The verifier reconstructs this literal tree through the canonical
graft identifiers
\[
 7,\ 9,\ 26,\ 38,\ 109,\ 164,\ 447,\ 678,\ 1617,\ 2404,\ 3569.
\]
Every intermediate is an established free basis tree. The final
identifier \(3569\) is one of the two free grade-\(18\) basis elements;
its exact rational remainder is itself with coefficient one.
This proves (1), including after any characteristic-zero scalar extension.
It establishes the optimality of the odd-leaf threshold \(39\).

## 5. The exact Engel-index boundary

**Corollary ExactEngelIndexBoundary.** For positive integers \(p\),
universal one-generator nilpotence in the variety of symmetric ternary
algebras satisfying \(L_a^p(b)=0\) holds exactly for \(p\le3\).
More precisely:

| Identity bound \(p\) | Dimension of the free one-generator algebra \(A_{3,p}\) | Optimal odd-leaf vanishing threshold |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(1\) | \(1\) | \(3\) |
| \(2\) | \(2\) | \(5\) |
| \(3\) | \(235\) | \(39\) |
| \(p\ge4\) | infinite | none |

For every \(p\ge4\), every internal-grade component is nonzero.
Here the threshold means vanishing of all words at or above that odd
number of leaves when all leaves lie in one fixed one-generated
subalgebra.

For \(p=1\), polarizing \(L_a(b)=0\) in its two copies of \(a\)
gives \(\mu=0\). The free algebra is the one-dimensional space
spanned by its nonzero generator.

For \(p=2\), put \(u=\mu(x,x,x)\) and write
\[
 P=L_x,\qquad Q(y)=\mu(x,u,y),\qquad R=L_u.
\]
The coefficients of \(L_{x+tu}^2=(P+2tQ+t^2R)^2=0\) give
\[
 P^2=0,\qquad PQ+QP=0,\qquad PR+RP+4Q^2=0.
\]
Now \(Px=u\) and \(Pu=Qx=0\). Applying the middle identity to
\(x\) gives \(Qu=Rx=0\), and applying the last gives \(Ru=0\).
Thus \(\operatorname{span}\{x,u\}\) is closed under the product,
and its only potentially nonzero basis product is
\(\mu(x,x,x)=u\). The explicit two-dimensional algebra with precisely
that nonzero basis product satisfies \(L_a^2=0\); it has \(u\ne0\)
and all five-leaf words vanish. This proves both exact entries for
\(p=2\). The dimension-235 theorem and (1) give the entries for
\(p=3\).

For \(p\ge4\), use the separate classical-example
[Engel-index-four obstruction](CUBIC_ENGEL_INDEX_FOUR_ONE_GENERATOR_OBSTRUCTION.md).
It supplies one algebra with \(L_a^4=0\) and a specified element having
nonzero tree monomials in every internal grade. That algebra also satisfies
\(L_a^p=0\) for every \(p\ge4\), so evaluation proves every such
universal component nonzero.

This obstruction is an additional input to this corollary; it is not a
premise of the dimension-235 theorem. It concerns whole-piece nilpotence
and does not decide whether cubic maps of Jacobian nilpotency index four
have a dimension-independent inverse-degree bound.

## 6. Reproduction and assurance

Run from the repository root:

    python3 research/jacobian/subtree-shuffling/verify_cubic_cube_zero_one_generator_algebra.py \
      --repo-root . \
      --input research/jacobian/subtree-shuffling/cubic_cube_zero_one_generator_certificates.jsonl.gz \
      --output /tmp/cubic_cube_zero_one_generator_verification.json

The verifier uses the Python standard library. It authenticates the
retained prefix sources, checks both initial coefficient fields,
regenerates the complete selected zero-piece certificates and verifies
the explicit sharp monomial. Missing, reordered or extra tail records
are rejected. The full dimension and threshold conclusions are emitted
only after the interval through grade \(55\) passes.

The [receipt](cubic_cube_zero_one_generator_verification.json) records
the actual replay. The
[source ledger](cubic_cube_zero_one_generator_source_ledger.json)
pins the proof, checker, exact inputs and the integral quotient argument.
The producer's search order is not needed for verification.

This is an internally reviewed exact computational proof with a written
finite reduction and base-change argument. Independent internal code and
mathematical review is distinguished from external human review or formal
verification; neither of the latter is claimed. No historical-priority
claim is made.
