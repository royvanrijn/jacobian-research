# The image field and polynomial functions of the axial sextic tuple

**Theorem SexticAxialThreePolynomialRecovery.**
Let \(k\) have characteristic zero, \(x,z\) be independent, and
\[
 A=x^4z^2+axz^3,\qquad B=x^5z+cx^2z^2,\qquad C=x^6+fx^3z+ez^2
\]
with \(a,c,f,e\in k\). Put \(L=k(A,B,C)\), \(t=z/x\),
\(X=x^2\), \(V=xz\), \(U=x^2z\), and \(H=x^3+cz\).
The field \(L\), its index in the binary source field, and the
ring of all its elements polynomial in the binary source are:

| Parameter locus | \(L\) | \([k(x,z):L]\) | \(L\cap k[x,z]\) |
|---|---|---:|---|
| \(B^2\ne AC\) | \(k(X,V)\) | \(2\) | \(k[x^2,xz,z^2]\) |
| \(a=c=f\ne0,\ e=0\) | \(k(t,C)\) | \(6\) | \(k[C,tC,t^2C,t^3C]\) |
| \(a=0,\ f=2c,\ e=c^2,\ c\ne0\) | \(k(U^2,UH,H^2)\) | \(10\) | \(k[A,B,C]\) |
| \(a=c=f=e=0\) | \(k(t,x^6)\) | \(6\) | \(k[x^6,x^5z,x^4z^2,x^3z^3,x^2z^4,xz^5,z^6]\) |

These are statements about the binary image field before imposing
any Keller lift. The transverse-lift restriction \(a=e=0\) for the
actual doubled square-member frame is established in the
[transverse reduction and inversion proof](SEXTIC_KELLER_TANGENT_CONIC_QUADRATIC_OUTER.md#lifting-the-binary-tuple-at-axial-weight-three).

## The nonconstant projective ratio

Set \(\theta=z/x^3\) and \(M=1+f\theta+e\theta^2\). The two ratios
\[
 R=\frac{B^2}{AC}=\frac{(1+c\theta)^2}{(1+a\theta)M},\qquad
 S=\frac{A^2}{BC^2}=\frac{\theta^3(1+a\theta)^2}{(1+c\theta)M^2}
\]
lie in \(k(\theta)\). They generate \(k(\theta)\) when \(R\)
is nonconstant, by the projective-ratio calculation in the
[nonconstant-ratio case](SEXTIC_KELLER_TANGENT_CONIC_QUADRATIC_OUTER.md#nonconstant-projective-ratio).
That calculation uses only the two displayed ratios. In brief,
the point \(\theta=0\) has \(R=1\) and \(S=0\). The only
other possible finite zero of \(S\) is at \(1+a\theta=0\).
For \(R\) to be a unit there both \(1+c\theta\) and \(M\)
must vanish simply, giving \(S\) a pole instead. At infinity
the degrees and leading coefficients in the displayed ratios
allow \(R=1,S=0\) only when \(a=0,\ e=c^2\ne0\). The orders
of \(S\) there and at zero are two and three. If the points
were over the same place their indices would sum to five,
exceeding \(\deg R\le2\) in that case. Hence the field index
divides three. Index three would require \(\deg R=3\) and
\(S\in\overline{k}(R)\). Then \(a,e\ne0\) and \(R\) has no
cancellation. If \(c\ne0\), the two zeros of \(R\) differ
in whether \(S\) has a pole. If \(c=0\), the triple zero
of \(R\) has a simple pole of \(S\). Both possibilities
contradict \(S\in\overline{k}(R)\). The recovered parameter
descends to \(k(R,S)\) by averaging over a finite Galois
extension containing the coefficients of an expression for it.

Since \(B/C=X\theta(1+c\theta)/M\), the field contains \(X\)
and \(V=\theta X^2\). The expressions for \(A,B,C\) in terms
of \(X,\theta\) give the reverse inclusion. Also \(x^2=X\)
and \(z=V/x\), and the involution \((x,z)\mapsto(-x,-z)\)
fixes \(X,V\) and moves \(x\). The index is two, and a
polynomial belongs to this fixed field exactly when all its
monomials have even total degree. Those monomials are
generated by \(x^2,xz,z^2\).

## The constant projective ratio

At \(\theta=0\) the ratio \(R\) is one. Comparing the
degrees and roots of \((1+c\theta)^2=(1+a\theta)M\) gives
\(a=c=f\ne0,\ e=0\), or \(a=0,\ f=2c,\ e=c^2\).

When \(a=c=f\ne0\), one has \(B=tC,\ A=t^2C\) and
\(C=x^6+at x^4\). Over \(k(t)\), this has degree six in
\(x\), giving the field and index. A polynomial in \(x,z\)
belongs to \(k[t][x]\) after writing \(z=tx\). Any such
polynomial in \(k(t,C)\) belongs first to \(k(t)[C]\).
Indeed coprime numerator and denominator polynomials in
\(C\) remain coprime under this substitution, by Bézout,
and a nonconstant denominator in \(C\) stays nonconstant
in \(x\). Since \(C\) is monic in \(x\), comparison of the
highest \(x\)-coefficient, followed by subtraction, shows
that the coefficients of the expression in powers of \(C\)
belong to \(k[t]\).

Now regard \(t^i C^j\) in \(k(z)(x)\). Its \(x=0\) order
is \(3j-i\) and its leading coefficient is \(a^j z^{i+j}\).
At a fixed order, different monomials have different
\(i+j\), so the terms of minimum order cannot cancel.
Thus every term in a polynomial expression has \(0\le
i\le3j\). Conversely every such monomial is polynomial and
a product of \(C,tC,t^2C,t^3C\). In particular the extra
generator is \(t^3C=z^3(x^3+az)\). The target algebra
\(k[A,B,C]=k[C,tC,t^2C]\) is the second Veronese of a
two-variable polynomial ring via \(C\mapsto u^2,\ t\mapsto
v/u\), hence is normal. The extra generator lies in the
image field and is regular in the binary source. Its
degree in \(t\) at \(C\)-degree one also shows that it lies
outside the normal target algebra.

When \(a=0,\ f=2c,\ e=c^2\), one has
\((A,B,C)=(U^2,UH,H^2)\). For \(c\ne0\), \(k(U,H)\) has
index five in \(k(x,z)=k(U,x)\): over \(k(U)\) the
rational function \(H=(x^5+cU)/x^2\) has degree five,
with coprime numerator and denominator. The sign
involution on the independent \(U,H\) gives index two
from \(k(U,H)\) to \(L\). The source ring \(k[x,z]\)
is integral over \(k[U,H]\) by the monic equation
\(x^5-Hx^2+cU=0\) and \(z=(H-x^3)/c\), and \(k[U,H]\)
is integral over its second Veronese \(k[A,B,C]\).
An element of \(L\) polynomial in the source therefore
belongs to this normal ring. Normality of the second
Veronese in either case follows by taking the fixed
ring of the sign involution on a polynomial ring:
an element of its fraction field integral over the
fixed ring lies in the polynomial ring by normality
and is fixed.

Finally, when all four coefficients vanish,
\((A,B,C)=x^6(t^2,t,1)\), so \(L=k(t,x^6)\), of
index six. In \(k(t)[x]\), membership in \(k(t,x^6)\)
requires all nonzero powers of \(x\) to have exponents
divisible by six, by the basis \(1,x,\ldots,x^5\) over
\(k(t,x^6)\). A source monomial \(x^i z^j\) has exponent
\(i+j\) in this expression, with coefficient \(t^j\).
Distinct \(j\)'s cannot cancel at fixed \(i+j\). This gives
exactly the sixth Veronese of the binary polynomial ring.
