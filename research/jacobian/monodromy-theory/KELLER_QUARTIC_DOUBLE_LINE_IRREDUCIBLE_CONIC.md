# A repeated line and irreducible conic in a quartic Keller component

**Proposition KellerQuarticDoubleLineIrreducibleConic.** Let \(R\)
be a degree-four component of a complex Keller map of degree at most
five in three variables, with \(R_4=z^2J\) for an irreducible
ternary conic \(J\). If the map is noninvertible, then \(R_3=zV\)
for a homogeneous quadratic \(V\). With the subscript zero denoting
restriction to \(z=0\), the binary quartic
\[
                         \Delta=V_0^2-4J_0(R_2)_0
\]
is zero or has a linear factor of multiplicity at least three.

The
[local argument for cubic vanishing at a double line](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_ONE_DOUBLE_LINE_CONIC_PENCIL.md#cubic-vanishing-along-the-fixed-line)
applies to the single component \(R\), with no assumption on the
leading forms of the other components. Indeed, if \(R_3|_{z=0}\)
is nonzero, the quartic fibre has isolated singularities.
Along \(z=0\) away from \(J\) its local leading plane equation
is a square times a unit, and the cited simple-, double- and
triple-zero tests apply to \(R_3|_{z=0}\). At an intersection of
\(z=0\) with \(J=0\), a smooth point or a quadratic jet of rank
at least two is resolved by the double-point argument. A rank-one
jet gives quadratic-source completion. A rank-zero jet makes
\(R\) affine-linear in the source direction of the intersection
point. In the transverse case the cubic coefficient classification
would make \(J\) reducible. In the tangent case it either gives
inversion or forces \(R_3|_{z=0}=0\). These tests use only the
geometry of \(z^2J\), exactness of the fibre form, and the
degree-five bound for the coordinate-completion theorem.
The general quartic fibre thus contradicts the exactness
obstruction as in the cited argument. This proves \(R_3=zV\).

Next use the
[exceptional discriminant calculation](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_ONE_DOUBLE_LINE_CONIC_PENCIL.md#the-exceptional-discriminant)
for this same individual component. Blowing up \(z=w=0\) in
the projective fibre, where \(w\) is its homogenizing coordinate,
gives exceptional equation \(J_0 Z^2+V_0 Z+(R_2)_0=0\) in
the chart \(z=wZ\). If the discriminant is nonzero its double
cover carries the residue \(dx/\sqrt{\Delta}\) on a base chart
with coordinate \(x\). Four simple roots give a nonzero
holomorphic form on the normalization. A double root, if no
root has higher multiplicity, gives a nonzero simple pole
on a normalized branch. In either case a loop with nonzero
period and its normal tube contradict exactness on the affine
fibre. These are fixed-component tests, before any use of the
parameter in the rank-one pencil proof. They yield the
asserted alternative for \(\Delta\).

The result applies in characteristic zero after embedding a
finitely generated coefficient field, with geometric irreducibility
over the algebraic closure.
