# Coprime widths are impossible in the singular alternating six-sheet profile

**Theorem SixSheetSingularOrdinaryCuspCoprimeWidthsExclusion.**
Let a plane Keller map of generic sheet degree six have the
alternating profile with one unramified omitted prime. In every
polynomial target frame the degrees of its two coordinates on
the normalization of the ramified curve have a common divisor
greater than one.

**Corollary SixSheetSingularOrdinaryCuspWidthAtLeastSix.**
Every branch projection width in this profile is composite and
at least six. The ramified curve has ordinary plane degree at least
eight in every polynomial target frame.

The [six-sheet cusp profiles](KELLER_BOUNDARY_CUSP_PROFILES_AND_OREVKOV_INVARIANT.md#consequences-in-generic-sheet-degree-six)
identify the irreducible ramified curve \(B\) in this profile. It
has affine-line normalization, one ordinary cusp, and all other
analytic branches are smooth. The ordinary cusp is alone over
its target point, because its local finite degree four and
another branch's degree three would exceed six. For the same
reason a self-identification of smooth branches has exactly two
branches. Their distinct source germs have local degree three
and are cyclic power maps. Thus the representation of each such
local complement group in \(A_6\) is abelian, generated by
3-cycles on disjoint sheet sets. The representation of
\(\pi_1(\mathbb C^2\setminus B)\) is transitive, and a smooth
meridian maps to a single 3-cycle.

This proof uses the degrees of a polynomial parametrization and
a deformation of the ramified curve. The argument leaves the
ordinary cusp group intact and uses the local abelian images
only at self-identifications of immersed branches.

## A coprime-degree deformation

Suppose the degrees on the normalization of the ramified curve are
coprime. At least one is odd. Interchange the target coordinates
if needed and denote the widths by \(n,d\), with \(n\) odd. Both
are at least five by the
[width-five lower bound](KELLER_ALTERNATING_ELLIPTIC_TRIPLE_POLES.md#directional-and-total-degree-bounds).
In the normalization parameter \(t\) write the map onto \(B\) as
\((p(t),q(t))\), with the cusp parameter at \(0\).

Consider the coefficient space of pairs \(P,Q\), of maximum
degrees \(n,d\), with \(P'(0)=Q'(0)=0\). Restrict to the open
subset where the leading coefficients are nonzero and the
order-two and order-three coefficient vectors at \(0\) are
linearly independent. This ensures that the germ at \(0\)
is an ordinary cusp. The image curve is normalized by the
parameter line: the map is finite onto its image since
\(P\) is nonconstant, and the field degree over
\(\mathbb C(P,Q)\) divides both \(n\) and \(d\), hence is one.

There is a nonempty Zariski open subset of this space whose
images have just the ordinary cusp, no other parameter over
its value, and at most ordinary nodes elsewhere. To see density,
vary the coefficients with the derivative constraints at zero.
At a nonzero parameter the two vanishing first derivatives
impose two independent conditions, and equality of its image
with the image at zero also imposes two. For two distinct
nonzero parameters, coinciding images impose two independent
conditions. Conditional on equality of the two images, the
two tangent vectors still vary freely. Failure of transversality
therefore imposes a third condition on the coefficient pair.
For three distinct nonzero parameters a common image imposes
four independent conditions. In each incidence the number
of conditions exceeds the dimension of the parameter tuple.
The independent evaluations and derivatives in these counts
follow by univariate Hermite interpolation, including the
derivative at zero. Coefficients through degree five suffice
for values and first derivatives at two distinct nonzero points
and the derivative at zero. Taking the closures of the images
of the bad incidences removes a proper closed subset. The
resulting open subset is path connected in the complex topology.

At infinity, no additional singularity can escape or enter
along a compact path of coefficients with the two leading
coefficients nonzero. More precisely, for \(|x|\) large the \(n\)
solutions of \(P(t)=x\) have leading terms
\((x/P_n)^{1/n}\zeta\), for the \(n\)-th roots of unity \(\zeta\)
and \(P_n\) the leading coefficient of \(P\). The corresponding
values of \(Q(t)\) have leading terms
\(Q_d(x/P_n)^{d/n}\zeta^d\), which are distinct since
\(\gcd(n,d)=1\). The asymptotics and separation are uniform on
compact paths with nonzero leading coefficients. Thus near
infinity the \(n\) images in each \(x\)-line move by a proper
isotopy with the parameter. One can construct an ambient isotopy
there with a vector field along the \(x\)-lines, equal to the
velocity of each moving point in a small disc about it and
zero away from the union of larger disjoint discs. The discs
can be chosen smoothly as the parameters and \(x\) vary, because
the corresponding points stay separated. For fixed \(x\) their
positions over a compact time interval are bounded. This
gives the required isotopy on the unbounded end without an
assumption about the positions of finite singularities.

## Transporting the monodromy

A sufficiently small perturbation of the original parametrization
into the open nodal locus carries a representation onto \(A_6\)
in which a smooth meridian is still a 3-cycle. Here is the
local gluing. Enclose the finitely many singularities in disjoint
small balls whose boundaries are transverse to the branch
discs. For a small perturbation the smooth curve outside these
balls is isotopic to the original outside the balls, with its
boundary links identified. On the bounded part this is isotopy
extension for the embedded smooth families with boundary.
On the unbounded part use the uniform separation just proved.
Inside the cusp ball the change is equisingular. For example,
put the coordinate of order two in the form \(s^2\) and write
the other coordinate as an even series plus \(s^3 h(s^2)\)
with \(h(0)\ne0\). Subtracting the even series and dividing by
\(h\) yields ordinary cusp charts varying analytically with
small changes of coefficients. The cusp ball and its boundary
complement therefore carry the original local representation.

Every other singular ball originally contained two immersed
branches. After perturbation it contains two smoothly embedded
discs, with their distinct boundary components identified
with the original ones. The original local representation
factors through the two meridian linking numbers, since its
image is abelian. The same linking numbers with the two
perturbed discs define a representation on the new local
complement, using the two original meridian images. Indeed the
first homology of each local complement is freely generated by
the two oriented meridians. To see this, bound a loop with a
disc in the ball, transverse to the smooth parts and missing
the isolated singular points. Its signed intersections with
the two branches give the linking numbers. Deleting small
discs at the intersections expresses the loop in homology as
the corresponding sum of meridians. The linking numbers are
independent of the spanning disc by intersection homology in
the ball. On the boundary complement they are identified
under the boundary isotopy. Hence the new representation
agrees with the exterior representation along each boundary,
and van Kampen glues it to a representation on the whole
complement. This is the ball decomposition used in
[Neumann–Norbury, proof of Theorem 1 in Section 3](sources/neumann-norbury-orevkov-invariant.pdf)
([author copy](https://researchers.ms.unimelb.edu.au/~norbury%40unimelb/papers/orevkov.pdf)),
with the equisingular cusp ball treated separately.

Choose a generic \(p\)-line with its intersection points outside
all singular balls. Its meridian images generate the original
group by the [punctured-line generation theorem](KELLER_DIRECTIONAL_RAMIFICATION_AND_POLAR_ENDS.md#meridians-and-polar-ends).
The perturbation preserves these images in the exterior and
therefore preserves surjectivity. Along any path in the open
nodal locus, the smooth exteriors are again locally isotopic,
and each singular ball now has equisingular cusp or node.
Together with the isotopy at infinity, these local isotopies
transport the representation along the entire path, preserving
the class of a smooth meridian.

## A degeneration with two meridians

The pairs in the open locus can be chosen arbitrarily near
\[
                              P_*(t)=t^n,\qquad Q_*(t)=t^2 .
\]
This pair belongs to the coefficient space before requiring
the leading term of \(Q\) and the ordinary cusp jet.
Write \(B_*\) for its image. Its equation in coordinates \(x,y\)
is \(x^2=y^n\), since \(n\) is odd. For the projection to \(x\), fix a generic
base value and loops generating the complement of the exceptional
projection values of \(B_*\). These loops can be kept in a
compact disk. On their union the \(n\) \(y\) points of \(B_*\)
are distinct. For all sufficiently close coefficient pairs
the \(n\) \(y\) points along the same loops remain distinct,
and their geometric braids are identified by continuity.
This assertion concerns a fixed compact union of loops and
does not require the link at infinity to remain fixed as
the leading coefficient of \(Q\) tends to zero.

Apply the affine Zariski–van Kampen presentation for a monic
projection, as in
[Artal Bartolo, Theorem 2.1](https://riemann.unizar.es/~artal/papers/artbraidmon.pdf).
The curves are finite over the \(x\)-line, so the relations
for \(B_*\) on the meridians of the generic \(x\)-line are
the relations of its exceptional-value braids. The generic
nearby curve imposes those same relations, since each of
the old loops is still a loop in its regular projection
locus and acts by the same braid. Equivalently, choose a
horizontal target basepoint of large constant \(y\) above
the compact disk so the loop of basepoints contracts in
the complement. The induced action of each old braid on
the images of the meridians is then trivial. Since a generic
\(x\)-line generates the nearby complement group, this gives
a meridian-preserving surjection from
\(\pi_1(\mathbb C^2\setminus B_*)\) onto that group.

Projection of \(B_*\) to \(y\), on the other hand, has width
two. Its generic transverse line thus generates its
complement group by two meridians. All positive smooth
meridians of this irreducible curve are conjugate. Composing
the surjection with the transported representation gives
two generating 3-cycles for \(A_6\), which is impossible.
If their supports are disjoint they preserve two blocks. If
their supports meet their union has at most five elements,
so at least one sheet is fixed. This contradiction proves the theorem.

For the corollary, if one width were a prime \(\ell\), repeatedly
subtract scalar multiples of powers of that coordinate from the
other to cancel positive degrees divisible by \(\ell\). Each
target shear strictly lowers the degree being reduced. The
remaining degree is positive because the branch normalization
has degree one onto \(B\) and a constant companion would make
\(B\) a coordinate line with first-coordinate degree one.
The remaining degree is therefore coprime to \(\ell\), which
the theorem excludes. The existing lower bound of five now
gives the composite lower bound of six.

In a fixed frame, the ordinary plane degree of \(B\) is the
larger of its two coordinate degrees on the normalization.
A generic affine line cuts \(B\) transversely in smooth
points, and its affine-linear equation pulls back to a
polynomial of that larger degree. Ordinary degree six would
force both coordinate degrees to be six. An invertible
linear target change can cancel one of their leading
coefficients, producing a smaller width, a contradiction.
Ordinary degree seven would give a generic linear
coordinate of prime width seven, also impossible.
