# Directional ramification of a plane Keller map

Let \(F:\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}\) be a
polynomial map with nonzero constant Jacobian, of generic sheet
degree \(N>1\). Let \(\pi:W\to\mathbb A^2_{\mathbb C}\) be the full
finite normalization in the source field. Use any polynomial
coordinate system \(p,q\) on the target and write \(P=p\circ F\),
\(Q=q\circ F\). For a reduced ramified branch component \(B_i\), let
\(w_i(p)=\deg(p|_{B_i^\nu})\) and let
\[
 a_i=\sum_{E\to B_i}(e_E-1)d_E .
\]
Here \(E\) runs through omitted primes, \(e_E\) is the normal
ramification index, and \(d_E\) is the degree on normalizations.
The weight \(a_i\) is the number of sheets minus the number of
cycles of a positive meridian, including its fixed sheets. Define
\(R_p=\sum_i a_i w_i(p)\).

**Theorem KellerDirectionalRamification.**
For any such coordinate \(p\), one has \(R_p\ge N\). The generic
affine line \(p=c\) pulls back in \(W\) to a smooth connected curve.
If \(g_p\) is the genus of its compactification and \(r_p\) the
number of points above \(q=\infty\), then
\[
 R_p=N+2g_p+r_p-2 .
\]
The number and individual pole orders of the polar branches of
\(Q\) on \(P=c\) are constant over all finite \(c\). In particular
\(R_p=N\) forces \(g_p=0\) and exactly two polar branches in every
fibre. In this equality case the generic affine line cover in
\(W\) has source \(\mathbb G_m\), and its map to the \(q\)-line is a
Laurent polynomial with pole orders summing to \(N\).
If \(p,q\) are a polynomial coordinate system on the target, their
two ramification costs cannot both equal \(N\).

### Meridians and polar ends

The full nonproperness curve \(A_F\) is the union of the images of
the omitted primes, by the finite open embedding and properness
of \(\pi\). A sequence escaping in the source with bounded image
has a subsequence converging to the omitted boundary. Conversely,
sequences in the original open source approaching an omitted
point have to escape in the source. Each component is polynomially
parametrized by its affine-line normalization, as in the
[boundary identity](SIX_SHEET_BOUNDARY_BUDGET_AND_MONODROMY.md#the-weighted-boundary-identity).
No component is isomorphic to \(\mathbb A^1\) by
[Chau, Theorem 3](sources/chau-rational-pairs-0804.3172v3.pdf).
These statements also apply after replacing the target by a
polynomial coordinate system, which preserves the Keller condition
and carries the nonproperness curve to its image. Thus no component
is in a fibre of \(p\). On each component normalization, \(p\) is
a nonconstant polynomial. It follows that \(A_F\) is finite over
the \(p\)-line. This applies to the reduced ramified curve
\(B\subset A_F\) as well.

For \(c\) outside finitely many values, the inclusion of the
punctured line \(\{p=c\}\setminus B\) into \(\mathbb A^2\setminus B\)
surjects on fundamental groups. Over the complement of the finite
exceptional \(p\)-values the projection is a locally trivial bundle
of punctured planes, by following the distinct roots of the finite
projection. Its fundamental group is generated by the fibre group
and lifts of generators of the punctured base. Choose a closed
\(p\)-disk containing the exceptional values and the paths for
these base generators. Because \(B\) is finite over \(p\), a large
constant \(q\) is disjoint from \(B\) over this disk. The lifted
base generators at this height contract in \(\mathbb A^2\setminus B\).
The group of the open bundle surjects onto the full complement
group by moving loops off the finitely many excluded vertical
fibres. This proves the claimed surjectivity.

By purity the restriction of \(\pi\) off \(B\) is finite étale.
It is connected by normality and integrality of \(W\), so the
punctured line cover is connected. For generic \(c\) the line also
avoids the singular values of the components of \(A_F\), their
mutual intersections and the finite nongeneric locus of the
finite ramification models. It meets every branch component
transversally. At each meeting with \(B_i\) its finite slice has
smooth cyclic germs over that value, with the cycles of the
meridian as its local degrees. The affine slice is smooth
and connected after restoring those points. Its compactification
gives a connected degree \(N\) morphism to \(\mathbb P^1_q\).
The finite ramification sum is \(R_p\). The ramification over
infinity is \(N-r_p\). Riemann–Hurwitz gives the displayed formula.
Deleting the omitted points gives the original smooth irreducible
generic source fibre without changing its genus.

For any finite \(c\), a disk \(\Delta\) about \(c\) and a large
radius \(T\) give a cylinder \(\Delta\times\{|q|>T\}\) disjoint
from \(A_F\), by its finite projection. The original map is a
degree \(N\) unbranched covering there. The cylinder retracts onto
one circle and its covers restrict to the same collection of
connected punctured disk covers for every \(p\in\Delta\).
A cycle of length \(j\) gives one pole of order \(j\) on the smooth
projective completion of the appropriate original fibre component.
Consequently the full multiset of pole orders is locally constant
and hence constant on the finite \(p\)-line. This follows by the
cylinder argument of [Chau, Theorem 4](sources/chau-rational-pairs-0804.3172v3.pdf).

Each irreducible component of \(P=c\) contributes at least one
pole. Otherwise \(Q\) would be constant on its projective
normalization, contrary to the nonzero derivative of \(Q\)
along the smooth source fibre. As \(r_p\ge1\), the smallest
possible ramification cost is \(N-1\), with equality forcing
\(g_p=0\) and \(r_p=1\). In that case every original fibre is
irreducible by the fixed pole count. By
[Neumann–Norbury, Theorem 2](sources/neumann-norbury-rational-fibres-1998.pdf),
a polynomial with rational generic fibre and all fibres
irreducible is a polynomial coordinate. In such a source frame
the Keller identity forces the other component of the map to be
linear in the second source variable with nonzero constant slope,
contrary to \(N>1\).

If \(R_p=N\), then \(2g_p+r_p=2\) gives \(g_p=0\) and \(r_p=2\).
The compactified generic line cover is a projective line, from
which the affine cover removes its two poles. Choosing these
points as zero and infinity makes the restricted function a
Laurent polynomial. This uses a coordinate on the individual
generic complex slice, or on the slice over an algebraic closure
of the parameter field. If \(R_p=R_q=N\), both components of the
map have irreducible rational generic fibres. The result of
[Chau, Theorem 2](sources/chau-rational-pairs-0804.3172v3.pdf)
then also implies invertibility.

## Six-sheet consequences

**Corollary SixSheetProjectionWidths.**
For a hypothetical six-sheet complex plane Keller map, the widths
in every polynomial target coordinate \(p\) obey the following
restrictions.

| Ramified meridians | Necessary restriction |
|---|---|
| One transposition | \(w_1(p)\ge 6\) |
| Two transpositions | \(w_1(p)+w_2(p)\ge 6\) |
| Transposition and 3-cycle | \(w_T(p)+2w_C(p)\ge 6\) |
| One 3-cycle | \(w_1(p)\ge 4\) |

For the first three rows the corresponding weighted sums of total
plane degrees of the branch components are at least seven. For
the last row the total plane degree of the branch component is
at least five. These statements hold in every polynomial target
frame.

For the table use the [six-sheet census](SIX_SHEET_BOUNDARY_BUDGET_AND_MONODROMY.md):
a transposition has weight one and a 3-cycle weight two. The
directional ramification theorem gives the first three rows and
gives \(w_1(p)\ge3\) for the single 3-cycle. Applying the theorem to
two independent generic linear coordinates in a given frame
gives equal costs of at least seven, with each width equal to the
total plane degree of its branch component.

To exclude width three for the single 3-cycle, write \(h(t)\) for
the cubic restriction of \(p\) to the branch normalization. It has
a critical point. The
[finite cusp classification](KELLER_BOUNDARY_CUSP_PROFILES_AND_OREVKOV_INVARIANT.md#consequences-in-generic-sheet-degree-six)
gives at most one point where the normalization is not immersive.
If a smooth analytic branch at \(t_0\) is critical for \(h\), at
least two of the three generic line punctures coalesce along that
same branch near \(h(t_0)\). Choose the meridians on a nearby line
using disjoint small disks about the limiting punctures and a
common stem into each disk. The coalescing meridians act on the
same triple, because the germ at the corresponding ramified
source point is the cyclic power map of degree three. Even if a
different branch shares that target value, its finite germ acts
on a disjoint set of sheets. A meridian about just one puncture
is trivial on the other germ. Thus at most two distinct triple
supports occur among the three generators. Two triples cannot
act transitively on six sheets: disjoint triples preserve their
two blocks, and intersecting triples leave a fixed sheet. This
contradicts the surjectivity of the punctured line group.

Hence all critical points of \(h\) must lie at the unique cusp
if there is one. The derivative of a cubic has degree two, so
both its zeros must then equal \(t_0\), and \(h-h(t_0)\) vanishes
to order three. All three meridians on a nearby line now lie in
the local complement group of a ball about the cusp value,
using their common stem. The image of that group preserves the
finite local sheet blocks. The cusp has local degree at most
five and no other branch through the cusp value. The finite
fibre has more than one point, so the local action is
intransitive. This too contradicts generation. It follows that
the single 3-cycle branch has width at least four in every
coordinate. An invertible affine change cancels a common top
power of \(t\) in the two coordinate restrictions when they have
equal degree. At least one resulting width is strictly below the
total plane degree of the branch, proving the lower bound five.

The width-five transposition examples investigated in the
[comparison note](https://github.com/alok/jacobian-two/blob/ded8e67fb47c155f83ec9bd68af6014499fc2d61/docs/research-log.md#2026-07-21-the-smallest-width-five-s6-near-miss-is-cyclic)
test the existence of a transitive complement representation.
The width-six lower bound here uses the actual original
polynomial source. The bounds leave higher-width curves and
the full degree-six admission problem open.

**Corollary SixSheetExceptionalFoldDegree.**
If a six-sheet complex plane Keller map with only one ramified
target curve, a transposition curve, has an exceptional polar fold
of type \((2,1,5)\), the original normalized source coordinates
\(a,b\) for that fold satisfy
\[
 \max(\deg_a P,\deg_a Q)\ \ge\ 42 .
\]
The fold prime has normal index two and residue degree one, so
its target normalization is the unique ramified branch used in
the table. Both coordinate restrictions to the fold line have
degree at least six. If both had degree six, an invertible
affine target change would cancel one leading term and contradict
the width bound for a target coordinate. Thus at least one has
restriction degree \(j\ge7\). The
[weighted fold obstruction](../../hessian/hc4-triangular-graphs/EXCEPTIONAL_FOLD_WEIGHTED_JACOBIAN_OBSTRUCTION.md)
gives \(a\)-degree at least \(6j\) for that coordinate in the same
original source frame. Unramified omitted primes are allowed and
there is no upper bound on \(b\)-degree.

## Stronger bounds for smooth finite normalization

The [smooth-normalization argument](KELLER_SMOOTH_NORMALIZATION_POLAR_BOUNDARY.md)
sharpens the six-sheet symmetric bounds to weighted directional costs
\(7,7,8\) and the sole-transposition fold to longitudinal degree at least
48. The [immersed-branch restriction](KELLER_IMMERSED_SOLE_BRANCH_PROJECTION.md)
raises the bound to 54 in the immersed sole-transposition slice and
requires composite width at least six in the immersed alternating case.
