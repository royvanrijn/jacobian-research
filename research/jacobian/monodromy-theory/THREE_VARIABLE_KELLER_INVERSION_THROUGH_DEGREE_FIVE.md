# Three-variable Keller inversion through degree five

**Theorem ThreeVariableKellerInversionThroughDegreeFive.**
Let \(K\) be a field of characteristic zero. Every polynomial map
\(F:K^3\to K^3\) of ordinary total degree at most five with nonzero
constant Jacobian determinant is a polynomial automorphism. In
particular, a noninvertible three-variable Keller map must have
ordinary degree at least six.

The degree bound is \(\max_i\deg F_i\). The case of degree at
most four is [Wang and Yan, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.19](sources/wang-yan-2610.36495v1.pdf).
For degree five, embed a finitely generated coefficient field in
\(\mathbb C\). It suffices to prove inversion after embedding.
After normalization at the origin, uniqueness of the formal
inverse gives its coefficients in the original coefficient
field by recursive comparison of homogeneous parts. If the
complex inverse is polynomial, the formal inverse has finite
support and gives the same two polynomial inverse identities
over \(K\).

## Reduction to a repeated line

Suppose a degree-five map is noninvertible. The
[top-rank and boundary result](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_ONE_FIXED_DOUBLE_LINE_NORMAL_FORM.md#consequence-for-quintic-inversion)
leaves exactly two independent quintic leading forms \(P_5,Q_5\).
Choose \(R\) to cancel the quintic degree. It is quartic, and
its leading form \(R_4\) is geometrically nonreduced. The
[irreducible double-conic exclusion](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_PURE_FIFTH_TRANSVERSE_DOUBLE_CONIC.md#exclusion-of-the-double-conic-boundary)
forces \(R_4\) to have a repeated line. The
[primitive pencil theorem](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_REPEATED_LINE_PENCIL.md)
now applies to the closed pencil generated by the ratio \(P_5/Q_5\).
Its primitive degree \(m\) is at most three.

## Primitive degree three

The quintic span is \(\langle z^5,z^2H\rangle\). The quartic
is a pure fourth power or \(zJ\), where \(J\) is a cubic member
different from \(z^3\). A pure fourth power gives inversion
by the [pure-quartic theorem](QUINTIC_KELLER_PURE_QUARTIC_AND_BINARY_TRIPLE_LINE.md).
For \(R_4=zJ\), take \(H=J\). The dehomogenization \(J(x,y,1)\)
generates the closed pencil field, since the pencil has
members \(z^3,J\). The
[closed cubic theorem](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_CLOSED_CUBIC_PENCIL.md)
then gives inversion.

## Primitive degree two

The quartic is \(z^4\), \(J^2\), or \(z^2J\), for a conic
member \(J\) coprime to the double-line member \(z^2\).
The pure-quartic theorem handles \(z^4\). In the \(J^2\) case
the repeated-line hypothesis requires \(J\) to be a product
of distinct lines. This case is
[excluded by the reducible square-conic theorem](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_REDUCIBLE_SQUARE_CONIC_PENCIL.md).

For \(R_4=z^2J\) with \(J\) irreducible, transversality to
\(z=0\) is [excluded by the stationary fibre argument](KELLER_QUARTIC_LINE_CONIC_STATIONARY_FIBRES.md).
In the tangent case that argument reduces the scalar to
\(R=z+z^2(q+c)\) with \(q=x^2+zy\).
The [two-span reduction](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_TANGENT_LINE_CONIC_SPANS.md)
gives \(\langle z^3q,zq^2\rangle\) or \(\langle z^5,z^3q\rangle\).
They are excluded by the
[divisible-span theorem](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_TANGENT_LINE_CONIC_Q_SPAN.md)
and the [pure-fifth theorem](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_TANGENT_LINE_CONIC_P_EXCLUSION.md).

If \(J\) is reducible, its two lines are distinct and
restrict independently to \(z=0\). Proportional
restrictions would put \(z^2,J\) in the same two binary
directions. Their ratio would be a degree-two rational function
of a linear pencil parameter, contrary to closedness.
We can therefore take \(J=xy\). The
[transverse leading-span calculation](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_TRANSVERSE_DOUBLE_LINE_CONIC_PENCILS.md)
leaves \(\langle z^5,z^3xy\rangle\) or
\(\langle z^3xy,z(xy)^2\rangle\).
The [pure-fifth reduced-line theorem](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_PURE_FIFTH_REDUCED_LINE_PENCIL.md)
and the [zero-parameter mixed theorem](QUINTIC_KELLER_RANK_TWO_ZERO_MIXED_REDUCED_LINE_PENCIL.md)
exclude noninvertibility in those spans.

## Primitive degree one

The two quintic leading forms and the quartic leading form
are binary in the same source directions. The
[binary quartic reduction](QUINTIC_KELLER_PURE_QUARTIC_AND_BINARY_TRIPLE_LINE.md#degree-four-exceptional-scalars)
uses cubic vanishing on a repeated line. It excludes
multiplicity two by the planar and affine scalar arguments
and multiplicity four by the pure-quartic theorem. The
multiplicity-three case reduces by affine changes to
\(R=x+x^2(z+xy)\) while keeping both quintic leading forms
binary. The [binary triple-line exclusion](QUINTIC_KELLER_BINARY_TRIPLE_LINE_SCALAR_EXCLUSION.md)
rules out this scalar. All primitive degrees have been exhausted.


## The remaining minimum-degree question

**Corollary ThreeVariableKellerMinimumOrdinaryDegree.**
Over any field of characteristic zero, the least ordinary total
degree of a noninvertible three-variable Keller map is either six
or seven.

The lower bound is the theorem above. The rational
[Alpöge–Gallagher example](../../EXTERNAL_MATHEMATICAL_TRANSFERS.md#stones-criterion-and-the-location-of-a-singleton-fibre)
has coordinate degrees \(7,6,4\). In the displayed coordinates
the top monomials of the three outputs are \(x^3y^3z\),
\(3x^3y^2z\), and \(-x^3z\), respectively. Its determinant is
\(-2\) and its three rational points over \((0,1,0)\) stay
distinct over every characteristic-zero field. Thus the degree-six
inversion question decides the minimum.
