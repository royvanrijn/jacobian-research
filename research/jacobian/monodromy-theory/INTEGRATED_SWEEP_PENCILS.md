# Integrated pencils: full-rank higher sweeps and their exact groups

This gives polynomial inputs to the all-rank completion theorem,
including a full-rank five-variable sweep with a proved A9 action
and an actual six-variable Keller completion.
It also classifies an unbounded triangular input class and shows
that it cannot supply the remaining exceptional PSL2(11) field.

The alternating actions here already have ordinary-affine witnesses.
The new result is a concrete higher-rank field and completion,
not a new abstract occurrence group or minimum-dimension theorem.

## 1. A finite coefficient construction in every rank

Let r>=1 and h(t) be nonconstant of degree d. Define
\[
 I_r(t,A)=\int_0^t(A-h(s))^r\,ds,\qquad N=rd+1.             \tag{1}
\]
The choice of lower integration endpoint changes only a polynomial
in A and is immaterial for the field pair.

For variables Y1,...,Yr put
\[
 F_r(Y)=-[T^{r+1}]\log(1+Y1T+\cdots+YrT^r),
\]
and remove its pure Y1 term:
\[
       H_r(Y)=F_r(Y)-F_r(Y1,0,\ldots,0).
\]
All these expressions are finite polynomials over characteristic0.

**KMFI109.** There is a polynomial normalized sweep
S=X(p)+gamma(1,p), p=(t,p2,...,pr), with
\[
 X1=h(t),\quad X_i=p_i+R_i(t,p2,\ldots,p_{i-1})\quad(2<=i<=r),
 \qquad \det DS=\gamma^r.                                  \tag{2}
\]
It is finite of degree N and has full rank DX=r.
Its exact field pair is the (r-1)-spectator stabilization of
\[
            k(A,B)\subset k(A,t),\qquad
                       B=(-1)^{r-1}I_r(t,A).               \tag{3}
\]

Here is an explicit finite algorithm for X.
For r1 use X=(h(t),t h(t)-integral h).
For r>=2 put
Y1=gamma+h, Y_i=gamma*p_(i-1)+q_i for2<=i<=r in
\[
                 H_r(Y)+(-1)^{r-1}I_r(t,Y1).               \tag{4}
\]
For j=2,...,r, in order, choose q_j to cancel the coefficient of
gamma^(r+1-j); for j=r make that coefficient p_r instead of0.
Then X_i=q_i and X_(r+1) is the constant term of (4).

The coefficient of q_j in the indicated equation is the unit
(-1)^(r-j+2). This follows from the logarithmic term
Y1^(r+1-j)Y_j. For j<r the term Y_(j+1) contributes the negative
of that coefficient times p_j; no later q enters.
For j=r the prescribed coefficient p_r has the same effect.
Hence q_j=p_j+R_j with the dependencies in (2).
The gamma^r coefficient cancels identically between H_r and
the leading tY1^r term of I_r. These observations prove that
the algorithm always terminates and that (4) is exactly
X_(r+1)+gamma*p_r.

The first r target equations recover gamma,p2,...,pr polynomially
from t,Y1,...,Yr. Holding those targets fixed, differentiation of
(4) gives (-1)^(r-1)(Y1-h(t))^r.
The first r equations have triangular unit Jacobian in
(gamma,p2,...,pr); moving t to the last position contributes the
same sign (-1)^(r-1). Their product is gamma^r, proving (2).

The leading t coefficient in I_r is the nonzero constant
(-h_d)^r/N. Thus t is integral over the target polynomial ring.
The triangular inverses make all other source coordinates
integral, proving finiteness. Equation(3) proves degree N,
because a polynomial of degree N gives that function-field degree.
The first r coordinates of X have determinant h'(t), proving
generic rank r.

Conversely, **every** normalized sweep with the triangular first
r coordinates in (2), and arbitrary polynomial X_(r+1), has
field pair (3). Eliminating its first r target equations gives
a polynomial Q(t,Y1,...,Yr) for its last target. The Jacobian
condition makes Q_t=(-1)^(r-1)(Y1-h(t))^r, so the integration
constant is a polynomial only in the first r targets.
Subtract it by a triangular target automorphism.
This proves the converse without any bound on the coefficients,
degrees of R_i or the original last component.

## 2. The complete rank-four monodromy

**KMFI110.** For r4 the geometric group in (3) is C5 for d1,
and natural A_(4d+1) for every d>=2.
Thus the entire triangular rank-four class (2) is finite and
cannot give exceptional degree11 PSL2(11).

Here is the monodromy proof in every degree.
For generic A, critical points are the d simple roots of h(t)=A,
each with index5. Their critical values are generically distinct.
To prove the latter assertion, parametrize the critical-value
curve by a=h(t), b=I_4(t,h(t)).
In its separable function field use differentiation with respect
to a. Endpoint terms vanish four successive times, giving
\[
                        \frac{d^4b}{da^4}=4!t.             \tag{5}
\]
The derivation preserves k(a,b), so t belongs to that field.
Thus the critical-value parametrization is birational and its
generic values at distinct roots of h(t)=A are distinct.
This is not an assertion about every specialized A.

Over the algebraic closure of k(A), the polynomial cover is
connected and generated by its finite branch five-cycles.
It is primitive: on a nontrivial system of blocks of size>1,
a single five-cycle cannot permute five blocks, since this
would move more than five letters. It fixes every block.
All finite branch generators would then act trivially on
blocks, contradicting transitivity.

For d>=2, N>=9, and the five-cycle fixes at least four points.
Jordan's prime-cycle theorem gives A_N inside the group
([Jones, Theorem1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.5169)).
All branch five-cycles are even, so the relative group is A_N.
To control the full group over k(A,B), its discriminant is square
when k is algebraically closed, as calculated below.
Hence the full geometric group is also A_N.
For d1 there is only one finite branch cycle and the field is
cyclic of degree5; it has no Keller completion.

For an arbitrary characteristic-zero coefficient field k and
h in k[t], d>=2, the arithmetic group is A_N or S_N according
as N=4d+1 is or is not a square in k.
The exact normal-closure constants are k(sqrt(N)).
Indeed P=I_4(t,A)-B has leading coefficient h_d^4/N and
P'=(A-h)^4. Since N=1 modulo4, the discriminant formula gives
\[
 \operatorname{Disc}(P)
     =N\left(\operatorname{Res}(P,A-h)^2/h_d^2\right)^2.     \tag{6}
\]
The geometric group is already A_N; its only possible arithmetic
enlargement is S_N, detected by (6).
This distinguishes geometric and arithmetic actions.

## 3. A fully specified full-rank degree9 map on ordinary A6

**KMFI111.** Set r4,h=t^2 in KMFI109. The resulting finite
five-variable sweep has degree9, full rank4 and group A9.
KMFI104 gives an actual polynomial Keller map on ordinary A6
with coefficients in an explicit quartic field.
Both geometric and arithmetic groups over that field are A9,
with no further normal-closure constants.
Its geometric finite-normalization class group is Z^8, with
canonical vector (4,0,0,0,0,0,0,0).

The degree9 inverse integral is
\[
 I_4(t,A)=A^4t-\tfrac43A^3t^3+\tfrac65A^2t^5
                              -\tfrac47At^7+t^9/9.
\]
The [model](integrated_sweeps_model.json) gives every X coordinate.
Use the critical source point p*=(1,0,0,0), whose target is
\[
                    Y*=(1,2/3,-8/15,23/315,1/15).
\]
At A1 the integral value is128/315, and exactly
\[
 315(I_4(t,1)-128/315)
  =(t-1)^5(35t^4+175t^3+345t^2+325t+128).                   \tag{7}
\]
The quartic R is irreducible over Q and coprime to t^2-1.
Choose a root alpha and put gamma0=1-alpha^2, which is nonzero.
The first four equations recover the regular point explicitly:
p1^0=alpha and, successively,
\[
 p_i^0=Y_i^*-\gamma0 p_{i-1}^0
                    -R_i(p1^0,\ldots,p_{i-1}^0),\quad2<=i<=4 .
\]
Equation(7) proves the last target equation.
No numerical root approximation or remaining coefficient system
is being used.

At p*, the following rational matrix gives the regular Jordan frame:
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0&0\\1&1&0&0\\4/3&1&1&0\\2/15&2/3&1&1
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \det T=1,\quad T^{-1}P(p*)T=J.
\]
Replace p_old=p*+T w and use the matched affine target change
from KMFI104, centering X(p*) to0.
Use a=(4,5,6,7), e=(1,5,6,7,8).
The regular stage point is
\[
 \theta0=\left(\gamma0,
  \operatorname{diag}(\gamma0^{-4},\gamma0^{-5},
                      \gamma0^{-6},\gamma0^{-7})
                         T^{-1}(p^0-p*)\right).
\]
Take L0=DE(theta0)^-1 and the rank4 finite rectifier of KMFI103,
computed modulo z^8. These are all finite operations in Q(alpha).
The model serializes every entry of L0, the curve jet h(z)
and the lower-unitriangular A(z), in the basis1,alpha,alpha^2,alpha^3.

With Theta=theta0+L0 phi and v_i=x^(e_i)y_i, the complete map is
\[
 F=(x\gamma,\ E_0(\Theta(v))/x,\
       E_1(\Theta(v))/x^5,E_2(\Theta(v))/x^6,
       E_3(\Theta(v))/x^7,E_4(\Theta(v))/x^8).              \tag{8}
\]
Every quotient is polynomial on all A6 by the verified finite
weighted-jet conditions. The whole determinant is exactly
gamma0=1-alpha^2 by KMFI104's determinant identity.
The two-sided field reconstruction there proves generic degree9
and A9 action. Formula(6) gives square class9, so arithmetic
equals geometric A9 over Q and after the coefficient extension.

The original finite sweep has normalization A5 and class group0.
Its full branch inertia is one5-cycle and four fixed sheets.
The fifth power is the identity, giving nine split geometric
primes over the new C0. Exactly two are retained and seven are
omitted unramified. There is one other omitted ramification prime,
of index5. KMFI108 therefore gives geometric Cl=Z^8 and the
stated canonical vector.

Here **geometric class group** means after extending the quartic
coefficient field to its algebraic closure. Splitting of residue
primes over Q(alpha) itself is not inferred from splitting after
that extension. The map and its arithmetic group are already
defined over the quartic field.

## 4. Scope and reproduction

This supplies a concrete full-rank rank4 input and actual A6
completion, including its exact field action and constants.
It shows that the higher-rank completion is usable beyond
rank-deficient controls. It does not construct PSL2(11).
A remaining rank4 PSL input must leave the entire triangular
first-four-coordinate class (2), not just alter coefficients
inside it. Nonstriangular couplings and other architectures
remain open; no universal action criterion is proved.
