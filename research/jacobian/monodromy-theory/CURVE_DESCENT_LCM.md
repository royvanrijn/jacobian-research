# Global denominator descent and the action spectrum of curve inputs

This strengthens [KMFI8](F11_AFFINE_COMPLETION.md) from one ramified
fiber to the complete curve. It excludes every natural PSL2(q) curve
input for odd q, and gives a permutation invariant that can be checked
before attempting affine completion. It does not classify arbitrary
higher-dimensional Keller extensions.

Work over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field k. A
*stabilized curve pair* means exactly

\[
 K=k(t,u_1,\ldots,u_j)\subset L=k(r,u_1,\ldots,u_j),
 \qquad t=f(r)\in k(r),\quad [k(r):k(t)]=n.
 \tag{1}
\]

The same u_i are independent on both sides. Arbitrary birational
identifications with prospective affine source and target coordinates
are allowed. An arbitrary higher-dimensional extension with the same
monodromy action need not have form (1).

## 1. The global least-common-multiple condition

**KMFI9.** For each branch value b of the rational curve cover, let

\[
 e_b=\gcd\{e_{b,1},\ldots,e_{b,s_b}\}
\]

be the gcd of all ramification indices in that fiber, including the
unramified sheets. If (1) admits a polynomial Keller model of ordinary
affine space, then

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{lcm}_{b} e_b=n.}
 \tag{2}
\]

This is necessary, not sufficient. The single-fiber coprimality
condition of KMFI8 and the point-stabilizer condition of KMFI1 remain
additional tests.

Here is the global denominator proof. Choose an unramified target
value as infinity, and a source coordinate r whose infinity is not in
that fiber. Write

\[
 f(r)=N(r)/Q(r),\qquad
 \deg Q=n,\quad Q\text{ squarefree}.
\]

The degree-n homogenizations N_h,Q_h are coprime. In putative actual
affine coordinates, write t=P(y)/Q_t(y) and r=a(x)/b(x), both reduced.
By quasi-finiteness and etaleness, coprime target polynomials remain
coprime after pullback. The argument in KMFI7 therefore gives a constant
mu in k* with

\[
 Q_t(F)=\mu Q_h(a,b)=\mu b^nQ(r).
\]

Consequently an element S of K* must satisfy

\[
 \boxed{S/Q(r)\in L^{*n}.}
 \tag{3}
\]

There is no assumption that t or r remains polynomial. This is the
global field-class condition behind (2).

Put B=k(u_1,...,u_j) and regard S as a rational function in B(t).
For a finite branch value c, at every r_i over c the valuation of Q(r)
is zero modulo n. It is either zero, or -n at the single chosen
source infinity. If m_c=ord_(t=c)(S), equation (3) gives

\[
 e_{c,i}m_c\equiv0\pmod n
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 m_c\equiv0\pmod{n/e_c}.
 \tag{4}
\]

The equivalence uses e_c|n and the gcd of the complete fiber indices.
At any other finite irreducible place of B(t), the cover is unramified;
all valuations of S there are multiples of n. This includes places
depending on the independent parameters, not only k-rational points.

At the selected unramified infinity, Q has a simple zero at every
point above it. Thus

\[
 m_\infty\equiv1\pmod n.
 \tag{5}
\]

The divisor of a rational function of t has degree zero. All nonbranch
finite places contribute multiples of n, including their residue
degrees. Equations (4)--(5) force 1 to lie in the subgroup of Z
generated by n and the integers n/e_c. That subgroup is

\[
 \gcd_c(n/e_c)\,\mathbb Z
   =\frac{n}{\operatorname{lcm}_c e_c}\,\mathbb Z.
\]

It contains 1 exactly when (2) holds. The valuations at the old curve
places still exist after any birational affine rechart, so moving them
to a new affine boundary cannot evade the argument.

A positive-genus source curve cannot become rational after adding
independent variables: a rational affine space dominating that curve
would, by restricting a general line, give a nonconstant map P^1 to a
positive-genus curve. Thus the rational-curve proof covers every
curve input that could have rational stabilized source.

## 2. A permutation invariant for the curve-induced spectrum

**KMFI10.** For a faithful transitive action (G,Omega) of degree n, define

\[
 c(g)=\gcd\{\text{cycle lengths of }g\},\qquad
 C(G,\Omega)=\operatorname{lcm}_{g\in G}c(g).
 \tag{6}
\]

Every c(g) divides n. A branch meridian with permutation sigma_b has
e_b=c(sigma_b). Hence (2) implies

\[
 \boxed{C(G,\Omega)=n}
 \tag{7}
\]

for an action realized by a Keller field pair induced from a curve
as in (1). This is only a necessary test for that realization class.

For prime-power degree p^a, all c(g) are powers of p. Condition (7)
therefore requires some c(g)=p^a, which means g is one n-cycle.
Consequently a prime-power-degree action without a full cycle has
no stabilized-curve Keller realization. For example, this excludes
the natural A_(2^a) actions: an even-length full cycle is odd and
does not belong to that alternating group.

For q odd and q>=3, take the natural PSL2(F_q) action on n=q+1
projective points. A nontrivial unipotent fixes one point, and a
split semisimple element fixes two; their c(g) equals one.
A nonsplit element of order d has only d-cycles, with
d dividing (q+1)/2. The nonsplit cyclic torus attains order
(q+1)/2. It follows that

\[
 \boxed{C\bigl(\operatorname{PSL}_2(q),\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)\bigr)
           =(q+1)/2<n.}
 \tag{8}
\]

Thus **no curve cover in any natural odd-q PSL2(q) action can become
an absolute Keller map by independent affine stabilization and
arbitrary birational source and target changes**. This includes
q=7,11,13, irrespective of whether a selected quotient curve has
genus zero or positive genus.

The proof of the cycle dictionary is elementary. Split matrices
have two eigenlines over F_q. Unipotents have one. A nonsplit torus
comes from norm-one multiplication in F_(q^2)*; modulo the scalar
pair {+/-1} it is cyclic of order (q+1)/2 and acts freely on
projective points. Its nontrivial elements have equal orbit lengths.
This also proves attainment in (8), not only an upper bound.

The invariant distinguishes actions of the same abstract group:
PSL2(7) has C=4 in degree eight but C=7 in its exceptional
degree-seven action. PSL2(11) has C=6 in degree twelve and C=11
in either exceptional degree-eleven action. Those odd-degree
actions pass (7); they have not thereby acquired Keller maps.

Only geometric monodromy enters (6). Replacing it by a larger
arithmetic normalizer would give a false test: natural PGL2(q)
contains a (q+1)-cycle, whereas the natural geometric PSL2(q)
group in (8) does not.

There is a sharper consequence for the surviving degree-eleven
curve inputs. For prime-power degree n, all e_b are powers of the same prime,
so (2) requires an actual totally ramified fiber. After Mobius
changes the curve cover is therefore polynomial, not merely a
rational function whose group contains an n-cycle.

In either exceptional degree-eleven PSL2(11) action, nontrivial
element indices are 4,6,8,10. A totally ramified eleven-cycle at
infinity contributes ten to the genus-zero budget of twenty.
The finite indices must sum to ten, leaving only (10) or (4,6).
The first would be a two-branch cyclic cover. Full PSL2(11)
therefore forces the polynomial passport

\[
 2^4 1^3\mid3^3 1^2\mid11.
 \tag{8a}
\]

Thus every potentially Keller-compatible stabilized degree-eleven
curve input is reduced to the retained Klein Shabat passport.
This does not prove uniqueness of its Nielsen classes here and
does not construct its affine Keller completion.

The full-cycle conclusion needs the prime-power hypothesis. For
example, natural A_6 has C=6 from cycle types (4,2) and (3,3),
but has no six-cycle because that permutation would be odd.
It passes this necessary composite-degree test; no realization
follows.

## 3. A degree-eight control beyond the earlier obstructions

There is an explicit rational cover with geometric natural PSL2(7)
monodromy which passes the finite-inertia and KMFI8 common-index
tests, but fails KMFI9. Let

\[
 F(v)=v^7+14v^5+63v^3+70v-7/v,\qquad
 P(v)=v^4+5v^2+1 .
 \tag{9}
\]

Then

\[
 F'(v)=7P(v)^2/v^2.
\]

The critical values are the two roots of z^2+1728, each with
fiber 3^2 1^2. The pole fiber is 7 1. Every fiber has gcd one.
All divisorial inertia fixes sheets, and every individual KMFI8
test gcd(e,8/e)=1 passes. But the lcm of the fiber gcds is one,
whereas n=8. The stabilized field pair is excluded by (2).

The group identification uses a classical modular cover, not the
passport alone. The X_0(7) formula

\[
 j(t)=\frac{(t^2+13t+49)(t^2+5t+1)^3}{t}
\]

is given in [Mestre, appendix p=7](https://wstein.org/msri06/refs/mestre-method-of-graphs/mestre-en.pdf);
the polynomial also appears as equation (6.1) in
[Fisher's paper](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~taf1000/papers/visible7.pdf).
Its modular geometric group is PSL2(7) on the eight lines of F_7^2.
The identity

\[
 (t^2+13t+49)(t^2+5t+1)^3-1728t
  =(t^4+14t^3+63t^2+70t-7)^2
\]

shows that the base change z^2=j-1728 has source t=v^2 and
z=F(v). A quadratic extension cannot intersect the simple PSL2(7)
Galois closure nontrivially, so geometric monodromy stays PSL2(7).
This is a derivation from the attributed modular identification.

For E(V)=V^8+14V^6+63V^4+70V^2-zV-7, exact calculation gives

\[
 \operatorname{Disc}_V E=-7^7(z^2+1728)^4.
\]

Thus arithmetic monodromy over Q(z) contains odd permutations and
must not be reported as the geometric group. No exact arithmetic
normalizer assertion is needed here.

This example demonstrates a genuine difference between local
fixed-sheet inertia, one-fiber congruences and global denominator
descent. It is retained as an excluded cover, not a live completion
proposal.

## 4. An actual Keller map induced from a curve

**KMFI11.** The new obstruction does not exclude all stabilized curve
pairs. The retained cubic weighted Keller map admits an exact
one-curve presentation. Define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 u&=1+xy,\qquad \gamma=1-\tfrac32xy+x^2z,\\
 A&=(u+u^2-2u^3\gamma)/x^2,\\
 B&=(1+2u-3u^2\gamma)/x,\qquad C=x\gamma .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
\]

Both displayed quotients are polynomials, and det D(A,B,C)=1.
With W=u*gamma, s=BC and t=AC^2 the inverse relation is

\[
 W^3-W^2+sW-t=0.
\]

Set

\[
 p=s-\tfrac13,\quad q=s/3-t-\tfrac{2}{27},\quad
 \ell=q/p,\quad \tau=q^2/p^3,\quad
 v=(W-\tfrac13)/\ell .
\]

Then

\[
 \tau v^3+v+1=0,\qquad \tau=-(v+1)/v^3.
 \tag{11}
\]

There are explicit field equalities

\[
 k(A,B,C)=k(\tau,\ell,C),\qquad
 k(x,y,z)=k(v,\ell,C).
 \tag{12}
\]

For target reconstruction, p=ell^2/tau, q=ell^3/tau,
s=p+1/3, t=s/3-q-2/27, B=s/C and A=t/C^2.
For source reconstruction use W=ell*v+1/3 and

\[
 \gamma=s-2W+3W^2,\quad x=C/\gamma,\quad
 u=W/\gamma,\quad y=(u-1)/x,\quad
 z=(\gamma-1+\tfrac32xy)/x^2.
\]

All identities are on nonempty rational opens, as appropriate for
field identification. The original polynomial map is defined on all
ordinary affine three-space and has the constant Jacobian claimed
above. Equation (12) therefore gives an actual absolute realization,
not only a rational chart.

The degree-three curve has two fibers of type 2 1 and a totally
ramified fiber 3. Its fiber gcds have lcm three, and its group is
S_3. The curve's cubic discriminant is -tau*(4+27*tau), nonsquare
geometrically and arithmetically; hence both groups are S_3.
This is a new field presentation of the retained
[weighted example](../../foundations/verified/PRIMITIVE_MONODROMY_ATOMICITY.md),
not a new proper-simple group realization.

## 5. The remaining scope

The group invariant C=n is not sufficient, and it is not a necessary
condition for an arbitrary higher-dimensional Keller field pair.
It applies to the curve-induced class (1). No argument here proves
that every Keller map belongs to that class. It would therefore be
incorrect to turn (8) into a general no-go for natural PSL2(q) maps.

The next unresolved proper-simple gates are exceptional odd-degree
actions such as degree eleven for PSL2(11), or genuinely
higher-dimensional field pairs beyond (1). Their construction still
requires full polynomial coordinates, exact generic field and group
control, and a constant full Jacobian. The complete action spectrum
remains open.
