# Rational degree-one fibers cannot hide primitive curvature

A rational homogeneous direction with a linear head and one linear
fiber coordinate must itself be linear under polynomial normalized
full rank. The full divisor-residue sequence, not just the trace,
forces its fiber shift to have zero Hessian.

This closes an all-degree family with an irreducible nonlinear critical
factor. In rank4 it includes every rank-one cubic perturbation built
over an irreducible rank3 quadric with its one-dimensional translation
kernel. It does not classify arbitrary cubic directions or nondegenerate
quadratic critical factors.

## 1. Rational homogeneous degree-one fiber rigidity

Work over characteristic-zero constants and use p=(q,z), q in k^m,
r=m+1>=2. Let C in GL_m, mu and lambda nonzero constants, and
\[
             W=(Cq,\mu z+T(q)),\qquad T(tq)=tT(q),          \tag{1}
\]
where T is rational. Let G be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of
positive degree. Assume the reconstructed
\[
                 X=(-\lambda G,\ G(W-\lambda p))
\]
is polynomial, normalized and of generic rank r.
Equivalently P=G*N with
N=DW+W*dlogG-lambda*I is nilpotent and X has that rank.

**KMFI164.** T is a linear polynomial. Thus H=G*W belongs exactly to
the old scalar-times-linear architecture KMFI121; no primitive rational
curvature survives these hypotheses.

First DW,W is cyclic even though W is rational.
Full rank makes N a regular nilpotent block.
If its DW-invariant Krylov span generated by W were proper, it would
be N-invariant and lie in ImN. A left kernel row w of N would kill W,
hence w*DW=lambda*w. Euler's identity DW*p=W gives w*p=0.
But KMFI124 makes p cyclic for P, hence for N, so a left kernel row
cannot kill p. This is the contradiction. This argument uses rational
homogeneous Euler identities only; it asserts no polynomiality of W.

The head projection of the Krylov span gives
Cq,C^2q,..., so q is cyclic for C.
The proof of KMFI162 works over k(q), hence applies to this rational
fiber coefficient while X remains polynomial.
It gives mu!=lambda, det(mu*I-C)!=0, a positive integer nu=deg_zG,
and, over k(q),
\[
                 G=J(q)(z+S(q))^\nu.
\]
The shift is independent of lambda:
\[
 S=\frac{T+dT(\mu I-C)^{-1}Cq}{\mu}
                  =dT(\mu I-C)^{-1}q.                    \tag{2}
\]
The last equality uses the degree-one Euler identity for T.
In particular S is rational homogeneous of degree1.

Put A=DW and l=z+S. The characteristic feedback identity has no pole
at the divisor l0 in k(q)[z]. Its separate logarithmic residue gives
\[
                    dl\,A^jW|_{z=-S}=0
                            \quad(0\le j\le m).          \tag{3}
\]
Head denominators are units at this generic graph divisor; they do
not cancel or alter its positive residue nu.

Write w_j for the last component of A^jW at z=-S.
The head component is C^(j+1)q and
w_(j+1)=dT*C^(j+1)q+mu*w_j.
By(3), w_j=-dS*C^(j+1)q.
Consequently
\[
           (dS*C+dT-\mu*dS)C^jq=0\quad(1\le j\le m).
\]
Cq,...,C^mq form a basis over k(q). Thus
\[
                              dT=dS(\mu I-C).             \tag{4}
\]
Symmetry of the Hessian obtained by differentiating(4) gives
\[
                            H_S C=C^tH_S.                 \tag{5}
\]
Euler homogeneity gives H_S*q=0. Equation(5) then gives
H_S*C^j*q=(C^t)^j*H_S*q=0 for all j.
Cyclicity forces H_S=0. In characteristic zero a rational function
with zero Hessian is affine; degree-one homogeneity makes S linear.
Equation(4) and homogeneity make T linear too.
No diagonalization of C or simple-eigenvalue assumption is used.

The graph l is only used as a divisor over the head function field.
No rational nonlinear source change is claimed to preserve normalization.
Only after S is proved linear does it define an allowed linear shear.
The entire accepted input is then covered by KMFI121.

An actual full-rank control, over omega^2-omega+1=0, is
W=(u,omega*z+(omega-1)u), G=u(z+u), lambda=1+omega.
It has S=u, linear T, and whole sweep Jacobian gamma^2.
A noncyclic head can satisfy(4)--(5) with a nonlinear rational S;
it is not a counterexample to the full-rank cyclicity argument.

## 2. Nonlinear irreducible critical factors with one translation direction

Work over algebraically closed characteristic-zero constants.
Let Q be an irreducible homogeneous polynomial of degree k>=2 on k^r
whose space of constant translation directions is exactly k*b,
with b nonzero. Let R be homogeneous of degree k+1 with partial_bR=0,
and let M in GL_r. Put
\[
                   a=Mb,\qquad V=QMp+aR.                 \tag{6}
\]
Here partial_bQ=0 by definition.

**KMFI165.** No nonzero homogeneous polynomial scalar F gives (6) a
normalized input of generic rank r, for any M or R as above.
In rank4, every rank3 irreducible quadratic Q with its radical line b
satisfies the hypothesis, so all corresponding primitive cubic
perturbations are excluded in every scalar degree.

The rank-two determinant lemma and Euler give exactly
\[
                         \det DV=(k+1)\det(M)Q^r.         \tag{7}
\]
Indeed M^-1*DV=Q*I+p*dQ+b*dR, with dQ*b=dR*b=0,
dQ*p=kQ and dR*p=(k+1)R.
For a hypothetical multiplier of degree s, Euler gives
 detD(FV)=(s+k+1)/(k+1)*F^r*detDV, while normalization gives
 detD(FV)=(-f)^r. Thus
\[
                    f=-\lambda FQ,\qquad
                    \lambda^r=(s+k+1)\det M\ne0.
\]
The trace equation is div(FV)=r*lambda*Q*F.
Since partial_bV=Q*a, KMFI160 excludes partial_aQ!=0.
Thus a belongs to the translation kernel of Q, and a=mu*b with mu!=0.

Choose b=e_z. Then Q,R depend only on q and
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}C&0\\L&\mu\end{pmatrix},\quad C\in GL_{r-1}.
\]
Set G=FQ, a polynomial, and divide the direction by Q:
\[
 W=V/Q=(Cq,\mu z+T(q)),\qquad T=Lq+\mu R/Q.
\]
This is the rational homogeneous degree-one situation of KMFI164,
with the same polynomial X=(-lambda*G,G(W-lambda*p)).
Hence T is linear and Q divides R.

Write R=Q*L1. Then V=Q*M'*p, where
M'=M*(I+b*L1). Because partial_bR=partial_bQ=0, L1(b)=0 and
detM'=detM. The accepted input would be scalar-times-linear with
scalar G=FQ. KMFI121 makes that scalar a product of linear forms
over the algebraically closed constants. Its irreducible nonlinear
factor Q is impossible. This proves the complete obstruction,
including the case R already divisible by Q.
No bound on scalar degree or on the coefficients of R is used.

The one-dimensional translation-kernel and irreducibility hypotheses
are exact scope conditions. This argument does not settle arbitrary
cubic directions whose fourth-power base is a nondegenerate rank4
quadric, nor all directions with the same rank3 critical quadric.

## 3. An explicit cubic passes the older local gates

Use p=(x,y,w,z) and
\[
 Q=x^2+y^2+w^2,\quad R=x^3,\quad b=e_z,\quad
 C=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0\\-1&2&0\\0&0&2\end{pmatrix},
 \quad M=\operatorname{diag}(C,5).
\]
Then V=QMp+5b*x^3 is primitive and
\[
               \det DV=150Q^4,\qquad
               \det(V,DVV,(DV)^2V,(DV)^3V)|_{p=(1,1,1,1)}
                         =-347733000.
\]
Thus the perfect fourth-power and generic cyclicity gates pass.
The last-coordinate translation coefficient is Q and its target
direction is 5b, so its directional derivative is zero: that
translation obstruction does not reject this matrix.

All projective base points are Q=x=0.
There dR=0 and DV=Mp*dQ. Since dQ*Mp=4Q, its square is zero
at every base point. The base-point nilpotency test therefore passes
everywhere, not just at a selected point.

Nevertheless KMFI165 excludes every scalar degree.
There is also an exact rational feedback defect independent of lambda.
For T=5x^3/Q, formula(2) gives
\[
 S=\frac{x^2(7w^2x+9w^2y+9x^3+9x^2y+9xy^2+9y^3)}{6Q^2}.
\]
At q=(1,1,1), the row dS*C+dT-5dS is
\[
                       (-106/81,\ 82/81,\ 26/27),
\]
so the full graph residue condition(4) fails.
An admissible determinant, cyclic derivative and nilpotent derivatives
at every base point still do not construct a normalized full-rank input.
