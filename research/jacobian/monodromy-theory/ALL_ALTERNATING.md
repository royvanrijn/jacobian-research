# Every natural alternating action of degree at least five occurs

Work over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic zero.

**KMFI14--15.** For every n>=5, there is a polynomial Keller self-map
of ordinary affine three-space with geometric generic monodromy
A_n in its natural degree-n action. For odd n the explicit family below
has determinant -3; for even n it has determinant -1.
The radicand construction also realizes S_n for every n>=3.

**KMFI16.** For every a>=3 the even family realizes A_(2^a), but its
field pair cannot be obtained from any curve cover by adjoining the
same independent variables to source and target, even after arbitrary
birational affine recharts. Thus the curve-generated spectrum is
strictly smaller than the full affine-space Keller spectrum.

The even construction is the level-two cyclic-cone family of
Kistner--Shaska,
[arXiv:2608.02863v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02863v1), Sections 3 and 6.
The odd construction extends their quadratic beta--Hermite lift by
replacing the quadratic radicand with a d-th power. We give the complete
algebra, rather than importing a generic-group assertion for that extension.
No priority claim is made. The
[versioned primary source](sources/alternating-realizations-manifest.json),
[exact checker](verify_all_alternating.py),
[model](all_alternating_model.json), and
[receipt](all_alternating_receipt.json) retain evidence.
Universal claims use the proofs below, with no formal verification or
independent whole-proof review asserted.

## 1. The global graded lift

We use the determinant and field identities proved in
[ALTERNATING_REALIZATIONS.md, Section 1](ALTERNATING_REALIZATIONS.md).
For L=1+x^p y, v=x^p z, if Q=L^p A and P=L^p B with polynomial
A,B vanishing at (L,v)=(1,0), then

\[
 F=(A/x^p,\ B/x^p,\ C),\qquad C=xL
 \tag{1}
\]

is polynomial everywhere on A^3, and

\[
 \det JF=-\operatorname{Jac}_{L,v}(P,Q)/L^{2p-1},\qquad
 k(x,y,z)=k(L,v,C),\quad k(F)=k(P,Q,C).
 \tag{2}
\]

In the even family we instead write w=xz-1 with p=1; its lift point
is (L,w)=(1,-1). Translating v to w does not change the determinant.
C is independent over the plane source and target fields, so both
degree and normal-closure permutation group are preserved.
These identities establish polynomiality and the constant Jacobian
on all affine space.

## 2. Arbitrary-power radicands: every odd A_n and every S_n

Fix d>=2 and p in {2,3}, and put n=d(p-1)+1. Define

\[
 M_{p,d}(t)=\int_1^t(\theta^d-1)^{p-1}\,d\theta.
\]

This polynomial has degree n and an exact p-fold root at 1.
Let

\[
 J_{p,d}=\int_0^1(\theta^d-1)^{p-1}\,d\theta\ne0.
\]

For every d-th root of unity omega,
M_(p,d)(omega)=(omega-1)J_(p,d): the primitive with value zero
at zero is a polynomial t times a polynomial in t^d.
Also M_(p,d)(0)=-J_(p,d) is nonzero.
Since n>p, M has another root zeta. It is neither zero nor any
d-th root of unity, and it is simple, since M' vanishes only at
those roots of unity. Put delta=zeta^d-1 !=0.

Choose g(L) by truncating (1+delta L)^(1/d) modulo L^p and adding
a multiple of L^p to make g(1)=zeta. Set

\[
 \rho=g(L)+L^p v,\quad
 Q=\frac{\rho^d-1-\delta L}{d},\quad
 P=\frac{dp}{\delta^p}
    \int_1^\rho(\theta^d-1-dQ)^{p-1}\,d\theta .
 \tag{3}
\]

All integrals here mean polynomial antiderivatives; Q is held constant
before its polynomial value is substituted. The interpolation gives
L^p|Q. Expanding the integral in Q, its term of Q-degree j has
vanishing order p-j in rho-1 at rho=1. Since rho-1 is divisible by L,
and Q by L^p, this term has L-order at least p-j+pj>=p.
Consequently L^p|P as well.

Let A=Q/L^p, B=P/L^p. At the lift point, rho=zeta and Q=0,
so P is proportional to M_(p,d)(zeta)=0. Thus A(1,0)=B(1,0)=0,
which proves that (1) is a polynomial map globally. Furthermore

\[
 \operatorname{Jac}_{L,v}(\rho,Q)=\frac{\delta}{d}L^p,\qquad
 P_\rho\big|_Q=\frac{dp}{\delta}L^{p-1},\qquad
 \operatorname{Jac}_{L,v}(P,Q)=pL^{2p-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]

Equations (2) prove det JF=-p.

The inverse rational coordinate formulas

\[
 L=(\rho^d-1-dQ)/\delta,\qquad
 v=(\rho-g(L))/L^p
 \tag{5}
\]

prove k(L,v)=k(rho,Q). At fixed Q, P is a polynomial of exact
degree n in rho. Therefore the actual map F has generic degree n.

We now prove its group. Write Z=1+dQ. Over the algebraic closure of
k(Q), the derivative P_rho is proportional to
(rho^d-Z)^(p-1). There are d simple roots of rho^d-Z,
each of ramification index p. Their critical values are distinct.
Indeed, if h^d=Z and omega ranges over the d-th roots of unity,
the primitive from 0 to h*omega has value
h^n*omega*J_(p,d), because n is 1 modulo d.
The shift from the lower integration limit 1 does not change their
distinctness. Infinity is totally ramified of index n.
The index sum is d(p-1)+(n-1)=2n-2, as required.

The geometric curve group is transitive and generated by the d
finite inertia generators. For p=2 these are single transpositions,
so the connected graph of their supports gives S_n.
For p=3 they are single three-cycles, so the connected-support
[three-cycle lemma](ALTERNATING_REALIZATIONS.md) gives A_n.
This proof includes every d>=2; it does not infer the group from
a bounded list of degrees.

The plane-field group over k(P,Q) contains this group computed after
algebraic closure of k(Q). For p=2 it is already S_n.
For p=3 the polynomial derivative is a constant in k* times
a square in k(Q)[rho]. The polynomial discriminant of P(rho,Q)-T
is therefore a square in k(Q,T), up to a constant in k*.
Since k is algebraically closed, this constant is also a square.
Thus the whole plane-field group is contained in A_n.
Together with (2), this proves:

**KMFI14.** Equation (3) realizes S_(d+1) for p=2 and A_(2d+1)
for p=3, d>=2, on ordinary A^3.

For example d=p=3 gives an actual degree-seven A7 map over a
quartic number field. Its coefficient zeta satisfies

\[
 2\zeta^4+6\zeta^3+12\zeta^2+13\zeta+9=0,\qquad
 \delta=\zeta^3-1,
\]

and one may take

\[
 g(L)=1+\frac{\delta L}{3}-\frac{\delta^2L^2}{9}
      +\left(\zeta-1-\frac{\delta}{3}
                      +\frac{\delta^2}{9}\right)L^3 .
\]

The checker retains the exact A and B (19 and 83 nonzero monomials
in L,v), verifies divisibility and the polynomial lift, and checks
the full quotient-Jacobian identity in that exact quartic field.

### 2.1 These odd examples really are independently stabilized curves

This point can be proved, not merely guessed from a chosen pencil.
For p in {2,3}, define

\[
 H_{p,d}(w)=
 \sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{j}
       \frac{(-1)^{p-1-j}w^j}{dj+1},\quad
 I(Z)=\int_0^1(\theta^d-Z)^{p-1}\,d\theta ,
\]
\[
 R=\frac{\delta^p}{dp}P+I(Z),\quad
 \ell=R/Z^{p-1},\quad \tau=\ell^d/Z,\quad
 w=\rho^d/Z .
\]

Equation (3) gives R=rho*Z^(p-1)*H_(p,d)(w), and hence

\[
 \boxed{\tau=w H_{p,d}(w)^d.}
 \tag{6}
\]

The target inverse is Z=ell^d/tau,
Q=(Z-1)/d and P=(dp/delta^p)(ell*Z^(p-1)-I(Z)).
The source inverse is rho=ell/H_(p,d)(w),
Z=ell^d/[w*H_(p,d)(w)^d], followed by (5).
It follows that the actual threefold field pair is exactly

\[
 k(F)=k(\tau,\ell,C)\ \subset\
 k(x,y,z)=k(w,\ell,C).
 \tag{7}
\]

The variables ell,C are independent: these formulas are birational
inverses of two-dimensional source and target coordinate systems,
and C is independent by (2).

The derivative of the fixed curve is

\[
 \frac{d}{dw}\bigl(wH_{p,d}(w)^d\bigr)
    =H_{p,d}(w)^{d-1}(w-1)^{p-1}.
\]

H_(p,d)(0) and H_(p,d)(1)=J_(p,d) are nonzero, and the same
identity shows H_(p,d) has no repeated root.
Its passport is therefore

\[
 d^{p-1}1\ \mid\ p\,1^{n-p}\ \mid\ n .
\]

The fiber gcds are 1,1,n, so it passes the global lcm condition.
For p=3 this yields a fixed polynomial A_(2d+1) cover

\[
 \tau=w\left(\frac{w^2}{2d+1}
              -\frac{2w}{d+1}+1\right)^d.
\]

Thus all odd alternating actions in this theorem are actual
curve-induced Keller examples, with both rational reconstructions.
For d=5 the degree-eleven group is A11, whose single-three-cycle
inertia already distinguishes it from exceptional PSL2(11);
this construction does not realize that smaller group.

## 3. Level-two cones: every even A_n, n>=6

Fix d>=3 and set

\[
 a=\frac{-3+\sqrt{-3(2d-1)}}{2(d-2)},\qquad b=1-a,\qquad
 \varphi(W)=aW^2+bW^d .
 \tag{8}
\]

Both a and b are nonzero. The following quadratic lift condition is exact:

\[
 E_d(a)=\frac{a^2}{3}
        +\frac{2a(1-a)}{d+1}
        +\frac{(1-a)^2}{2d-1}=0,
\]
\[
 3(d+1)(2d-1)E_d(a)
  =2(d-2)^2a^2+6(d-2)a+3(d+1).
 \tag{9}
\]

Formula (8) solves this quadratic. This gives explicit coefficients
in a quadratic number field for every even degree 2d>=6.

Put L=1+xy, w=xz-1, T=Lw and W=Lw^2. Define polynomials with
zero integration constants by

\[
 R'(W)=2\varphi'(W)/W,\qquad S'(W)=\varphi'(W)R(W),
\]
\[
 Q=T+\varphi(W),\qquad
 P=L+R(W)T+S(W).
 \tag{10}
\]

Since phi starts in degree two, R and S are multiples of W,
and P,Q are divisible by L after T=Lw, W=Lw^2.
Let A=Q/L, B=P/L.
At (L,w)=(1,-1), T=-1,W=1 and phi(1)=1, so Q=0.
Eliminating T gives

\[
 \Pi(W,Y)=\frac{(Y-\varphi(W))^2}{W}
       +R(W)(Y-\varphi(W))+S(W),\qquad
 \Pi_W=-\frac{(Y-\varphi(W))^2}{W^2}.
 \tag{11}
\]

At Y=0, Pi(W,0) has no constant term and equals
-minus the integral from 0 to W of phi(s)^2/s^2.
Hence P(1,-1)=Pi(1,0)=-E_d(a)=0.
Both A and B vanish at the lift point. Therefore

\[
 F_{2d}(x,y,z)=(A/x,\ B/x,\ xL)
 \tag{12}
\]

is polynomial on every point of A^3.

The brackets in (L,w) are {L,T}=L, {L,W}=2T and {T,W}=W.
Substituting (10), terms cancel using R'=2phi'/W and S'=phi'R,
giving Jac(P,Q)=L. Thus **det JF_(2d)=-1** by (2).
The full Jacobian proof is independent of d.

Furthermore

\[
 k(L,w)=k(T,W)=k(W,Q),\quad
 T=Q-\varphi(W),\quad
 L=T^2/W,\quad w=W/T .
 \tag{13}
\]

At generic Y=Q, the rational function Pi(W,Y) has a simple pole
at W=0 and a pole of order 2d-1 at infinity.
The latter follows either from (11), whose leading term is
-b^2 W^(2d-2), or from the leading coefficient
-b^2/(2d-1) of Pi. Its degree is therefore 2d, which is the actual
generic degree of (12).

The d simple roots of phi(W)=Y are the finite critical points,
each of local degree three. Their critical values are distinct
for generic Y. Indeed, they are S(W) at those points.
Since deg(S)=2d-1 and deg(phi)=d, the field degree

[k(W):k(S(W),phi(W))]

divides both 2d-1 and d, and is therefore one.
Thus the parametrization W -> (S(W),phi(W)) is birational;
over generic Y its d source points have d distinct image values.
The pole inertia has type (2d-1,1), and the index sum
2d+(2d-2)=4d-2 is Riemann--Hurwitz.
The transitive geometric curve group is generated by d
single three-cycles, so it is A_(2d).

As before, this computation over the algebraic closure of k(Y)
must be compared with the group over k(P,Q).
Write D=W(Pi(W,Y)-U), a polynomial of degree 2d whose leading
coefficient is lambda=-b^2/(2d-1) and whose constant coefficient
is Y^2. Equation (11) gives

\[
 WD'=D-(Y-\varphi)^2,\qquad
 \operatorname{disc}_W D
  =(-1)^{d+1}(2d-1)
     \left(\frac{\operatorname{Res}_W(D,Y-\varphi)}{bY}\right)^2 .
 \tag{14}
\]

The coefficient is a square in k. The plane-field group is thus
contained in A_(2d) and contains the geometric curve A_(2d).
The independent C in (2) proves the assertion for the full affine map.

**KMFI15.** Every even natural A_(2d), d>=3, occurs on ordinary A^3.
Together with KMFI14, this proves occurrence of **every natural A_n
for n>=5**. The A6 and A8 members are replayed in their exact
quadratic fields, including lift values, field inverses, the
Jacobian, degree and a discriminant identity control.

## 4. An actual higher-dimensional obstruction to curve reduction

Recall the cycle-gcd invariant C(G,Omega) and the necessary
C=n condition for independently stabilized curve fields in
[KMFI9--10](CURVE_DESCENT_LCM.md).

For the natural action of A_(2^a), the gcd of cycle lengths of
any permutation is a power of two dividing 2^a.
A gcd of 2^a would require one full 2^a-cycle, which is odd,
so no such element belongs to A_(2^a). The maximum possible gcd
is therefore at most 2^(a-1). It is attained by two disjoint
2^(a-1)-cycles, whose product is even. Hence

\[
 C(A_{2^a},\{1,\ldots,2^a\})=2^{a-1}<2^a.
 \tag{15}
\]

For a>=3, KMFI15 gives an actual affine-space Keller realization
of the same action. KMFI10 says no independently stabilized curve
field pair in that action can admit any such model. Therefore
the field pair of (12), including any further independent
stabilization, has no presentation as an independently stabilized
curve pair after arbitrary birational source and target changes.

**KMFI16.** These are explicit actual Keller extensions beyond the
curve-generated class, beginning with A8 over Q(sqrt(-21)).
In particular, C=n is **not a necessary condition for general
affine-space Keller monodromy**. Its role is exactly the previously
proved curve-field condition. This gives concrete counterexamples
to extending that condition to arbitrary field pairs.

The alternating power-two action at n=4 also fails the curve test,
but (8)--(9) require d>=3 and do not realize A4.
For natural A3, the nontrivial nilpotent obstruction excludes
realization. The abstract A4 action remains unresolved here.

## 5. What remains for the original objective

The occurrence family is now substantially larger: all natural
symmetric and all natural alternating actions in the ranges above
have actual polynomial affine-three-space witnesses.
Only the natural actions are asserted; another transitive action
of the same abstract group needs its own analysis.

These families do not decide arbitrary faithful transitive actions.
They do not realize the natural degree-twelve PSL2(11) action or
either exceptional degree-eleven action.
Their general groups are strictly larger: A11 or A12 are not
PSL2(11). Keeping the same radicand or level-two-cone architecture
forces single-three-cycle generation, so changing interpolation
coefficients within these constructions cannot produce PSL2(11).

The A8 control shows why the negative curve results alone cannot
settle natural PSL2(11): actual proper-simple affine-space maps
can lie outside independent curve stabilization.
The full action spectrum and the specified PSL2(11) realization
or action-level exclusion remain open.
