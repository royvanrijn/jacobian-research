# The degree-eight coefficient row is a unit: global recovery without charts

14 November 2026. PF2RULED28 closes the entire all-zero-coefficient
stratum retained by [PF2RULED27](DEGREE_EIGHT_AFFINE_REDUCTION.md).
In both marked coefficient fields there are explicit polynomials C_i
of degree at most3 such that

    C0*D0+C1*D1+C2*D2+C3*D3+C4*D4=1.                 (1)

This exact polynomial identity excludes every common zero of the D_i,
including special parameter values. It also recovers the root constant
polynomially on the entire remaining necessary locus:

    Zstar=sum_(i=0)^4 C_i*E_i.                         (2)

Thus the degree-eight necessary problem is a single four-parameter core,
with no coefficient-pivot division and no missing coefficient-zero case.
Its nonzero-kappa solutions remain unresolved. This is not an exclusion
of the whole degree-eight case or a construction of a finite map.

## Equality of the global elimination and saturation ideals

Let H_i=D_i*Z-E_i and A_i=D_i*Zstar-E_i. From (1),

    sum C_i*H_i=Z-Zstar,
    H_i=D_i*(Z-Zstar)+A_i.                            (4)

Consequently the old compatibility ideal generated by E5 and the five
H_i equals the ideal generated by Z-Zstar,E5 and the five A_i. This
is equality of ideals, not only a pointwise generic elimination. It
introduces no variable denominator and uses no discarded rank stratum.

The nonzero condition also transfers exactly, since

    (t*M(s)*Z-1)-(t*M(s)*Zstar-1)=t*M(s)*(Z-Zstar).

The full old saturated presentation is therefore isomorphic, after
eliminating Z, to

    ( E5, D_i*Zstar-E_i for i=0,...,4,
      t*M(s)*Zstar-1 ) in K[c4,c5,c6,c7,t].            (5)

The checker replays the universal algebraic identities underlying (4)
and the saturation identity, alongside the actual unit certificates.
The known relation sum C_i*A_i=0 is retained; no independence or
dimension of the six compatibility equations is asserted.

The exact degree bounds are

    deg_q Zstar<=18,
    deg_q(D_i*Zstar-E_i)<=22,
    deg_q(M(s)*Zstar)<=22.

They use the checked multiplier degree3 and the predecessor's exact
E degree bound15, with deg_q D_i and deg_q M(s) at most4. They are
upper bounds, not assertions of optimality or solvability. The compact
