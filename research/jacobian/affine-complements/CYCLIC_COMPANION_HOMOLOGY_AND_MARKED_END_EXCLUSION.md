# Cyclic companion homology and a new marked-end exclusion

15 November 2026. PF2RULED65 excludes a whole unbounded part of the
all-odd primitive-binomial frontier of PF63. If 3 divides the first
pair p*q, the cyclic-order3 companion alternative cannot be the end
of an actual quartic Keller normalization. Its branched end has
noncyclic homology and zero ramification-knot class, whereas that
marked class forces the actual rank2 source end to have cyclic
homology. This uses the full marked source, not just knot determinant,
unmarked group order, or the orbit graph.

Two general mechanisms underpin the exclusion: at every finite degree,
cyclic companion pieces contribute explicit direct summands to the
end homology; a primitive canonical marking bounds the number of
generators after quotienting by that marking. No polynomial coefficient
search or upper bound on winding, source poles or target degree is used.

## 1. A marked generator bound in every smooth source degree

Retain PF61's smooth affine N containing U=A2, with b omitted disjoint
affine lines. Its entire integral lattice Q is negative definite of
rank b, and its end M has

    H1(M;Z)=Z^b / Q Z^b.

The canonical boundary class is the image kappa of the vector
k=(e_i-1) in this quotient. Suppose k is primitive, equivalently
gcd_i(e_i-1)=1. An integral change of generators puts k first in a
basis of Z^b. It follows immediately that

    H1(M)/<kappa> is generated by at most b-1 elements.       (1)

For each prime ell this also bounds its mod-ell dimension by b-1.
If kappa=0, (1) applies to H1(M) itself. This is an all-field-degree
statement under the retained smoothness, boundary and primitive-vector
hypotheses; those hypotheses are not automatic for all higher-degree
normalizations. If the vector is not primitive, the mod-ell bound
still holds whenever ell does not divide all of its entries.

For the surviving quartic b=2 and k=(1,0). Its kappa is the homology
class of the ramification knot K_R. In particular

    [K_R]=0  ==>  H1(M) is CYCLIC.                          (2)

Indeed e_R=0 leaves only e_D to generate the quotient. A finite group
with two generators is not enough once this marking is zero.

## 2. Cyclic companion replacement, at arbitrary cover degree

Let E(J) be the exterior of any knot, with preferred meridian m and
longitude l. In a connected cyclic a-sheeted exterior cover E_a(J),
there is one boundary torus, generated by the closed lifts m^a,l.
There is a splitting with explicit peripheral maps

    H1(E_a(J))=Z*[m^a] direct-sum H1(Sigma_a(J)),
    [l]=0.                                                  (3)

Here Sigma_a(J) is the cyclic branched cover; its H1 is not assumed
finite. To prove the peripheral assertion, lift a Seifert surface:
its loops have linking number zero, so the whole surface lifts and
bounds l. In the infinite cyclic cover, the chain exact sequence for
multiplication by t^a-1 gives

    0 -> H1(E_infinity)/(t^a-1) -> H1(E_a) -> Z -> 0.

The closed meridian m^a maps to a generator of the last Z. Filling
along it kills this chosen summand and produces Sigma_a(J), proving
(3), including its integral splitting. Hillman--Sakuma (1997), complete
PDF6/printed314, independently records the integral exterior/branched
splitting for knots; the argument here also keeps the peripheral maps.

Now cut a finite branched knot cover along a companion torus. Suppose
the image of the companion knot group is cyclic on each of its sheet
orbits, of lengths a_1,...,a_j. Each lifted companion piece is E_a_i(J).
On its only boundary, both attaching generators map into the displayed
free summand of (3), with no branched-cover summand. Mayer--Vietoris
therefore gives

    H1(M)=H1(M_unknotted) direct-sum
                         direct-sum_i H1(Sigma_a_i(J)).      (4)

M_unknotted replaces these companion pieces by cyclic covers of the
unknot exterior, with the same peripheral identifications. This is
an integral homology replacement, not a homeomorphism assertion.
One can see (4) by successively gluing the single-boundary companion
pieces: the H0 map is injective at each attachment, and each extra
summand in (3) is untouched by all gluing relations. Any marked knot
lying in the retained pattern piece has its old homology class in
the first summand and zero components in the added summands.

This applies in all finite degrees, with any number of cyclic companion
orbits, and can be used successively in iterated decompositions. The
whole covering need not be regular or cyclic. It does require actual
cyclic restrictions on the companion pieces; a general transitive
noncyclic companion cannot be replaced this way. Together (1) and (4)
give a general marked generator obstruction beyond the free-cycle
obstruction of PF62.

## 3. Torus companions: full groups, not just determinants

For coprime p,q>=2, the Alexander module of T(p,q) is

    Z[t,t^-1]/(Delta),
    Delta=(t^(pq)-1)*(t-1)/((t^p-1)*(t^q-1)).                (5)

Here is a direct module calculation. For <a,b|a^p=b^q>, send a to
t^q and b to t^p. The first boundary is (t^q-1,t^p-1). Its kernel
over the Laurent polynomial UFD is generated by

    ((t^p-1)/(t-1), -(t^q-1)/(t-1)),

since the two numerator quotients are coprime. Lifting the relator
gives (sum_(i=0)^(p-1) t^(qi), -sum_(i=0)^(q-1) t^(pi)), which is
Delta times that kernel generator. This proves (5) as a module, not
only an Alexander determinant. The chain sequence in Section2 then
gives H1(Sigma_a)=Z[t,t^-1]/(Delta,t^a-1).

For odd coprime p,q, evaluation at the four roots of t^4-1 gives

    Delta=1 mod (t^4-1), if p=1 or q=1 mod4;
    Delta=t^2 mod (t^4-1), if p=q=3 mod4.

Both residues are units, so

    H1(Sigma_4(T(p,q)))=0.                                  (6)

For the triple cover use Delta(1)=1: t-1 is a unit modulo Delta,
so t^3-1 can be replaced by Phi3=t^2+t+1. If neither p nor q is
divisible by3, Delta modulo Phi3 is 1 or t^2, again a unit. If 3|p,
the quotient sum_(i=0)^(q-1) t^(pi) becomes q, whereas
(t^q-1)/(t-1) is 1 or 1+t, a unit modulo Phi3. Thus Delta is q
times a unit there. Interchange p,q for the other case. Consequently

    H1(Sigma_3(T(p,q))) =
        0,                         if 3 does not divide pq;
        (Z/q)^2,                   if 3 divides p;
        (Z/p)^2,                   if 3 divides q.           (7)

Coprimality prevents both p,q being divisible by3. The direct-square
groups in (7) cannot be inferred from their orders alone. The primary
Hillman--Sakuma page explicitly warns that identical Alexander
polynomials can give different homology groups for general knots;
(5) supplies the extra module information used here.

## 4. The unknotted four-sheeted pattern, uniformly in the winding

Use PF63's actual primitive-binomial parameters

    c=pq-p-q,  k=rc+1,
    z^r=h=M^k L^r,  beta=M^-c L^-1,
    mu=z*beta,  lambda=h*mu^(-rk).                            (8)

For odd p,q>=3 the companion image is cyclic, and the complete
peripheral S4 classification has only two possibilities:

* C4: M is a 4-cycle, z is a 3-cycle, tau=mu is an adjacent
  transposition; r=9 mod36 after the determinant-square gate.
* C3: M is a 3-cycle with one fixed sheet, z is a 4-cycle, tau joins
  its three-letter orbit to the fixed sheet; 4|r and 9|k.

PF63 proves these are the exhaustive possibilities, not selected
representations. In either case a=M^q,b=M^p and beta=M^-c determine
the companion image; conjugating M and the allowed tau gives one
simultaneous-conjugacy class in each nonempty alternative. Indeed the
centralizer of a 4-cycle permutes its adjacent transpositions, and
the centralizer of a 3-cycle permutes its three possible joins.
The alternatives have odd and even r, respectively, and do not overlap.

In (4), replace the cyclic companion pieces by unknot pieces. Then L
is killed, and the pattern is the simple four-sheeted branched cover
of the torus knot T(r,k), with presentation

    <z,M | z^r=M^k>,  mu=z*M^-c.                             (9)

We compute its integral homology by lifted edges and all meridian
fillings, not by a Seifert-manifold identification. In both cases rk
is even. The ramification longitude has the same filled homology
class as h, since the lift mu^2 of the ramified meridian is filled.

### C4 pattern

Put r=3s and k=4t; c=4C+3. With M=(0123), tau=(01) and
z=tau*M^-1, a spanning-tree calculation leaves variables A,B,x,y,w.
Here A is the fixed-sheet z loop, B its length3 loop and X=x+y+w
is the length4 M loop. The lifted relators and the three meridian
fillings reduce integrally to

    s*B=t*X,   3s*A=t*X,   A+B=c*X.

For explicit filling checks the other relations are

    A=(2C+2)*X-y,   w=(C+1)*X,   B=(C+1)*X-x.

Together with x+y+w=X they give A+B=cX by eliminating variables
with coefficient1. Now 3 times the first relator plus the second
after substituting A=cX-B gives

    (3s*c-4t)*X=-X=0,

using k-rc=1. The remaining group is cyclic generated by B with
sB=0, and the ramification class h=sB is zero. Thus

    H1(M_pattern)=Z/(r/3),   [K_R]=0.                        (10)

### C3 pattern

Put r=4a and k=3b, and d=a*c, so 3b-4d=1. Choose
M=(012), tau=(03), z=tau*M^c. For c=3C+1 or 3C+2, the same
lifted-edge calculation leaves the length4 z loop A, the length3
M loop X=x+y+w and the fixed M loop v. Its filling relations imply

    A=c*(X+v).

Explicitly, for c=3C+1 they give y=w=-C*X, x=(2C+1)*X and
A=C*X+x+c*v. For c=3C+2 they give x=w=(C+1)*X,
y=-(2C+1)*X and A=(C+1)*X-y+c*v. All eliminations have unit
coefficients. The remaining two relators have matrix, on (X,v),

    [ d-b    d   ]
    [  d    d-3b ].                                        (11)

Its determinant is b*(3b-4d)=b. Its entries generate the unit ideal:
they generate d,b, and 3b-4d=1. Its Smith form is therefore (1,b).
The ramification class h=aA=bX vanishes in this group of order b.
We obtain

    H1(M_pattern)=Z/(k/3),   [K_R]=0.                        (12)

These computations use unrestricted s,t,c or a,b,d subject to the
displayed exact Bezout identity. Finite parameter checks below are
controls, not an interpolation argument.

## 5. Full marked-end formulas and the new exclusion

Combining (4), (6), (7), (10) and (12) gives the following complete
homology formulas for every all-odd PF63 representation:

| Companion image | H1 of the four-sheeted branched end | Ramification class |
|---|---|---|
| C4 | Z/(r/3) | 0 |
| C3, 3 does not divide pq | Z/(k/3) | 0 |
| C3, 3 divides p | Z/(k/3) direct-sum (Z/q)^2 | 0 |
| C3, 3 divides q | Z/(k/3) direct-sum (Z/p)^2 | 0 |

The ramification knot lies in the cable pattern. Replacement preserves
its class and contributes no extra coordinates to it, so its zero
class in the table is valid even when the companion summands are
nonzero. Since p,q are odd and >=3, the last two groups are always
noncyclic: they contain two nontrivial cyclic summands of the same
order. Equation (2) excludes both last rows as actual quartic ends.

The new necessary parameter gate for the ENTIRE all-odd primitive
actual-binomial class is therefore

    r=9 mod36; OR
    (4|r AND 9|k AND 3 does not divide pq).                   (13)

This is stricter than PF63's coarse gate. It is not sufficient for
an actual source lattice, linking form, affine-cover extension or
Keller map. For instance p=3,q=5 has c=7. Its former C3 alternative
was every r=32 mod36, and ALL those windings are now excluded. At
r=32, k=225 and the end group is Z/75 direct-sum (Z/5)^2, with
zero ramification class. PF63's Zannier theorem supplies polynomial
eta0 immersions at these parameters, with finite multiple values
unclassified. No assumption of their nodality is needed for the
necessary infinity exclusion; it asserts that none can be the branch
of the surviving marked quartic packet.

Every remaining row has cyclic end of order

    D=r/3 in C4,   D=k/3 in the remaining C3 case.             (14)

Both are odd and divisible by3. Using the actual source matrix
Q=[[-n_R,c_src],[c_src,-n_D]], PF61 then requires

    D|n_D, D|c_src,
    n_R*(n_D/D)-D*(c_src/D)^2=1,
    gcd(n_R,D)=1.                                          (15)

Also c_src is odd because detQ is odd and n_D even. In particular
3 cannot divide n_R. For ALL such all-odd target first pairs and
windings, this excludes the complementary source order1, every odd
complementary order, and every complementary order2 mod3, uniformly
in the unramified pole order. The source order2/q1 row is closed
for this entire target class. Remaining complementary orders are
0 or4 mod6. Common-pencil sectors with 3|n_R are excluded too.
These extend PF61's fixed-knot source restrictions to unbounded
first pairs and windings, without changing that historical receipt.

higher-degree Keller exclusion, or finite Keller construction.

noncyclic companion restrictions, and the passing rows of (13)
remain subject to the genuine source, linking and affine algebra
obligations. The distinction between an arbitrary cubic primitive
index and the shared index attached to an actual quartic binary
coordinate remains essential: PF64's arbitrary branch-avoiding
generator is not automatically a source-coordinate resolvent.
