# Canonical quadrics and the actual source residual cover

Claim: PF2RULED58. Written unbounded proofs with exact algebra controls.

## 1. Scope and result

Put A=C[u,v]. Let B be a finite locally free A-algebra of rank d>=3,
and assume W=Hom_A(B,A) is invertible as a B-module. This is the
Gorenstein-cover hypothesis. It holds for the entire surviving quartic
Keller normalization N by PF2RULED46: N is smooth and its fibres consist
of simple points and ordinary quadratic folds. It is NOT imposed silently
on every normal higher-degree Keller normalization.

Let E be the kernel of evaluation at1 on W. It is free of rank d-1 over
A: evaluation splits by Tr/d, and Quillen--Suslin applies. Evaluation
of sections gives a closed embedding

    j: Spec B -> P_A^(d-2),       j*O(1)=W.                 (1)

Every fibre in THIS embedding is arithmetically Gorenstein, with Hilbert
function 1,d-1,d,d,... and Artinian h-vector (1,d-2,1). If d>=4 its entire
homogeneous ideal is generated by a free module of quadrics of rank

    rank F = d(d-3)/2.                                      (2)

These are all-degree statements under the explicit Gorenstein hypothesis,
proved below without importing a structure theorem for an arbitrary
projective embedding. In degree4 there are exactly two global quadrics:

    N = V(Q1,Q2) in P_A^2,    O_N(1)=omega_N=O_N(R).        (3)

Their intersection is the WHOLE finite normalization, including all
omitted primes. Thus the conditional full-pencil gate of PF2RULED57 now
applies unconditionally to every actual surviving quartic, with no source
pole-order or ruling restriction. This does not construct a Keller pair.

After pulling (3) back by the actual source F:U=A2->Spec A, remove the
diagonal section U from N x_A U. The remainder V is a smooth finite flat
degree3 cover of U, isomorphic to the pullback of the NORMAL cubic
resolvent C3. It maps etale and quasi-finitely to N. The off-diagonal
U x_A U alone is generally only an open subset of V; finiteness belongs
to this FULL residual cover.

## 2. Why the canonical linear system works in every degree

Work first over an algebraically closed residue field k=C. Write C for
a fibre algebra of length d. Choose a Frobenius functional lambda on C,
so lambda(ab) is a nondegenerate pairing. Identify C^vee with C via
lambda. Under this identification E is the hyperplane

    H=ker(lambda),              H^perp=k*1.                 (4)

It generates the unit ideal of C. Otherwise H would be contained in a
maximal ideal. Both have codimension1, so lambda would be proportional
to the corresponding residue evaluation. That functional has a
nonzero pairing radical when d>=2, a contradiction. Since k is infinite
there is q in H outside all finitely many maximal ideals, hence q is a
unit of C.

The ratios H/q contain1 and generate C as an algebra when d>=3. Indeed,
if the generated algebra D were proper, it would have dimension d-1 and
equal H/q. The nondegenerate Frobenius pairing lambda(qab) would vanish
on D x D, since D is an algebra and lambda(qD)=0. But its orthogonal
complement has dimension1 whereas dim D=d-1>1. This is impossible.
The canonical map is therefore a closed immersion in the affine chart
defined by q on this fibre.

Also the products H*H span C. If a is orthogonal to their span, then
aH is contained in H^perp=k*1. If aH is nonzero, a times some element
of H is a nonzero scalar, making a a unit; then dim(aH)=d-1>1, a
contradiction. If aH=0, the unit-ideal property of H makes a=0. Thus
the orthogonal complement is zero. Higher products span C as well,
since CH=C. Consequently the homogeneous coordinate ring has the
Hilbert function stated in Section1.

The linear form q is a nonzerodivisor of that coordinate ring, because
q is a unit in C. Its Artinian quotient has dimensions (1,d-2,1).
The degree1 multiplication pairing takes values in C/qH; identify this
one-dimensional quotient by c -> lambda(c/q). Its radical on H is

    lambda((h/q)*H)=0  <=>  h/q in k*1  <=>  h in k*q.      (5)

Thus the induced degree1 pairing on H/kq is nondegenerate. There is
no degree1 socle, the degree2 socle has dimension1, and the quotient
is Gorenstein. This proves arithmetic Gorensteinness of the canonical
embedding rather than inferring it from mere nondegeneracy.

If n=d-2>=2, diagonalize this nondegenerate degree1 pairing. The
Artinian ideal is generated by

    z_i*z_j (i!=j),         z_i^2-z_1^2 (i>=2).            (6)

All degree3 products vanish already modulo these quadrics: for each i
choose j!=i and write z_i^3=z_i*z_j^2=0. This argument works for EVERY
n>=2. It proves quadratic generation, not just a dimension count. For
n=1 (d=3), a cubic generator is necessary; that case is not claimed to
have a quadratic ideal.

Globally, W and its tensor powers are finite projective A-modules:
an invertible B-module is a direct summand of a finite free B-module,
and B is finite locally free over A. Evaluation E -> W generates W
by the fibre argument and Nakayama. It defines a morphism j. Around
each base point, lift a fibre q as above and shrink the base so q
trivializes W on the whole finite inverse image. The ratios of E/q
generate B by Nakayama (the generated A-subalgebra is an A-submodule
of the finite module B). This proves the closed immersion locally
on the base, and hence globally. No single global q is asserted.

The multiplication map Sym^2 E -> pi_*(W^2) is surjective by the
product argument and base change. Its kernel F is projective, hence
free over A, and has rank (d-1)d/2-d=d(d-3)/2. Fibrewise the entire
ideal is generated by F: lift (6) through the nonzerodivisor q and
use graded Nakayama. Coherent Nakayama on P_A^(d-2) then proves
global generation of the ideal sheaf by these quadrics. The d=4
fibre ideals are complete intersections of two quadrics; their global
generators form the corresponding relative regular sequence. This
establishes (1)-(3).

For a Gorenstein Keller normalization, the relative dualizing sheaf W
is omega_N because omega_A is trivial. Thus its canonical class is
the nonzero boundary class of PF2RULED57. Formula (1) is compatible
with that obstruction: local affine charts do not supply a global
affine complete-intersection presentation. For d>=5 the number in (2)
exceeds the codimension d-2, so the canonical ideal is not a complete
intersection. This does not bound the field degree or exclude those
quadratic presentations. Non-Gorenstein higher-degree normalizations
remain outside this construction.

The associated conic-pencil determinant is a nonzero binary cubic on
EVERY target fibre. In the first row it has three distinct roots; in
the other two it has one double and one simple root. A projective
pencil member of rank1 occurs exactly in the two-fold fibre (the last
row), at its unique double root. These are precisely the double values
of the immersive branch normalization Ctilde=A1->C. This is a statement
on the projective pencil, where [u:v] is never (0,0); polynomial
coefficient pairs with a common zero are not pencil sections there.

The 2026 Landesman--Vakil--Wood corrigendum explicitly warns that a
nondegenerate embedding of a Gorenstein finite scheme need not be
arithmetically Gorenstein: four reduced points, three on one line,
are a counterexample. Their every quadric contains that line. The
canonical Frobenius argument above and the actual fibre table supply
the missing property here. We use neither that incorrect unrestricted
uniqueness statement nor an arbitrary four-point embedding.

## 4. The normal cubic and unavoidable global singular members

Let Q1,Q2 be symmetric 3-by-3 matrices over A representing the two
global quadrics (halves of mixed coefficients are allowed over C).
The binary cubic det(u Q1+v Q2) is fibrewise nonzero by Section3,
so its projective zero scheme is a finite flat cubic cover. Its
generic roots are the three partitions of the four points into two
pairs. The full quartic has S4 monodromy, so this cubic is connected.

Use the oriented quartic/cubic discriminant identity retained in
PF2RULED57 from Wood (2011), Section3. The quartic is the FULL normal
B, whose discriminant is unit*G_R. The cubic determinant algebra has
the same discriminant. It is normal: a nonzero normalization index
at a height-one DVR adds an even positive discriminant exponent,
impossible for this reduced discriminant, and finite flatness gives
S2. Thus this is the actual C3, including above every affine double
branch value. Constant convention factors do not change the argument.

PF2RULED56 excludes a globally monogenic normal C3. Accordingly
PF2RULED57 now applies to the globally existing pencil: for EVERY
nonzero polynomial coefficient pair (lambda,mu),

    det(lambda Q1+mu Q2) is nonzero and nonconstant.          (7)

Identical vanishing would give a rational root of the irreducible
cubic. A unit would give a pencil section avoiding C3; its affine-line
complement over A2 is trivial, making C3 monogenic. Local smooth
members exist. A globally everywhere-smooth member is impossible.
Changing either free module basis does not remove this constraint.

## 5. Pullback to the source and exact residual normal form

Base change (3) by the actual etale source F:U=A2->Spec A. The map
N x_A U -> U is finite flat of degree4. Its diagonal section lies
in its etale locus, so its image is both open and closed. Consequently

    N x_A U = U disjoint_union V,       V -> U finite flat3. (8)

Both N x_A U and V are smooth: projection to the smooth N is a
base change of the etale F. Projection V->N is etale and quasi-finite.
The scheme V contains every remaining point of N over a source image,
including points on R or D; removing them gives only the ordinary
off-diagonal U x_A U.

The canonical line bundle is trivial on the diagonal U. Its three
coordinate sections give a unimodular row in C[x,y]^3. Complete that
row to a matrix by Quillen--Suslin. After this source-side projective
change, the diagonal section is [1:0:0]. Write the linear terms at
that point of the two conics as a_i Y+b_i Z. Their determinant is
nowhere zero, since (8) is etale along the diagonal. It is therefore
a nonzero constant in C[x,y]. A source-side change of pencil basis
puts the two equations in the exact form

    Q1=XY+aY^2+bYZ+cZ^2,
    Q2=XZ+dY^2+eYZ+fZ^2,       a,...,f in C[x,y].           (9)

These changes use source polynomials. They are NOT asserted to descend
to the target field or target polynomial ring; PF2RULED52 remains intact.

Projection away from the diagonal point maps V isomorphically to the
binary cubic in P^1_U with equation

    D(Y,Z)=dY^3+(e-a)Y^2Z+(f-b)YZ^2-cZ^3=0.                (10)

Indeed Y Q2-Z Q1=D, and on either Y!=0 or Z!=0 exactly one equation
solves for X. The diagonal is already an open-and-closed component,
so no infinitesimal residual component is lost at [1:0:0]. Directly,

    -4 det(u Q1+v Q2)=D(v,-u).                            (11)

Thus V is the pullback of the full normal C3, after a source-side
projective change of pencil parameters. Alternatively both are normal
finite U-covers with the same generic S3-set: the stabilizer of one
of four letters acts on its other three letters exactly as on the
three two-pair partitions. This proves the identification in (8)
without confusing an open off-diagonal fibre product with its finite
completion. Equation (11) is an identity for arbitrary six source
polynomials, not a bounded ansatz.

All double values of C miss F(U) by PF2RULED46. Hence F^(-1)(C) is
a smooth, possibly disconnected reduced affine curve, and the cubic
V->U is simply branched along it. Its components need not have
normalization A1. PF2RULED56's power-difference theorem therefore
cannot be applied to this pulled-back branch curve without proving
its hypotheses. Neither V nor its off-diagonal source open has been
shown to be a plane, rational, monogenic or finite etale over N.
