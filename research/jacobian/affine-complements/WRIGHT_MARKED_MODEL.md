# The surviving pair is Wright's index-three ring with its source open

14 November 2026. PF2WRIGHT1 identifies the remaining zero-period model
as an affine surface together with its actual polynomial source open
and its omitted prime. This is stronger than identifying an abstract
index-three affine-line torsor. The comparison is conditional on an
actual compatible completion, whose existence is still unknown.

## Normalize the only surviving boundary pair

The [period obstruction](VOLUME_PERIOD_OBSTRUCTION.md) leaves
B=(t,s^2), E=(t^2-s^2,s^2t), up to an automorphism of S preserving the
whole pair and opens. In the [global source coordinates](SOURCE_COORDINATES.md)
X,Y on U, its Cox slice is

s=X, alpha=1+X^2Y, t=1+X^2*alpha, beta=-alpha^2-Y.

Let f0=beta*t^2, f1=beta*s*t, f2=alpha*t, f3=alpha*s.
These generate the entire affine ring A=Gamma(N,O_N), since the
all-height section-generation proof makes V1 generate the functions
on S\B. Put

\[
 A1=XY,\qquad B1=X^2Y,\qquad D=X^3Y+X,\qquad C=1+B1+D^2.
\]

Direct identities in the actual ring C[X,Y] give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 f3&=D,\\
 f2&=C,\\
 f1&=-A1-D-2B1D-D^3,\\
 f0&=-Y-C^2-2A1D-B1D^2.
 \end{aligned}\tag{1}
\]

Conversely, in A put

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D&=f3,\\
 B1&=f2-f3^2-1,\\
 A1&=-f1-D-2B1D-D^3,\\
 Y&=-f0-f2^2-2A1D-B1D^2.
 \end{aligned}\tag{2}
\]

Substitution recovers the displayed source monomials exactly. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\Gamma(N,O_N)=\mathbb C[Y,XY,X^2Y,X^3Y+X]
          \ \subset\mathbb C[X,Y].}\tag{3}
\]

The equality takes place inside the specified polynomial source ring.
It preserves the open embedding, not just an abstract surface class.
No inverse rational coordinate with an untracked pole was substituted.

## The equations and the omitted prime

In coordinates Y,A1,B1,D the entire defining ideal is generated by

\[
 A1^2-YB1,\qquad A1(B1+1)-YD,\qquad B1(B1+1)-A1D.
 \tag{4}
\]

The small exact elimination certificate checks this full kernel, not
just equations satisfied on a parameterized subset.
The omitted curve is the reduced prime

\[
 R=V(Y,A1,B1+1)\simeq\mathbb A^1_D.\tag{5}
\]

Indeed on Y!=0 the source coordinate is X=A1/Y; on B1+1!=0 it is
X=D/(B1+1). Equations(4) recover the other generators and show these
charts are exactly the images of the corresponding source charts.
They cover all of C[X,Y], since Y=0 makes B1+1=1 there. Their complement
in Spec(A) is precisely(5). Thus the actual open is N\R, with no
additional deleted points or divisors.

Near R, B1 is invertible. Put u=A1/B1, v=D. Equations(4) give

\[
 B1=uv-1,\qquad A1=u^2v-u,\qquad Y=u^3v-u^2,
 \qquad X=u^{-1}.\tag{6}
\]

This identifies the neighborhood D(B1) with
Spec C[u,v,(uv-1)^-1]. The whole R lies here and has equation u=0.
Consequently its normalized divisorial values on the source coordinates
are

ord_R(X)=-1, ord_R(Y)=2, ord_R(A1)=1,
ord_R(B1)=ord_R(D)=0.

In particular the infinity fibre of this specified projection X has
multiplicity one. That fact follows from the explicit uniformizer u;
it is not inferred from the reduced support or from an abstract bundle.
The [earlier multiple-fibre control](WRIGHT_CONNECTION.md) remains valid
for other polynomial source coordinates.

## The volume and finite-map dictionary

On the overlap dX wedge dY=-u du wedge dv. It extends across R with
a simple zero there. The normalized period-zero form of PF2PERIOD1
pulls back to -dX wedge dY in these source coordinates. Thus the
comparison also preserves the canonical ramification form, up to a
nonzero constant, and the actual omitted valuation.

The remaining compatible finite-map problem is exactly to find P,Q
in the subring(3) such that:

- their source Jacobian is a nonzero constant;
- A is finite over C[P,Q];
- [C(X,Y):C(P,Q)]=4.

These conditions imply the required volume divisor on the normal affine
surface. Conversely every compatible finite quartic realization supplies
such a pair after the proved normalizations. Finiteness is a separate
condition; a Jacobian identity or an equality of fraction fields does
not establish it. No such pair is constructed here.

## Historical identification and verification boundary

The subring(3) is the index-three case singled out by David Wright in
*Affine surfaces fibered by affine lines over the projective line*,
Illinois J. Math.41(1997),589-605,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1215/ijm/1256068982).
The [author-uploaded primary copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258233253_Affine_surfaces_fibered_by_affine_lines_over_the_projective_line)
was readable in this pass; its discussion following Theorem3.3 names this
same remaining ring. The publisher PDF still presented a service check.
No theorem from that paper is needed for(1)-(6), and no novelty priority
is claimed for the residue method or this known ring.

[marked-ring certificates](wright_marking_certificates.json) and
[checker](verify_volume_period.py) verify both ring inclusions, the full
kernel, both source-open charts, the reduced omitted prime and the

proofs, without formal verification, independent whole-proof
implementation or external review.
