# Peripheral cone torsion and the marked generator budget

15 November 2026. PF2RULED70 adds an integral torsion obstruction to
PF69's all-degree base-genus criterion. Boundary-torus gluings cannot
remove a quotient carried by repeated exceptional-fibre orders. The
ramified boundary-knot marks map to zero in this quotient. Its number
of generators must therefore fit the smaller marked source budget.
Keeping the individual knots gives the all-degree improvement even
when the canonical vector is not primitive.

In the remaining planar S4/S4 quartic sector this forces the even
first exponent to be EXACTLY2, at unrestricted winding and source
orders/poles. Three other noncyclic companion sectors acquire explicit
coprimality gates below. The result does not close their passing rows,
the entire even-first-pair class or the full research goal.

## 1. A quotient that kills every filling-core mark

Use PF69's connected graph G of pieces P_v before filling their
external boundary tori. Select orientably Seifert-fibred pieces,
with oriented regular fibre h_v. Define

    Q_v=H1(P_v;Z)/<im H1(boundary P_v), [h_v]>.

If P_v has boundary, [h_v] is already in its boundary image. The
extra displayed generator permits the same definition for a closed
piece. Map all unselected piece homology to zero. Each gluing map
comes from a boundary torus, so Mayer--Vietoris gives, integrally,

    H1(M;Z) ->> H1(G;Z) direct-sum direct-sum_v Q_v.           (1)

As in PF69 the splitting of the free graph quotient is not canonical.
The direct sum of Q_v is a QUOTIENT, not an asserted subgroup or
direct summand of the end homology.

Every core of an attached filling solid torus maps to zero in (1).
Indeed a complementary primitive slope on its boundary maps to its
core generator. The same class is a boundary-torus class in P_v,
which is zero in Q_v; and any loop contained within a piece maps
to zero in H1(G). This proof works for ALL filling slopes and every
meridian cycle, including fixed sheets. Thus if kappa is any integral
combination of filling-core classes, (1) factors through

    H1(M)/<kappa> ->> H1(G) direct-sum direct-sum_v Q_v.       (2)

For the actual smooth source of PF61, the canonical boundary mark
is kappa=sum(e_i-1)[K_i], and the ramified K_i are precisely filling
cores over the branch end. Hence (2) applies. This does not assert
that kappa is zero in H1(M), or that any other interior knot mark
is killed. A nonzero ramification-mark control is retained below.

No cyclic companion hypothesis, minimal JSJ decomposition,
incompressible cutting tori, or Seifert-fibration-preserving filling
is assumed in this argument.

## 2. Exact Seifert quotient and its prime dimensions

Let a selected piece have orientable base genus g and cone orders
alpha_1,...,alpha_j. Orders1 may be included without effect. The
standard Seifert presentation, after abelianizing and killing the
regular fibre and all boundary generators, gives

    Q_v=Z^(2g) direct-sum T(alpha_1,...,alpha_j),
    T(alpha)=(direct-sum_i Z/alpha_i)/<(1,...,1)>.             (3)

To derive it, take the circle bundle over the base punctured at
the cone values. With cone loops x_i, boundary loops d_l and regular
fibre h, the abelian relations are

    alpha_i*x_i+beta_i*h=0,
    sum_i x_i+sum_l d_l+b*h=0.

Handle generators have no abelian relations. Killing h,d_l yields
exactly (3). The possible section integer b and every beta_i disappear;
no oriented linking form is being computed or discarded as an
equivalence. Cochran--Tanner, arXiv1207.5042v1, complete PDF3-4,
Section3's model and explicit orientable presentation independently
check these relations. We import no homology-cobordism theorem from
that paper. The boundary version follows directly by leaving its
unfilled base boundary loops in the same model.

For a prime ell let N_v(ell)=#{i:ell divides alpha_i}. Tensoring
(3) with F_ell leaves N_v generators and one nonzero diagonal
relation if N_v>0. Therefore

    dim_Fell Q_v/ell Q_v = 2g+max(N_v(ell)-1,0).              (4)

This uses the FULL group, not only its order. For example, four
cones of order o give T=(Z/o)^3. Two cones a,b give T=Z/gcd(a,b).
For disjoint lists of orders with mutually coprime least common
multiples, T of the combined list splits as the direct sum of their
T groups: Chinese remaindering separates the diagonal relation.
This is useful when the two torus exponents are coprime.

## 3. The all-degree marked budget

In PF61's SMOOTH affine extension N containing A2 with b omitted
ENTIRE DISJOINT A1s, end homology is coker of a rank-b integral
lattice. The individual end knot K_i is the image of the i-th
standard covector: its relative Thom class pairs with the dual
homology generators by Kronecker delta. This is the same Thom-class
argument used for the canonical sum in PF61, applied to each divisor.

Let I be ANY subset of omitted primes whose end knots are known to
be filling cores, and set s=|I|. The quotient H1(M)/<[K_i]:i in I>
is generated by the other b-s standard covectors. Since every one
of the killed marks maps to zero in (1), at EVERY prime ell,

    b1(G)+sum_v[2g_v+max(N_v(ell)-1,0)] <= b-s.              (5)

In an actual smooth finite normalization, I includes all ramified
omitted primes. No primitivity of the canonical vector and no
restriction on their ramification indices is needed for (5).
Some unramified omitted primes may also be included if their end
knots are actually identified with fixed-sheet filling cores; this
identification must be proved, not assumed from being unramified.

There is also a useful version when only the canonical combination
is tracked. Its vector has entries k_i=e_i-1. If that integer
vector is primitive, PF65 proves

    H1(M)/<kappa> can be generated by at most b-1 elements.

Combining this fact with (2)-(4) gives the alternative bound

    b1(G)+sum_v[2g_v+max(N_v(ell)-1,0)] <= b-1.              (5a)

For a nonprimitive vector, (5a) still holds at ell whenever some k_i
is nonzero mod ell; from that combination alone only the rank-b
bound follows at its bad primes. Tracking the individual knots in
(5) removes that limitation. These statements hold at ALL finite
field degrees and ALL cabling lengths under their explicit smooth,
whole-line, orientable-base and actual-mark hypotheses; these are
not automatic for arbitrary higher-degree normalizations. Finite
end homology separately forces the free part to vanish as in PF69.

For the quartic b=2,k=(1,0), kappa=[K_R]. Thus

    H1(M)/<[K_R]> is CYCLIC,
    so every quotient in (2) must be CYCLIC.                 (6)

Unlike the earlier implication "R mark0 forces cyclic H1", (6)
also applies to representations with a NONZERO R mark.

## 4. Cone lists in all remaining planar noncyclic quartic sectors

Use the ACTUAL primitive-binomial two-stage hypotheses of PF63:
coprime first exponents, c=pq-p-q, k=rc+1, actual preferred longitude
with identity monodromy. All odd first pairs are already closed by
PF67. For even first pairs write e for the even exponent and o for
the odd exponent; gcd(e,o)=1. The surviving noncyclic torus images
have generator orders2 or4 on the even side and3 on the odd side,
so 3|o. The S4/S4 order3,4 genus-one sector was closed by PF69.

Source cone orders are computed on the FIBRE-ORBIT base of PF69.
If a target cone has order alpha and its induced base permutation
has a cycle of length l, its lifted cone order is alpha/l. This is
the local unbranched orbifold-cover rule: the target alpha-rotation
has an index-l subgroup, leaving stabilizer order alpha/l. It is
valid with nontrivial fibre monodromy as well. The fibre degree is
not multiplied into this cone order.

The following are the ENTIRE direct sums of Q_v for the companion
and cable pieces in each planar noncyclic sector. Trivial cyclic
factors are omitted. These are quotients, not formulas for H1(M).

| Companion/cable images | Companion quotient | Cable quotient |
|---|---|---|
| S4/S4, planar | Z/(e/2) + Z/e + Z/(o/3) | Z/(r/3) |
| A4/S3 | Z/(e/2) + Z/(o/3) | Z/(r/2) |
| S3/S3 | Z/(e/2) | 0 |
| S4/C2 | Z/(o/3) | Z/r |

Here + denotes direct sum, e is a multiple of4 in S4/C2, r is an
odd multiple of3 in planar S4/S4, r is even in A4/S3, and r is
odd in S4/C2. These divisibilities follow from the actual generator
and root permutations. They are not inferred from abstract group
orders without their peripheral action.

For completeness, the lists giving the table are as follows.

* Planar S4/S4: the even generator is a transposition on four
  sheets, giving cone orders e/2,e,e; the odd generator is a 3-cycle
  with a fixed sheet, giving o/3,o. Their coprime prime supports
  split (3) into Z/(e/2)+Z/e and Z/(o/3). The cable root is a
  3-cycle with a fixed sheet, giving cones r/3,r and quotient Z/(r/3).
* A4/S3: the even generator is a double transposition, giving
  e/2,e/2; the odd generator gives o/3,o. In the cable's three-sheet
  orbit the root is a transposition, giving r/2,r. Its fixed-sheet
  piece has only one cone, so its quotient is zero.
* S3/S3: the companion's three-sheet orbit gives e/2,e,o/3;
  the fixed-sheet companion gives e,o and quotient0. The cable's
  three-sheet root is a 3-cycle, giving only one cone r/3; its
  fixed-sheet piece again has a single cone. Both cable quotients0.
* S4/C2: the companion generator cycles give e/4,o/3,o. In the
  two-sheet cable orbit the root is the identity, giving TWO cones
  r,r. Each fixed-sheet cable piece has only one cone and quotient0.

PF63's complete finite permutation classification covers all these
cycle types, including exchanging p and q. The cone orders themselves
are unrestricted actual integers. They are NOT periodic mod12.

## 5. New unbounded exclusions

In planar S4/S4, if e>2, any prime dividing e/2 appears in BOTH
Z/(e/2) and Z/e. The quotient in the table is noncyclic, contradicting
(6), for every source pencil/order/pole and every winding. Thus

    planar S4/S4 requires e=2,
    gcd(o/3,r/3)=1.                                         (7)

For e=2 its companion quotient is Z/2+Z/(o/3). Since r is odd,
the combined table entry is cyclic exactly under the second
condition in (7). Together with PF69 this excludes EVERY S4/S4
representation with even first exponent e>2, not only the residue
classes having 4-cycle generator image. Passing e=2 cases remain open.

The other noncyclic necessary gates from (6) are

    A4/S3: gcd(e*o/6,r/2)=1,
    S4/C2: gcd(o/3,r)=1.                                    (8)

In A4/S3 the two companion factors are coprime and combine to
Z/(eo/6), which gives (8). The S3/S3 quotient is already cyclic,
so this method alone gives no extra restriction in that sector.
Passing (7) or (8) asserts neither marked linking compatibility
nor existence of a finite affine cover.

The same mechanism recovers the older cyclic exclusions WITHOUT an
Alexander-module calculation. In C3 with 3|one first exponent, the
other exponent appears as THREE companion cones, giving (Z/other)^2
and violating (6). In C4 with 4|e, FOUR order-o cones give (Z/o)^3.
When e=2 mod4, the fibre degree is2 and the companion quotient is
Z/o; together with cable Z/(r/3), (6) recovers gcd(o,r/3)=1 from
PF68. The common-source and linking exclusions of PF66-68 remain
separate additional restrictions.
