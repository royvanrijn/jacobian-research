## 1. An integral Thom proof, with the whole plane kept visible

Write B=Y minus U, the union of the r+m boundary curves. Let Z be the
finite set of singular or intersection points of this curve divisor.
Cohomology supported on finitely many points in a smooth oriented real
four-manifold vanishes in degrees below four. The supports exact
sequence therefore identifies

    H^2_B(Y;Z) = H^2_(B minus Z)(Y minus Z;Z).

The right side is the direct sum of the degree-two Thom groups of the
disjoint smooth complex curves B_i minus Z. Each such curve is connected
after removing finitely many points, so it is Z for each component.
The generators are exactly the oriented divisor Thom classes. The
supports pair sequence, since H^1(U;Z)=H^2(U;Z)=0, now gives

    H^2_B(Y;Z) -> H^2(Y;Z) is an isomorphism.          (6)

Each divisor Thom class maps to c1(O(B_i)). Algebraic localization,
Pic(A2)=0 and O(A2)^*=C*, give the FREE divisor basis of Pic(Y), as
in PF109. The two bases coincide under c1, proving (1) integrally.
This is stronger than PF109's rational compactification injection;
the sealed predecessor proof remains valid and is not rewritten.

The whole-plane condition is essential twice: contractibility gives
the two cohomology vanishings in (6), and constant units remove
algebraic divisor relations. In the retained genuine finite A1-quotient
control with wrong open C* times C, H^1(U) is nonzero and the principal
relation 2D+E kills the canonical vector (8,4)=4(2,1). It is not an
exception to (1).

Every loop in Y can be moved off the real-codimension-two divisor B,
then contracted in U. Thus pi_1(Y)=0. Use the connected actual radial
end and a compact resolved core X from PF98/109. Poincare--Lefschetz
duality and the connected boundary, with H_1(X)=0, give H_3(X)=0 and
H^3(X)=0. The universal coefficient theorem then makes H_2(X) free;

## 3. A general link-complement lemma and reconstruction from F

Here is a topological lemma independent of algebraic realizability.
Let W be any oriented rational homology three-sphere, drill s knots
K_i, and let E be their exterior. Let S=<[K_i]> in H=H_1(W), and
lambda_W be the nonsingular torsion linking pairing. Then the inclusion
E->W induces an isomorphism

    Tor H_1(E) -> S^perp under lambda_W.               (8)

Indeed the pair sequence has exact segment

    0 -> Z^s*(meridians) -> H_1(E) -> H -> 0.          (9)

Here H_2(W)=0 and the normal disks generate H_2(W,E). In particular
torsion injects into H. A torsion loop in E bounds a multiple in E;
that bounding chain misses every K_i, so its image links all K_i by
zero. Conversely lift h in S^perp to x in H_1(E). Over Q the meridians
are a basis. Their rational coefficients for x, modulo integers, are
lambda_W(h,[K_i]), as seen by puncturing a bounding multiple in W.
All these coefficients are integers. Subtract that integral meridian
combination from x; its rational class is zero, giving a torsion lift
of h. This proves (8), including its injectivity assertion.

Consequently

    |Tor H_1(E)|=|H/S|,
    S=H iff H_1(E) is torsion-free.                   (10)

When S=H, identify H_1(E) injectively with Q^s using rational meridian
coordinates. Its meridians are the standard basis and its preferred
longitudes are the rows of F. By (9), the meridians and these longitudes
generate H_1(E) integrally. This proves the first two assertions in
(3). Alternatively a relation a among the core generators is zero in
H exactly when F*a is integral: one direction follows by pairing;
the converse follows from nonsingularity of lambda_W and generation
by the K_i. This gives the last presentation and the full pairing.

Any further integral filling b_i*m_i+c_i*L_i is now the quotient of
M_F by those explicit vectors, including cases with free homology.
There is no unresolved integral row defect in this regime. For an
arbitrary cover with S proper, (8) explains precisely the information
PF101's F can lose. Individual row defects can all be zero while the
unseen quotient H/S is still nonzero; generation must be checked.

For partial coverage, the actual omitted knots not already in the
drilled link must generate H/S by Section2. At least g are required.
Each costs at least one in the retained ALL-NORMAL finite-flat Euler
ledger PF2OMIT6,

    d-1=sum_D e_D*f_D + sum_(D,q) eta_q, eta_q>=0.

Subtract the costs of known omitted primes to obtain (4). Every

## 4. A larger fibre-transitive cable sector

PF104's exact cable transfer extends beyond its root z=mu*lambda.
Let the old cover have joint boundary lattices (e_i,0),(a_i,f_i),
and let M be a common multiple of both peripheral permutation orders.
Choose an integer b with

    gcd(e_i,f_i*b-a_i)=1 for EVERY boundary orbit.     (11)

For ANY coprime r,k>1 with r=1 modulo M and k=b modulo M, assign
rho(z)=rho(mu)^b*rho(lambda). The root relation
z^r=mu^k*lambda^r holds. If r*u-k*v=1, then the new meridian is
mu^u*lambda^v*z^(-v), whose action is mu^(u-b*v)=mu, since
u-b*v=1 modulo M. The new longitude action is again lambda. Thus
the full joint peripheral orbits and the original transitive image
are retained, not just the cycle lengths.

On a peripheral orbit the action mu^b*lambda has order
e_i*f_i/gcd(e_i,f_i*b-a_i), so (11) makes the covered cable-space
regular fibre transitive. The same integral two-port proof as PF104
applies: the component has torsion-free H_1=Z^2; pushforward is
injective with index e_i*f_i; the two columns m_new,L_old have that
determinant and hence form an integral basis. It follows that

    m_old=r*m_new,
    L_new=r*L_old + a_i*(1-r^2)/e_i * m_new.          (12)

The diagonal coefficient is integral because e_i divides M and
r=1 modulo M. These equations do not depend on the residue of k.
No new finite-pattern guess or deeper word expansion is involved.
Iteration, with R=product r_j, gives

    H_1(W_final)=H_1(W_initial),
    [K_i,final]=R*[K_i,initial],
    F_final=R^2*F_initial+diag((1-R^2)*a_i/e_i).       (13)

Therefore an initial proper subgroup S of H generated by all cores

persists even when some individual core orders or row defects drop.
