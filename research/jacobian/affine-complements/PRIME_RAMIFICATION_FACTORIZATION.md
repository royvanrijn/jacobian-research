# Prime ramification forbids a larger intermediate polynomial plane

14 November 2026. PF2FACTOR1 is a written consequence of normality,
canonical divisors, finiteness and finite etale triviality over complex affine
space. It applies to both quartic packets from QPK4 and QCL4, including
singular finite normalizations. It does not require a ruled completion.

## General factorization theorem

Let N be an integral normal affine complex surface with

\[
  [K_N]\ne0\text{ in }\operatorname{Cl}(N).
\]

Suppose pi:N -> A2 is finite and dominant and its ramification divisor
is a reduced irreducible prime R. If

\[
 \pi=H\circ G,\qquad G:N\longrightarrow\mathbb A^2,\quad
 H:\mathbb A^2\longrightarrow\mathbb A^2
\]

are dominant morphisms, then H is an automorphism.

Equivalently, if A=C[pi1,pi2] and B=Gamma(N,O_N), there is no strict
intermediate polynomial plane

\[
 A\ \subsetneq\ C[g_1,g_2]\ \subseteq\ B,
 \qquad g_1,g_2\text{ algebraically independent}.
\]

The condition that the intermediate generators lie in B is essential.
No assumption on the unit group is needed: finiteness supplies
surjectivity of the intermediate map.

## Proof

First write A=C[pi1,pi2], C=C[g1,g2] and B=Gamma(N,O_N).
Since B is finite over A, both intermediate morphisms are finite:
C=A[g1,g2] is generated by two integral elements, and any finite set
of A-module generators for B also generates B over C. In particular
G is surjective.

At every codimension-one point, N is regular. The nonzero rational
two-form

\[
 \omega_G=dg_1\wedge dg_2
\]

is regular there. Its canonical Weil divisor D_G is effective.
Since its class is [K_N] rather than zero, D_G is nonempty. This argument
uses the regular codimension-one locus and works even when N has isolated
singularities.

The chain rule gives

\[
 R=\operatorname{div}(d\pi_1\wedge d\pi_2)
   =D_G+\operatorname{div}\big(J_H(g_1,g_2)\big).
 \tag{1}
\]

Dominance in characteristic zero makes J_H nonzero. If J_H is
nonconstant, its pullback is nonconstant because G is dominant.
A nonconstant polynomial J_H has a zero on A2, and finite surjective G
has a point over that zero. Thus its pullback is a nonunit, with a
nonempty effective principal divisor on the normal affine surface.
The right side of (1) would be a sum of two nonempty effective divisors,
contradicting the reduced prime left side. Hence J_H is a nonzero constant.

The finiteness established above is essential; a constant Jacobian
alone would not suffice. It now makes H finite as well as etale.

A connected finite etale cover of complex A2 is trivial: analytically it
is a finite covering of the simply connected space C2. Hence H has degree
one and is an isomorphism. Finite etale triviality is the standard
geometric input here, not a result of the symbolic checker.

## Application to the actual quartic packets

PFN2 supplies the normal affine finite normalization. QPK4 gives one
ramified prime of transverse index two in either packet. The other
possible omitted prime is unramified. Thus the full ramification divisor
is the reduced prime R. QCL4 identifies [K_N]=[R] as a primitive nonzero
class, in either the rank-one or rank-two boundary class group.

All the hypotheses above therefore hold for both surviving quartic
finite-normalization packets. Any proposed polynomial plane inside B
that strictly contains the target algebra A is excluded.

This does not exclude the actual source polynomial ring C[x,y]:
the source open U=A2 embeds in N, so B is a subring of C[x,y], and its
coordinate generators need not be regular on N. Nor does the theorem
exclude every birational coordinate pair or an abstract A2 complement.
It excludes an intermediate polynomial plane whose two coordinates are
regular on the finite normalization.

## A direct use in the pole-four leading atlas

In the [pole-four atlas](POLE_FOUR_EXCLUSION.md), one branch has
leading sections u=mu*tau and r=3mu*v-mu^3*tau. The corresponding
global functions satisfy

\[
 X=\mu T+\chi,\qquad
 Z=3\mu Y-\mu^3T+\zeta.
\]

The candidate pair is polynomial in T,Y. Its outer Jacobian is

\[
 (12\mu^2-12\chi-6\kappa)T-12Y
 -6\kappa\mu-3\ell+4\mu^3.
\]

It is nonconstant because the coefficient of Y is -12. Thus (1)
already contradicts reduced prime ramification. This use only requires
the divisor part of the theorem, before invoking finite etale triviality.
