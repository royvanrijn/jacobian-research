# The residual omitted ledger forces a primitive S6 cover

14 November 2026. PF2OMIT8 adds a global matching constraint to the
[residual affine ledger](RESIDUE_LEDGER.md). Retain its degree-six
plane Keller hypotheses, with an immersed e2,f1 degree(6,10), gap21
prime R, and all additional boundary primes counted. Its remaining
ramified prime S has distinct target image B, index two or three and
residue degree one. Call the first image C. At most one omitted
unramified prime D is present, with image H.

Every remaining finite cover has monodromy S6 and no proper intermediate
function field between the target and source fields. This does not
construct any remaining cover or identify B,H. It rules out replacing
their global attachment problem by a degree-two/degree-three tower.
The target equations and actual exceptional target values remain unknown.

## 1. The complete affine inertia list

All finite ramification lies in the omitted boundary, because the
original source is Keller. The two ramified primes have distinct images
and each has residue degree one. A small meridian about a generic point
of C therefore acts on the six geometric sheets as one transposition,
with four fixed letters. A generic meridian about B acts as one
transposition in residual rows A,B, or one 3-cycle in row C.

The omitted unramified prime adds no inertia cycle. If H is distinct
from C,B, its meridian is the identity in the finite normalization,
although the source map loses one sheet there. If H=C or H=B, the
total target meridian is still the corresponding transposition, and
one of its fixed sheets is additionally omitted. This distinction is
between a prime's omission flag and its target image's monodromy.

The retained residue components at C or B need not each have degree
one. Their geometric sheets are fixed by inertia; decomposition into
global retained residue extensions is separate and is not computed here.

## 2. A small-cycle generation lemma

Let G be a transitive permutation group normally generated by single
2-cycles and single 3-cycles, with at least one 2-cycle. Then G is the
full symmetric group on its letters.

To prove this, let T be the normal subgroup generated by all 2-cycles
of G. Make a graph with one vertex per letter and one edge per such
transposition. The group T is the product of the symmetric groups on
the connected components: edge transpositions of a connected graph
generate its symmetric group. Since T is normal, G permutes the
components. Transitivity makes their common size m the same, and
m>=2 since a transposition exists.

A single 3-cycle cannot move these components. Moving even two of
them would move at least 2m>=4 letters. Every generating transposition
also fixes the components. The same is true of all their conjugates,
which remain cycles of lengths two or three. Normal generation then
makes G fix each component, so transitivity forces there to be only
one. Consequently T is already the full symmetric group.

Both hypotheses about cycles matter. A 4-cycle can exchange two blocks
of size two; a product of two 3-cycles can permute three such blocks.
The checker retains these transitive imprimitive controls. They are
group-theoretic controls, not geometric cover realizations.

## 3. Apply the lemma to the actual finite normalization

Remove C union B from the target. The finite normalization restricts
to a connected etale cover: it is a nonempty open of an integral N.
Thus its monodromy G is transitive.

The complement's fundamental group is normally generated by the small
branch meridians. Indeed a loop bounds a disk in the simply connected
ambient C2. A transverse disk meets the curve in finitely many smooth
points; deleting small disks around them expresses the original loop
as a product of conjugates of meridians. Singular curve points can
be avoided by general position. The omitted unramified image does not
need to be removed for this normal-generation statement.

Section1 supplies precisely the small cycles required in Section2.
Therefore the residual cover has

    G=S6, |G|=720, source stabilizer S5.                (M1)

This is a normal-generation argument. It does not assume that two
arbitrarily chosen meridians themselves generate the complement group.
Nor does it infer global monodromy just from a finite list of local
permutation samples.

The point stabilizer S5 is maximal in S6. If a subgroup containing it
also moves its fixed letter, the S5 orbit on the other five letters
makes that subgroup transitive. Its index over S5 is then six, so the
subgroup is S6. By Galois correspondence (M1) gives

    no strict intermediate field L subset M subset K,
    [K:L]=6, [Galois closure:L]=720.                   (M2)

Here L is the target function field and K is the source function field.
In particular this cover is not Galois, and no nontrivial degree-two
or degree-three normalization tower realizes the same source extension.

## 4. Sign cover and the ramification/omission distinction

The sign character gives the quadratic subfield of the Galois closure.
Using the trace/discriminant ledger PF2OMIT4, its equations are

    rows A,B: z^2=H_C*H_B,
    row C:    z^2=H_C,                                (M3)

up to a square and a nonzero constant. It is a subfield of the Galois
closure, not of K: S5 contains odd permutations and hence is not a
subgroup of A6. Inferring a quadratic intermediate plane cover from
the raw discriminant would contradict (M2).

In row C the 3-cycle meridian is even, so the sign cover misses B,
although S ramifies there with index three and is omitted. In row A
an additional distinct H has identity monodromy, although D is omitted.
If H shares C or B, its omitted sheet remains one of the fixed letters
of a nonidentity target meridian. Thus neither identity inertia nor
the sign cover determines the whole omitted boundary.

The normal closure of a transposition in S6 is S6; the normal closure
of a 3-cycle is A6. Accordingly, the maximal Galois quotient with trivial
inertia at the generic point of B is trivial in rows A,B, and is the
sign double cover in row C. This is a divisorial statement, not an
assertion of etaleness at points of C intersect B.
These quotient statements do not remove B from the original source
cover, or turn that quadratic quotient into an intermediate field of K.

## 5. Current global matching gate

A proposed completion of the residual ledger must supply the actual
curves C,B and their global complement relations, with a transitive S6
representation and exactly the inertia classes in Section1. In row A,
the additional omitted H still needs its actual reconstruction-pole
prime, even when its normal-cover inertia is the identity.

The pointwise and radial fibre formulas of PF2OMIT6--7 remain the
required exceptional-value checks. Neither an S6 permutation model nor
primitivity supplies source coordinates, a normal algebra, the omitted
image equations or a global source A2 open.

[verify_global_monodromy.py](verify_global_monodromy.py) replays the
exact permutation groups, normal closures, inertia/deletion rows and
sharpness controls. Normal generation of the actual curve complement
and field correspondence are the written steps above.
[global_monodromy_ledger.json](global_monodromy_ledger.json) preserves
the actual image equations as null. The full research goal remains open.
