# Canonical Keller receivers, their inertia, and affine quotient obstructions

14 November 2026. The clean coefficient-torus receiver extends to the entire good-generator chart in every rank N>=3. Its trace-zero version exists for N>=5, including rank five; the two-trace version exists for N>=6 as a receiver, before the stronger polynomial-section threshold N>=7.

The resulting canonical correspondences retain all generator lines. Their geometric automorphism groups are completely determined: trivial in even rank, and either trivial or mu2 in odd rank. This rules out a natural marked realization section from BS_N, even for stack-valued enhancements which still retain the good generator line.

Two independent affine landing obstructions explain why a quotient receiver is not itself a smaller Keller self-map of affine space. The global invariant-theoretic quotient contracts one explicit affine line in every degree. The source level C=1 has a nonconstant unit and cannot become affine space after identity stabilization. These are exact restrictions on the named operations, not universal dimension lower bounds.

## 1. Normalized inverse polynomials and ordered roots

Work over R=Z[1/2]. For every N>=3 let I_N be the coefficient scheme of
\[
 E(S)=S+bS^2+\pi S^3+\sum_{j=4}^N a_j\pi^jS^j-c/2
 \tag{1}
\]
where pi, the leading coefficient L and Disc(E) are units. Here L=pi if N=3 and L=aN*pi^N if N>=4. In particular aN is a unit in ranks at least four. This is an affine open of dimension N over R.

The normalized quadratic-gauge map from [URKC1](../../foundations/verified/UNIVERSAL_RELATIVE_KELLER_MAP.md) is
\[
 t=1+xy,\quad q=t^2z+y^2(1+3t),\quad \Pi=tq,
\]
\[
 B=y+3xq+\sum_{j=4}^N j a_jt^2x^{j-2}q^j,\qquad
 C=x(5-3t)-x^3z-\sum_{j=4}^N(j-2)a_j(xq)^j,
 \tag{2}
\]
with output (Pi,-B/2,C). Its determinant is one. Over I_N its whole preimage is the finite etale algebra B_N=O_(I_N)[S]/E; the inverse-derivative localization is automatic on this chart.

The residual scaling is
\[
 a_j\mapsto\lambda^{j+1}a_j,\quad
 (\pi,b,c)\mapsto(\lambda^{-2}\pi,\lambda^{-1}b,\lambda c),
 \quad E_{\lambda p}(S)=\lambda E_p(S/\lambda).
 \tag{3}
\]
It is defined even when intermediate coefficients vanish.

Let G_N be the open in A^N with coordinates r1,...,rN on which the Vandermonde and d1*d3 are units, where
\[
 P(T)=\prod_i(T-r_i)=\sum_{j=0}^N d_jT^j,\qquad d_N=1.
\]
These conditions are homogeneous under common scaling. The map
\[
 \pi=d_3/d_1,\quad b=d_2/d_1,\quad c=-2d_0/d_1,\quad
 a_j=d_jd_1^{j-1}/d_3^j
 \tag{4}
\]
gives E=P/d1. It identifies G_N with the full ordered-root frame of B_N over I_N. Thus G_N->I_N is an S_N torsor, finite etale of rank N!, without a clean-coefficient-torus assumption.

Conversely, from a target in I_N recover the monic polynomial P=E/L and d1=1/L, d3=pi/L. An ordering of its roots recovers a point of G_N. This proves (4) and its inverse over arbitrary base rings, including nonreduced rings.

Scalar multiplication on G_N commutes with S_N, and (4) intertwines it with (3). Since the root differences are units, a root tuple is a nowhere-zero vector defining a generator line, so
\[
 H_N=G_N/\mathbb G_m
\]
is exactly the good-generator open in P^(N-1). It is affine: it is the standard projective open defined by the nonvanishing of the homogeneous product of discriminant, d1 and d3.

## 2. The full canonical receiver and its constrained versions

**UARECV1.** For every N>=3 there is a natural equivalence
\[
 \boxed{\mathscr D_N=[I_N/\mathbb G_m]\simeq[H_N/S_N].}
 \tag{5}
\]
It is obtained by quotienting G_N by the two commuting actions, using that its maps to I_N and H_N are respectively an S_N torsor and a G_m torsor.

This is the full good-generator Keller receiver: an object is a finite etale rank-N algebra together with a good primitive generator line. It includes intermediate zero coefficients; the clean represented receiver QGDR1 is its old coefficient-torus open.

The universal rank-N root algebra descends by (3). In particular there is a representable finite etale morphism
\[
 \boxed{[H_N/S_{N-1}]\longrightarrow[H_N/S_N]}
 \tag{6}
\]
of degree N, and a classifying morphism rho_N:D_N->BS_N. On I_N the cover (6) is exactly the complete Keller fibre (1).

For any base scheme S over R and rank-N finite etale algebra A/S, one has
\[
 \boxed{S\times_{BS_N}\mathscr D_N
       \simeq H_A,}
 \tag{7}
\]
where H_A is the good-generator open in P(A). Its characteristic coefficients d1,d3 are units and its elements are primitive. The isomorphism follows either from the ordered-root torsor or by describing an object together with an identification of its algebra with A. This works for line subbundles locally generated by theta, not only for rational points.

Consequently rho_N is representable by schemes, smooth and surjective of relative dimension N-1. Its geometric fibres are integral rational opens in projective space. Over infinite fields, H_A(k) is nonempty and dense. Over bases with infinite residue fields, good residue-field generator coordinates lift after a Zariski shrinking, giving Zariski-local correspondences. In general there are etale-local choices.

These are correspondences S<-H_A->D_N. No choice of one line is included.

As a polynomial-map corollary, promoting all N-3 original seed coefficients gives a determinant-one map on A^N which realizes every rank-N finite etale algebra over every infinite field of characteristic other than two, for every N>=3. Only a primitive generator with d1*d3 nonzero is needed, and that open in A is nonempty over every algebraic closure and hence over every infinite field. Formula (4) gives the complete fibre. The generic monic polynomial and its ordered-root permutation action give arithmetic and geometric S_N monodromy in every such characteristic; the linear-coefficient normalization is birational. Its generic degree is N. The characteristic-free primitive-monodromy argument in UATRACE1 supplies absolute stable atomicity. This recovers the original full-dimensional construction while extending the low-rank characteristic scope of the earlier compressed statement.

### Effective polynomial gluing on vector bundles

The receiver does more than descend the finite algebra. Let L be its tautological line bundle, with the convention that a local coordinate of weight one transforms by lambda. The seeds a_j are sections of L^(j+1), while pi,b,c are sections of L^(-2), L^(-1), L.

The covariance (3) glues the complete polynomial formulas into a morphism
\[
 \boxed{F_{\mathscr D}:
 L\oplus L^{-1}\oplus L^{-2}
 \longrightarrow L^{-2}\oplus L^{-1}\oplus L.}
 \tag{7a}
\]
Each summand in (2) has exactly its required weight, so there are no coordinate-transition denominators to clear. The two ordered determinant bundles both identify with L^(-2); the relative Jacobian is identically one. The morphism is representable and etale.

Its target has the tautological section (pi,b,c). Its complete fibre is the universal root cover (6), canonically. Locally its root coordinate S has weight one and the equation E(S)=0 takes values in L, so the local identifications glue under S->lambda*S.

Thus polynomial landing and gluing are effective on these rank-three vector bundles over the line-decorated receiver, including all its coefficient-zero strata. This does not select a line for A or trivialize the bundles over BS_N. A fixed affine-space landing after forgetting the line is a stricter problem.

### Constraints descend without new quotient calculations

More generally let Z be any S_N- and G_m-invariant locally closed subscheme of G_N. The same argument gives
\[
 [\, (Z/S_N)/\mathbb G_m\,]\simeq[\, (Z/\mathbb G_m)/S_N\,],
 \tag{8}
\]
and its pullback over A is exactly the corresponding constrained generator-line scheme. Geometry such as smoothness, rationality and existence of rational points must be checked on that scheme; it does not follow merely by taking a quotient.

For the following two constraints it is explicit.

| Receiver | Rank range here | Pullback over A | Relative dimension |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Unconstrained | N>=3 | good open in P(A) | N-1 |
| Trace zero | N>=5 | good open in P(ker Tr) | N-2 |
| First and second trace zero | N>=6 | good open in the projective trace quadric | N-3 |

For trace zero, the required coefficient functions d1,d3 restrict to nonzero elementary symmetric functions on the root hyperplane. Their indices N-1,N-3 lie between two and N-1 for N>=5; the monomial argument in UATRACE1 applies. This proves nonempty smooth rational geometric fibres and field realizability over every infinite field of characteristic other than two, including rank five.

For both traces zero, the trace form has reduced rank N-1 or N-2, at least four in the stated range. Its primitive good open is smooth and geometrically integral. The coefficient conditions correspond to e_(N-1), e_(N-3), with indices at least three for N>=6, and are nonzero on the root quadric. Exactly as in UADOUBLE1, over an infinite field the good open has a point if and only if the trace quadric has a zero outside its radical. It is rational when it has such a point. A quadratic point is not asserted to lift Zariski locally in a general family.

The threshold distinctions matter. Trace-zero quartics have d3=0 and miss this compiler chart. Double-trace quintics also have d3=0. Trace-zero cubics do give a receiver by additionally setting b=0; this is a target constraint rather than deleting a promoted seed coordinate, so no affine coefficient-family self-map is claimed from that restriction.

In rank six the two-trace receiver has dimension three, although no root-free seed-only polynomial section was supplied by the earlier rank-six construction. A three-dimensional receiver is not a Keller self-map on A3.

## 3. Exact inertia and the general section obstruction

**UAINERT1.** Every geometric stabilizer of D_N is trivial or mu2. It is trivial for all even N. For odd N it equals mu2 exactly when E is odd:
\[
 b=c=0,\qquad a_j=0\quad\text{for every even }j.
 \tag{9}
\]
The same description restricts to all constrained receivers in (8).

Indeed the stabilizer at an inverse polynomial consists of scalars preserving (3). The nonzero cubic coefficient forces lambda^2=1. Since two is invertible, these possibilities are lambda=1,-1. A nonzero even-degree leading coefficient excludes minus one when N is even. In odd rank minus one fixes precisely the odd polynomials (9).

Equivalently, an algebra automorphism preserving a primitive line sends theta to lambda*theta. It fixes all characteristic coefficients, so the units d1,d3 force
\[
 \lambda^{N-1}=\lambda^{N-3}=1,\qquad\lambda^2=1.
\]
The action on a primitive generator is faithful, so the line stabilizer has exactly the same description.

When the involution occurs, its root permutation has cycle type
\[
 1\,2^{(N-1)/2}.
\]
The squarefree odd polynomial has the simple root zero and nonzero opposite pairs. There is no larger projective-homothety group hidden in these receiver strata. This is a classification of the inertia of this specified receiver, not of all stable polynomial self-equivalences.

The stacks in (5) are smooth Deligne--Mumford stacks, with geometric inertia of order at most two even in characteristics dividing N!. In even rank they are represented by affine schemes: H_N is affine, its free finite S_N quotient is affine, and that quotient is the quotient stack.

### Why stack-valued decorations do not automatically give a section

Let rho:E->BS_N be any stack morphism and suppose it has a section. At a geometric split algebra, functoriality on automorphisms gives
\[
 S_N\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{E}(e)
       \longrightarrow S_N,\qquad\text{composite}=1.
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus its inertia must admit a split surjection onto S_N at that object. If rho is representable, the second arrow is injective, so it must be an isomorphism.

For D_N its image has order at most two, while N!>2 for every N>=3. Hence
\[
 \boxed{\rho_N:\mathscr D_N\to BS_N
             \text{ has no section for every }N>=3.} \tag{11}
\]
This persists after every extension of constants.

More generally any proposed enhancement E with a morphism E->D_N over BS_N also has no section: composition would contradict (11). Adding auxiliary decorations or gauge automorphisms which still preserve this good generator line cannot repair the obstruction. The image of its automorphisms on the algebra remains too small, even if the kernel grows.

A viable different receiver must therefore discard the selected generator line, change the requested notion of naturality, or use a correspondence rather than a section. Having a sufficiently large automorphism group is a necessary test, not a sufficient existence theorem.

This concerns a marked realization and natural identification of its fibre with A. It does not prohibit a set-theoretic choice on isomorphism classes or the constant unmarked stable class of an already universal promoted map.

## 4. The stack quotient is etale; the global coarse quotient contracts a line

Keep any set J of retained seed coordinates among a4,...,aN, with all other seeds zero. If N>=4 retain aN for generic degree N; take J empty at N=3. Write r=|J| and w_j=j+1. Promote the seeds as identity coordinates. The source and target weights are
\[
 (w_j;\,1,-1,-2),\qquad (w_j;\,-2,-1,1).
 \tag{12}
\]
These are the same representation up to permuting the three moving coordinates. Thus the map (2) induces a representable etale self-map of
\[
 \mathscr Q_J=[\mathbb A^{r+3}/\mathbb G_m],
 \qquad\dim\mathscr Q_J=r+2.
 \tag{13}
\]
To see etaleness, base change by the target presentation A^(r+3)->Q_J. The fibre product is the original affine source, and its map to that presentation is precisely the determinant-one Keller map. Etaleness descends from this cartesian presentation. Its generic degree is N, retained by taking invariants: on the generic c!=0 chart, c and its source pullback C provide the same weight-one transcendental coordinate.

**UAQFAIL1.** The induced morphism of global affine invariant-theoretic quotients
\[
 \overline F_J:Q_J=\operatorname{Spec}
              k[a_j,x,y,z]^{G_m}\longrightarrow Q_J
\]
is not quasi-finite, in every characteristic other than two, for every such J and every N>=3.

Set every seed to zero. The resulting closed invariant subspace has quotient
\[
 Q_0=\operatorname{Spec}k[u,v]\simeq A^2,\qquad
 u=xy,\quad v=x^2z.
\]
The restriction on invariant rings is surjective, so Q0 is a closed subscheme of Q_J. Put
\[
 R=(1+u)^2v+u^2(4+3u),\qquad D=2-3u-v.
\]
On this subspace C=xD. Every nonconstant target invariant with all target seeds zero contains a positive power of c: its other weights are -1 and -2. Therefore the entire closed affine line
\[
 \boxed{\Gamma=\{a_j=0,\quad v=2-3u\}\simeq A^1}
 \tag{14}
\]
maps to the invariant-theoretic vertex.

For a direct lift take x=1,y=u,z=2-3u and all seeds zero. It has C=0 identically; all nonconstant target invariants vanish. Distinct u remain distinct in the source quotient by the invariant xy. This proves positive-dimensional fibre without a degree search.

For the cubic quotient itself one can write
\[
 (u,v)\longmapsto
 \left(-\tfrac12(u+3R)D,\;(1+u)RD^2\right).
 \tag{15}
\]
Its determinant is -D^2, and it contracts the displayed line to the vertex. This is compatible with the original three-dimensional determinant-one identity: passing to a coarse quotient has changed the morphism's etale property.

The contraction is not repaired by isomorphisms or identity stabilization: Gamma times a fixed auxiliary point remains a positive-dimensional fibre. Resolving, removing or filling divisors would be a new operation requiring its own proof. In particular (14) does not rule out another affine completion or another polynomial compiler.

### The coarse ambient space is also singular as soon as a seed is retained

For r>=1, Q_J has dimension r+2 and is singular at its vertex. In the invariant monoid, the following monomials are indecomposable:
\[
 xy,\quad x^2z,\quad
 a_jy^{w_j-2k}z^k\quad
     (0<=k<=\lfloor w_j/2\rfloor).
 \tag{16}
\]
The last monomials contain one a_j and no x. A nontrivial invariant factor without that a_j would use only negative-weight variables, which is impossible. The first two are similarly indecomposable.

Their independent classes in the vertex cotangent space give
\[
 \operatorname{edim}_{0}Q_J\ \ge\
 2+\sum_{j\in J}(\lfloor w_j/2\rfloor+1)
 >r+2,
 \tag{17}
\]
because every seed weight is at least five. This proves singularity uniformly. It is separate from the contraction: when r=0 the coarse quotient is smooth A2 but its induced map still fails to be etale.

The affine invariant-theoretic quotient in this section is a good-moduli quotient, not a geometric orbit quotient. Distinct nonclosed orbits can have the same image. There is no conflict between etaleness of (13) and the contraction in (14); one cannot apply an etale descent theorem for coarse spaces without its additional hypotheses. On Gamma the source orbit is closed: x is nonzero and at least one of y,z is nonzero, since v=2-3u. Its image has all positive-weight coordinates zero and has a nonclosed orbit unless it is the origin. Thus closed-orbit preservation, one of the relevant coarse-descent requirements, fails concretely.

## 5. Target-dependent normalization and the affine source gate

**UACTARGET1.** For every retained-seed family above, the actual source level C=1 has a nonconstant invertible regular function. It is therefore not affine space over any field of characteristic other than two, and neither is its product with any affine space.

Indeed every term of C in (2) is divisible by x. Write C=xK. On C=1, x*K=1, so x is a unit. It is nonconstant: the specialization
\[
 a_j=0,\quad x=s,\quad y=0,\quad
 z=(2s-1)/s^3
 \tag{18}
\]
defines a map from Gm_s into C=1 and sends x to s. Since polynomial rings over fields have only constant units, C=1 and all its identity stabilizations cannot be affine spaces.

The level is nonetheless smooth: the promoted Keller map is etale and the target coordinate projection c is smooth, so their composition C is smooth. Thus smoothness and the expected dimension do not remove the affine source obstruction.

Since c has weight one, the quotient of the target c!=0 chart is represented by c=1. The source quotient is represented by precisely this C=1 level. It supplies a regular etale map on these quotient charts, but does not directly produce a self-map of affine space.

### Even-rank polynomial coefficients do not remove a target pole

For even N>=4, the finite target chart has a global weight-one unit
\[
 \nu=a_N\pi^{N/2}.
\]
Consequently its scaling action has a global section nu=1. In this section aN=pi^(-N/2), so its target is an explicit discriminant open in
\[
 G_{m,\pi}\times A^{r+1}
\]
when r retained seeds include aN. The other r-1 seeds and b,c are free.

The source section is
\[
 a_N\Pi^{N/2}=1.
\]
Because Pi=tq is independent of every seed, eliminate aN. Before the discriminant restriction the source becomes
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Spec}k[a_j\ (j\ne N),x,y,z,1/(tq)].}
 \tag{19}
\]
It has the expected dimension r+2, and the descended map is etale. Its source still has the nonconstant unit tq; its target has the unit pi. To obtain a polynomial map on the whole affine spaces, one must provide a new affine completion and prove regular extension across the omitted divisors. A section of the finite target quotient alone supplies neither.

In rank four, (19) gives an etale map between open subsets of three-dimensional affine spaces, realizing all rank-four algebras over infinite fields, without proving a universal Keller self-map on all A3.

For the double-trace rank-six receiver retain only a6. The target section a6*pi^3=1 has inverse polynomial
\[
 E=S+bS^2+\pi S^3+\pi^3S^6-c/2,\qquad\pi\ne0.
 \tag{20}
\]
Its source is the corresponding discriminant open in A3 minus {tq=0}. It realizes precisely the algebras satisfying the smooth trace-quadric criterion. In characteristic zero its trace condition is not universal over all field extensions: binary rank six has only two blocks, so the classical binary trace form is anisotropic. Thus both the arithmetic and the affine source requirements remain visible.

Equations (18)--(19) are invariants of the specific charts under isomorphism and identity stabilization. They do not preclude filling a boundary by another construction. Normalizing b instead of c is also a different source-level problem, not settled by the C=1 argument.

## 6. Exact polynomial landing for coefficient profiles

The even-rank chart raises a more precise question: could its negative powers of Pi cancel in the original source coordinates? One all-degree rule answers this, and extends the earlier common positive power shifts.

**UAPROFILE1.** Replace each constant seed a_j in (2) by any rational function f_j(Pi) in k(Pi). The resulting rational map has determinant one wherever defined. The map extends polynomially on the original affine source if and only if:

- in characteristic zero, every f_j belongs to k[P];
- in odd characteristic p, every f_j is Laurent polynomial, with negative powers permitted only at indices j congruent to2 modulo p and only with pole order one or two.

At every pi!=0 where the profiles are defined, the complete fibre is exactly (1) with a_j=f_j(pi). For N>=4 with f_N nonzero the generic degree is N; the seed-free case has degree three. In characteristic zero its generic arithmetic and geometric monodromy are S_N, and every resulting polynomial map is absolutely stably atomic. No such general positive-characteristic monodromy claim is made.

For the Jacobian statement write A=DF_a with the seeds held fixed, and let v be the sum of f'_j(Pi)*partial_(a_j)F_a. Its first coordinate is zero. Since Pi is seed-independent, the total derivative is
\[
 A+v\,d\Pi,\qquad d\Pi\,A^{-1}=e_1^t.
\]
The determinant lemma therefore gives det(A+v*dPi)=det(A)*(1+e1^t*v)=1. This works for rational profiles and in every characteristic other than two.

First exclude every finite pole alpha!=0 after extending constants to an algebraic closure. The divisor Pi=alpha is prime: its equation is primitive and linear in z, and t is a unit there. With S=x/t one has
\[
 xq=\alpha S,\qquad
 t^2x^{j-2}q^j=\alpha^jS^{j-2}.
\]
The coordinate S is transcendental on that divisor. If the rational profiles have a pole of maximal order M at alpha with leading coefficients u_j, the B and C leading residues are respectively
\[
 \sum_j j u_j\alpha^jS^{j-2},\qquad
 -\sum_j(j-2)u_j\alpha^jS^j.
\]
Distinct j give distinct powers in each sum. Both residues can vanish only when j*u_j=(j-2)*u_j=0 for every j; since two is invertible, every u_j must be zero. This contradicts the maximal pole. Thus there are no finite nonzero poles, including inseparable denominator cases after constant extension. Every f_j is Laurent polynomial.

Write f_j(P)=sum_m u_(jm)P^m. For landing at zero, use the prime t=1+xy. Modulo t one has x=-1/y and q=y^2, with y and q units. If C has a negative-profile pole of maximal order M, its t-adic leading residue is
\[
 -\sum_j (j-2)u_{j,-M}(-1)^j y^{j-2M}.               \tag{20a}
\]
Distinct j have distinct powers of y, so it cannot vanish unless every coefficient vanishes. Thus any negative profile must have j-2=0 in k. In characteristic zero there are none. In odd characteristic p these indices have j=2 in k, a nonzero scalar.

For these remaining indices the C contribution vanishes identically. A pole of order M>2 in B has leading residue
\[
 \sum_j j u_{j,-M}(-1)^{j-2}y^{j+2-2M},              \tag{20b}
\]
which again cannot cancel. Hence M<=2. Conversely, at such an index the B terms are
\[
 j u_{j,-M}t^{2-M}x^{j-2}q^{j-M}
\]
and are polynomial for M=1,2, while their C terms are zero. This proves both directions without assumptions about individual coefficient signs or a bounded degree range.

For pi nonzero, fixing the first output coordinate fixes every f_j(Pi), so the scheme-theoretic fibre is the constant-seed fibre. The inverse polynomial is irreducible over k(pi,b,c), since it is a nonconstant degree-N polynomial in S minus the independent constant parameter c/2. Its derivative is nonzero, giving degree N.

For completeness, the characteristic-zero symmetric monodromy has a uniform quadratic-tilt proof. Over an algebraic closure of k(pi), put
\[
 h(S)=S+\pi S^3+\sum f_j(\pi)\pi^jS^j.
\]
The critical-root parametrization of h(S)+bS^2-c/2 is
\[
 b(r)=-h'(r)/(2r),\qquad c(r)=2h(r)-rh'(r).
\]
The envelope identity dc=2r^2*db recovers r^2 from k(b(r),c(r)). The function b(r) has a simple pole at zero because h'(0)=1, so it is not in k(r^2). Thus the parametrization is birational. Also b(r) is separable in characteristic zero. For generic b all critical points are simple and their critical values are distinct: a generic multiple parametrization of a critical value would contradict birationality. The finite branch inertias are consequently transpositions.

They generate the full geometric monodromy. A nontrivial quotient by the subgroup they generate would give a connected cover of P1 branched only at infinity; Riemann--Hurwitz gives 2g-2=-d-r<-2 for degree d>1 with r>=1 points above infinity, impossible. A transitive permutation group generated by transpositions is S_N. The arithmetic group is then S_N as well. Primitive-monodromy atomicity applies to the polynomial extensions.

This contains the positive power-shift construction as a special case. For the even-rank target section f_N(P)=P^(-N/2), polynomial landing fails in the original coordinates in every characteristic other than two: N=4 would need characteristic two to satisfy j=2, and every even N>=6 has pole order greater than two. Rewriting or completing the source would require another mechanism.

In characteristic zero every polynomial profile is regular at Pi=0, so the rank-at-most-three boundary formula UABOUND1 still applies there by specialization. The exceptional positive-characteristic negative profiles may change that boundary; the old Pi-zero formula is not imported onto them.

## 7. A compiler-independent fixed-space obstruction

The inertia test concerns a receiver which retains a line. There is a second structural test even after that line is forgotten.

**UAEQUIV1.** Let G be a finite group and let F:V->W be a G-equivariant polynomial map between finite-dimensional linear representations over a characteristic-zero field, with constant nonzero Jacobian. For every subgroup H<=G, the restricted morphism
\[
 F^H:V^H\longrightarrow W^H
 \tag{21}
\]
is a Keller map between affine spaces of the same dimension. If y in W^G has finite etale fibre X, then its complete fibre in (21) is X^H, the finite set of H-fixed points with its scheme structure.

At every H-fixed source point, DF is an invertible H-equivariant linear map, and so is its inverse. Restricting to invariant tangent vectors gives an isomorphism. Hence the determinant of (21) never vanishes over an algebraic closure and is a unit of its polynomial coordinate ring, therefore a nonzero constant. The dimensions agree already from DF at the source origin. Fixed-point equalizers commute with the fibre product defining X, proving the assertion about the complete fibre. No generic-fibre or unmarked identification is substituted.

Suppose G=S_N and the marked fibre is its natural transitive N-point set, as required by a functorial realization of a rank-N algebra with its identification. It has no G-fixed point for N>=2. Thus the restriction F^G must be a Keller map with an empty geometric fibre.

In dimensions zero and one this is impossible: the zero-dimensional spaces are one point, and a one-variable characteristic-zero Keller polynomial is linear. Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\dim V^{S_N}\ge2.}                           \tag{22}
\]
If that dimension is exactly two, the construction supplies a non-surjective plane Keller map and hence a counterexample to JC(2). Conditional on JC(2), at least three invariant directions are required.

More generally every subgroup H for which the natural N-point action has other than one fixed label forces dim(V^H)>=2 in characteristic zero. If dim(V^H)=2 and the fixed-label count is zero or greater than one, the same restriction gives a plane Keller counterexample. This is an all-subgroup necessary condition on canonical equivariant architectures.

A vector bundle over BS_N is a linear S_N representation. A polynomial marked Keller realization over BS_N therefore yields exactly this test on the geometric split object. In particular, for the natural permutation bundle A together with r trivial line bundles,
\[
 \dim(A\oplus k^r)^{S_N}=r+1.
\]
Using A alone is unconditionally impossible for such a marked realization. Using A plus one trivial line would give a plane counterexample. A plus two trivial lines is the first of these architectures not excluded by the fixed-space argument; no construction in that dimension is asserted.

This conclusion is independent of the quadratic-gauge compiler and its coefficient supports. Its scope is polynomial maps between linear representation bundles with a specified natural marked fibre. It does not forbid a noncanonical universal map, nonlinear group actions, a different kind of receiver, or a correspondence. It is not a lower bound for ktdim, whose realizations do not require such natural equivariance.

| Can all generator choices and their polynomial maps be retained canonically? | Yes: (5)--(8) and (7a), including coefficient-zero boundaries. | A section forgetting the line and a global stable-map moduli object are not supplied. |
| Can a good generator line be selected naturally with its algebra identification? | No: inertia at most two cannot retain S_N for N>=3. | A receiver discarding the line, a different naturality notion, or a correspondence. |
| Can a natural marked lift use the permutation bundle alone? | No: (22); one extra trivial line would already imply a plane counterexample. | A different representation, nonlinear action or receiver requires its own construction. |
| Does a stack quotient lower dimension? | Yes: the etale quotient morphism (13). | Its ambient object is a quotient stack, not affine space. |
| Does its global coarse quotient yield a Keller map? | No: the explicit curve (14) is contracted, and (17) gives singular vertices. | Another model with independently proved polynomial extension. |
| Does normalization by target c yield affine source? | No: C=1 has a nonconstant unit. | Other target levels or another completion require a separate affine source proof. |
| Does an even-rank free target section solve the source problem? | No: (19) retains an inverted divisor, and UAPROFILE1 proves its pole cannot cancel in the original source. | A different affine filling and regularity proof. |

These no-go statements do not replace the separate field-versality criterion UABINARY1 or stable-class defects UATRACEMOD1 and UADOUBLEMOD1. A canonical correspondence, a field-versal parameter space, an affine polynomial self-map and a functorial section are different achievements.

The unresolved problem is to characterize a receiver that forgets the generator line but still supports polynomial incidence, and to find an affine source mechanism for a genuinely different normalization. Repeated calculations of the existing coefficient-torus stabilizer or the contracted coarse map will not close either gate.
