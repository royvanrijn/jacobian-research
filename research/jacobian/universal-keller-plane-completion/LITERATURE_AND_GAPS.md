# Published line rigidity, saturation precedent and the exact remaining boundary

Reviewed 15 November 2026. [Proof](PROOF.md), [source pins](sources.json). This audit distinguishes established inputs from their compiler applications.

[Janusz Gwozdziewicz, Injectivity on one line (1993)](https://arxiv.org/html/alg-geom/9305008v1), Theorem1.1 and Lemma2.3, proves the plane Keller line-injectivity criterion. The same source explicitly uses Abhyankar--Moh embedded-line rectification and Newton-polygon similarity. HC4MYGL0 already imports the exact theorem in the HC(4) research; it is reused here rather than introduced as a new theorem. The primary HTML was read. Its prose contains a composition-inverse typo in the final proof; only the stated theorem, Lemma2.3 and standard rectification input are imported.

This input closes a real gap in the previous Universal Keller analysis. A target factor after a retained profile is Keller on the generic signal plane and fixes one line pointwise. It is therefore invertible, with the exact native landing ideal from UANATIVEORBIT1. The earlier UACUBICDESC1 branch with a native target factor of degree greater than one is empty. This is an application of a published theorem, not a solution of JC(2).

[Anton Trushin, Contracted divisors and Degree-Two Maps (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26390v1), Proposition3.1.1(1)-(2), publicly states polynomial rational saturation under absence of contracted divisors over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. This hypothesis is weaker than quasi-finiteness. UAQFSAT1 already disclaimed novelty; this supplies precise earlier attribution for the overlapping assertion. Its elementary arbitrary-field quasi-finite argument remains valid, with priority unverified. The later degree-two claims of this paper are not needed.

The plane completion is a different use of the same geometry. Its Jacobian is 1-SY, not a constant. The line theorem applies to a Keller FACTOR, and embedded-line rectification allows its use on either side of a plane factorization. It does not make the completed map invertible. The resulting irreducible-Jacobian/embedded-line indecomposability proof is written in full; novelty of that general observation and the native application has not been established.

The exact remaining distinction is now between polynomial intermediate coordinates on the completed plane and intermediates having a genuine boundary pole. Whole-map absolute stable atomicity allows additional possibilities, including moving the signal and stabilization mixing. A new generic plane solution must also land polynomially on the whole original source, including sigma=0, preserve the complete canonical marked scheme and glue effectively. No published result reviewed here supplies those missing certificates.
