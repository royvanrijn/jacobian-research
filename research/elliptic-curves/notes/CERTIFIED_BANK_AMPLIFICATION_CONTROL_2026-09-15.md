## Question and equal charges

The earlier early-generator experiment reached actual new-generator anchors,
but its 28 later gains did not repay complete rebuilding costs. The independent
bank certificate subsequently preserved every selection on 34 retained rebuilds
with 30.17% lower complete CPU. That engineering result cannot be subtracted
retrospectively from an old search. This successor executes both policies again
on disjoint controls, using the same cheaper constructor in both arms.

Both arms start from the same independently certified M18 subgroup and the same
16 retained generic parent anchors. Each reconstructs and verifies its initial
bank, exporting at most 48 centres. Both retain and certify every point cloud.
The fixed arm keeps its original search subgroup and bank. The refreshed arm
rebuilds from the complete admitted subgroup after each positive cloud below the
three-direction target and visits anchors using the latest admitted generators
first. A cloud that overshoots the target is retained in full.

Every visited anchor receives its factor-free box and at most two distinct
retained neighbour boxes at height 125,000, subject to the common stops. No fitted
radius or residue score enters selection. Only exactly equivalent completed
boxes on the same actual anchor are skipped, including infinity. Exact boundary
probes record neighbour coordinates beyond the old 125,000 range and outside
previously completed same-anchor boxes. An absent probe is UNKNOWN and never
prunes a search; a successful coordinate probe does not assert a rational point.

Each arm has the unchanged 40 CPU-second working launch allowance, including
imports, initial bank, every rebuild, independent minimum/selection certificates,
map preparation, point search, whole-cloud admission and native rank checks.
Atomic operations may overrun that launch allowance. Mandatory final replay is
also charged in complete isolated child-process CPU. The worker process has a 100 CPU-second hard limit, and the supervisor bounds
each arm by 150 wall seconds; point calls have five-second wall limits. The
working meter and reported complete CPU include child point-search processes.
The declared campaign budget is checked against the complete child totals. Retained
seed/parent construction is the common input interface. Shared input preflight,
known-transcript development and postmortems are separately reported with
candidate-only charge sensitivities.

## Predeclared causal audit

After both blocks finish, every actual later candidate gain on a new-generator
anchor is bound to its complete pre-call subgroup, native anchor word, rational
point, quartic square and executed coordinate map. Old-bank and individual
coefficient-deletion maps are constructed without target input. The audit first
checks P and -P, then the larger fixed dictionary

    ±(P + sum k_i G_i),   each k_i in {-1, 0, 1},

using every generator admitted above M18 before that call. At most two such
generators give at most eighteen representatives. The same dictionary is used
on old, deleted and actual charts. Winning coordinate squares are checked by
mapping back to the curve. A surviving witness is a finite visibility change,
not a minimum over an entire subgroup coset or an arithmetic exclusion.

The mechanism audit is capped at 300 CPU seconds; the translated audit at 90.
They run no point search and preserve prefixes on failure. Existing numerical
Schur/cascade diagnostics remain retrospective, with no prospective use of the
missing points or inference from a one-dimensional tautological correlation.
