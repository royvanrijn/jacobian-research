# Exceptional soluble-versus-Sha panel

The five large-jump curves have **62 certified independent soluble residual
classes**. The fixed-field `u=-1` control supplies a certified
**18-dimensional residual Selmer subspace with pairing rank 16**. These are
subspace results: the full Selmer complements on all five exceptional
curves remain **UNKNOWN**.

The useful invariant is the **rank of the Cassels–Tate pairing on a
specified, certified Selmer subspace**. It detects Sha without finding
points on the covers. It gives a necessary bound on possible soluble
dimension, not a criterion proving that the remaining radical is soluble.
No new prospective large-jump predictor is certified by this panel.

`U` be the displayed subspace of `Sel_2/G`. Write `q` for its witnessed
soluble dimension and `rho` for its restricted pairing rank.

| Fixed-field u=-1 | 1 | 18 | 16 | [0,2] | 1 |

The target reference groups are their specialized generic MW17/MW16
groups. The control reference group is generated by the separately known
point `Q` below; it is not asserted to be the specialization of a generic
rank-one family. All reference groups have `epsilon_M=0`.
Intervals concern **U only**, not the complete soluble space `W_G`.

The target point and independence proofs are replayed from the
[eleven-fibre certificate](../../artifacts/generated-results/elliptic-curves/exceptional_soluble_selmer_panel_v1.json).
The extension constructs an additional labelled binary-quartic presentation
for every selected target class, with exact map and rational witness.
Their zero pairing entries follow from global Kummer witnesses; they are
not uncomputed arithmetic entries filled with zeros. The control's 153
arithmetic entries and eight additional checks are independently replayed
from the [existing Fisher-formula certificate](FIXED_CUBIC_U_MINUS1_CASSELS_TATE_2026-09-05.md).

This is an intentionally asymmetric calibration: the target subspaces
were selected from known points; the control subspace was constructed
from inherited squareclasses and local conditions. Consequently the
target rows cannot validate a point-blind classifier. In particular, a
full target Selmer group might also contain substantial Sha.

## What the invariant proves before point discovery

Put `S=Sel_2(E)/G`, `W=W_G`, and let `b` be the residual pairing. For
certified subspaces `U,T ⊂ S`, a complete rectangular matrix for
`b:U×T→F_2` defines

\[
 \rho(U,T)=\operatorname{rank}(b|_{U\times T}),\qquad
 K(U,T)=U\cap T^\perp.
\]

These depend on the subspaces, not their ordered bases. If `dim U=d`, then

\[
 \dim K(U,T)=d-\rho(U,T),\qquad
 q\leq\dim(U\cap W)\leq d-\rho(U,T),
 \tag{1}
\]

where `q` is the dimension of any certified soluble subspace of `U`.
Moreover the image of `U` in `Sha[2]` has dimension at least `rho(U,T)`.
Exactly `2^d-2^(d-rho(U,T))` elements of `U` lie outside the tested
annihilator and are therefore proved insoluble. Other elements can also
be insoluble; this is a certified count, not the total insoluble count.
Enlarging `T` can only increase the obstruction rank and shrink `K(U,T)`.

**Proof.** Rational Kummer classes pair to zero with every Selmer class,
so `U∩W ⊂ K(U,T)`. Apply rank-nullity to the map `U→T*`.
The quotient `U/(U∩W)` injects into `Sha[2]`, proving the dimension bound.
Counting elements outside the kernel gives the displayed count. QED.

For `T=U`, `rho` is even. On the control, `rho=16` proves at least
sixteen Sha dimensions and excludes `262140` of the `262143` nonzero
residual classes. The excluded fraction of all classes is
`1-2^-16 = 65535/65536`. This obstruction computation used no rational
point on any inherited cover.

A useful refinement does **not** require a complete pairing matrix:
if an independent, unconditional certificate supplies `dim S≤s_max`,
then any certified restricted pairing rank `rho` gives

\[
 \operatorname{rank}E(\mathbf Q)
 \leq r+s_{\max}-\rho-\epsilon_M.
 \tag{2}
\]

Indeed `dim Sha[2]≥rho` and `dim S=dim W+dim Sha[2]`. Thus an upper
envelope together with a nondegenerate minor can already exclude a jump.
Neither an upper envelope nor a nonzero target minor is available here;
no whole-curve rank upper bound is emitted.

For a **complete** residual pairing,

\[
 \operatorname{rad}(S)/W=2\Sha[4],\qquad
 \rho(S,S)=\dim\Sha[2]-\dim 2\Sha[4].
 \tag{3}
\]

This is the precise limitation of first-stage CT rank. A nonzero rank
certifies Sha; zero rank allows rational points and higher-divisible Sha.
For example the abstract symplectic module `(Z/4)^2`, paired by
`(ad-bc)/4 mod Z`, restricts to zero on its nonzero two-torsion subgroup.
This is an algebraic illustration, not a newly constructed elliptic-curve
example. A positive solubility conclusion needs rational witnesses or a
further global argument excluding the surviving Sha. See
[Fisher, Introduction and Theorem 3.1](https://antsmath.org/ANTSXV/papers/ANTS-XV_fisher.pdf)
and the [residual exact sequences](../../elkies-k3/RATIONAL_SOLUBILITY_AND_RESIDUAL_SELMER_THEOREMS.md).

## A soluble and an insoluble cover with the same invariants

Use the pinned anchor `theta^3+A theta+B=0` of the
[fixed-field experiment](FIXED_CUBIC_FIELD_VARYING_CURVE_EXPERIMENT_2026-09-04.md).
On its `u=-1` curve,

\[
 Q=(A+1,A-B+1),\qquad \eta=\delta(Q)=[\theta^2-\theta+A+1].
\]

The new checker constructs a pointed cover for `eta` and independently
certifies all **19** classes `w_1,…,w_18,eta` by exact good-prime
square characters, using primes no larger than **199**. Hence the
inherited eighteen classes remain independent modulo `<eta>`, justifying
the residual control row above. This also proves `Q` is non-torsion and
its rank-one subgroup has `epsilon_M=0`.

On the augmented subspace the matrix is `diag(B_CT,0)`: dimension 19,
rank 16, radical dimension 3, soluble dimension in `[1,3]`. Precisely
`524280` classes have a certified nonzero pairing partner. Two radical
dimensions modulo the witnessed point remain unresolved.

The soluble point cover and all eighteen certified insoluble basis covers
have **exactly the same `I=c4(E)/16`, `J=c6(E)/32`**. The exact `I,J`
are in the [comparison certificate](../../artifacts/generated-results/elliptic-curves/exceptional_soluble_vs_sha_comparison_v1.json).
It records a nonzero pairing partner for every insoluble basis cover.
Thus the Jacobian invariants, their discriminant, and the shared cubic
algebra cannot distinguish solubility **of classes on this curve**.
This does not rule out curve-level correlations across other samples.

## Cover cost versus demonstrated jump

For a point on the rational short model, write `P=(a,b)` and
`E:y²=x³+A_E x+B_E`. The stored quartic starts from

\[
 g_P(t)=\tfrac14(t^4-6at^2-8bt-3a^2-4A_E).
\]

Its marking is the degree-four map `pi=2 phi-P`, not the birational chart
`phi`. PARI `hyperellred` supplies a rational parameter matrix `M` and
the checker proves the homogeneous identity
`g_P(M(X,Z))=k² g_red(X,Z)` exactly. The raw infinity witness is
transported to `(X,Z)=M^-1(1,0)`, `Y=1/(2k)`. Both the witness and the
map back to the original model are retained and checked. No minimization
over all equivalent covers or representatives modulo generic MW is claimed.

The cost policy counts numeric slots, charging a rational `n/d` one sign
bit plus the bit lengths of `|n|` and `d` (zero gets one numerator bit).
Total presentation cost includes the fixed input curve, five quartic
coefficients, parameter matrix, scaling, short point, and witness. The
fixed map template is shared. These are explicit dictionary costs, not a
universal description-length invariant.

| Curve | Demonstrated jump | Original quadric coefficient bits | Reduced quartic numerator/denominator bits | Total presentation slot bits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 356 | 12 | 414–472 | 256–357 | 1759–2538 |
| 385 | 12 | 464–520 | 276–403 | 1974–2750 |
| 398 | 14 | 414–417 | 193–204 | 1350–1377 |
| 400 | 12 | 370–372 | 171–183 | 1212–1234 |
| 543 | 12 | 392–407 | 189–217 | 1327–1517 |

All new pointed witnesses cost 10 slots-bits. The point's complexity is
present in the model and map. Sorting the total costs gives exact
successive span thresholds **inside this frozen independent-basis
dictionary**; the artifact records each list. They are upper bounds for
the unrestricted witness filtration, and all depend on points already
known. Equal demonstrated jumps of twelve occur with substantially
different recorded costs; the jump-fourteen row is not the most costly.
The data supply no monotone complexity-versus-jump law.

On the **same control curve**, the eighteen insoluble basis quartics use
111–121 coefficient bits, while the soluble point quartic uses 218
(1615 total presentation slots-bits). Small stored equations do not put
soluble classes first in this dictionary. The control quartics had an
earlier minimization as well as reduction; the new pointed quartics use
reduction only. This is a comparison of exact recorded presentations,
not a comparison of their optimal complexities. The certificate also
retains `H^6/max(|I|^3,J^2)` to remove scalar normalization from coefficient
height; it remains dependent on the parameter coordinates and is not a
solubility invariant.

## Complement feasibility and reproduction

The [preserved feasibility record](../../artifacts/generated-results/elliptic-curves/exceptional_selmer_feasibility_v1.json)
contains exact worker input, software, limits, logs, and the two historical
BNF transcripts. The existing 356/385 factor-hinted PARI 2.19 runs each
timed out after 1200 seconds in class-group relation collection; they
were not repeated. New Sage 10.9/PARI 2.17.3 workers for 398/400/543
received **only curve equations**, with 45 seconds, 256 MB initial PARI
stack, and a 1 GiB observed-RSS cap each. No factor hints were supplied.
They stopped during `ellrankinit`: 398 at the memory cap, 400/543 at the
time cap. That stage includes factorization and field/class setup, so
the logs do not isolate a more specific bottleneck. The code retained
checkpoints before termination and ran no point search. Missing output
is never interpreted as a zero complement.

The cheap replay rechecks all 62 target Kummer covers, all inherited CT
arithmetic, the 19-class control independence, 63 new quartic transports
and witnesses, presentation costs, and preserved probe-log hashes. It
does not repeat the descent probes or the old bounded radical searches.

```sh
sage -python elliptic-curves/cas/compare_exceptional_soluble_vs_sha.sage --check
sage -python -m unittest elliptic-curves/tests/test_exceptional_soluble_vs_sha.py
```

Regenerate the deterministic comparison with `--write`. The separate
bounded feasibility runner is:

```sh
python3 elliptic-curves/cas/run_exceptional_selmer_feasibility.py \
  --workdir artifacts/local/elliptic-curves/exceptional-selmer-fresh
```

Its outcomes are resource observations, not deterministic mathematical
invariants. A completed future descent must still bind its returned
classes and maps before extending the pairing panel. The mathematically
useful next addition is an independently constructed target Selmer class
with a certified nonzero pairing partner, or a certified dimension upper
envelope enabling (2). Repeating the forced-zero point block cannot
resolve the complement.
