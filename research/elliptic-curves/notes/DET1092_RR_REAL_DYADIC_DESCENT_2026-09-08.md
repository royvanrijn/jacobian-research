- the dimensions of the true and fake 2-adic Kummer images;
- whether the inherited class `D0=[K_C-2P0]` lies in `2J(Q2)`.

No exceptional point enters these local algorithms. The nine source-only

## Small local equations replace a global-field computation

**Verified application.** The retained
[preflight](../../artifacts/generated-results/elliptic-curves/det1092_rr_dyadic_preflight_v1/protocol.json)
made one bounded `factorpadic` call per exact sextic and retained its
approximations. Those approximations suggested manageable local factors;
they are not used as irreducibility or completeness certificates below.
The independent dimension proof uses only rational polynomial arithmetic
and finite residue trees.

For a monic sextic `q`, choose the largest integer `s` for which

\[
f(x)=2^{-6s}q(2^s x)\in\mathbf Z_2[x].
\]

This is an exact coefficient-valuation rule, not a coordinate search.
Put `D=v2(disc(f))` and `N=max(64,2D+16)`. All cases satisfy the frozen
512-bit precision cap. Replace the coefficients by their least integer
residues modulo `2^N`, obtaining a small monic integer sextic `g`.
Every coefficient congruence and both discriminant valuations are replayed.

**New deduction: certified preservation of the local branch Galois set.**
Let `alpha` be any root of `f`. Its conjugates are integral. Write
`u=v2(f'(alpha))`; then `0<=u<=D`. Coefficient closeness gives
`v2(g(alpha))>=N>2u` and `v2(g'(alpha))=u`. Strong Hensel lifting in
`Q2(alpha)` gives a unique root `beta` of `g` with

\[
v_2(\beta-\alpha)\ge N-u>D.
\]

No two distinct roots of `f` can be this close, since their separation
valuation is bounded by `D`. The six resulting roots of `g` are distinct,
and uniqueness makes their matching Galois equivariant. Thus the two
sextics have the same local branch Galois set. This transfers torsion and
theta data; it is **not** a claimed isomorphism of the two curves or their
Jacobians, or a transport of their full Kummer images.

## Resolvents and exhaustive Hensel certificates

**New deduction and verified application.** Let `alpha1,...,alpha6` be the
roots of `g`. The following polynomials have rational integer coefficients:

\[
R_2(z)=\prod_{i<j}(z-\alpha_i-\alpha_j),\qquad\deg R_2=15,
\]

\[
R_\theta(z)=
\prod_{\{S,S^c\},\ |S|=3}
\left[z-\left(\sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i-
                   \sum_{i\notin S}\alpha_i\right)^2\right],
\qquad\deg R_\theta=10.
\]

The constructor takes characteristic polynomials of the additive
operators on exterior powers of the six-dimensional multiplication
matrix. The independent checker uses Newton sums and inclusion–exclusion
on ordered distinct triples instead. Both resolvents are verified
squarefree, so these numerical labels identify their respective Galois
sets without collisions.

Nonzero genus-two 2-torsion classes are exactly the 15 unordered branch
pairs. Odd theta characteristics are the six branch points; even ones are
the ten unordered `3+3` partitions. Therefore

\[
\#J(\mathbf Q_2)[2]=1+\#\{z\in\mathbf Q_2:R_2(z)=0\},
\]

and a rational theta exists exactly when `g` or `R_theta` has a
`Q2` root.

All three polynomials are monic integral, so every rational 2-adic root
is integral. The complete residue tree starts at `Z2`. At each ball,
substitute `x=a+2^d z`, remove its common coefficient power of2, and test
both residues modulo2. A nonroot is rejected; a simple root closes with
Hensel's lemma; a multiple root is subdivided. The independent checker
rebuilds every ball directly from the original polynomial and verifies
complete child coverage and disjoint terminal root balls.

The entire panel uses 365 tree nodes, with maximum depth17. The fixed
limits were 4096 nodes and depth64 per polynomial. Thus a zero root count
is an exhaustive local obstruction, not a bounded rational-point miss.
All trees, including zero-root trees, are retained in the
[dyadic certificate directory](../../artifacts/generated-results/elliptic-curves/det1092_rr_dyadic_theta_v1/replay.json).

**Established literature, verified application.** The local index formula
gives `dim J(Q2)/2J(Q2)=2+dim J(Q2)[2]`. The even-degree fake kernel is
generated by `D0`; it vanishes precisely when the canonical class has a
rational half. These are the local-index and kernel results of
[Poonen–Schaefer, Theorem11.3 and Lemma12.10](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/descent.pdf),
applied to the inherited rationally pointed genus-two curves.

## Complete real images

**Verified application.** Exact Sturm sequences count the real branch
points. Complex conjugation on branch pairs determines `J(R)[2]`;
the compact real Lie-group index formula yields

\[
\dim J(\mathbf R)/2J(\mathbf R)
=\max(0,r/2-1),
\]

where `r` is the real branch count. The inherited rational point is
checked, and the real fake kernel is zero. Eight members have dimension
zero. The two remaining members have four real branches and dimension1.

For each required inherited divisor, the signs of its Kummer representative
are the signs of

\[
h_j(\theta)=(-1)^{d_j}g_j(\theta)(T_0-\theta)^{d_j}.
\]

There is no floating-point root decision. The trace form

\[
H_j=(\operatorname{Tr}(h_j(\theta)\theta^{a+b}))_{0\le a,b<6}
\]

has signature equal to the sum of those signs at real roots: each real
factor contributes its sign, and each complex factor has signature zero.
An exact invertible rational congruence diagonalizes `H_j`. The independent
checker reconstructs its entries by Newton recurrence and verifies the
entire congruence identity.

On scale-0131232 the first three generic divisors give signatures `4,4,0`;
on scale-0487239 the first gives signature `0`. Signature zero among four
real roots proves mixed signs, hence a nontrivial Kummer class even after
quotienting by rational scalar signs. This attains the known dimension1
and certifies the complete image. These are generic divisor indices2 and0,
respectively, not newly searched rational points.
