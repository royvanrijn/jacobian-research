# A point-independent condition must retain the native cover class

The genus-two formulation of the successful triple uses a known rational
origin P0 to write the exact condition

\[
[(t,y)-P_0]\in\psi(A(\mathbf Q)).
\]

The [previous proof](GENUS_TWO_DESCENT_ORGANIZES_THE_NATIVE_TRIPLE_LIFT.md)
explicitly labels that origin as retrospective. There is a tempting
point-independent replacement using the rational canonical divisor
I=∞+ + ∞−:

\[
\beta:H\longrightarrow J(H),\qquad P\longmapsto[2P-I].
\]

**This replacement loses the solubility condition.** Every rational point
of H satisfies β(P)∈ψ(A(Q)). In fact, the pullback of ψ along β is
the split cover consisting of four copies of H. It is not the connected
genus-five native carrier.

The correct point-independent condition retains the affine class map

\[
\boxed{\lambda:H(\mathbf Q)\longrightarrow
(\mathbf Q^*/\mathbf Q^{*2})^2,
\qquad (t,y)\longmapsto([h(t)],[f(t)]),\qquad
\lambda(t,y)=(1,1).}
\]

Here H:y²=hfg and the native quadratics are those of
`01333,0b2d0,19e45`. None has a rational root, so this formula applies
at every finite rational t. The product equation then forces g(t) to
be square. All infinity and branch interpretations remain part of the
proper cover; they are not assigned the displayed nonzero-value formula.

This is a **solubility** condition for the specified native maps. It does
not assert their independence or predict the complete original rank.
At t=−288/65 the triple has certified quotient rank two relative to the
rank-17 generic subgroup, within the retained rank-24 fibre.

## Why canonical doubling gives an automatic lift

Let J=J(H), K⊂J[2] be the subgroup generated by the three rational
Weierstrass pairs, φ:J→A=J/K the quotient isogeny, and ψ:A→J the dual,
so ψφ=[2]. All these objects are defined over Q before supplying a point.

Choose a root α of h and let W=(α,0); let W′ be its conjugate.
Since W and W′ are Weierstrass points,

\[
2W\sim I,\qquad [W'-W]=[W+W'-I]\in K.
\]

The map over Q(α)

\[
F_h(P)=\phi([P-W])
\]

is unchanged when W is replaced by W′. Galois descent therefore makes
Fh a morphism H→A defined over Q, without choosing a rational point of H.
Moreover,

\[
\psi F_h(P)=2[P-W]=[2P-I]=\beta(P).
\]

This proves the automatic lift for every rational P. Since ker ψ is the
dual of the rational group K, it consists of four rational points;
translating Fh by them gives the four sections of the pulled-back cover.
This argument does not assume H(Q) is nonempty.

More generally, replacing a degree-one Abel map by P↦[dP-D], for a
rational divisor D of degree d, multiplies its geometric action on
the 2-torsion characters by d. Odd d preserves the characters, possibly
with a constant twist; even d kills them. The native branch divisors
supplied by the three irreducible quadratics all have even degree.
They cannot supply an odd-degree normalization just by taking integral
linear combinations. A degree-one Picard torsor formulation must keep
its twist information; canonical doubling is not a substitute.

## Exact symbolic and finite-field checks

Write F=hfg. Away from y=0 the divisor [2P−I] has the degree-two
Mumford representative

\[
u(X)=(X-t)^2,\qquad
v(X)=y+\frac{F'(t)}{2y}(X-t).
\]

The checker verifies the cleared identity v²≡F(X) modulo u, using
y²=F(t), and the three exact identities

\[
\operatorname{Res}_X((X-t)^2,q_i(X))=q_i(t)^2.
\]

Thus evaluating the quadratic factors on the doubled divisor gives
trivial squareclasses. This agrees with the geometric section Fh above.
Factor evaluation as the isogeny descent map on degree-zero divisors is
described in [Arnth-Jensen and Flynn, §2](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/flynn/genus2/af/artlong.pdf).

The fixed finite-field diagnostic checks every finite non-branch point
of H at the already used primes:

| Prime | Points checked | Native classes 00 / 01 / 10 / 11 | Doubled-divisor class 00 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 131 | 140 | 28 / 42 / 32 / 38 | 140 |
| 137 | 120 | 28 / 36 / 28 / 28 | 120 |

A bit 1 denotes a nonsquare. At p=131, for example, (t,y)=(0,96)
lies on H and has native values (34,19,24), with class 01. The doubled
divisor returns the squares (108,99), with class 00. It does not lift
to the original native cover over F131, although its doubled-divisor
isogeny test passes.

These are finite-field counterexamples to the replacement, not rational
nonlifting-point certificates. Whether the current H has rational
points outside its native trivial class remains UNKNOWN.

## Same H and same isogeny, but an insoluble twist

Before inspecting the retained rational lift, the checker chooses the
first native quadratic irreducible modulo 131. It is h, whose reduction
is

\[
\bar h(t)=t^2+11t+34\in\mathbf F_{131}[t].
\]

Compare the original soluble carrier with the single auxiliary twist

\[
C_3^{(131,1)}:\qquad
131a^2=h(t),\quad u^2=f(t),\quad131v^2=g(t).
\]

The latter has the **identical** product quotient

\[
H:\ y^2=hfg,\qquad y=131auv,
\]

and therefore the same genus-two Jacobian, its absolute simplicity,
and its Richelot isogenies. But it has no Q131-point:

- If t is 131-integral, irreducibility of h modulo 131 makes h(t) a unit.
- If v131(t)<0, the leading term dominates and
  v131(h(t))=2v131(t), again even.
- Above t=∞, the leading coefficient of h is a unit.

Each case contradicts the odd valuation required by h(t)=131a²
(or its homogeneous leading-coefficient equation at infinity). Thus
the proper twist is locally insoluble, including its infinity points.

The original cover has the exact retained rational lift at −288/65.
This is a matched auxiliary-cover comparison; the twisted cover's
coordinates are not new rational points on the original elliptic fibres,
and the negative result is not a new low-rank control.

More generally the covers

\[
d_1a^2=h(t),\quad d_2u^2=f(t),\quad d_1d_2v^2=g(t)
\]

all have the same H. The required class is λ(P)=([d1],[d2]). The
solubility distinction resides in that class, which neither H nor its
Jacobian alone specifies.

## How to preserve the condition in future work

If any rational Q0∈H(Q) is used as an origin, the descent map satisfies

\[
\delta_\psi([P-Q_0])=\lambda(P)\lambda(Q_0)^{-1}.
\]

Consequently the original native target is

\[
\delta_\psi([P-Q_0])=\lambda(Q_0)^{-1},
\]

which is the zero class only when Q0 itself lifts to the original native
cover. The retained P0 was chosen from precisely such a lift. Silently
replacing it by an arbitrary point would change the target coset.

The mathematical event to detect is therefore still a rational point
on H **in the specified affine descent class**, followed by independent
original-curve rank accounting. Before a soluble point is available,
retain the native squareclass target or an equivalent torsor with its
twist. A large isogeny Selmer group, or automatic lifting of [2P−I],
does not establish this event. No visibility feature follows from it.

The remaining global implication is unchanged: determine which
rational points of H have λ=(1,1). This turn closes a proposed
point-independent shortcut and supplies the precise replacement; it
does not claim a new rank bound or a complete solution set.
