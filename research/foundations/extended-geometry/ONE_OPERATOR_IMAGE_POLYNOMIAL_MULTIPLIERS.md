# One-operator Image theorems for polynomial multipliers

## Theorem (regular leading coefficient or shifted monomial)

Let \(B\) be a commutative \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra, let \(c\in B\), and
let \(P\in B[z]\). Consider either of the following hypotheses.

- \(P\) has degree \(r\geq1\) and its leading coefficient \(a\)
  is a non-zero-divisor.
- \(P=a(z-b)^r\) for \(a,b\in B\) and \(r\geq1\).

Then the image \(I\) of \(D=c\partial_z-P\) is a Mathieu subspace.
Write \(f=\sum_i f_i z^i\) and \(C=B[a^{-1}]\).
The eventual image radical consists exactly of the \(f\) satisfying
\[
 f\in\sqrt{(P,c)B[z]},
 \qquad c f_i\text{ is nilpotent in }C\text{ for every }i.              \tag{1}
\]
These conditions describe an ideal. In the shifted monomial case they
also have a formulation in terms of the constant term at the center
\(b\). For integers \(d\geq0\) and \(N\geq1\), there is a computable
integer \(q=q(r,d,N)\geq N\) such
that, for \(\deg_z f\leq d\), membership of \(f^j\) in \(I\) for
\(N\leq j\leq q\) suffices for (1). For each such \(f\), one cutoff
works for all polynomial multipliers \(g\).

Van den Essen and Zhao, arXiv:1012.2017v1, Theorem 6.2 with
\(d=r\geq1\) and \(\alpha=0\), proves the zero-radical assertion over
\(\mathbb C\) for \(\partial_t-t^r\). Their Corollary 6.4 treats
scalar multiples of monomials over \(\mathbb C\). The following
field argument treats every nonconstant polynomial multiplier,
and finite universal identities and a denominator argument give
the ring statement.
[Mathieu Subspaces of Univariate Polynomial Algebras, Conjecture 6.1, Theorem 6.2 and Corollary 6.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2017v1#page=10)

## Lemma (zero eventual radical over characteristic-zero fields)

Let \(K\) be a characteristic-zero field, \(c\ne0\) an element
of \(K\), and \(P\in K[z]\) nonconstant. If every sufficiently
large power of \(f\in K[z]\) is in
\(\operatorname{Im}(c\partial_z-P)\), then \(f=0\).

For any coefficient ring in which the leading coefficient of
\(P\) is a unit, subtracting images of successive top monomials
gives each polynomial a remainder of degree less than \(\deg P\).
The remainder is unique because the degree of a nonzero image is
the preimage degree plus \(\deg P\). This criterion commutes with
base change by its coefficient recursion.

Suppose \(f\ne0\). If \(f\) is constant, its powers have nonzero
remainders. For nonconstant \(f\), take the finitely generated
\(\mathbb Q\)-subalgebra of \(K\) generated by all coefficients of
\(f,P\), the two leading-coefficient inverses and \(c,c^{-1}\).
The remainder identities expressing the tail hypothesis hold in
this subalgebra by injectivity. Reduction at a maximal ideal
preserves the degrees and \(c\ne0\), and its residue field is
a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q\) by Zariski's lemma. We may
therefore assume the coefficient field is a number field, and
enlarge it to contain a root \(\alpha\) of \(P\).

For a rational prime \(p>\deg P+1\), reduce at a prime above \(p\)
where \(P,f,c,\alpha\) are integral and \(c\) and the leading
coefficient of \(P\) are units. Only finitely many rational primes
are excluded by these requirements. We claim that over the residue
field the polynomial differential operator satisfies
\[
 D^p=-P^p\qquad\text{as an operator on polynomials}.                    \tag{2}
\]
For a derivation \(\delta\) and multiplication \(M_u\), put
\(D=\delta+M_u\). The commutator with \(M_v\) is
\([D,M_v]=M_{\delta v}\). In characteristic \(p\),
\((\operatorname{ad}D)^p=\operatorname{ad}(D^p)\), by expanding
the commuting left and right multiplication operators on
endomorphisms. Thus \(D^p-\delta^p\) commutes with every \(M_v\)
and is multiplication by \(D^p(1)\). To compute this multiplier,
expand \(D^n(1)\) as a sum over set partitions of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\),
using a factor \(\delta^{j-1}u\) for each block of size \(j\).
In the induction, multiplication by \(u\) adds a
singleton and differentiation adds the new label to an existing
block. For \(n=p\), partitions with \(k_j\) blocks of size \(j\)
have coefficient \(p!/\prod_j (j!)^{k_j}k_j!\). This coefficient is
zero in the residue field except for the single block and the
all-singleton partition. Consequently
\(D^p(1)=u^p+\delta^{p-1}u\). In our case
\(\delta=c\partial_z\) has \(\delta^p=0\) on polynomials,
\(u=-P\), and \(\delta^{p-1}P=0\) by the bound on \(p\).
This proves (2).

If a polynomial \(h\) with integral coefficients belongs to
the image in characteristic zero, the unique polynomial preimage
is integral at every such prime. Indeed, top-down division of
\(h=Du\) computes every coefficient of \(u\) using only divisions
by the unit leading coefficient of \(P\). Reducing that identity
and using (2) gives
\[
 (D^{p-1}h)(\alpha)=0\pmod{\mathfrak p}                                \tag{3}
\]
because \(P(\alpha)=0\).

Let \(N\) be a starting order for the image tail. If \(f(\alpha)=0\),
write \(s=\operatorname{ord}_\alpha(f)\geq1\) and, for
\(p\equiv1\pmod s\), take \(h=f^{(p-1)/s}\). For sufficiently large
\(p\) this is in the image. If \(f(\alpha)\ne0\), use
\(s=\operatorname{ord}_\alpha(f-f(\alpha))\geq1\) and take
\(h=f^N(f-f(\alpha))^{(p-1)/s}\). Its expansion is a linear
combination of image powers of order at least \(N\). Dirichlet's
theorem supplies arbitrarily large primes \(p\equiv1\pmod s\);
when \(s=1\) every prime qualifies. Exclude the finitely many primes
where the first nonzero coefficient at \(\alpha\), or the nonzero
\(f(\alpha)\) in the second case, is not a unit. Then \(h\) has
order exactly \(p-1\) at the reduced \(\alpha\). In the
differential operator \(D^{p-1}\) the coefficient of
\(\partial_z^{p-1}\) is \(c^{p-1}\), and every lower derivative of
\(h\) vanishes at \(\alpha\). The resulting value
\(c^{p-1}(p-1)!\) times the unit leading coefficient of \(h\)
contradicts (3).

### A finite remainder test after inverting the leading coefficient

On \(C[z]\) the image of \(D\) equals the image of
\((c/a)\partial_z-P/a\), with \(P/a\) monic if \(C\ne0\).
Fix \(r,d,N\) and use the universal coefficient ring
\(S=\mathbb Q[Y,A_0,\ldots,A_{r-1},X_0,\ldots,X_d]\).
Set \(G=z^r+\sum_{i<r}A_i z^i\) and
\(F=\sum_{i=0}^dX_i z^i\). Write \(\rho\) for the polynomial
remainder of \(Y\partial_z-G\), which commutes with base change
by the leading-coefficient division. Over \(\mathbb C\), every
common zero of the coefficients of \(\rho(F^j)\) for \(j\geq N\)
satisfies \(YX_i=0\) for all \(i\). Indeed, at any such point
where \(Y\ne0\), the field result gives \(F=0\).

By the Nullstellensatz, powers of each \(YX_i\) are in the ideal
of these remainder coefficients over \(\mathbb C\).
Each identity of ideal membership uses finitely many indices and
polynomial coefficients of finite degree. With those bounds it is a
finite linear system over \(\mathbb Q\), so it also has a rational
solution. Thus for some \(q\) all the power identities hold over
\(\mathbb Q\) using indices in \([N,q]\). One can compute \(q\) by
testing, for successive \(q\), whether each indicated polynomial
lies in the radical of the rational ideal from those indices.
For a polynomial \(p\) and ideal \(K\), this is the Gröbner basis
test \(1\in (K,1-U p)S[U]\). The equivalence follows by inverting \(p\)
in \(S/K\), which gives the zero ring exactly when \(p\) is
nilpotent there.

If \(f^j\in I\) for \(N\leq j\leq q\), evaluate the power identities
at \(Y=c/a\), \(X_i=f_i\), and the coefficients of \(P/a\) in
\(C\). The remainder coefficients vanish by the kernel property,
so each \(c f_i\) is nilpotent in \(C\). The same assertion is
automatic if \(C=0\). Since every image lies in \((P,c)B[z]\),
\(f^N\in I\) implies the first condition of (1).

### Producing divisible preimages

First consider any polynomial \(P\) that is a non-zero-divisor in
\(B[z]\). Suppose \(f^N\in(P,c)B[z]\) and \(c^k f^k=0\) for some
\(k\geq1\). For any integer \(L\geq0\), every marked power
\(h=gf^m\) with \(m\geq k+(L+3k)N\) satisfies
\[
 h\in P^{L+2k}B[z],\qquad c^k h=0.                                    \tag{4}
\]
To see the divisibility, expand \((f^N)^{L+3k}\) in
\((P,c)^{L+3k}\) and multiply by the remaining factors, including
\(f^k\). Terms containing \(c^k\) vanish and the others contain
\(P^{L+2k}\).

On the ring in which \(P\) is inverted, write
\(S_0(h)=c\partial_z(h/P)\). This operator lowers divisibility by
\(P\) by at most two while the current exponent is at least two,
by the product rule applied to \(\partial_z(P^{j-1}v)\).
It commutes with multiplication by \(c\), with one factor of \(c\)
in each iterate, so (4) gives \(S_0^k h=0\). Since
\(D(-h/P)=h-S_0 h\), the polynomial
\[
 v=-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{S_0^j h}{P}\in P^L B[z]
 \qquad\text{satisfies}\qquad Dv=h.
\]
The equality descends by injectivity of localization at \(P\).
When the leading coefficient \(a\) is regular, \(P\) is regular
on \(B[z]\), and the localized nilpotent
coefficients \(c f_i\) from (1) are already nilpotent in \(B\).
Their finite generated ideal is nilpotent, so \(c^k f^k=0\) for
some \(k\). This proves (1) sufficient and the marker uniformity
for every polynomial with regular leading coefficient.

### Descent through leading-coefficient torsion

For a shifted monomial, translate so that \(P=az^r\). Translation
commutes with \(\partial_z\), preserves degrees and transforms the
coefficients of \(cf\) by invertible linear combinations over \(B\),
preserving nilpotence of those coefficients.
Suppose (1) holds in an arbitrary \(B\). Choose a representation
\(f^N=P u+c v\), and first work in the finitely generated
\(\mathbb Q\)-subalgebra generated by \(a,c\) and the coefficients of
\(f,u,v\). The finitely many localized nilpotence conditions are
identities \(a^s(c f_i)^j=0\) for suitable exponents. These
identities hold in that subalgebra as well. Its Noetherian property
gives an integer \(\tau\geq1\) annihilating its \(a\)-power torsion
ideal \(T\) by \(a^\tau\), and \(T\cap a^\tau B=0\) there:
if \(a^\tau b\in T\), then \(b\in T\) and \(a^\tau b=0\).
First work with a marker over this subalgebra. The cutoff over
the original ring will then follow by specializing indeterminates
for the marker coefficients.

In the quotient by \(T\), if nonzero, \(\bar a\) is regular and
each \(\bar c\bar f_i\) is nilpotent. Thus (4) for large \(m\)
gives both a preimage of \(\overline{g f^m}\) divisible by
\(\bar a^\tau\) and divisibility of \(\overline{g f^{m-M}}\) by
\(\bar a^\tau\), where \(M=2\tau N\). With \(B\) denoting the chosen
coefficient subalgebra for now, lift to obtain
\[
 h-Dw\in T[z],\qquad g f^{m-M}=a^\tau w_0+h_0,qquad
 h=g f^m,\quad w\in a^\tau B[z],\quad h_0\in T[z].
\]
In the zero quotient take \(w=w_0=0\).
The difference \(h-Dw-f^M h_0\) lies in \(T[z]\cap a^\tau B[z]=0\).
Expanding \((f^N)^{2\tau}\) in \((P,c)^{2\tau}\) gives
\(f^M h_0\in c^\tau T[z]\) because \(P^\tau T[z]=0\).
Write \(H\) for integration with zero constant term. On \(T[z]\),
\(DH=c-PH\) and \((PH)^\tau=0\). Hence
\[
 D\left(H\sum_{j=0}^{\tau-1} c^{\tau-1-j}(PH)^j h_1\right)
   =c^\tau h_1
 \qquad(h_1\in T[z]).
\]
This proves \(h\in I\).

To see that the cutoff can be uniform in \(g\) over the original
ring, do the entire torsion argument over the polynomial extension
of the fixed Noetherian subalgebra by any finite number of
indeterminates. Its \(a\)-power torsion is generated by the old
\(T\) and has the same bound \(\tau\), by coefficient comparison.
The proof of (2) uses \(g\) only as a factor, with the same cutoff
and the same nilpotence exponent \(k\) in the quotient. Use as many
indeterminates as there are coefficients of \(g\), then specialize
them to those coefficients in the original ring. The resulting
polynomial preimages give the claimed uniformity.

For the equivalent radical description in the translated coordinate,
the first condition in (1)
implies \(f\in\sqrt{(a,c)B[z]}\) and, at \(z=0\), \(f_0^N\in cB\).
If \(c f_0\) is nilpotent in \(C\), then so is \(f_0\) because
\(f_0^{N+1}\in(c f_0)B\). Conversely, if \(f\in\sqrt{(a,c)B[z]}\)
and \(f_0\) is nilpotent in \(C\), then \(f_0^N\in(a,c)B\) for
some \(N\) and \(a^s f_0^j=0\) for some \(s,j\).
Modulo \(c\) these imply \(f_0^{sN+j}=0\). Thus
\(f\in\sqrt{(z,c)B[z]}\) as well. The intersection with
\(\sqrt{(a,c)B[z]}\) is \(\sqrt{(az^r,c)B[z]}\), as seen by
testing the prime ideals containing the product of \(a\) and
\(z^r\) modulo \(c\). The nilpotence conditions are closed under
addition and polynomial multiplication because the nilradical of
\(C\) is an ideal. Thus in the shifted case (1) is equivalently
\(f\in\sqrt{(a,c)B[z]}\), with \(f(b)\) and the coefficients of
\(cf\) nilpotent after inverting \(a\).

For example, take \(B=\mathbb Q[u,v]/(uv)\), \(a=u\), \(c=v\),
\(P=u z^r\) and \(f=v+uz\). Then \(f^m=v^m+u^m z^m\), and for
\(m\geq\max(2,r)\) this is the image of
\(v^{m-1}z-u^{m-1}z^{m-r}\). The two summands use the derivative
and multiplier terms on opposite components of the coefficient ring.
