# The Mathieu property for split rank-one homogeneous spaces

Let $K$ be any characteristic-zero field, let $G=\mathrm{SL}_{2,K}$,
and let $H\subset G$ be a closed subgroup with reductive identity
component. Then the normalized Reynolds kernel on $K[G/H]$ is
Mathieu–Zhao exactly when $H=G$. For every proper $H$, one fixed pair
of $K$-regular functions has all positive pure moments zero and
infinitely many nonzero marked moments. The same assertion holds for
split $\mathrm{PGL}_2$, by taking the inverse image of its stabilizer
under the central isogeny.

## Witness and scalar covariance

The moment inputs are Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 5.1 of
[the complex classification](../../../results/mathieu-reductive-homogeneous-spaces/main.tex).
For the three binary forms

$$
 f_4=X^2Y^2,\qquad f_8=X^8+14X^4Y^4+Y^8,\qquad
 f_{12}=XY(X^{10}+11X^5Y^5-Y^{10}),
$$

respectively put $b=n/2,\ a=b-1$ for $n=\deg f$. For the two rows
$u,v$ of a determinant-one matrix define

$$
 U=f(u),\quad F=f(v),\quad R=f(v+Fu)/F,\quad P_f=U^a R^b,\quad Q_f=F^a.
$$

The constant Taylor term of $f(v+tu)$ is $F$, so $R$ is polynomial.
The pair is invariant under simultaneous right multiplication by any
subgroup fixing $f$. For $f_4,f_8,f_{12}$ the fixing groups include
the split torus normalizer, $2O,2I$, respectively. The cited moment
formulas give nonzero marked values at every positive $m$ for $f_4$,
and when $28m-3$ or $66m-5$ is prime for $f_8$ or $f_{12}$,
respectively. These standard forms and their moments are defined over
$\mathbb Q$. Uniqueness of the normalized left-invariant functional and
base change transport each rational moment to any characteristic-zero field.

The construction works for a scalar multiple of a conjugate form.
Indeed let $f'(w)=\lambda f(wg)$ for $g\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\overline K)$
and $\lambda\in\overline K^\times$. Right translation by $g$ preserves
the trace, since it commutes with all left translations. For the scalar
calculation write $H_k^{(B)}=[t^k]f(v+tu)^B$, with $B=bm$
and $A=am$. The marked moment keeps only the summand
$U^A F^{a+k-B}H_k^{(B)}$ with $k=B-b$. The left diagonal torus
gives the summand weight $nA-(n-2)k-na$. This summand scales by
$\lambda^{A+a+k}=\lambda^{(n-1)m-1}$. The same weight argument for the
pure moment selects $k=B$, so a zero pure moment remains zero.
Consequently any such $f'$ defined over $K$ and fixed by $H$ produces
a witness over $K$ with the same nonzero marked orders.

## Choice of binary form

For each subgroup $L$ used below, the space of invariant binary forms
of degree $n$ is the kernel of the difference between the representation
coaction and the trivial coaction, so it commutes with field extension. When this
invariant space is a geometric line, a nonzero $K$-vector in it is
automatically a scalar multiple of the corresponding conjugate form.

- If $H^\circ$ has positive dimension and $H\ne G$, then $H^\circ$
  is a torus and $H\subseteq N_G(H^\circ)$. Use the quartic invariant
  line of this normalizer. Over $\overline K$, the diagonal torus
  fixes only the line spanned by $X^2Y^2$ in degree four, and the
  Weyl representative fixes that vector.
- If $H$ is finite and central, choose the split torus normalizer.
  For a noncentral geometric cyclic subgroup, its centralizer is a
  $K$-torus and contains $H$. For a geometric binary dihedral subgroup
  of order $4r$, $r\ge 3$, the cyclic subgroup of order $2r$ is
  generated by the elements of order greater than four. It is
  characteristic and hence defined over $K$. Its centralizer is a
  $K$-torus normalized by $H$. Use the quartic line in each case.
- For geometric $Q_8$, use its $K$-normalizer. Geometrically $Q_8$
  is normal in $2O$, as the preimage of the normal Klein four subgroup
  in $S_4$. Its centralizer is the center by irreducibility on the
  defining two-dimensional module. Hence its normalizer has order at most
  $2|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_8)|=48$, and equals $2O$. Use the octic
  invariant line of the normalizer.
- For geometric $2T,2O,2I$, use the respective invariant spaces in
  degrees $8,8,12$ of $H$ itself. The displayed octic is fixed by $2T$
  because $2T\subset 2O$. In all three cases the space has dimension
  one. A ratio of two independent invariant forms of degree $n$
  would define a nonconstant invariant map $\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1$
  of degree at most $n$. A generic fiber includes a full orbit of the
  projective group, of size $12,24,60$, respectively. Each size exceeds
  $n$.

The geometric finite case list is the classification in
[Flicker, pp. 214–215](https://www.ariel.ac.il/wp/yuval-flicker/wp-content/uploads/sites/316/2023/06/94.FiniteSubgpsSL2Manusc.pdf).
The pair defined by the resulting form descends as a regular pair on
$G/H$, and the quotient trace equals the restriction of the group trace
on right-invariant functions. When $H=G$ the quotient is a point and the trace is the
identity. A surjective $K$-group morphism onto split $\mathrm{SL}_2$
or $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ also pulls back the witness if the image of the
source stabilizer is proper. The induced map of homogeneous spaces
preserves normalized traces by equivariance and uniqueness.
[Homogeneous quotient traces](HOMOGENEOUS_QUOTIENT_TRACE_AND_COUNTEREXAMPLE_PULLBACK.md#2-quotient-geometry-and-trace-restriction)

## Example over the rationals

The torus of matrices with rows $(a,b),(-b,a)$ and
$a^2+b^2=1$ over $\mathbb Q$ gives the form $f=(X^2+Y^2)^2$.
With $g$ the determinant-one matrix whose columns are $(1,i)$
and $(i/2,1/2)$, one has $f(X,Y)=-4f_4((X,Y)g)$.
Thus the marked moments are $(-4)^{3m-1}$ times those of $f_4$.
The first is $8/15$, and all positive marked moments are nonzero.
