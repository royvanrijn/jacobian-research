# Boundary conditions for pure winding-one cubic sphere phases

Let \(K\) have characteristic zero. On \(2xy+t^2=1\) write a regular
phase in the Laurent chart as
\[
 f=x^{-1}\bigl(C(x)+B(x)t+D(x)t^2+E(x)t^3\bigr),
 \qquad C,B,D,E\in K[x].
\]
Assume \(E(0)\ne0\) and \(2\le\deg E\le5\). Use the normalized sphere
functional \(\Lambda\) of the
[proper volume argument](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_PROPER_VOLUME_OBSTRUCTION.md).

**Theorem.** If \(\Lambda(f^m)=0\) for all sufficiently large \(m\),
then \(E\) has a geometric root \(r\) of multiplicity at least four.
If its multiplicity is exactly four, then
\[
             (x-r)^3\mid D,\qquad (x-r)^2\mid B,\qquad C(r)=0.    \tag{1}
\]
In particular, in the horizontal unit-center cases of least degree seven,
the only remaining leading coefficients are a scalar times \((x-r)^4\)
or \((x-r)^4(x-s)\), with \(r,s\) nonzero and distinct in the latter
case. Both cases must satisfy (1).

We use the compactification and all boundary estimates of the proper
volume argument away from finite nonzero roots of \(E\) of multiplicity
at least three. The exponential period on the compact real sphere has
constant term one after normalization. To rule out a polynomial period,
regularity of the volume form over all finite phase values can be relaxed
at the central fiber as follows.

## A period obstruction at horizontal poles

**Lemma.** Let \(Y\) be a smooth irreducible complex algebraic surface, let
\(f:Y\to\mathbb A^1\) be proper and nonconstant, and let \(\omega\) be a
rational top form. Suppose at every point over \(f=0\) one of the
following holds.

- The form is regular.
- Its only local polar divisor is smooth and is given in local
  coordinates by \(v=0\), and \(f\) is a monomial in the other
  coordinate times a unit.

Then the exponential period of \(\omega\) on any compact closed cycle
disjoint from its polar divisor is either zero or has infinitely many
nonzero Taylor coefficients. The second condition allows a pole of any
order. A disjoint union of smooth horizontal simple poles can always
be put in this form by resolving the central fiber together with the
pole divisor.

For the last assertion, blow up the points needed to make the union of
the central fiber and the pole divisor have normal crossings. A blowup
of a smooth point on a single simple polar component creates no polar
exceptional component, since the Jacobian supplies one factor of the
exceptional parameter. The strict transforms of the polar components
stay smooth, disjoint and horizontal. Near one of them the normal
crossings expression for \(f\) is therefore \(h(v,z)z^b\), with \(h\)
a unit and \(b\ge1\). Absorb the unit into \(z\) on an étale chart.
Blowups in this application may all be supported on the boundary of the
affine sphere, since only the neighborhood of its polar curves is used.

To prove the lemma use the graph of \(f\) with coefficient module
\(\mathcal O_Y(*P)\), where \(P\) is the reduced polar divisor of
\(\omega\). This localization and its graph image have the rational
\(V\)-filtration along the base coordinate at zero. On the polar chart
\(f=z^b\) this is the exterior product of the localized connection in
\(v\) with the graph module of \(z^b\), and the filtration comes from
the graph factor. On the other charts it is the usual graph filtration
with regular coefficients. One can use a neighborhood of the central
fiber in these tests, since the filtration away from zero is the whole
module.

Write a top form on a polar chart as \(h(v,z)v^{-a}\,dv\wedge dz\),
with \(h\) regular and \(f=z^b\). In the Bernstein equation for the
coefficient \(v^{-a}\), differentiation in \(z\) alone gives
\[
 \partial_z^{b}\big(v^{-a}f^{s+1}\big)
 = b^b\prod_{j=1}^b(s+j/b)\,v^{-a}f^s.
\]
All the roots are negative. Thus \(v^{-a}\delta_f\) belongs to
\(V^{>0}\) in the graph module. Multiplication by \(h\) preserves
this filtration. At a regular point use the negativity of the
ordinary Bernstein polynomial and the natural map from the graph
module with regular coefficients into its localization. These local
tests put the global top-form class into \(V^{>0}\) after proper
direct image. The tests can be made in étale coordinates, or on the
analytification where the same \(V\)-filtration is obtained. The
filtration statements used here are those of Chen–Dirks–Mustaţă,
Corollary 5.15, Example 5.19 and Theorem 8.1, as in the
[proper-volume period lemma](../../../results/primitive-balanced-image-spectrum/proper-volume.tex).

The Fourier argument in that lemma now applies to the cyclic module
generated by the top-form class. On the complement of \(P\), analytic
multiplication by \(e^{-\tau f}\) after specializing the twisted
de Rham complex makes its relations integrate to zero on the given
closed cycle. A nonzero polynomial period would give a quotient of the
cyclic module onto \(\delta_0\) under inverse Fourier transform. Its
generator is in \(V^{>0}\) and the corresponding piece of
\(\delta_0\) vanishes, a contradiction.

## A triple root

Work over \(\mathbb C\), after embedding a finitely generated
coefficient field. Write \(u=t^{-1}\), \(v=x-r\), and use
\[
 L(u,v)=E(r+v)+uD(r+v)+u^2B(r+v)+u^3 C(r+v),\qquad
 f=\frac{L(u,v)}{u^3(r+v)}.
\]
Up to a regular unit and sign the volume form is \(u^{-2}du\wedge dv\).
Write \(e_j,d_j,b_j\) for the coefficients at \(v^j\) of \(E,D,B\)
in these coordinates. At a triple root \(e_3\ne0\). The following
rescalings give simple-root limiting polynomials for
\(u^{-3\alpha}L(u,u^\alpha w)\).

| First nonzero coefficient | \(\alpha\) | Limiting polynomial |
|---|---:|---|
| \(d_0\) | \(1/3\) | \(e_3w^3+d_0\) |
| \(d_1\) | \(1/2\) | \(e_3w^3+d_1w\) |
| \(b_0\) | \(2/3\) | \(e_3w^3+b_0\) |

In each row, bounded \(f\) forces \(|v|=O_M(|u|^\alpha)\). Outside
a sufficiently large disc at that scale the leading \(e_3 v^3\)
dominates the lower terms and gives \(|f|>M\). One can take an
appropriate root of \(u\) for the local rescaling. In the rescaled
disc a bounded phase imposes a sublevel condition of size
\(O_M(|u|^{3-3\alpha})\), and each root is simple. Thus the
transverse area in \(v\) is \(O_M(|u|^{2(3-2\alpha)})\). Since
\(3-2\alpha>1\) in every row, squared volume is finite.

If all three coefficients vanish, boundedness instead gives
\(|v|=O_M(|u|)\) by the same domination estimate. On the chart
\(v=uw\) of the ordinary blowup the phase is regular, and on \(u=0\)
it is
\[
 r^{-1}(e_3w^3+d_2w^2+b_1w+C(r)).
\]
The volume form has only a simple pole there and this divisor is
horizontal. Its missing point \(w=\infty\) lies over infinite phase
values. The local pole is therefore covered by the lemma, regardless
of the roots of this polynomial at phase value zero.

## A quadruple root

At a quadruple root \(e_4\ne0\). If \(d_0\ne0\) or \(d_0=0\) and
\(d_1\ne0\), scale by \(v=u^\alpha w\) with \(\alpha=1/4\) or
\(1/3\), respectively. The limit of \(u^{-4\alpha}L\) is
\(e_4w^4+d_0\) or \(e_4w^4+d_1w\), with four simple roots. As
above the bounded-phase transverse area is
\(O_M(|u|^{2(3-3\alpha)})\), giving a regular volume form over finite
phase values near this boundary.

Next let \(d_0=d_1=0\), and suppose \((d_2,b_0)\ne(0,0)\).
Now \(|v|=O_M(|u|^{1/2})\), with limiting polynomial
\(e_4w^4+d_2w^2+b_0\) in \(w=v/u^{1/2}\). For a nonzero root use
the toric chart \((v,z)\) with \(z=v^2/u\). This is a smooth
chart along the open divisor of weights \((1,2)\) in \((v,u)\).
There \(f=v^{-2}H(v,z)\), the volume form is a unit times
\(v^{-2}dv\wedge dz\), and
\[
 H(0,z)=\frac{z}{r}(e_4 z^2+d_2z+b_0).
\]
At a nonzero simple root of \(H(0,z)\), the sublevel estimate gives
area \(O_M(|v|^4)\). At a double root \(z_0\), if
\(\partial_v H(0,z_0)\ne0\), scale \(z-z_0=v^{1/2}h\) and use
the limiting simple-root quadratic. This gives area
\(O_M(|v|^3)\). Both estimates make the squared volume finite. If
\(\partial_v H(0,z_0)=0\), boundedness forces
\(|z-z_0|=O_M(|v|)\), so blow up in the chart \(z-z_0=vh\).
The bounded phase extends to a quadratic with nonzero quadratic
coefficient on the exceptional divisor, and the volume form has a
simple horizontal pole there.

The zero root in the \(w\)-chart can occur only if \(b_0=0\) and
\(d_2\ne0\), and is then double. In the region
\(|v|\le\epsilon |u|^{1/2}\) near that root, boundedness forces
\(|v|=O_M(|u|)\). Indeed if \(|v|/|u|\) is large then
\(u d_2v^2\) dominates \(E(r+v),u^2 B(r+v),u^3 C(r+v)\), using the
smallness of \(\epsilon\). In the ordinary chart \(v=uw\) the phase
on the exceptional divisor is \(r^{-1}(d_2w^2+b_1w+C(r))\); the
volume form has only a simple horizontal pole. The neighborhoods of
this divisor and the nonzero roots are disjoint over finite phase
values, as is also seen from the different rescaled root clusters.

The remaining case with \(d_0=d_1=d_2=b_0=0\) and \(b_1\ne0\)
has scale \(v=u^{2/3}w\), with limiting polynomial
\(e_4w^4+b_1w\). At nonzero roots use the toric coordinates
\(v=s^2 z,\ u=s^3 z\). The function is \(s^{-1}H(s,z)\), with
\(H(0,z)=(e_4z+b_1)/r\), and the volume form is a unit times
\(s^{-2}ds\wedge dz\). Blowing up its simple root of \(H(0,z)\)
gives a linear phase on the new divisor and a simple horizontal
pole. Near the zero cluster \(u^2 b_1v\) dominates when
\(|v|/|u|\) is large and \(|v|/|u|^{2/3}\) is small. Thus the
ordinary chart \(v=uw\) covers the bounded part of this cluster.
On its exceptional divisor the phase is \(r^{-1}(b_1w+C(r))\)
and the volume has a simple horizontal pole. This completes the
exclusion unless \(\operatorname{ord}_r D\ge3\) and
\(\operatorname{ord}_r B\ge2\).

With these two divisibilities, put \(v=s^3 z,\ u=s^4 z\). This
unimodular monomial chart includes the divisor of weights \((3,4)\)
and its crossing with the divisor of weights \((1,1)\). Writing
\(E=v^4 E_4, D=v^3 D_3, B=v^2 B_2\) gives
\[
 f=\frac{z(E_4+sD_3+s^2B_2)+C}{r+v},\qquad
 \omega=\frac{\pm 1}{(r+v)s^2 z}\, ds\wedge dz .
\]
On \(s=0\) the function is \((e_4z+C(r))/r\), while on \(z=0\)
and the ordinary chart \(v=uw\) the phase value is \(C(r)/r\).
These charts cover the bounded-phase part at the root. Indeed
\(|v|=O_M(|u|^{3/4})\) by domination. If \(v/u\) is bounded,
the ordinary chart applies; otherwise \(s=u/v\) is small and
\(z=v^4/u^3\) is bounded. The chart's missing endpoint on \(s=0\)
has \(z=\infty\), hence infinite phase value. If \(C(r)\ne0\),
the only pole over phase value zero is at \(s=0,z=-C(r)/e_4\ne0\).
Here \(f\) is a transverse coordinate along \(s=0\), so the pole
of order two also satisfies the lemma. This proves the necessity of
\(C(r)=0\).

All local models used here are ordinary or toric blowup charts of the
boundary, or estimates on the original charts. Complete them to a
smooth resolution of the pencil. On the bounded-phase regions with
finite squared volume the resolution has a regular volume form. On
the displayed bounded-phase models the pencil is already regular and
the additional resolution can be taken outside these models. The
only possible polar divisors meeting the central fiber in the cases
excluded above are the disjoint horizontal divisors already discussed.
The lemma contradicts eventual purity. Geometric roots and the
divisibilities descend to the stated characteristic-zero setting by
the coefficient-field embedding. The final assertion uses the
[leading-face reduction](MIXED_CUBIC_SEPTIC_LEADING_FACE_REDUCTION.md),
which independently excludes a sole root of multiplicity five in the
horizontal septic case.

## Consequences in degree seven

**Corollary.** A regular winding-one cubic of least sphere degree seven
with \(E(0)\ne0\) can be eventually pure only in one of the horizontal
quadruple-root cases of the theorem, with the three divisibilities
in (1). There the polynomial
\[
 g(w)=e_4w^4+d_3w^3+b_2w^2+c_1w
\]
must have at least two distinct geometric roots. Here \(e_4,d_3,b_2,c_1\)
are the indicated Taylor coefficients at \(r\). Every eventually pure
phase in this septic class is pure at every positive order. It has one
fixed marker \(x\) with infinitely many nonzero mixed moments. In
degree five the root ratio \(r/s\) differs from \(-1\) and ranges
over a finite algebraic set.

Apply the [single-root exclusion](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_SINGLE_BOUNDARY_ROOT.md)
to \(g\). To see why the pure tail suffices for purity at every order,
apply the period lemma to the form \(f\omega\). Over the central fiber,
the only old polar divisors in these two cases are in the quadruple-root
charts. In the ordinary chart \(v=uw\), \(f\) vanishes along \(u=0\)
and cancels the simple pole. In the chart \(v=s^3z, u=s^4z\) we have
\(f=z\) times a unit near \(s=z=0\), because \(C(r)=0\) and
\(\partial_z f=e_4/r\) there. Hence \(f\omega\) has no pole on
\(z=0\) and has only the pole on \(s=0\), with \(f\) a complementary
coordinate. Other boundary charts over the central fiber have a regular
form. The exponential period of \(f\omega\) is the negative derivative
of that of \(\omega\). If the latter is polynomial, the lemma forces
its derivative to be zero. Normalizing at zero gives the constant
period one.

Now use the
[quadruple-root marker calculation](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_QUADRUPLE_ROOT_MARKERS.md)
with the degree-seven bound and starting order one.

The [degree-five leading-coefficient exclusion](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_FIVE_DEGREE_CM_EXCLUSION.md)
eliminates that alternative. The current existence question is recorded
in the [research status](../../STATUS.md#moments-and-vanishing).

**Corollary (Varying cubic invariant at a quartic leading coefficient).**
In the degree-four alternative an eventually pure phase of least sphere
degree seven has nonzero discriminant and nonconstant projective cubic
invariant.

The fixed marker above makes every eventually pure phase here unsafe,
while the [constant-invariant safety theorem](CONSTANT_CUBIC_INVARIANT_ELLIPTIC_MODULI_BRIDGE.md)
makes every pure constant-invariant phase of least degree seven safe.
The discriminant-zero identity forces constant projective invariant,
including the zero pair in that theorem's definition. Thus it is excluded
as well.
