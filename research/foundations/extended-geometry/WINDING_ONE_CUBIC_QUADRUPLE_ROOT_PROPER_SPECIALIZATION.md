# Weighted specialization for quartic-leading winding-one septics

Work over a field of characteristic zero. On \(2xy+t^2=1\)
put \(U=1+x\), \(v=Ut\), and let \(\Lambda\) be the normalized
sphere functional. Consider a regular winding-one phase
\[
 f=\frac Ux\left((v^2-1)(v+d)+\sum_{j=1}^7 x^j B_j(v)\right),
 \qquad B_j(v)=a_jv+b_j,\quad a_6=a_7=0,
\]
with constant \(d\).

## Theorem (Weighted specialization for quartic-leading septics)

Suppose that \(f\) has least sphere degree seven.

1. If \(d=0,1,-1\), then infinitely many positive moments are nonzero.
2. There exists a finite set \(S\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}\),
   disjoint from \(\{0,1,-1,2\}\), such that eventual purity
   requires \(d\) algebraic with a conjugate in \(S\).

The second conclusion follows from a nonempty Zariski open of the
parameter line containing \(2\). The argument gives finiteness of
the exceptional set.

These statements apply after the
[depressed-translation normalization](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_CONSTANT_DEPRESSED_COEFFICIENTS.md#reduction-to-one-polynomial-of-the-base-variable)
if the original coefficient of \(v^2\) has degree at most two.
In that normalization \(d\) is its original value at \(x=0\).
The translation preserves least sphere degree seven in this case.
The possible degree-seven terms
before translation come from \(b_5\) and \(c_7\). The depressed
coefficient \(p_5\) equals \(b_5\), and if \(b_5=0\) then the
depressed coefficient \(q_7\) equals \(c_7\).

### The moment equations in the two charts

In degree seven, eventual purity implies vanishing at every
positive order by the
[quadruple-root boundary corollary](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_LOGARITHMIC_BOUNDARY.md#consequences-in-degree-seven).
Set \(P_0(T)=(T^2-1)(T+d)\) and
\(B(X,T)=\sum_{j=1}^7 X^jB_j(T)\). The
[fixed interval formula](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_QUADRUPLE_ROOT_SPARSE_COEFFICIENTS.md#the-fixed-interval-formula)
gives \(M_1=\Lambda(f)=b_1\), so \(b_1=0\). The general moment is
\[
 M_m=\sum_{\ell=1}^m\binom m\ell
       \sum_{k=\ell}^m\binom{m-1}{k-1}
       \frac12\int_{-1}^1 P_0(T)^{m-\ell}
                         [X^k]B(X,T)^\ell\,dT.              \tag{1}
\]
The term without tail factors vanishes by angular degree.
As in the
[third-order calculation](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_QUADRUPLE_ROOT_THIRD_ORDER_TAIL.md#moment-and-inverse-period-equations),
the first two inverse deficits yield the equations
\[
 N_2=\sum_{j=1}^5\binom{-2/3}{j-1}a_j=0,\qquad
 N_3=\sum_{j=1}^7\binom{-1/3}{j-1}(b_j-da_j/3)=0.         \tag{2}
\]
Throughout the calculation \(b_1=a_6=a_7=0\).

Write \(A=\mathbb Z_{(101)}\). All coefficients of the equations
used below belong to \(A[d]\). Indeed the interval integrals
of powers of \(T\) in (1) have denominators at most \(3m+1\),
with \(m\le16\), and the denominators in (2) are prime to \(101\).
Give \(a_j,b_j\) weight \(j\) and a new variable \(h\) weight one.
For \(M_m\), each summand indexed by \(k\) in (1) has weight \(k\).
Multiply it by \(h^{m-k}\). Homogenize \(N_2,N_3\) to weights
five and seven, respectively. These equations define a closed
subscheme \(Z\) in weighted projective space over the chosen base
\(A[d]\) or \(A\) at fixed \(d\). Its structure morphism is proper
by the finite generation and degree-zero part of the graded
coordinate algebra. [Stacks Project, Lemma 31.31.4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0800)

The chart \(h\ne0\) has coordinates \(a_j/h^j,b_j/h^j\), and
identifies the equations with (1) and (2). At \(h=0\) the inverse
equations reduce to \((110/243)a_5=0\) and \((728/6561)b_7=0\).
Both scalars are units in \(A\). The only summands retained in
the moment equations are those at \(k=m\), so the equations
there are
\[
        \frac12\int_{-1}^1 [X^m](P_0(T)+B(X,T))^m\,dT=0.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
Here \(a_5=b_7=0\). In particular, if the ideal generated by
(3) contains positive powers of every remaining variable, the
projective locus at infinity is empty.

### A reduced special fiber

The zero tail defines a section \(s=[1:0:\cdots:0]\) of \(Z\).
Suppose that in a fiber of \(Z\) the chart \(h\ne0\) is the
reduced origin and the infinity locus is empty. Write \(z\)
for the column of affine coefficient variables. At this fiber,
the affine equations generate the ideal of the origin, so their
linear parts span its cotangent space. Choose as many rows
as variables with an invertible linear minor. Over the base
these rows have the form \(C(z)z\), since each vanishes at
the zero section. The determinant of \(C(0)\) is nonzero
at the chosen base point. On \(h\ne0\) and \(\det C(z)\ne0\),
the equations force \(z=0\). This argument gives the local
generation of the ideal of the section that also follows
from Nakayama's lemma.

The complement of this open subspace in \(Z\) is closed and
proper over the base. Its image is closed and misses the
chosen base point. Over the complementary open of the base,
the whole solution space lies in the section. This open
contains every generalization of that point.

The [exact checker](verify_winding_one_cubic_proper_specialization.py)
verifies the following two reduced fibers at residue
characteristic \(101\):

| \(d\) in the residue field | Moment range |
| ---: | --- |
| 0 | \(1\le m\le12\) |
| 2 | \(1\le m\le12\) |

In each affine chart the ideal contains every coefficient
variable. In each infinity chart it contains the twentieth
power of every remaining variable. For \(d=0\), apply the
reduced-fiber argument over \(\operatorname{Spec} A\) to get
the zero tail over \(\mathbb Q\). For variable \(d\), apply it
over \(\operatorname{Spec} A[d]\). The resulting open contains
the characteristic-zero point \(d=2\) and the generic point.
The complement of its characteristic-zero restriction is a
proper closed subset of the affine line over \(\mathbb Q\).
Removing the three fixed centers excluded by the theorem gives
the finite set \(S\) of geometric points.

### The centers \(d=1\) and \(d=-1\)

For \(d=1\) use tail order one and the same proper
compactification. The checker verifies the infinity identity
at \(101\) with moments through order twelve. For the affine
chart, first use \(N_2\) to solve
\[
 a_5=-\frac{243}{110}
        (a_1-2a_2/3+5a_3/9-40a_4/81).
\]
Then solve \(M_2,\ldots,M_7\) for \(b_2,\ldots,b_7\) in order.
At \(M_j\), \(b_j\) enters only linearly, with coefficient
\(j\int_{-1}^1 P_0(T)^{j-1}\,dT/2\). The checker verifies
that all these pivots are units in \(A\). Each solved polynomial
has zero constant term. The residual equations have the four
free variables \(a_1,\ldots,a_4\).

Modulo \(101\), the residual ideal of \(N_3,M_8,\ldots,M_{12}\)
contains the tenth powers of all four free variables. Hence the
special projective fiber has support the zero tail. To control
the lifting of this possibly nonreduced point, the checker
computes the total-degree-three jets of the exact residual
equations \(N_3,M_8,\ldots,M_{16}\) over \(\mathbb Q\). Their
constant and linear parts vanish exactly. It row-reduces their
quadratic coefficients over \(A\), using four unit pivots,
and verifies the exact cancellation of the quadratic part
of every remaining row. The resulting initial forms are the
quadratic parts of the pivot rows and the cubic parts of
the remaining rows. They are leading forms of actual equations
obtained by the same \(A\)-linear combinations of the residual
polynomials. Their reductions generate an ideal that again
contains the tenth power of each free variable.

If a nonzero tail satisfied the equations in characteristic zero,
a specialization of its finitely generated coefficient algebra
to a number field would preserve the equations and a nonzero
coefficient. This uses localization at that coefficient before
taking a maximal ideal. At a place over \(101\), the associated
number-field point extends to the valuation ring by properness.
Its special point is the zero tail, which lies in \(h\ne0\).
Thus all its affine coefficients lie in the maximal ideal.
If every free variable is zero then the solved coefficients
are zero. Otherwise, let \(\pi\) be a uniformizer and let
\(e>0\) be the smallest valuation among the free variables.
Divide them by \(\pi^e\). Their residues form a nonzero vector.
For a residual row combination of order \(j=2\) or \(3\),
divide the equation by \(\pi^{je}\) before reduction.
Its initial form then vanishes on this vector. The verified
power memberships rule this out. Finally,
\(f(x,t)\mapsto-f(x,-t)\) preserves moment vanishing and sends
\(d\) to \(-d\), giving the conclusion at \(-1\).

Run the checker from the repository root with Python 3 and
Singular 4 on the path.

```sh
python3 research/foundations/extended-geometry/verify_winding_one_cubic_proper_specialization.py
```

The checker uses rational interval coefficients and exact
standard-basis reductions. It also checks the
