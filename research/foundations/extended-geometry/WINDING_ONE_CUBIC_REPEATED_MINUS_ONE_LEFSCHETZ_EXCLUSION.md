# Lefschetz exclusion at a double cubic root over \(x=-1\)

Use the normalized quartic-leading winding-one septic and the
depressed cubic \(Y^3+p(x)Y+q(x)\) of the
[Lefschetz exclusion](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_QUADRUPLE_ROOT_LEFSCHETZ_EXCLUSION.md).
Put \(u=x/(1+x)\) and \(z=1/u\). Write
\[
 P(z,Y)=Y^3+p(1/(z-1))Y+q(1/(z-1)).
\]
The finite fibers near \(z=0\) are \(zP(z,Y)=\zeta\), with period
form \(\alpha=-Y\,dz/z\).

**Theorem RepeatedMinusOneLefschetzExclusion.** Suppose that
\(d(0)^2\ne1\), the interior critical points are Morse with
distinct nonzero critical values, and the model and large-circle
connectivity at \(u=1\) are given by one of the
[smooth pole cases](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_REPEATED_CENTER_LEFSCHETZ_EXCLUSION.md).
Suppose that \(P(0,Y)=(Y-r)^2(Y+2r)\) with \(r\ne0\).
Define the analytic critical branch \(h(z)\) by
\(P_Y(z,h(z))=0\), \(h(0)=r\), and write
\[
 P(z,h(z))=az+\beta z^2+O(z^3).
\]
If
\[
 a\ne0,\qquad
 G=\frac{h'(0)}{2r}+\frac{\beta}{4a}+\frac{5a}{4(3r)^3}\ne0,
 \tag{1}
\]
the positive sphere moments do not vanish eventually. The
conditions hold on a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the
double-root locus in each of the three pole strata.

The base change below yields an elliptic component attached at two
points to the rest of the degenerate curve. The analogous stable
replacement is discussed for a tacnodal plane quartic by
[Casalaina-Martin, §11.5](https://www.claymath.org/library/proceedings/cmip018c.pdf).
Here the local model and the period coefficients are computed directly.

## The finite model near value zero

Embed the finitely generated coefficient field into \(\mathbb C\).
The charts away from \(z=0\) and the nonseparating inverse-branch
circle at large value are the same as in the original arguments.
Over \(z=0\), the graph \(\zeta=zP(z,Y)\) is smooth at finite
\(Y\). At \(Y=\infty\), the coordinate \(h_\infty=1/Y\) gives
\[
 \zeta h_\infty^3=z(1+p(1/(z-1))h_\infty^2+
                         q(1/(z-1))h_\infty^3).
\]
The derivative in \(z\) is a unit at \(z=h_\infty=0\), so the graph
is smooth there as well. For a nonzero finite \(\zeta\), the
section at \(h_\infty=0\) has zero residue for \(\alpha\), since
\(z=\zeta h_\infty^3(1+O(h_\infty^2))\).

The rational component \(z=0\) of the zero fiber meets the rest
at the simple root \(-2r\) and at the double root \(r\).
At the simple root the two branches make an ordinary node.
Let \(\delta_s\) be its oriented vanishing cycle. Along a fixed
small \(z\)-circle lifted to the root near \(-2r\),
\(\int_{\delta_s}\alpha\) has a nonzero limit as \(\zeta\to0\),
namely \(-2\pi i(-2r)\) for positive orientation in \(z\).

At the double root, set \(\phi(z)=P(z,h(z))\) and
\(w_0=(Y-h(z))\sqrt{3h(z)+(Y-h(z))}\), taking either local
square root with nonzero initial value. Then
\(P=w_0^2+\phi(z)\). Set
\[
 \xi=z\sqrt{\frac{\phi(z)}{az}},\qquad
 w=w_0\sqrt{\frac{z}{\xi}},
\]
choosing the two units with constant term one. The exact analytic
equation is
\[
                   \zeta=\xi(w^2+a\xi).
 \tag{2}
\]
Completing the square in \(\xi\) gives the isolated \(A_3\) model
\(a(\xi+w^2/(2a))^2-w^4/(4a)\).

After \(\zeta=s^4\) put \(\xi=s^2Z\), \(w=sW\).
The smooth scaled curve is
\[
             Z(W^2+aZ)=1,qquad R=ZW,qquad R^2=Z-aZ^3 .
 \tag{3}
\]
Its compact completion is an elliptic curve. The two points where
\(W\) has a pole are \(Z=0\) and \(Z=\infty\), both simple poles.
For small \(s\ne0\), the local smoothing in a fixed disk in \(w\)
is (3) with neighborhoods of these two points removed. Indeed the
two solutions \(\xi\) of (2) stay in a small coordinate
neighborhood for all sufficiently small \(w,\zeta\), and
\(|w|\le\epsilon\) is precisely \(|W|\le\epsilon/|s|\).
Its first homology maps onto elliptic homology with kernel
generated by either one of the two boundary circles.

The chart at either attachment to (3) can be written
\[
 t=s/w,qquad \xi=w^2 L(t),\qquad L(1+aL)=t^4 .
\]
The branches \(L(0)=0,-1/a\) are simple. On each chart the new
smoothing parameter is \(s=wt\), so both attachments contribute
one positive twist on going around \(s=0\). The branch \(L(0)=0\)
attaches to the rational component. The simple node at \(-2r\)
contributes four positive twists after the same base change.
Outside these neighborhoods the fibration extends over the
parameter disk with no local variation.

## Orthogonality at the four local vanishing directions

Suppose that the inverse-branch period is zero, as required by
the [inverse-period criterion](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_QUADRUPLE_ROOT_INVERSE_PERIOD.md)
under eventual purity. Denote its transported circle by \(\gamma\),
and let \(T\) be the monodromy around \(\zeta=0\). The two
attachment circles of (3) have opposite compact classes. The
circle on the rational component side has compact class
\(\pm\delta_s\), because together with the simple-node circle it
bounds the smoothed part of that component. The corresponding
period identities hold as well. The elliptic region contains no
poles, while the only puncture of the rational region is the section
at \(Y=\infty\), where the residue was shown to vanish.

The variation under \(T^4\), integrated against \(\alpha\), is
therefore, up to the single intersection convention,
\[
            6\langle\gamma,\delta_s\rangle
                                      \int_{\delta_s}\alpha .
\]
The factor six is the sum of the two attachment twists and the
four twists at the simple node. The zero period and the nonzero
limit of the simple-node period force
\(\langle\gamma,\delta_s\rangle=0\). Thus \(\gamma\) also pairs to
zero with the boundary of the local elliptic region.

For the two elliptic directions, calculate the coefficients of
\(s\) and \(s^3\) in the period of \(\alpha\) on a fixed compact
cycle of (3), away from its two punctures. Put
\(c_1=(3r)^{-1/2}\) and \(c_3=5/(8(3r)^{7/2})\).
The inverse of \(w_0=(Y-h)\sqrt{3h+(Y-h)}\) has linear
coefficient \((3h)^{-1/2}\) and cubic coefficient
\(5/(8(3h)^{7/2})\). Also
\[
 z=\xi-\frac{\beta}{2a}\xi^2+O(\xi^3),\qquad
 w_0=w\left(1+\frac{\beta}{4a}\xi+O(\xi^2)\right).
\]
It follows that the two odd coefficient forms of \(\alpha\) are
\[
 -c_1 W\frac{dZ}{Z},\qquad
 -\left(K ZW+c_3 W^3\right)\frac{dZ}{Z},\qquad
 K=-c_1\left(\frac{h'(0)}{2r}+\frac{\beta}{4a}\right).
 \tag{4}
\]
The expansions are uniform on the chosen cycles. On the core
curve, write \(\eta=dZ/R\). Differentiating
\(R/Z,R,R/Z^2\) gives the identities modulo exact differentials
\[
 [W\,dZ/Z]=-2a[Z\eta],\qquad
 [W\,dZ]=\frac23[\eta],\qquad
 [W^3\,dZ/Z]=-\frac{4a}{3}[\eta].
\]
Thus (4) gives nonzero multiples of \([Z\eta]\) and \([\eta]\)
respectively, by (1). These classes are independent in elliptic
de Rham cohomology. The first is a second-kind differential with
a single double pole at infinity and the second is holomorphic.
A primitive of a combination with nonzero first coefficient
would have a single simple pole, which is impossible on a
positive-genus compact curve. A holomorphic exact differential
on such a curve is zero.

Monodromy can be taken to be the identity outside the disjoint
local neighborhoods. Consequently \((T-1)\gamma\) is represented
there by local cycles, with the simple-node contribution already
zero. A boundary circle of the local elliptic region is represented
on a fixed small \(w\)-circle by an analytic branch in \(\zeta\).
Its period is analytic in \(\zeta\), even on the branch where
\(z\) tends to zero, since that branch has
\(z=\zeta\cdot(\text{analytic unit in }w,\zeta)\) on the circle.
Hence boundary periods cannot contribute to the coefficients
of \(s\) and \(s^3\). The vanishing period of
\((T-1)\gamma\) and (4) imply that its projection to elliptic
homology is zero. Its representative in the local elliptic
region is therefore a multiple of the boundary class.

On elliptic homology the monodromy is the automorphism of (3)
induced by \((Z,W)\mapsto(-Z,-iW)\) or its inverse, from changing
the chosen fourth root \(s\). Its square is the elliptic
hyperelliptic involution, which acts as \(-1\). Thus
\(T^{-1}-1\) is invertible on the elliptic quotient. The boundary
class pairs to zero with every local cycle. By symplecticity,
for any local cycle \(\delta\),
\[
 0=\langle (T-1)\gamma,\delta\rangle
   =\langle\gamma,(T^{-1}-1)\delta\rangle .
\]
Combined with the boundary orthogonality, this proves that
\(\gamma\) is orthogonal to the whole local vanishing homology.

## Compact spanning and the parameter conditions

For other finite critical values the ordinary node argument of the
Lefschetz exclusion applies. The original total space is still
smooth and rational, so the same logarithmic spanning argument
shows that a general compact fiber has zero image in the total
space's first homology. For the handle argument, make a small
local perturbation of the \(A_3\) normal form
\(a(\xi+w^2/(2a))^2-w^4/(4a)\) by adding \(\varepsilon w\).
Its three critical points are Morse with distinct values,
since their \(w\)-coordinates satisfy \(w^3=a\varepsilon\)
and their critical values are \(3\varepsilon w/4\).
Use a cutoff supported near the double-root chart. For small
\(\varepsilon\) the differential stays nonzero on the cutoff
annulus. On a fixed small reference fiber the local cycles stay
within the original smoothing region under the resulting smooth
identifications. The disk handle description now shows that
the interior node cycles, the simple-node cycle at zero, and
the local cycles of the double-point smoothing span compact
homology. The span is orthogonal to \(\gamma\), a contradiction
to the nonseparating inverse-branch circle.

Finally the repeated-root condition can be parametrized by
\(r\ne0\) and the two prescribed values
\(p(-1)=-3r^2\), \(q(-1)=2r^3\). Each pole stratum is
irreducible under this parametrization. At fixed \(r\) the
first jets of \(p,q\) and the second jet of \(q\) at \(z=0\)
can be varied independently of those two values and the top
coefficients, using lower variations divisible by \(x(1+x)\).
In particular, one can take \(h'(0)=0\), \(\beta=0\), and
\(a\ne0\), making \(G\ne0\). The interior Morse and distinct
nonzero-value guards hold on a dense open by the same jet
incidences as in
[GenericQuarticLeadingSepticExclusion](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_QUADRUPLE_ROOT_GENERIC_EXCLUSION.md#existence-of-a-dense-open-exclusion).
In this restricted parameter space the needed variations of
\(f\) are \((1+x)^2\) times the same jet polynomials, since
the coefficient variations now carry one additional factor of
\(1+x\). They preserve the repeated root and still give all
the required jets at interior points. This verifies nonempty
applicability of (1) on each of the three strata.

**Corollary SimultaneousRepeatedBoundariesExclusion.** The same
nonvanishing holds with \(d(0)^2=1\) if the center value
\(\zeta_c\) is nonzero, distinct from the interior critical
values, and the center has order one or two in the sense of the
[first-order](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_REPEATED_CENTER_LEFSCHETZ_EXCLUSION.md)
or [second-order center calculation](WINDING_ONE_CUBIC_SECOND_ORDER_CENTER_LEFSCHETZ_EXCLUSION.md).

The blowup at the center is disjoint from the model at \(z=0\),
so the normalized finite surface remains rational and the
large-circle connectivity is unchanged. The condition
\(\zeta_c\ne0\) keeps the center period calculation separate
from the double and simple points over \(z=0\).
The corresponding center calculation supplies orthogonality
to all local cycles there, and the proof above supplies it at
value zero and at every interior critical value. Perturbing
in disjoint neighborhoods for the handle count includes each
of these local cycles in the same compact spanning argument.
