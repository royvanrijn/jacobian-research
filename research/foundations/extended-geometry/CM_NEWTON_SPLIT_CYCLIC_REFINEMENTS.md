# Selective nonvanishing at split primes from a larger cyclic action

A prime can split the cyclic coefficient field used in a sphere
moment and still permute the finer eigenspaces of a larger deck
action. In the family below the finer lines come from a three-point
cover. Frobenius fixes the only coherent line used by the moment
and kills other mixed eigenlines at the same primes. Both the
connected conductor and the dimension of its primitive Jacobian
factor grow.

## Finer characters at a split prime

Take integers \(R\ge2\), \(A,B>0\), and \(n>R(A+B)\) with
\(\gcd(n,R)=1\). Put \(a=RA\), \(b=RB\), and \(e=A+B\), and
suppose \(\gcd(n,b,e)=1\). Consider the normalized cyclic cover
\[
                    W^n=z^{n-b}(z^R-c)^e,
                    \qquad c\ne0.
\]
It is connected by the gcd assumption. It also has a presentation
with deck group of order \(Rn\) on a three-point base. In fact
\(u=z^R\) gives
\[
                    W^{Rn}=u^{n-b}(u-c)^{Re}.
\]
Conversely \(z^{n-b}=W^n/(u-c)^e\), and \(\gcd(R,n-b)=1\), so
\(z\) belongs to the field generated by \(u,W\). The original
deck generator is the \(R\)-th power of the generator in the
three-point model. The latter has branch exponents
\(n-b,Re,(R-1)n-a\). It is a cyclic Fermat quotient, and the
characterwise computation below uses that extra deck action.
[Aoki, §1](https://rikkyo.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/16621)

For a primitive character \(i\) modulo \(n\), set \(x=i/n\). Its
finer characters are the indices \(\ell\bmod Rn\) with
\(\ell\equiv i\bmod n\), parametrized by \(t=\ell\bmod R\).
The three branch exponents are nonzero modulo \(n\), hence each
of these finer characters sees all three branches. Its coherent
dimension, in the convention that deck action on \(W\) has
character one, is the bit
\[
  h_x(t)=-1+\{t/R-Bx\}+\{ex\}+\{-t/R-Ax\}\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Indeed the coherent dimension in character \(\ell\) is minus one
plus the sum of the three fractional branch contributions. Writing
\(\ell=i+jn\), the first term is the fractional part of
\(\ell/R-B(x+j)\), the second of \(e(x+j)\), and the third of
\((R-1)\ell/R-A(x+j)\). The finer character and its conjugate
have coherent dimensions summing to one.
[Bouw, Lemma 4.3 and §5](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1017513122376)

**Theorem (Stable rank under the finer deck action).** At a good prime \(p\equiv1\pmod n\) with
\(p\nmid R\), absolute coherent Frobenius preserves the original
character \(i\) and acts on its finer characters by
\(t\mapsto pt\pmod R\). Its stable rank in character \(i\) is the
sum of the lengths of those cycles on which every bit \(h_x\) is
one. A cycle containing a zero bit contributes zero.

For the nonzero step, let a finer source and target both have
coherent dimension one. With \(N=Rn\), write \(e_0=n-b\),
\(e_1=Re\), and put \(r_j=\{\ell e_j/N\}\) for \(j=0,1\).
The usual finite eigensheaf generator is
\(W^\ell/[u^{\lfloor\ell e_0/N\rfloor}(u-c)^{\lfloor\ell e_1/N\rfloor}]\),
and the coherent line is represented by its quotient by \(u\).
Frobenius multiplies the target generator by
\(u^g(u-c)^h/u^p\), where \(g=\lfloor pr_0\rfloor\) and
\(h=\lfloor pr_1\rfloor\). Thus its entry is the coefficient at
\(u^{p-1}\) in \(u^g(u-c)^h\). The source dimension one gives
\(r_0+r_1>1\), and the fractional parts at the target each lie
between zero and one. Hence \(g+h>p-2\), so
\(0\le p-1-g\le h<p\). The entry is a nonzero binomial term.
This proves the rule, since a cycle of nonzero maps of lines is
invertible, whereas a step through dimension zero kills the return.
The stable rank of coherent Frobenius gives the \(p\)-rank, and
the same statement on a deck summand gives the contribution of
that summand.
[Bouw, §5](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1017513122376)
· [Lin–Mantovan–Singhal, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09632v1#S2.SS4)

The selected character one always has the bit one at \(t=0\) and
bit zero elsewhere, since \(Re<n\). Its unique coherent line
therefore survives at every good split prime. The complementary
character has the opposite bits on the conjugate labels, so the
selected conjugate pair is ordinary. The rule can still kill
lines in other mixed pairs. The construction uses a larger
commuting cyclic action and rank-one lifts. The three-point
deck action has multiplicity at most one on first cohomology in
every character, since a character sees at most three branches.
Its rational group algebra on the Jacobian therefore has dimension
twice the genus, giving CM. This is a different setting from
the [split trace-norm test](CM_NEWTON_INFINITE_MOMENT_FAMILIES.md#selective-certification-by-a-split-trace-norm).

## A growing connected family with a failed mixed pair

**Corollary (Selective mixed-pair failure).** Take \(n=2^k\ge16\), \(R=3\), \(A=2\), \(B=1\), and
\(e=3\). The connected model is
\[
                       W^n=z^{n-3}(z^3-c)^3 .
\]
At good primes with \(p\equiv1\pmod n\) and \(p\equiv2\pmod3\),
the finer label zero is fixed and the two other finer labels are
interchanged. The bits on the six intervals below are obtained by
substitution in the displayed formula. A primitive \(i\) lies on
none of the endpoints.

| Interval for \(x=i/n\) | \((h_x(0),h_x(1),h_x(2))\) | Stable rank |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| \((0,1/6)\) | \((1,0,0)\) | 1 |
| \((1/6,1/3)\) | \((1,0,1)\) | 1 |
| \((1/3,1/2)\) | \((0,1,0)\) | 0 |
| \((1/2,2/3)\) | \((1,1,0)\) | 1 |
| \((2/3,5/6)\) | \((0,0,1)\) | 0 |
| \((5/6,1)\) | \((0,1,1)\) | 2 |

For example \(i=n/4+1\) is a unit with \(i/n\in(1/6,1/3)\).
Its coherent dimension is two and its stable rank is one. The
conjugate character has coherent dimension one and stable rank
zero. Thus a mixed pair fails at the same primes where the
selected character one survives. There are \(n/4\) primitive
conjugate pairs altogether. Each contributes one to the \(p\)-rank
except an outer pair, with smaller label \(i<n/6\), which contributes
three. Counting the odd positive integers below \(n/6\) gives the
exact primitive \(p\)-rank
\[
                          \frac n4+2\left\lfloor\frac{n/6+1}{2}\right\rfloor
\]
in dimension \(3n/4\). Both the surviving and failing character
conditions hold for every good such prime. Their rational prime set
has natural density \(1/(2\varphi(n))\), or density one half
relative to the rational primes split modulo \(n\).

## Sphere moments at those primes

**Theorem (Nonzero sphere moments).** Fix a phase over a characteristic-zero field with highest literal
weight \(j+2i=n\) and top angular polynomial
\(z^{-3}(z^3-c)^3\). All strictly lower-weight terms are arbitrary.
At a good coefficient place above a prime of the indicated class,
put \(m=(p-1)/n\). Angular-constant height at order \(m\) is at
most \(p-1\). The unique interval denominator divisible by \(p\)
is the top denominator, so
\[
       p\mathcal A(f^m)\equiv[z^{3m}](z^3-c)^{3m}
             =(-c)^{2m}\binom{3m}{m}\ne0\pmod p.
\]
Here \(3m<p\) and \(c\) is a unit at a good place. This is the
same selected coherent scalar as in the split-prime rule.
A fixed number-field specialization preserving \(c\ne0\) and the
leading face gives the result over a general characteristic-zero
field, with only finitely many extra rational primes excluded.

For instance the regular lift
\[
       8c^3 y^3t^n+3c^2t^n-3cx^3t^{n-6}+x^6t^{n-12}
\]
has least sphere degree \(n+3\) by its normal form. Every phase with
the prescribed face is an absolute nonpower even in the sphere
function field. In the coordinates \(z=x/t^2\), its order at
\(t=\infty\) is \(-n\), and its leading angular function has
order \(-3\) at \(z=0\). Both orders would be divisible by any
proper power exponent, contrary to \(\gcd(n,3)=1\).
