# Projective line discriminants at residue-prime torsion levels

The residual projective line discriminant takes the same value
for a nonordinary factor and a wildly ramified ordinary factor.
This equality persists at every residue-prime torsion level.
The determinant double cover separates them, with a normalized
difference that decreases with the level.

## The exact line and determinant-cover exponents

Let \(K/\mathbb Q_p\) be finite unramified, with \(p\) odd.
Let \(G\) be a dimension-one, height-two \(p\)-divisible group
over \(\mathcal O_K\) with cyclotomic determinant. At a split
degree-one good prime of an abelian variety with exact real
multiplication, each coefficient factor is of this form away
from the polarization degree and the order index.

Let \(\mathcal L_n\) be the Galois set of cyclic subgroups of
order \(p^n\) in its geometric \(p^n\)-torsion. These are the free
rank-one summands, and \(M_n=|\mathcal L_n|=p^{n-1}(p+1)\).
Let \(\mathcal L_n^+\) be its product with the two-element set
acted on by the quadratic character of the mod-\(p\) determinant,
with diagonal Galois action. For a finite Galois set \(S\), write
\(a(S)=v_p(\operatorname{disc}(B_S/K))/|S|\), where \(B_S\) is
its finite étale algebra, the discriminant uses the maximal
order and \(v_p(p)=1\). This quantity is unchanged by
unramified base change, which commutes with maximal orders
and discriminants.

**Theorem.** Put
\[
 A_n=n-\frac{1}{p-1}+\frac{2p^{1-n}}{p^2-1}.
\]
If \(G\) is supersingular, then \(a(\mathcal L_n)=a(\mathcal L_n^+)=A_n\).
If \(G\) is ordinary, trivialize its étale quotient at level
\(p^n\) by unramified base change. Write its extension parameter
as a unit \(q\in1+p\mathcal O_{K'}\) modulo \(p^n\)-th powers.
Let \(s\in[0,n]\) be the largest exponent for which \(q\) is a
\(p^s\)-th power in \(1+p\mathcal O_{K'}\), and put \(t=n-s\).
Then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 a(\mathcal L_n)&= A_n-\frac{2(p^n-p^t)}{(p-1)M_n},\\
 a(\mathcal L_n^+)&=a(\mathcal L_n)+\frac1{M_n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
An ordinary factor with nontrivial wild image on \(G[p]\)
has \(t=n\) at every level. Its projective line discriminant
equals the supersingular discriminant at every level. Their
determinant-cover exponents differ by \(1/M_n\).

When the global coefficient image is full at level \(p^n\), these
sets give global fields of degree \(M_n\) and \(2M_n\). The
general linear group acts transitively on lines and the determinant
character is surjective on a line stabilizer. At \(n=1\) the
formulas recover the
[residual line comparison](CM_NEWTON_RESIDUE_PRIME_TORSION_RAMIFICATION.md#a-degree-2p1-field-for-each-factor).

## Primitive formal points and supersingular lines

**Lemma.** Let \(x_n\) be the coordinate of a point of exact
order \(p^n\) in a one-dimensional formal group of finite height
\(h\) over \(\mathcal O_K\). Put \(Q=p^h\). Then \(K(x_n)/K\)
is totally ramified of degree \(D_n=(Q-1)Q^{n-1}\), and its
different has normalized valuation \(n-1/(Q-1)\).

The series \([p](X)/X\) has constant \(p\), all positive
coefficients below degree \(Q-1\) divisible by \(p\), and a unit
at degree \(Q-1\). Its lower Newton polygon gives
\(v_p(x_1)=1/(Q-1)\). If \([p](x_n)=x_{n-1}\) and \(n\ge2\),
one must have \(v_p(x_n)<1/Q\), since otherwise every term of
\([p](x_n)\) has valuation at least one. At valuation below
\(1/Q\) the degree-\(Q\) term is strictly dominant. Induction
gives \(v_p(x_n)=1/D_n\). This uses the usual height argument
for the valuation of primitive points.
[Smith, Theorem 4.4 and Corollary 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.04809)

Let \(P_j\) be the monic distinguished polynomial of \([p^j](X)\)
under Weierstrass preparation, with \(P_0=X\). The quotient
\(P_n/P_{n-1}\) has degree \(D_n\) and precisely the primitive
roots. The denominator \(D_n\) of the valuation forces the
ramification index of \(K(x_n)\) to be at least \(D_n\).
Thus this quotient is the minimal polynomial and is Eisenstein.
The invariant differential \(\omega=f(X)dX\) satisfies
\([p^n]^*\omega=p^n\omega\) with \(f\) a unit. Hence
\([p^n]'(x_n)\) has valuation \(n\). In differentiating the
distinguished factorization at \(x_n\), the contribution of
\(P_{n-1}(x_n)\) has valuation
\(v_p([p^{n-1}](x_n))=1/(Q-1)\). The derivative of the
Eisenstein minimal polynomial now gives the different.

For a supersingular height-two group, each line is generated by
a primitive formal point. Its point field has \((p-1)p^{n-1}\)
automorphisms \([a]\), for units \(a\bmod p^n\), since all the
primitive points are conjugate and \([a](x_n)\) is in \(K(x_n)\).
The fixed field is the line field. The point field is generated
integrally by its uniformizer \(x_n\), also over the line field.
The contribution of \(a\ne1\) to the relative different is
\[
 D_n\,v_p([a](x_n)-x_n)=p^{2v_p(a-1)}.
\]
Formal subtraction changes the difference of coordinates by
a unit, and \([a-1](x_n)\) has order \(p^{n-v_p(a-1)}\).
The sum over the nonidentity units is
\[
 (p-2)p^{n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(p-1)p^{n-k-1}p^{2k}
                  =p^{n-1}(p^n-2).
\]
Subtracting this sum divided by \(D_n\) from the normalized
point different proves \(a(\mathcal L_n)=A_n\). The line
field is totally ramified of degree \(M_n\).

## The ordinary line orbits

Work over the finite unramified \(K'\) where the étale quotient
of \(G[p^n]\) is constant. The connected part is then
\(\mu_{p^n}\): its Cartier dual is étale and its generic
character is trivial by the determinant hypothesis.
The fiber over a generator of the étale quotient is a
\(\mu_{p^n}\)-torsor. The fppf Kummer sequence and
\(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_{K'})=0\) represent it by
\(X^{p^n}=q\) for a unit \(q\). The Teichmüller part can be
removed because it is a \(p^n\)-th power. The \(p\)-adic logarithm
on \(1+p\mathcal O_{K'}\) shows
\(s=\min(n,v_p(q-1)-1)\), with \(s=n\) when \(q=1\).
This is independent of the representative and of further
unramified enlargement. At level \(p\) the parameter is the
same unit modulo \(p\)-th powers, since multiplication by
\(p^{n-1}\) sends the generator torsor to its pushout along
\(\mu_{p^n}\to\mu_p\). The
[residual Kummer argument](CM_NEWTON_RESIDUE_PRIME_TORSION_RAMIFICATION.md#the-full-torsion-field)
shows that \(s=0\) is equivalent to nontrivial wild image
at level \(p\).

In the chart where the étale coordinate is a unit, a line
has a unique generator with that coordinate one. This chart
is the set of roots of \(X^{p^n}-q\). If \(t>0\), write
\(q=q_0^{p^s}\) with \(v_p(q_0-1)=1\). One factor is
\(X^{p^t}-q_0\), Eisenstein after translating by one, of
degree \(p^t\) and normalized different \(t\). For
\(j=1,\ldots,s\) the remaining factors are
\[
 P_j(X)=
 \frac{X^{p^{t+j}}-q_0^{p^j}}{X^{p^{t+j-1}}-q_0^{p^{j-1}}}.
\]
A root is \(q_0^{1/p^t}\) times a primitive \(p^{t+j}\)-th
root of unity. The two units differ from one with valuations
\(1/p^t\) and \(1/\varphi(p^{t+j})\), respectively. The latter
is strictly smaller. As the degree is \(\varphi(p^{t+j})\),
the factor is Eisenstein after translation. The denominator
in the quotient expression at a root has valuation \(1/(p-1)\),
so its derivative gives normalized different \(t+j-1/(p-1)\).
If \(t=0\), the chart is the set of \(p^n\)-th roots of
unity up to a \(K'\)-scalar, with the same list interpreted
as one linear field and the cyclotomic shells. The first
chart thus has one field of degree \(p^t\), and one of
degree \(\varphi(p^\ell)\) for each \(\ell=t+1,\ldots,n\).

The other chart has lines \([1:y]\) with
\(y\in p\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\). The line \(y=0\) is fixed.
For \(v_p(y)=k\), \(1\le k\le n-1\), put \(m=n-k\) and
use the unit parameter \(b=(y/p^k)^{-1}\bmod p^m\).
If the upper triangular action on the first chart is
\(a\mapsto\chi a+c\), the action here is
\(b\mapsto\chi b+p^kc\bmod p^m\). Here \(\chi\) is the
cyclotomic character and \(c\) the Kummer translation for
the chosen root of \(X^{p^n}=q\). These \(b\)'s correspond
to the roots of
\[
     \frac{X^{p^m}-q^{p^k}}{X^{p^{m-1}}-q^{p^{k-1}}}.
\]
The full affine set has coordinates \(\eta\zeta_{p^m}^b\),
where \(\eta\) is the chosen \(p^n\)-th root of \(q\)
raised to \(p^{2k}\). The denominator selects \(p\mid b\).
One has \(v_p(\eta-1)\ge p^{-\max(m-k,0)}\): for \(m>k\)
compare the terms of \((1+Y)^{p^{m-k}}-q\), and for \(m\le k\)
use \(\eta\in1+p\mathcal O_{K'}\).
Since \(k\ge1\), this bound exceeds \(1/\varphi(p^m)\).
Each translated root of the quotient has valuation
\(1/\varphi(p^m)\). The same Eisenstein and derivative
argument gives a totally ramified field of degree
\(\varphi(p^m)\) and normalized different \(m-1/(p-1)\).

The two charts and the fixed line give the ordinary exponent
\[
 \frac{p^t t +\sum_{\ell=t+1}^{n}\varphi(p^\ell)(\ell-1/(p-1))
              +\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\varphi(p^\ell)(\ell-1/(p-1))}
      {M_n}.
\]
At \(t=n\) the numerator is \(M_n A_n\), by
\(\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\varphi(p^\ell)=p^j-1\). Replacing \(t=0\)
by general \(t\) adds \(2(p^t-1)/(p-1)\) to the numerator:
this is the change when the first \(t\) cyclotomic shells
and their linear field are replaced by a degree-\(p^t\)
field of normalized different \(t\). This proves the line formula.

For the determinant cover, the extra quadratic character
corresponds over \(K'\) to a tamely ramified quadratic extension.
All the line fields in the calculation are totally ramified.
On a line field of even degree the extra character becomes
unramified or trivial. On one of odd degree it remains tamely
ramified and contributes \(1/(2M_n)\) to the cover's normalized
discriminant, by transitivity of the different. The parity
also follows by representing the quadratic extension by the
square root of a unit times \(p\). There are exactly two
odd-degree fields in the ordinary calculation, of degrees
\(p^t\) and one, and none in the supersingular case. This
proves the double-cover formula.

**Proposition.** The ordinary projective line algebra over
the original \(K\) is the product of \(n+s+1\) totally
ramified fields. The supersingular line algebra is one
totally ramified field. In the ordinary determinant cover,
the quadratic extension ramifies over each odd-degree field
and splits over each even-degree field.

The two charts and \(v_p(y)\) in the second chart are
invariant under the decomposition action. In the first
chart the inertia orbit degrees \(p^t\) and
\(\varphi(p^{t+j})\) for \(1\le j\le s\) are distinct.
Thus each inertia orbit in each chart is stable under the
decomposition action, and the previously computed fields
descend with the same degrees and with residue degree one.
In the second chart, fixing the unit parameter modulo \(p\)
forces the diagonal ratio to be one modulo \(p\), because
the translation there is divisible by \(p\). The ratio has
the same squareclass as the determinant. In the first chart,
choose the affine origin in the inertia orbit of degree
\(p^t\), which is \(p^s\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) under the
Kummer parametrization. Its stability forces the translation
of every decomposition element to be divisible by \(p^s\).
To fix an element \(a\) of the remaining shells, which have
\(v_p(a)<s\), the diagonal ratio must then reduce to one
modulo \(p\). Thus the quadratic character is trivial on
the stabilizers of all even-degree ordinary orbits. The
odd-degree assertion follows from the parity computation
above.

At level \(p\), the single-factor tame degrees and normalized
differents also agree with the elliptic-curve calculations of
[Ozeki–Yoshida, Theorems 2.1–2.2, 2.5 and 2.13](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06554v1).
Those calculations include the individual point fields at
the first level. The line formulas above use the affine
Kummer orbits and their complementary chart at every level.

## Full division-field discriminants

**Corollary.** For either reduction type, the full \(p^n\)-division
field of \(G\) and the full projective \(p^n\)-division field
both have normalized different in
\[
              \left[n-\frac1{p-1},\, n+\frac1{p-1}\right).
\]
Thus the width of the range for these normalized exponents is
at most \(2/(p-1)\), uniformly over all levels.

Fontaine's different bound for finite flat group schemes killed by
\(p^n\) gives the upper endpoint for the full division field.
Transitivity of the different gives it for the projective subfield.
The full division field contains \(K(\mu_{p^n})\) by the determinant.
This cyclotomic field has normalized different \(n-1/(p-1)\),
as follows by differentiating \(\Phi_{p^n}\) at a primitive root.
For an ordinary factor, after the unramified base change above,
the projective upper triangular representation has the ratio of
diagonal characters equal to the cyclotomic character, so its
projective field also contains the cyclotomic field. For a
supersingular factor, the line field is a subfield of the full
projective field and its normalized different \(A_n\) supplies
the lower bound.
[Fontaine, Théorème 1](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~fontaine/varabZ.pdf)
