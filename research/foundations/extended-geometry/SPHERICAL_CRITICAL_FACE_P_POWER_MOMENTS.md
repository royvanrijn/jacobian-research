# Prime-power moments at the critical weighted root multiplicity

The proper-volume criterion requires the roots of a weighted angular
face to have multiplicity below its weight. At equality there are
regular phases whose bounded phase regions have infinite squared
volume. An interval pole gives a different obstruction on a family
of such faces. It detects a coefficient that reduces to evaluation
at the critical root, even after arbitrary compatible lower terms
are added.

## A fixed phase at infinitely many prime-power orders

Let \(d,B,n\) be positive integers, put \(b=dB\), and choose an
odd integer \(s\ge3\) with \(bs<n\) and \(\gcd(s,dn)=1\).
Over a characteristic-zero field take \(c\ne0\) and a polynomial
\(U(Z)\) of degree \(\delta\le B\) with \(U(0)U(c)\ne0\).
On the sphere \(2xy+t^2=1\), prescribe the top angular polynomial
\[
                 Q(z)=z^{-b}(z^d-c)^n U(z^d)
\]
on literal weight \(j+i/d=n\). All strictly smaller Laurent
weights are arbitrary subject to regularity and the strip
\[
                         n-2b\le j+i/d\le n .
\]
Here \(z=x/t^{1/d}\) denotes the angular coordinate for the weight.
In particular the coefficient at angle \(i\) on the prescribed
face is the coefficient of \(x^i t^{n-i/d}\). All its exponents
of \(z\) are divisible by \(d\), so these heights are integral.

**Theorem.** Every fixed compatible phase \(f\) has nonzero unmarked
sphere moments at all orders
\[
        M=\frac{sp^r-1}{n},\qquad r\ge1,\qquad sp^r\equiv1\pmod{dn},
\]
for sufficiently large rational primes \(p\). For a fixed such prime
there are infinitely many of these orders as soon as \(s\) belongs to
the subgroup generated by \(p\) in \((\mathbb Z/dn)^\times\).
For example the progression \(p\equiv s^{-1}\pmod{dn}\) works, of
natural density \(1/\varphi(dn)\). Here the sphere functional
\(\Lambda\) takes the angular constant term and the normalized
interval integral.

Write \(q=p^r\) and work over an integral coefficient place of good
reduction, with \(p>s\). The maximum angular-constant height of
\(f^M\) is at most \(nM=sq-1\). Its support lies in an interval
of width \(2bM<2q\) below this height. Thus the interval denominators
divisible by \(q\) can only be \(sq\) and \((s-1)q\), and the latter
has odd height and integrates to zero. Since \(sq<p^{r+1}\), all
denominators have \(p\)-valuation at most \(r\). Reducing the
normalized moment therefore leaves only the top angular coefficient.
With \(Z=z^d\) it is
\[
 sq\Lambda(f^M)
      \equiv [Z^{BM}](Z-c)^{sq-1}U(Z)^M\pmod p .
\]
This is the interval-pole operation of the
[strip criterion](CM_NEWTON_DIRECT_STRIP_MOMENTS.md#a-strip-criterion)
applied to a rational weight. At angular zero the weighted height
equals the literal height, and only top-weight factors can reach
the upper endpoint, so fractional strictly lower weights cause
no change in the congruence.

In the residue field, the coefficients of \((Z-c)^{sq-1}\) in
degrees \(0\le j<q\) are \((-c)^{sq-1}c^{-j}\). Indeed
\((1-Z/c)^q=1-(Z/c)^q\), so modulo \(Z^q\) the polynomial
\((Z-c)^{sq-1}\) equals \((-c)^{sq-1}/(1-Z/c)\). The target
\(BM<q\), while \(U(Z)^M\) has degree at most \(BM\). Hence
\[
             sq\Lambda(f^M)
              \equiv (-c)^{sq-1}c^{-BM}U(c)^M\ne0\pmod p .
\]
This can be read directly as the geometric-series form of the
base-\(p\) calculation. The good primes are independent of \(r\).
For general characteristic-zero coefficients, take one number-field
specialization of a finitely generated coefficient algebra in which
\(c,U(0),U(c)\) and the leading coefficient of \(U\) are units.
It preserves the face and the support bounds. Nonzero specialized
moments imply nonzero original moments, with only finitely many
rational primes excluded for that one specialization.

## Regular phases and divergent bounded-phase volume

There is an explicit regular phase for every set of parameters. Put
\(w=x^d-ct\) and take
\[
       f_0=\frac{t^B}{x^b}\,w^{n-2b}(w^2-c^2)^b U(x^d/t).
\]
Here \(t^B U(x^d/t)\) is a polynomial since \(\delta\le B\).
Write \(u=x^d\). The factor \((w^2-c^2)^b\) is
\((u^2-2ctu+c^2(t^2-1))^b\), whose coefficient at \(u^j\) for
\(j\le b\) is divisible by \((t^2-1)^{b-j}\). Thus at \(u^i\)
with \(i<B\), the full numerator is divisible by
\((t^2-1)^{b-i}\). This suffices for the factor of order
\(b-di=d(B-i)\) needed to regularize the negative angle \(di-b\)
on the sphere. The leading weighted part is obtained by keeping
\(w^2\) from each factor \(w^2-c^2\), and gives the stated \(Q\).
The remaining deficits are at most \(2b\).

The phase \(f_0\) stays bounded when \(w\) lies in a fixed disk
and \(|t|\) tends to infinity. There \(t^B/x^b=(t/(ct+w))^B\)
and \(U(x^d/t)=U(c+w/t)\) are bounded. In these coordinates the
pullback expression for the sphere volume form is a nonzero
constant times
\[
                        \frac{dw\wedge dt}{d(ct+w)} .
\]
On the indicated region its squared density has divergent integral,
since the \(t\)-density is comparable to \(|t|^{-2}d^2t\).
The map from \((x,t)\) to \((w,t)\) there is a finite unramified
cover, so the divergence holds on the actual sphere region.
Every root with \(z^d=c\) has multiplicity exactly \(n\) in the top
angular polynomial. These examples lie at the strict boundary of
the [weighted proper-volume theorem](SPHERICAL_WEIGHTED_FACE_PROPER_VOLUME_OBSTRUCTION.md#the-bilateral-weighted-criterion).

The same face certifies that \(f\) is an absolute nonpower whenever
\(\gcd(b,n)=1\), or when \(U\) has a simple nonzero root different
from \(c\). To see this, on the weighted cover at \(t=\infty\)
the leading angular function of a proper power would be a proper
power in \(\overline{k}(z)\). The orders at \(z=0\) and at the
critical roots are \(-b\) and \(n\), and a simple root of \(U(z^d)\)
instead supplies order one.

## Uniform density and a supersingular quotient

Fix \(d=1\) or \(d=2\) and \(B\), take \(n=3^h>5dB\), and
set \(s=5\). The groups of units modulo \(dn\) are cyclic of
order \(2\cdot3^{h-1}\), since \(d=1\) or 2 and \(n\) is odd.
The residue 5 is a generator, as it has order two modulo 3 and
\(v_3(5^{2\cdot3^j}-1)=1+j\). Each generator residue of \(p\)
therefore gives infinitely many admissible \(r\). The total density
of those prime classes is \(1/3\) for every \(h\ge2\), independent
of \(n\).

For \(d=1,B=2,U(Z)=Z-c'\) with \(c'\ne0,c\), the auxiliary
cover before removing the multiple branch would be
\[
       W^n=z^{n-2}(z-c)^n(z-c').
\]
After replacing \(W\) by \(W/(z-c)\), the normalized cyclic cover
has three fully ramified points with exponents \(n-2,1,1\) and
genus \((n-1)/2\). At the generator primes some power of \(p\)
equals \(-1\pmod n\), so the whole normalized Jacobian is
supersingular by the Fermat quotient criterion.
[Aoki, Introduction](https://rikkyo.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/16621)
· [Hermitian-curve proof for this criterion](CM_NEWTON_SUPERSINGULAR_BINOMIAL_STRIPS.md#coherent-frobenius-on-a-supersingular-quotient)

The coefficient used here is an evaluation at the removed branch.
The character-one coherent space of the normalized cover in this
example is zero, since the reduced finite polynomial has degree
\(n-1<n\). Thus the arithmetic obstruction can work at a pole
where the corresponding normalized coherent entry is unavailable.

## Exact replay and a limitation of the top pole

For \(d=2,B=2,n=37,s=3,p=5,r=2\), take \(c=1\) and
\(U(Z)=Z-2\). Add \(xt^{36}+2yt^{32}\) to \(f_0\), including a
nonintegral weighted gap. Direct multiplication in the literal
chart gives \(75\Lambda(f^2)\equiv1\pmod5\), recovering the
order-two moment from the pole at \(3\cdot5^2\).
[Exact rational replay](verify_spherical_critical_face_p_power_moments.py)

If the multiplicity \(n\) is increased to \(n+\ell\) with
\(\ell>0\) and \(\deg U+\ell\le B\), the same moment order
and support width give a zero top residue. In degrees below \(q\),
the factor \((Z-c)^{sq}\) contributes only its constant term,
leaving a polynomial \((Z-c)^{\ell M-1}U(Z)^M\) of degree at
most \(BM-1\). This shows precisely where this top-pole test
ends in the displayed family.
