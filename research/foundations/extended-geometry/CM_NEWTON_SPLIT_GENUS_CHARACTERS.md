# Genus characters of a failed Frobenius pair

Let \(B/K\) be an abelian threefold whose full geometric rational
endomorphism algebra is a totally real cubic field \(F\), with the
endomorphisms defined over \(K\). At a good prime \(v\) of absolute
residue degree one with odd residue characteristic \(p\) split in \(F\),
write the relative polynomial of Frobenius \(\pi_v\) as \(X^2-a_v X+p\). Set
\(D_v=a_v^2-4p\) and \(E_v=F(\sqrt{D_v})\). At a one-failed-pair prime,
let \(\mathfrak q\) be the failed coefficient prime of \(F\) and
\(\mathfrak Q\) the unique prime of \(E_v\) above it. Then
\(\mathfrak Q/\mathfrak q\) has ramification degree two and residue
degree one. The field \(E_v\) is CM, and in this case it is the full
geometric rational endomorphism field of the reduction.
[Local ramification and Frobenius fields](CM_NEWTON_FROBENIUS_RAMIFICATION_AND_DENOMINATORS.md#inertia-and-the-frobenius-weyl-group).

**Theorem.** Fix \(B/K\) as above. For every integer \(R\ge1\), outside
a set of relative natural density zero among the degree-one split
primes, each one-failed-pair prime has at least \(R\) independent
genus characters of \(E_v\) with each prescribed even sign pattern
on the three prime ideals occurring in \((\pi_v)\). The characters
are unramified quadratic characters obtained by base change from
\(F\). The three ideal classes therefore span a space of dimension
two in \(\operatorname{Cl}(E_v)/2\operatorname{Cl}(E_v)\), with their
principal product as the only relation. In particular the class
\([\mathfrak Q]\) lies outside \(2\operatorname{Cl}(E_v)\).
If \(F\) has class number one, it generates a direct summand of order two
in \(\operatorname{Cl}(E_v)\).

More quantitatively, there is a constant \(c_F>0\) such that at
primes \(v\) of this type with \(p\le X\), the number of independent
witnesses for each even sign pattern is at least
\(c_F\log\log\log X\) with at most
\(O_{B,K}(1/\log\log\log X)\) exceptional proportion, relative to all
degree-one split primes up to \(X\). This assertion is unconditional.

## Quadratic characters from split auxiliary primes

Take the auxiliary rational primes \(\ell\) split completely in the
normal closure of the ray class field of \(F\) of modulus
\(4\mathcal O_F\) with no infinite places. Their natural
density \(\rho>0\) is the reciprocal of the degree of this fixed
Galois extension of \(\mathbb Q\). Delete the finitely many primes at
which any of the integral representations below are unavailable.
For each remaining \(\ell\), choose one coefficient prime
\(\mathfrak l\) above it. Ray reciprocity gives a generator
\(\beta_\ell\equiv1\bmod4\mathcal O_F\) for \(\mathfrak l\). Its field
norm has absolute value \(\ell\) and is congruent to one modulo four.
It is therefore \(\ell^*=(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell\).
[Artin's ray splitting criterion](https://public.csusm.edu/aitken_html/Translations/Artin1924.pdf#page=21).

The extension \(F(\sqrt{\beta_\ell})/F\) is ramified at just
\(\mathfrak l\) among the finite places. At odd places this
follows from the valuations of \(\beta_\ell\), and at dyadic places the
monic polynomial for \((1+\sqrt{\beta_\ell})/2\) is integral with unit
discriminant. If \(v_{\mathfrak l}(D_v)=1\), then
\(E_v(\sqrt{\beta_\ell})/E_v\) is unramified at every place. At
\(\mathfrak l\) the two quadratic radicands have odd valuation,
so their ratio has even valuation and gives a split or unramified
quadratic local extension. At all other places the extension from
\(\beta_\ell\) is already unramified or split. The infinite places of
\(E_v\) are complex.

For \(\ell\ne p\) with \(v_{\mathfrak l}(D_v)=1\), the witness sign
pattern is the vector of the three Frobenius signs of
\(F(\sqrt{\beta_\ell})/F\) at the coefficient primes over \(p\).
At a one-failed-pair place, the three primes occurring in \((\pi_v)\)
are \(\mathfrak Q\) and one prime from each of the two ordinary split
pairs, all with exponent one. Their residue degrees over the three
coefficient primes are one. The witness sign pattern is therefore
the vector of values on these three classes under the unramified
character of \(E_v\). Distinct choices of \(\ell\) give independent
squareclasses of \(\beta_\ell\) in \(F\), by their odd valuations at
different primes. They remain independent over \(E_v\), since each
\(\beta_\ell\) is a unit at \(\mathfrak q\) and \(D_v\) has odd
valuation there. Unramified reciprocity gives independent characters of
\(\operatorname{Cl}(E_v)\). If all even sign patterns occur then their
evaluations show that the principal product is the only mod-two
relation. If \(F\) has class number one,
\(\mathfrak Q^2=\mathfrak q\mathcal O_{E_v}\) is principal. A character
with value \(-1\) at its class splits off the order-two subgroup
generated by that class.

## Joint law with the torsion image

Enlarge \(K\) to contain the normal closure \(L\) of \(F\) and fix the
three embeddings \(\sigma_i:F\to L\). Every degree-one split prime
under consideration splits completely in this base extension. Its
degree-one lifts have the same Frobenius polynomial, and conversely
every degree-one prime of the enlarged field lies above a split
degree-one prime of the original field, apart from finitely many
ramified primes. All statements for the three possible labels at once
therefore descend with their relative proportions. Over the enlarged
field and a degree-one \(v\), the \(i\)-th coefficient prime of \(F\)
is the one induced by \(\sigma_i\) and reduction modulo \(v\).

For each auxiliary \(\ell\) adjoin the square roots of its three
conjugates \(\sigma_i(\beta_\ell)\) to \(K\). Fix a prime \(\mathfrak L\)
of \(L\) over \(\ell\) and lift the embeddings \(\sigma_i\) to
automorphisms of \(L\). If \(\sigma_i^{-1}\mathfrak L\) and
\(\sigma_j^{-1}\mathfrak L\) induce the same prime of \(F\), some
\(h\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\) sends the former to the latter.
Then \(\sigma_j h\sigma_i^{-1}\) lies in the trivial decomposition
group of \(\mathfrak L\), so \(\sigma_i\) and \(\sigma_j\) agree on
\(F\). Hence the three induced coefficient primes are distinct.
Thus each prime of \(L\) over \(\ell\) has odd
valuation on exactly one of the three conjugates, and all three occur.
The parity persists in \(K\), since the chosen \(\ell\) are unramified
there. Consequently the square-root extension has Galois group
\(\{\pm1\}^3\), and the squareclasses are independent across distinct
\(\ell\) by their disjoint support.

For any finite collection of sufficiently large \(\ell\), the joint
torsion image on the \(B[\ell^2]\) is the product of the groups
\[
 G_\ell=\{(g_1,g_2,g_3)\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z/\ell^2)^3:
                    \det g_1=\det g_2=\det g_3\}.
\]
Use Serre's independence extension and apply Lombardo there. The
image over the current \(K\) contains this same product and is
contained in it by the coefficient action and the polarization.
The determinant kernel in each group is the product of three perfect
special linear groups, so its abelianization is the scalar cyclotomic
determinant. Perfectness follows from elementary generators over the
local ring and the commutator with a diagonal unit having square
different from one modulo \(\ell\).
[Lombardo, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.1703)
· [Serre, Theorem 1 and §3.1](https://www.college-de-france.fr/media/jean-pierre-serre/UPL8570957518086251784_independance.pdf).

The intersection of the total square-root compositum with the total
torsion compositum is an elementary abelian two-extension of \(K\).
By the preceding abelianization it is contained in the compositum
of the unique quadratic subfields of the cyclotomic determinants.
These subfields are \(K(\sqrt{\ell^*})\). They already lie in the
square-root compositum because
\(\prod_i\sigma_i(\beta_\ell)=\ell^*\). This proves equality. Thus at each
\(\ell\) the signs on the three square roots are uniform subject to
their product being the quadratic character of the common determinant.
Across \(\ell\) these joint sign and torsion laws are independent.

Under the uniform torsion law the probability of
\(v_{\mathfrak l}(D_v)=1\) is \((\ell-1)/\ell^2\). Indeed the
determinant is square modulo \(\ell\) with probability \(1/2\).
For a fixed square determinant there are two repeated-root traces.
The nonscalar class for each has proportion \(1/\ell\) in the
determinant fibre. With determinant fixed modulo \(\ell^2\), the
trace lifts of a nonscalar matrix are uniform, since trace and the
differential of determinant are independent. Exactly one in \(\ell\)
of these lifts still has discriminant zero modulo \(\ell^2\).
Scalar matrices make no contribution. This gives the stated probability.
[Local discriminant count](CM_NEWTON_FROBENIUS_DISCRIMINANT_SIEVE.md#number-of-ramified-prime-ideals).

The discriminant condition forces the product of signs to be one.
Conditional on it, each of the four even sign patterns has probability
\(1/4\). At a degree-one prime these signs on the conjugates
\(\sigma_i(\beta_\ell)\) are exactly the Frobenius signs at the three
labelled coefficient primes. It follows that, for each fixed even
pattern and any finite set of auxiliary primes, the witness indicators
are independent Bernoulli variables under the Chebotarev law, with means
\[
                    \mu_\ell=\frac{\ell-1}{4\ell^2}.
\]
The sum of the means diverges by
Chebotarev for the fixed ray splitting field. For a finite collection
with mean sum \(M>R\), the upper limiting proportion with fewer than
\(R\) witnesses for some pattern is at most \(4M/(M-R)^2\) by the
variance bound. Increasing the fixed collection proves the density
claim for every \(R\).

## A growing unconditional count

Take the auxiliary primes up to \(Y=(\log X)^\theta\) for a fixed
sufficiently small \(\theta>0\). For the first and second moments it
suffices to count Frobenius in the joint fields for one or two
auxiliary primes. Use their normal closures over \(\mathbb Q\) to
apply the unconditional effective Chebotarev estimate over \(K\).
Their absolute degrees \(n\) are bounded by a fixed power of \(Y\).
Indeed the division modulus has at most two prime-square factors, the
square-root fields have bounded degree, and the number of conjugates
over \(\mathbb Q\) is bounded in terms of \(K\). Each normal closure
is unramified outside a fixed set of rational primes and the two chosen
auxiliary primes. Its absolute discriminant \(\Delta\) therefore
satisfies \(\log|\Delta|\ll_{B,K}n\log(2Y)\). At any ramified rational
prime \(q\) use the local different bound \(e-1+e v_q(e)\) and sum
over primes above \(q\). This bounds its contribution to
\(\log|\Delta|/n\) by \(\log q+\log n\).

Apply Lagarias–Odlyzko Theorem 1.3 to these normal closures over the
enlarged \(K\). The required \(\log X\ge 10n(\log|\Delta|)^2\) follows
for sufficiently small \(\theta\). Their Theorem 1.4 gives the Stark
bound with \(m_L=4\), since the top field is normal over \(\mathbb Q\).
Any exceptional zero \(\beta\) consequently satisfies
\(1-\beta\gg_{B,K}Y^{-C}\) for a fixed \(C\), by the degree and root
discriminant bounds. On choosing \(\theta\) smaller as needed, both
Chebotarev error types are \(O_{B,K,A}(X(\log X)^{-A})\) for every
fixed \(A\), also after summing over conjugacy classes. Pullback to
the normal closures does not change group means. The higher-degree
and finitely excluded places, and places with residue characteristic
at the selected auxiliary primes, give negligible errors.
[Lagarias–Odlyzko, Theorems 1.3–1.4](https://aareyanmanzoor.github.io/assets/articles/lagarias-odlyzko.pdf).

Taking means over degree-one split primes up to \(X\), the witness
count for each pattern has mean \(M+o(1)\) and variance at most
\(M+o(1)\), where partial summation in the fixed ray splitting class
gives
\[
          M=\sum_{\ell\le Y}\mu_\ell
             =\frac{\rho}{4}\log\log\log X+O(1).
\]
The sum is over the chosen auxiliary primes. Summing the uniform
errors over \(O(Y^2)\) pairs justifies the moment comparison.
Chebyshev's inequality gives at least \(M/2\) witnesses for every
pattern outside proportion \(O(1/M)\). This proves the claimed rate,
for example with \(c_F=\rho/10\).

## An exact control at 113

For the [level-97 threefold](CM_NEWTON_SPLIT_FAILURE_WITH_FULL_RESIDUAL_IMAGE.md),
use \(y^3-y^2-2y+1=0\) and \(a_{113}=-3y^2-y+8\). A witness at
\(\ell=13\) is
\[
 \beta_{13}=16y^2-8y-35\equiv1\pmod{4\mathcal O_F},
 \qquad N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\beta_{13})=13.
\]
The norm is the determinant of multiplication on \(1,y,y^2\), with
columns \((-35,-8,16),(-16,-3,8),(-8,0,5)\). Both \(\beta_{13}\) and
\(D_{113}\) vanish at \(y=5\) modulo \(13\). The valuation of
\(D_{113}\) there is one because its norm \(-76433539\) is divisible
by \(13\) exactly once. In the coefficient places \(y=34,89,104\)
modulo \(113\), the residues of \(\beta_{13}\) are \(109,107,90\).
Their signs are \((+,-,-)\), and the failed place is \(y=104\).
This gives one unramified character detecting its ramified class.

The theorem controls the genus of the ramified class on the
one-failed-pair locus. The relative density of that locus itself
remains open.
