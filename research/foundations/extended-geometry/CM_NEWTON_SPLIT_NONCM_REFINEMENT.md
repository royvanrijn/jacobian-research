# Selective split-prime moments in a varying non-CM family

A larger cyclic deck action can force a mixed coherent eigenspace
to lose rank while preserving the line used by a sphere moment.
Here the preserved line belongs to a varying four-point quotient
whose primitive factor is a square of an abelian surface up to
geometric isogeny. Ordinary reduction of that surface gives the
selected nonvanishing at a set of rational split primes of density
\(1/8\). A different mixed pair is nonordinary at the same primes.

## The family and the moment theorem

Take any integer \(a\equiv2\pmod9\), and put \(c=1-a^4\).
In particular \(c\ne0,1\) and \(v_3(c)=1\). Define the smooth
normalizations of the covers
\[
      C_c:\quad W^8=z^3(z^5-1)(z^5-c),\qquad
      D_c:\quad V^8=u^3(u-1)^5(u-c)^5.
\]
Write \(J^{[8]}\) for the Jacobian factor given by the primitive
order-eight deck characters. Both covers are connected.

**Theorem.** The factor \(J^{[8]}(C_c)\) has dimension twenty and
is not potentially CM. On a set of rational primes of natural
density \(1/8\), lying in
\[
                 p\equiv1\pmod8,\qquad p\equiv2,3\pmod5,
\]
the character-one coherent line is invertible under absolute
Frobenius. At the same primes character three has coherent
dimension three and stable rank one, so its mixed pair fails.

For every fixed sphere phase whose largest literal weight is
\(j+i=8\), with angular polynomial \(z^{-5}(z^5-1)(z^5-c)\)
there, this certifies
\(\mathcal A(f^{(p-1)/8})\ne0\) on a set of the same density.
All compatible strict lower terms are arbitrary. The phases are
absolute nonpowers, including in the sphere's rational function
field. They have least sphere degree eight.

## The ordinary quotient

With \(u=z^5\), the function field of \(C_c\) has presentation
\[
                        W^{40}=u^3(u-1)^5(u-c)^5 .
\]
Conversely \(z^3=W^8/((u-1)(u-c))\) recovers \(z\) using
\(\gcd(3,5)=1\). The refined deck group has order forty and the
original order-eight group is generated by the fifth power of
the refined generator. Taking \(V=W^5\) gives the degree-five
quotient to \(D_c\). In characteristic different from five the
pullback identifies its coherent cohomology with the fifth-root
subgroup invariants of that of \(C_c\), by the degree relation
for trace and pullback. Thus the order-eight summand from \(D_c\)
consists of the refined characters divisible by five.

On \(D_c\) put \(s(u)=(u-c)/(u-1)\). This involution exchanges
\(0,c\) and \(1,\infty\). For \(G(u)=u^3(u-1)^5(u-c)^5\),
\[
 G(s(u))G(u)=(1-c)^{10}\left(\frac{u(u-c)}{u-1}\right)^8.
\]
Consequently \(\tau:(u,V)\mapsto(s(u),a^5u s(u)/V)\) is a
rational involution of the normalized cover. It squares to one
because \(u s(u)\) is invariant under \(s\), and it conjugates
the order-eight deck generator to its inverse.

The primitive factor \(J^{[8]}(D_c)\) is defined over \(\mathbb Q\)
by the Galois-invariant rational primitive deck idempotent. Its
dimension is four, since each primitive character sees four
branches and has first cohomology of rank two. The involution
\(\tau\) exchanges each primitive character with its conjugate.
Its plus and minus projectors therefore cut out rational
abelian surfaces \(B_+\), \(B_-\), whose sum is an isogeny to
\(J^{[8]}(D_c)\). On this factor the square of the deck generator
acts as \(i\in\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\), and it anticommutes with
\(\tau\). It gives a geometric isogeny between the two surfaces.
The real subfield \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\) commutes with \(\tau\)
and acts on each surface.

### Effective exclusion of CM

Use the valuation at 3, where \(c\) is a uniformizer. Blow up
the marked base \(\mathbb P^1\) at \(u=c=0\) to separate the
marked sections \(u=0,c\). The two components of the nodal
base meet at the point with local equation \(us=c\). At this node
the substitution \(V/u\) gives the equation
\[
                     (V/u)^8=(u-1)^5(1-s)^5.
\]
The right-hand side is a unit. The normalization is therefore
étale over the node, with eight geometric nodes above it, since
eight is prime to three. The main component has reduction
\((V/u)^8=(u-1)^5\). On the exceptional component, use \(U=u/c\)
and \(V/c\); the equation reduces to
\((V/c)^8=-U^3(U-1)^5\). Both components are connected and
genus zero, each with two fully ramified branch points. Away
from the nodes the normalization is smooth, since the sections
are disjoint and their ramification is tame. This gives a
semistable model after an unramified extension if needed to
split the local unit roots.

The dual graph has two vertices joined by eight edges. Its
first homology has rank seven and is the character lattice of
the Jacobian's torus. The deck group acts regularly on the
edges, so the graph homology is the augmentation representation.
In particular the primitive order-eight part has toric rank
four. The full Jacobian is totally toric at this place, and its
isogeny factors remain totally toric after finite local extension.
A positive-dimensional CM factor would instead acquire good
reduction after a finite extension. Thus neither abelian surface
\(B_\pm\) has a geometric CM isogeny factor, and \(J^{[8]}(D_c)\)
is not potentially CM.
[SGA 7 I, Exposé IX, (12.3.7) and §12.4](https://grothendiecksga.com/read/sga7/en/9-12.html)
· [Serre–Tate, Theorem 6](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1968/88-3/p05)

The same node model over \(\mathbb C[[c]]\) gives nontrivial
primitive toric degeneration at \(c=0\). Thus the primitive
factor varies geometrically with the branch parameter. Also,
the primitive factor of \(D_c\) pulls back to an isogeny factor
of the primitive factor of \(C_c\). Its non-CM reduction
obstruction therefore applies to the larger factor as well.

### Ordinary primes of the quotient

Sawin's density theorem applies to \(B_+\), an abelian surface
over \(\mathbb Q\) with no geometric CM isogeny factor. Its
ordinary primes have density one. At good primes away from
the fixed isogeny exceptions, the two surfaces have isogenous
geometric reductions. Hence \(J^{[8]}(D_c)\) is ordinary at
a density-one set of rational primes. In particular this
conclusion retains density \(1/8\) after imposing the two
congruences of the theorem.
[Sawin, Theorem 2.3](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.crma.2016.01.025.pdf)

## The surviving and failing characters

At a split prime for the original order-eight action, write
\(x=i/8\) for a primitive character \(i\), and \(t=\ell\bmod5\)
for a refined character \(\ell\equiv i\bmod8\). The four branch
exponents in the degree-forty model are \(3,5,5,27\). The
coherent dimension at this finer character is
\[
                  -1+\{t/5-x\}+2x+\{-t/5-x\} .
\]
This follows by summing the fractional branch contributions.
Each primitive original character has five such finer
characters, each with first cohomology of rank two. The
primitive factor consequently has dimension twenty.
[Bouw, Lemma 4.3](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1017513122376)

Frobenius on the coefficients acts on the finer labels by
\(t\mapsto pt\bmod5\). For \(p\equiv2,3\pmod5\), label zero
is fixed and the other four labels comprise a single cycle.
For \(i=1\) the finer dimensions in order \(t=0,1,2,3,4\) are
\((1,0,0,0,0)\). For \(i=3\) they are \((1,1,0,0,1)\).
The fixed label in original character \(i\) comes from
character \(5i\bmod8\) of \(D_c\), since it is represented by
\(\ell=5j\equiv i\bmod8\). Hence ordinary reduction of
\(J^{[8]}(D_c)\) gives a nonzero map on each of these two
fixed lines. The other pieces of character three have a
zero-dimensional step on their orbit. They have zero stable
rank, so the full original character-three space has rank
one in dimension three. This establishes the selective
failure at the same primes as the surviving line.

## Sphere lift and exact replay

A regular lift of the prescribed angular face is
\[
                -32c y^5t^3-(1+c)t^8+x^5t^3.
\]
Indeed the negative angle \(-5\) has top height thirteen in
the literal chart and the term \(-32c y^5t^3\) has its required
coefficient \(c\). At weight \(j+i\), passage to the normal
sphere basis preserves the highest total degree at each angle.
Thus the prescribed support gives least degree eight, even
with all strictly lower-weight terms. In the function field
with coordinates \(z=x/t,t\), the order at \(t=\infty\) is
\(-8\), and the leading angular function has order \(-5\) at
\(z=0\). A proper power would make both orders divisible by
its power exponent, impossible since \(\gcd(8,5)=1\).

At order \(m=(p-1)/8\), angular-constant height is at most
\(p-1\). The unique interval denominator divisible by \(p\)
is the top denominator, so at a good place
\[
 p\mathcal A(f^m)\equiv[z^{5m}](z^5-1)^m(z^5-c)^m\pmod p .
\]
The right side is the character-one Hasse–Witt scalar of
\(C_c\), which survives by the quotient argument. A fixed
number-field specialization of the lower coefficients proves
the same conclusion for arbitrary characteristic-zero fields.
It preserves the rational leading coefficients, hence the
arithmetic family and the density of certifying primes.

For \(a=2\), so \(c=-15\), the exact replay gives the quotient
scalars \((13,13,13,13)\) modulo 17 and \((57,20,20,57)\)
modulo 73 in characters \(1,3,5,7\). In the original cover,
character one has scalars 13 and 20, and character three has
matrices \(\operatorname{diag}(0,13,0)\) and
\(\operatorname{diag}(0,57,0)\), respectively. At 17 it also
checks the normalized order-two moment on the literal sphere
lift by rational integration.
