# The one-operator Image theorem for polynomial multipliers

## Theorem (the full polynomial one-operator Image assertion)

Let \(B\) be any commutative \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra, \(c\in B\), and
\(P\in B[z]\). The image \(I\) of \(D=c\partial_z-P(z)\) on \(B[z]\)
is a Mathieu subspace. This proves Conjecture 6.1 of van den Essen and Zhao.
For upper bounds \(r,d\) on the degrees of \(P,f\) and an integer \(N\geq1\),
there is a computable \(q=q(r,d,N)\geq N\) such that
\[
                 f^j\in I\quad(N\leq j\leq q)
\]
implies \(gf^m\in I\) for every fixed \(g\in B[z]\) and all sufficiently
large \(m\). The eventual threshold can depend on the coefficient data
and on \(g\).

The [field obstruction](ONE_OPERATOR_IMAGE_POLYNOMIAL_MULTIPLIERS.md)
produces a nilpotent coefficient ideal. We use it to reduce mixed powers
to coefficients killed by powers of the nonconstant coefficient ideal.
The shifted moment estimate from the
[constant-coefficient theorem](ONE_OPERATOR_IMAGE_MATHIEU.md)
then gives preimages with enough divisibility to lift across the
remaining torsion.
[Van den Essen and Zhao, Conjecture 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2017#page=10)

## Reduction to a finite coefficient algebra

Write \(P=a+\nu(z)\) with \(\nu(0)=0\). Let \(J\) be generated by the
coefficients of \(\nu\) and \(F\) by the coefficients of \(f\). For
\(r=0\) apply the constant-coefficient theorem. For \(r\geq1\) take
\(q\) to be the maximum of the constant-coefficient window and the
field windows for nonconstant multiplier degrees \(1,\ldots,r\).

Pass to the fraction field of the quotient by any prime ideal of \(B\).
If both \(c\) and \(J\) have nonzero images, the multiplier is
nonconstant and the finite field window forces \(f=0\) there. Thus
\(cJF\) is in the nilradical. This ideal is finitely generated, so for
some \(s\geq1\) we have
\[
                             c^sJ^sF^s=0.                         \tag{1}
\]
The image of \(D\) is contained in the polynomial ideal \((c,P)B[z]\),
so \(f^N\in(c,P)B[z]\).

Fix \(g\) and replace \(B\) by the \(\mathbb Q\)-subalgebra generated by
\(c\), the coefficients of \(P,f,g\), and the coefficients of one
preimage for each power in the finite hypothesis. The hypotheses and
(1) hold in this Noetherian subalgebra. Every preimage constructed in it
also works over the original ring.

## Reduction to markers killed by powers of \(J\)

Put \(T=\bigcup_{j\geq1}\operatorname{Ann}_B(J^j)\). Choose
\(\ell\geq1\) with \(J^\ell T=0\) by the ascending chain condition.
We reduce to eventual membership of \(xf^m\) for every fixed \(x\in T[z]\).

Write \(f^{3sN}=P^{2s}V+c^s W\), possible since \(f^N\in(c,P)B[z]\).
For \(b=m-3sN\geq0\), put \(U_0=Vgf^b\) and define
\[
 U_{j+1}=(2s-2j-1)P' U_j+P U_j'\qquad(0\leq j<s).
\]
The polynomial identities
\[
 D(-c^jP^{2s-2j-1}U_j)=
 c^jP^{2s-2j}U_j-c^{j+1}P^{2s-2j-2}U_{j+1}
\]
sum to \(gf^m\equiv c^s(U_s+Wgf^b)\pmod I\) in the quotient
\(B\)-module. The polynomial \(U_s\) results from a fixed
differential operator of order at most \(s\) applied to \(Vgf^b\).
For \(b\geq2s\) the Leibniz expansion expresses \(c^sU_s\) as a sum of
scalar multiples, depending on \(b\), of terms \(x f^{b-2s}\) for
finitely many fixed markers \(x\). Each such marker contains the
factor \(c^s f^s\), since at most \(s\) derivatives strike the \(b\)
copies of \(f\). The same holds for \(c^sWgf^b\).
Equation (1) makes all these markers elements of \(T[z]\).
Eventual membership for those markers suffices for \(g\).

## Preimages modulo the coefficients killed by powers of \(a\)

Let \(U=\bigcup_{j\geq1}\operatorname{Ann}_B(a^j)\), choose \(k\geq1\)
with \(a^kU=0\), and put \(\bar B=B/U\).
We have \(U\cap a^kB=0\), since \(a^k v\in U\) implies \(v\in U\).
Suppose first \(\bar B\ne0\), so the image of \(a\) is regular, and
omit bars on elements in formulas. Let \(J'=(\bar J:a^\infty)\).
Then \(a\) is regular on \(C=\bar B/J'\) if \(C\ne0\).
Also \(J'^\ell\) kills the image of \(T\) in \(\bar B\): a power of
\(a\) carries each product of \(\ell\) elements of \(J'\) into
\(\bar J^\ell\), and that power cancels in \(\bar B\).

Over the localization at \(a\) set \(\Psi(h)=h(z+ct/a)\), with \(t\)
independent. On \(C[z]\) the operator is \(D_0=c\partial_z-a\),
and the finite window holds there. The coefficient estimate in the
regular case of the constant-coefficient proof gives a fixed \(m_0\)
with \(\Psi(f^{m_0})\) coefficientwise in \(aC[z,t]\). Thus in the
localization of \(\bar B\) we can write
\[
   \Psi(f^{m_0})=aV_0+\eta,\qquad
   V_0\in\bar B[z,t],\quad \eta\in J'\bar B[a^{-1}][z,t].
\]
For \(C=0\) take \(V_0=0\). Fix \(x\in T[z]\).
For \(m=nm_0+i\) with \(0\leq i<m_0\), expand
\(\Psi(x f^m)=\Psi(x f^i)(aV_0+\eta)^n\). Terms with at least
\(\ell\) copies of \(\eta\) vanish because \(J'^\ell\) kills the
coefficients of \(\Psi(x)\). Other terms have at least \(n-\ell+1\)
factors \(a\). Their denominators are bounded by those of \(\ell-1\)
copies of the fixed \(\eta\) and the finitely many \(\Psi(x f^i)\).
Thus for any prescribed \(R\), the coefficients of \(\Psi(x f^m)\)
eventually lie in \(a^R\bar B\).

On the localized submodule killed by \(\bar J^\ell\), the inverse
of \(D=D_0-\nu\) is
\[
               D^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(D_0^{-1}M_\nu)^jD_0^{-1},
\]
where \(M_\nu\) is multiplication by \(\nu\). Each \(M_\nu\) contributes
one factor of \(\bar J\) and \(D_0^{-1}\) is linear over the localized
coefficient ring. The finite derivative sum for \(D_0^{-1}\) gives
\[
 \Psi(D_0^{-1}h)(z,t)=-a^{-1}L_u\bigl(\Psi(h)(z,t+u)\bigr),
 \qquad L_u(u^i)=i!.
\]
On shifted coefficients each \(D_0^{-1}\) therefore costs at most one
power of \(a\), and each multiplication by \(\nu\) costs at most \(r\).
Take \(R=2k+\ell+(\ell-1)r\) in the shifted estimate and set \(t=0\)
after applying the inverse. For all large \(m\) this gives a preimage
of \(\bar x\bar f^m\) in \(a^{2k}\bar B[z]\) killed by \(\bar J^\ell\).

## Lifting and correcting the preimage

Divide the last preimage by \(a^{2k}\) in \(\bar B[z]\). The
quotient is still killed by \(\bar J^\ell\) since \(a\) is regular.
Lift it to \(B[z]\). Multiplication by \(a^k\) puts the lift in
\(T[z]\), since its products with \(J^\ell\) belong to \(U[z]\).
Thus the preimage lifts to \(w\in T[z]\cap a^kB[z]\) with
\[
                         xf^m-Dw\in (U\cap T)[z].
\]
For a fixed \(M\) the same shifted estimate at \(t=0\) gives
\(a^{2k}\)-divisibility of \(\bar x\bar f^{m-M}\) for large \(m\).
Divide by \(a^{2k}\), lift, and multiply the lift by \(a^k\) to write
\[
           xf^{m-M}=a^k v+h_0,\qquad v\in T[z],\quad h_0\in(U\cap T)[z].
\]
If \(\bar B=0\), both conclusions hold with \(w=v=0\).
The difference \(xf^m-Dw-f^M h_0\) belongs to both \(U[z]\) and
\(a^kB[z]\), so it is zero.

Take \(b_0=k+\ell\) and \(M=2b_0 N\).
On \((U\cap T)[z]\), multiplication by \(P\) and the endomorphism
\(M_P H\) both have zero \(b_0\)-th powers, where \(H\) is integration
with constant term zero. In a word of length \(b_0\), each factor
contributes \(a\) or a coefficient in \(J\). Integration preserves
coefficient ideals, so each word is killed by \(a^k\) or \(J^\ell\).
Now \(f^M h_0\in c^{b_0}(U\cap T)[z]\) since \(f^N\in(c,P)B[z]\).
This module belongs to \(I\), by the identity on \((U\cap T)[z]\)
\[
 D H\sum_{j=0}^{b_0-1}c^{b_0-1-j}(M_P H)^j
     =c^{b_0}\,\mathrm{id}-(M_P H)^{b_0}=c^{b_0}\,\mathrm{id}.
\]
Hence \(xf^m\in I\) for all large \(m\). The initial marker reduction
proves the theorem.
