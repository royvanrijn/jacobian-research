# Denominators of horizontal Hodge vectors at split primes

The first occurrence of every denominator power in a horizontal Hodge
vector distinguishes the ordinary and nonordinary rank-two factors of a
Hilbert–Blumenthal abelian variety. A tensor of the horizontal vectors gives
a single denominator ideal whose growth records the distribution of
\(p\)-ranks. For a real-cubic multiplication threefold, maximal growth of
this ideal is equivalent to density-one ordinarity.

## Exact first degree of each denominator power

Use a Hilbert–Blumenthal point \(x\) over a number field \(K\) and the
algebraic curve germ through \(x\) from the
[projective \(p\)-curvature criterion](CM_NEWTON_SPLIT_PROJECTIVE_P_CURVATURE.md).
The parameter \(t\) and its direction at zero are fixed in characteristic
zero, with all partial Kodaira–Spencer coordinates nonzero. Fix an
embedding of the real multiplication field and write \(H\) for its
rank-two de Rham summand and \(\omega\) for its Hodge line. Let \(Y(t)\)
be the horizontal lift of a fixed nonzero vector in \(\omega(0)\).
After discarding finitely many primes, the initial vector is primitive
in the fiber lattice, the parameter and frames are integral, and the
Kodaira–Spencer coordinate remains nonzero on reduction.

**Theorem.** Let \(\mathfrak p\) be such an unramified good prime, of
characteristic \(p\) split completely in the real multiplication field.
For each integer \(r\ge 1\), the first degree at which the vector
coefficient has valuation at most \(-r\) is
\[
 n_r=
 \begin{cases}
   p^r,&\text{the rank-two factor is ordinary},\\
   p+p^2+\cdots+p^{2r},&\text{the rank-two factor is nonordinary}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The valuation of that coefficient is exactly \(-r\). The valuation of a
vector is the minimum valuation of its coordinates, with \(v(p)=1\).
An integral change of frame with integral inverse preserves the
integrality of a truncated vector series, so the first degrees do not
depend on using a Hodge-adapted frame.

**Proof.** Work over the Witt ring of the residue field and choose a frame
with \(\omega(t)\) generated by the first basis vector on the curve.
Normalize the initial vector to \(e_1\), which is harmless at primes
where it is primitive. Write \(U(t)\) for the horizontal matrix with
\(U(0)=I\), \(\sigma\) for Witt Frobenius, and \(\Phi(t)\) for crystalline
Frobenius on the lift \(t\mapsto t^p\). These matrices satisfy
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_k(t)&=\Phi(t)\Phi^\sigma(t^p)\cdots
               \Phi^{\sigma^{k-1}}(t^{p^{k-1}}),\\
 Y(t)&=\Phi_k(t)U^{\sigma^k}(t^{p^k})\Phi_k(0)^{-1}e_1 .
 \end{aligned}
\]
The identity follows by horizontality and evaluation at zero. Relative
Frobenius and Verschiebung have composite multiplication by \(p\).
On reduction \(\bar\Phi(t)\) has image the conjugate line \(C(t)\) and
kernel \(\omega^\sigma(t^p)\). These are the same crystalline identifications
used in the projective criterion. [Lam–Litt, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13175).

Write \(\bar\Phi(t)=c(t)e_2^{\mathsf T}\) with \(c(t)\) generating \(C(t)\)
and put \(h(t)=e_2^{\mathsf T}c(t)\). The conjugate line is horizontal.
Thus \(h(0)\) is a unit in the ordinary case. In the nonordinary case
\(C(0)=\omega(0)\), and \(h(t)\) has a simple zero. Indeed the second
coordinate of the equation \(\nabla c\in C\) at zero gives
\(h'(0)=-A_{21}(0)c_1(0)\), where \(A_{21}(0)\) is the chosen
Kodaira–Spencer coordinate in this frame. The iterated reduction is
\[
 \bar\Phi_k(t)=c(t)
       \left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1} h^{\sigma^j}(t^{p^j})\right)e_2^{\mathsf T}.
\]

In the ordinary case take \(k=r\). The reduction of \(\Phi_k(0)\)
has rank one, image \(C(0)\), and kernel \(\omega^{\sigma^k}(0)\).
The determinant has valuation \(k\), since the elementary divisors of
a single crystalline Frobenius are \(1,p\). By the adjugate formula,
\(b=p^r\Phi_k(0)^{-1}e_1\) is integral with nonzero reduction in
\(\omega^{\sigma^k}(0)\). The reduction is nonzero because \(e_1\)
lies outside the image on reduction.

In the nonordinary case take \(k=2r\). For the fiber lattice \(M\), let
\(F=\Phi(0)\sigma\). The image and kernel of the reduction of \(F\) are
both \(C(0)=\omega(0)\), so \(F^2M\subseteq pM\). The determinant
valuation of \(F^2\) is two, giving equality. The integral semilinear
automorphism \(G=F^2/p\) commutes with \(F\), and hence its reduction
preserves \(C(0)\). It follows by applying \(G^{-r}\) that
\(b=p^r\Phi_k(0)^{-1}e_1\) again has nonzero reduction in
\(\omega^{\sigma^k}(0)\) in the twisted lattice.

In either case the coefficients of \(p^rY(t)\) in degrees less than
\(p^{k+1}\) are integral. The Frobenius identity uses only the coefficients
of \(U^{\sigma^k}(s)\) of degrees less than \(p\) in this range, and the
horizontal recursion gives integral coefficients up to degree \(p-1\).
The scalar series \(e_2^{\mathsf T}U^{\sigma^k}(s)b\) starts on reduction
with a nonzero term of degree one in \(s\). Its constant coefficient is
zero, and its linear coefficient is nonzero by the Kodaira–Spencer
coordinate and \(U'(0)=-A(0)\). Therefore the leading nonzero reduction
of \(p^rY\) is in degree \(p^k\) in the ordinary case and
\(p^k+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}p^j\) in the nonordinary case. Both degrees are
less than \(p^{k+1}\). This proves the claimed first degrees and
valuations. \(\square\)

In particular the maximum denominator exponent through degree \(N\) is
exactly the number of thresholds \(n_r\le N\). It is bounded by
\(\lfloor\log_p N\rfloor\) for \(N\ge1\) in either case. The logarithmic
growth rates \(1\) and \(1/2\) accord with the rank-two comparison between
logarithmic growth and Frobenius slopes, including the ordinary and
supersingular Legendre disks. The result here gives the exact first
coefficient for each denominator exponent and a specified Hodge initial
vector. [Chiarellotto–Tsuzuki, JIMJ 8 (2009), §7](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1474748009000012).

For an exact check, take the Legendre family at \(\lambda=2+t\) in the
frame \((\omega,\nabla_{\partial_t}\omega)\). With
\(L=\lambda(1-\lambda)\), the horizontal lift \((u,w)^{\mathsf T}\)
through \((1,0)^{\mathsf T}\) satisfies
\(Lu'=-w/4\) and \(Lw'=-Lu+L'w\). Recursion over \(\mathbb Q\) gives the
following first degrees. The \(\lambda=2\) elliptic curve has \(j=1728\).

| \(p\) | First degrees for successive powers of \(p\) |
|---:|:---|
| 3 | \(12,120,1092\) |
| 5 | \(5,25,125,625\) |
| 7 | \(56,2800\) |
| 13 | \(13,169\) |

The [existing exact check](verify_cm_newton_split_projective_jets.py)
tests the first vector and projective denominators at the equivalent
Legendre parameter \(\lambda=-1\). These finite checks serve as examples
of the characteristic-zero coefficient recursions.

## One fixed tensor and a prime-weighted denominator law

Return to all \(d\) rank-two summands and their horizontal Hodge vectors
\(Y_i(t)\) on the same curve germ. Use the fixed section
\[
 W(t)=Y_1(t)\otimes\cdots\otimes Y_d(t).
\]
Its coordinates satisfy linear differential equations over \(K(t)\).
The connection is algebraic over the function field of the curve, which
is finite over \(K(t)\), so its restriction of scalars is a finite
dimensional differential system over \(K(t)\).

Let \(S\) be the set of good degree-one primes \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\)
whose rational characteristic \(p\) splits completely in the real
multiplication field. Exclude the fixed finite set used above. Let
\(f_{\mathfrak p}\) be the \(p\)-rank of the fiber at \(\mathfrak p\),
equivalently the number of ordinary components. Define the truncated
denominator ideal supported on \(S\) by
\[
 \mathfrak D_N(W)=\prod_{\mathfrak p\in S}\mathfrak p^{e_{\mathfrak p}(N)},\qquad
 e_{\mathfrak p}(N)=
 \max\!\left(0,\ \max_{0\le j\le N}-v_{\mathfrak p}([t^j]W)\right).
\]
For \(1\le r\le d\) put
\(\Theta_{\ge r}(X)=\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S,\,p\le X,\,f_{\mathfrak p}\ge r}\log p\).

**Theorem.** As \(N\to\infty\),
\[
 \log \mathrm N\mathfrak D_N(W)
   =\sum_{r=1}^d\Theta_{\ge r}(N/r)+O_{K,d}(\sqrt N).
\]

**Proof.** Suppose \(\sqrt N<p\le N\). Through degree \(N\) any
denominator in a component has exponent at most one. Only ordinary
components can supply denominators, and any such term has degree at
least \(p\). Hence
\(e_{\mathfrak p}(N)\le\min(f_{\mathfrak p},\lfloor N/p\rfloor)\).
For an ordinary component reduce
\(c_i=p[t^p]Y_i\) and \(v_i=Y_i(0)\) modulo \(p\).
The vector \(c_i\) is nonzero in \(C_i(0)\), and \(v_i\) is outside it,
so \(c_i,v_i\) are independent. If \(r\le f_{\mathfrak p}\) and
\(rp\le N\), reduction of \(p^r[t^{rp}]W\) is the sum of the tensors indexed
by the \(r\)-element subsets of the ordinary components. Such a tensor
uses \(c_i\) on the subset and \(v_i\) off it. Indeed a summand of
valuation \(-r\) must choose degree at least \(p\) in \(r\) different
components, forcing each such degree to be \(p\) and all the other
degrees to be zero. The resulting tensors are independent in the
product of the chosen two-element bases for the ordinary components,
with a fixed nonzero tensor on the others. The sum is nonzero. Thus
\[
 e_{\mathfrak p}(N)
   =\min(f_{\mathfrak p},\lfloor N/p\rfloor)
   =\sum_{r=1}^d{\boldsymbol 1}_{f_{\mathfrak p}\ge r,\ p\le N/r}.
\]

At \(p>N\) the exponent is zero. For \(p\le\sqrt N\) the preceding
vector theorem gives \(e_{\mathfrak p}(N)\le d\lfloor\log_p N\rfloor\)
by expanding the tensor coefficient. Each such prime ideal contributes
at most \(d\log N\), and there are at most \([K:\mathbb Q]\pi(\sqrt N)\)
of them. The bound \(\pi(X)=O(X/\log X)\) gives \(O_{K,d}(\sqrt N)\) for
their total, and also for their contribution to the sum of the
\(\Theta_{\ge r}\). Summing the exact expression for the large primes
proves the formula. \(\square\)

For \(d=3\), enlarge \(K\) to make the \(\ell\)-torsion rational for one
prime \(\ell>3\). At a degree-one place the relative Frobenius trace \(a\)
has \(|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(a)|\le8p^{3/2}\). Two nonordinary components would
give \(p^2\mid N_{F/\mathbb Q}(a)\) and then \(a=0\) for \(p>64\).
The absolute trace is \(6\bmod\ell\) by rational torsion, which
excludes \(a=0\). Discarding the finite exceptional set, write
\(\Theta_S\) and \(\Theta_{\rm ord}\) for the weights of all and ordinary
places in \(S\). The formula becomes
\[
 \log \mathrm N\mathfrak D_N(W)=
   \Theta_S(N)+\Theta_S(N/2)+\Theta_{\rm ord}(N/3)
   +O_K(\sqrt N).
\]
Because \(K\) contains the normal closure of \(F\), its degree-one
places outside the ramification set lie over rational primes split
completely in \(F\). The prime ideal theorem, with higher residue degrees
contributing \(o(X)\), gives \(\Theta_S(X)\sim X\). By partial summation,
density-one ordinarity is therefore equivalent to
\(\log \mathrm N\mathfrak D_N(W)=\frac{11}{6}N+o(N)\).

This equivalence concerns the particular Hodge tensor. The entire
fundamental matrix for an individual summand already has a \(p\)-denominator
at degree \(p\) in both Newton types: some constant basis vector is outside
the conjugate line, and its \(p\)-jet detects the nonzero curvature.
Arithmetic holonomy bounds using fixed coefficientwise denominator types
and archimedean conformal-size hypotheses do not establish the maximal
rate of the displayed denominator ideal. In particular, the exact formula
here gives a rate indexed by the ordinary prime set itself, whereas the
printed denominator types involve specified products of least common
multiples. [Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang, Theorems 6.0.2, 7.0.1 and 8.0.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15403).
