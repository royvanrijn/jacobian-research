# Projective Frobenius spectra and Newton polygons in arbitrary rank

Let \(A/K\) be a polarized abelian variety over a number field with a
Rosati-compatible CM coefficient field \(E\), maximal real subfield \(F\),
\(e=[F:\mathbb Q]\), and relative rank \(h=\dim(A)/e\ge2\). We use the
covariant weight convention with Hodge–Tate weights 0 and 1. The Hodge
multiplicity \(m_\sigma\) is the number of weights 1 in the relative
component \(V_\sigma\). Conjugation sends it to \(h-m_\sigma\). A pair
is mixed if \(0<m_\sigma<h\), and definite otherwise.

Pass to a finite extension defining the action and polarization, containing
a normal closure \(N\) of \(E\), and satisfying the finite-extension
hypotheses of the [simultaneous Newton inequality](../../../unpublished/cm-newton-polygon-constraints/main.tex).
Also take connected auxiliary \(\ell\)-adic algebraic monodromy, with
\(\ell\) split in \(N\). For an absolute degree-one good place \(v\) away
from the auxiliary and exceptional primes let \(a_j(v)\in E\) be the
elementary symmetric Frobenius coefficients, with \(a_0=1\) and
\(\delta=a_h\). Compatibility is as in the simultaneous Newton proof.
The polarization identities are
\[
 \bar\delta=p^h/\delta,\qquad \bar a_j=p^j a_{h-j}/\delta.
\]
For \(\mathbf k=(k_1,\ldots,k_{h-1})\) with all \(k_j\ge0\) and
\(w=\sum j k_j\equiv0\pmod h\), put
\[
 I_{\mathbf k}(v)=\delta^{-w/h}\prod_{j=1}^{h-1}a_j(v)^{k_j}.
\]
Define \(E_0\subseteq E\) by adjoining these values over all such places
and \(F_0=E_0\cap F\). The identities give
\(\overline{I_{\mathbf k}}=I_{\mathbf k^{\mathrm{rev}}}\) with
\(k^{\mathrm{rev}}_j=k_{h-j}\). Thus \(E_0\) is totally real or CM.

## Compression by the projective spectrum field

**Theorem.** Put \(d=[F:F_0]\). The classes of conjugate embedding pairs
with identical projective Frobenius spectra up to reversal at all good
absolute degree-one places are the restriction fibres over embeddings of
\(F_0\). Every class has size \(d\). It has a common unordered Hodge
signature and, at every good split absolute degree-one place, a common
unordered pair of Newton polygons. If \(E_0=F_0\), every mixed class is
balanced and each of its component polygons equals its conjugate.

For a mixed signature, the representative polygons from the \(e/d\)
classes satisfy all the simultaneous Newton inequalities with \(e/d\)
in place of \(e\). Hence the numerical ordinarity criterion and numerical
lower \(p\)-rank bounds apply to the reduced signature, with the resulting
\(p\)-rank bound multiplied by \(d\). A definite signature is ordinary
at all the split places under consideration. The field \(E_0\) is
unchanged by further finite extensions and independent of the auxiliary
prime after passage to a common connected extension. It is generated by
the coefficients \(I_{\mathbf k}(v)\) at a density-one set of single places
\(v\), in the sense that each such place generates all of \(E_0\).

### Projective coefficient comparison

Fix an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Choose
\(\eta^h=\delta\) for a multiset of \(h\) nonzero eigenvalues. Its
normalized coefficients are \(x_j=a_j/\eta^j\), with normalized determinant
one. Changing the determinant root acts on \(x_j\) by \(\zeta^j\) for
\(\zeta^h=1\). The monomials above are exactly the monomial invariants of
this action. It suffices to use \(x_j^h\) and the invariant monomials with
all exponents less than \(h\). If two vectors agree on these invariants,
their supports agree. On that support the coordinate ratios \(\xi_j\)
are \(h\)-th roots of unity. The character
\((k_j)_j\mapsto\prod\xi_j^{k_j}\) on the product of copies of
\(\mathbb Z/h\) is trivial on the kernel of \((k_j)\mapsto\sum j k_j\),
by the invariant comparisons with exponents represented in \([0,h-1]\).
It therefore factors through the image in \(\mathbb Z/h\). A character
on a subgroup of this cyclic group extends to the whole group, so
\(\xi_j=\zeta^j\) for a common \(\zeta\). This proves that the projective
spectrum, meaning the eigenvalue multiset up to a common scalar, is
determined by the invariants. Reversal means \(x_j\mapsto x_{h-j}\), or
equivalently inverting the normalized eigenvalues.

Each invariant is a regular class function on the algebraic monodromy
group \(G\), since the determinants are units in its coordinate ring.
Chebotarev makes oriented equality of these functions equivalent to
equality at all the indicated Frobenius places. Absolute degree-one
conditions remove a density-zero set of primes. If equality up to
reversal holds at all such places, their Frobenius classes belong to the
union of two closed loci, one for each orientation. Since connected
\(G\) is irreducible, at least one locus is all of \(G\). Thus the
comparison can be made with a fixed choice of relative orientation for
each pair. Agreement in that orientation is precisely agreement on
\(E_0\). If \(E_0\) is CM, \(E=FE_0\), and over a fixed embedding of
\(F_0\) the pairs can all be oriented by one chosen embedding of
\(E_0\). If \(E_0\) is real, both orientations restrict to the same
embedding. This proves the fibre description and size.

Finite extensions preserve the connected monodromy group and hence
preserve all these equalities. Each proper subfield of \(E_0\) identifies
two embeddings that differ on some invariant on \(G\). The corresponding
equality locus then has density zero by algebraic Chebotarev. There are
only finitely many subfields, proving the single-prime statement.
Compatibility of the \(E\)-valued coefficients gives independence of
\(\ell\) by passing to a common connected extension.
[Rajan, *On strong multiplicity one for l-adic representations*, Theorem 3](https://repository.ias.ac.in/38317/1/2-PUB.pdf).

### From projective spectra to local polygons

For two oriented embeddings with equivalent projective spectra, the
representations
\[
 \operatorname{Sym}^h V_\sigma\otimes (\det V_\sigma)^{-1}
\]
have equal characters on \(G\), since the central scalar cancels. Their
semisimplifications agree. The Hodge–Tate weight multiset is unchanged
under semisimplification here: these representations are de Rham at the
chosen \(\ell\)-adic place, and the de Rham category is closed under
subquotients with exact filtered comparison. For a mixed component the
two extreme weights are \(-m_\sigma\) and \(h-m_\sigma\), both attained.
For a definite component every weight is zero. The extremes therefore
recover the multiplicity in the mixed case and distinguish mixed from
definite in all cases. The input comparison of weights for abelian
varieties is [Faltings, *p-adic Hodge theory*](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~bhattb/almost_purity_2011/Faltings_-_p-adic-Hodge-theory.pdf).

At a good split absolute degree-one place the sum of slopes in the
oriented component is its Hodge multiplicity. Label the local coefficient
components using the embedding of \(N\) induced by that place. If these
labels are compared with a different fixed embedding of \(N\), both
oriented labels are simultaneously translated by the same Galois
element. The invariant comparisons are preserved under this translation
and the Hodge–Tate argument applies also to the translated labels.
For two oriented mixed
components with equal projective spectra the two slope lists differ by a
constant, the valuation of the common scalar. Their sums agree, so the
constant is zero. Definite pairs have the polygons with all slopes zero
and all slopes one. When \(E_0\) is real the mixed component can also be
compared with its own conjugate in the same orientation of projective
data, so \(m_\sigma=h/2\) and the two polygons coincide.

Write \(C_j(P)\) for the sum of the two rounded slope prefixes of length
\(j\) in a pair. The original bound becomes
\[
 d\sum_{P\text{ a class representative}}C_j(P)\le ej-1.
\]
Since the sum is integral it is at most \((e/d)j-1\). The zero slopes
also occur in copies of \(d\) on these classes. This proves the
compression and lets one apply the [symbolic classification and sharp
numerical minimum](CM_NEWTON_EXTREMAL_POLYGONS.md) to the compressed
signature. In particular, a reduced signature with one multiplicity-one
mixed pair, with two mixed pairs in rank two, or with one balanced pair
in rank four is ordinary. At \(h=2\) the coefficient field is real,
generated by \(a_1^2/\delta\), and the result recovers the
[projective trace-field bound](CM_NEWTON_PROJECTIVE_TRACE_FIELD.md).

## The additional constraint from a real projective field

**Corollary.** Suppose \(E_0\) is real and there are \(B>0\) mixed classes
in the reduced signature. Then \(h=2H\) and the \(p\)-rank bound is
\[
 f_p(A)\ \ge\ d\left[h(e/d-B)+2\left(\left\lceil\frac{B+1}{2}\right\rceil+
                             \mathbf1_{H\ge2}\right)\right].
\]
The bound is attained among numerical polygons subject to the compressed
inequalities and conjugation symmetry of each mixed component.

In one such component there are equally many zeros and ones, say \(z\)
of each. The least endpoint polygon in the symbolic classification is
conjugation symmetric when the endpoint counts are equal. For
\(1\le j\le H\) its penalty over the ordinary pair is
\(2\mathbf1_{z<j}\), and the ordinary slack is \(Bj\). Consequently
\[
 \#\{i:z_i\ge j\}\ge\max(0,B-\lfloor(Bj-1)/2\rfloor).
\]
These lower counts are \(\lceil(B+1)/2\rceil\) at \(j=1\), 1 at \(j=2\)
when \(H\ge2\), and zero at all larger \(j\). All are attained
simultaneously by choosing that many components with \(z\ge1\), exactly
one with \(z=2\) if \(H\ge2\), and the rest with \(z=0\). The excess
penalties and slack for \(j>H\) repeat those for \(h-j\). Summing the
lower counts gives the formula, as a pair contributes \(2z\) zero slopes.

## Linear linkage and examples with nonreal projective field

**Proposition.** For two oriented projectively equivalent embeddings,
\(V_\sigma\) and \(V_\tau\) become isomorphic over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) over a finite extension if and only if the
complete Hodge profiles \(\gamma\mapsto m_{\gamma\sigma}\) and
\(\gamma\mapsto m_{\gamma\tau}\) agree for \(\gamma\in\operatorname{Gal}(N/\mathbb Q)\).

When the profiles agree, at a split degree-one prime the ratio of
oriented determinants is a unit at every prime of \(N\) above \(p\),
by the profile equality. Off \(p\) the determinants are units by the
Weil and polarization identities. Its absolute value at each complex
embedding is one, so the ratio is a root of unity in the fixed number
field \(N\). By connectedness and Chebotarev the ratio is identically
one on \(G\). Projectively equivalent spectra with identical determinant
have all coefficient vectors related by one of the \(h\) scalar roots
of unity. The loci for these finitely many relations are closed and
cover \(G\). Only the relation with scalar one contains the identity,
since the trace there is \(h\). Irreducibility of \(G\) and semisimplicity
of Tate representations give the isomorphism. Conversely an isomorphism
gives equal determinants at all the split places even after extending
the base, hence identical valuations at their conjugate primes and equal
complete profiles. This generalizes the rank-two
[determinant-profile argument](CM_NEWTON_PROJECTIVE_GEOMETRIC_CASES.md).

**Corollary.** In relative rank three there are absolutely simple examples
with full geometric endomorphism algebra \(E\), nonreal \(E_0\), and
arbitrarily large \([F:F_0]\). Each example has density-one ordinary
reduction after finite extension. In particular projective compression
can hold without an isogeny decomposition of the underlying variety.

Use \(B=\operatorname{Jac}(y^5=x^4-x-1)\), of dimension six with the
coefficient field \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)\). There are five branch
points, all of exponent one, including infinity. Replacing the deck
generator changes the displayed exponent list to the all-twos list in
Moonen's table. The component Hodge multiplicities are \(0,1,2,3\) up to
choice of orientation, as in the
cyclic-cover formula of [Moonen, family 16](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.3400).
The polynomial has Galois group \(S_4\) and discriminant \(-283\).
Its splitting field meets \(K_0\) trivially, since the only possible
quadratic intersection would have to be both \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-283})\)
and \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{5})\), and \(S_4\) has no larger abelian quotient.
[Zarhin, Corollary 1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605028) gives
\(\operatorname{End}^0_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(B)=K_0\). The theorem
forces its projective field to be nonreal, since it has an odd-rank
mixed component. The only nonreal subfield of \(K_0\) is \(K_0\) itself.

Now let \(M=\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)^+\) for an odd prime \(q\ne5\), with
\(d=[M:\mathbb Q]\ge2\), and set \(E=K_0M\). The fields are disjoint
by ramification. Extend the coefficient action on \(B^d\) and use the
weight-zero CM scalar twist of the [rank-two geometric construction](CM_NEWTON_PROJECTIVE_GEOMETRIC_CASES.md).
That construction uses the scalar to switch weights 0 and 1 at a
definite pair and preserves them elsewhere, in any relative rank. Switch
exactly one of the \(d\) definite pairs of the extended action. The
projective data are unchanged by the scalar twist, so \(E_0=K_0\).
Its mixed class has rank three and smaller multiplicity one, which gives
the stated ordinarity. The summands are absolutely irreducible after
finite extension by the endomorphism calculation for \(B\) and the
scalar twist. Distinct oriented embeddings over \(K_0\) have different
full Hodge profiles: a conjugate taking the first embedding to a switched
definite orientation sends the second to an unswitched one over the same
embedding of \(K_0\). Embeddings over different embeddings of \(K_0\)
are already separated by the projective field. By the proposition and
the Tate endomorphism theorem the geometric centralizer is exactly
\(E\). A field has no nontrivial idempotent, giving absolute simplicity.
The good reduction assertion for these twists can also be transported
from the ordinary reductions of \(B\) using the scalar construction.
The base \(B\) has simple signature and full geometric endomorphism
field, so it also lies in the scope of Cantoral Farfán–Li–Mantovan–Pries–Tang.
The twists for \(d\ge2\) have \(d\) mixed pairs despite their absolute
simplicity and coefficient endomorphism field.
[Cantoral Farfán–Li–Mantovan–Pries–Tang, Assumption 2.5 and Theorem 4.18](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11174v1#S4.SS5).
