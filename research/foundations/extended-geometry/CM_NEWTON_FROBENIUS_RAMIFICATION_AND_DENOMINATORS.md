# Frobenius ramification and denominator tests for real multiplication

Let \(F\) be totally real of degree \(g\ge2\), let \(L\) be its
normal closure, and let \(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_g\) be its embeddings
into \(L\). For a \(g\)-fold with Rosati-compatible real multiplication by
\(F\), at a good prime of absolute residue degree one where the action is
defined and integral at \(p\), the relative Frobenius polynomial is
\(X^2-aX+p\) with \(a\in\mathcal O_F\). Its scalar determinant follows
from the alternating pairing on each rank-two factor. The degree
polynomial and trace comparison give the relative trace, as in the
[CM norm argument](CM_NEWTON_PROJECTIVE_TRACE_FIELD.md). Write
\(a_i=\sigma_i(a)\), \(D_i=a_i^2-4p\), and
\(M=L(\sqrt{D_1},\ldots,\sqrt{D_g})\). We consider odd \(p\)
unramified in \(L\). These calculations also apply to the prime-field
models in the [split polynomial obstruction](CM_NEWTON_SPLIT_POLYNOMIAL_OBSTRUCTION.md).

## Inertia and the Frobenius Weyl group

For every prime \(\mathfrak P\) of \(L\) above \(p\), let
\(I_{\mathfrak P}=\{i:v_{\mathfrak P}(a_i)>0\}\). Each valuation is
normalized by \(v_{\mathfrak P}(p)=1\).
If \(i\notin I_{\mathfrak P}\), then \(D_i=a_i^2(1-4p/a_i^2)\) is
a square in \(L_{\mathfrak P}\) by Hensel's lemma. If
\(i\in I_{\mathfrak P}\), then \(D_i=-4p(1-a_i^2/(4p))\) is in
the local square class of \(-p\). Consequently the decomposition
group over \(L\) is trivial if \(I_{\mathfrak P}\) is empty.
Otherwise it is the ramified group of order two that exchanges
the two roots exactly for the indices in \(I_{\mathfrak P}\).
In particular its residue degree over \(L_{\mathfrak P}\) is one.
This description applies to all the \(\mathfrak P\) over \(p\).

Identify \(\operatorname{Gal}(M/L)\) with the subgroup of
\((\mathbb Z/2)^g\) acting by these root exchanges. The subgroup
generated by inertia above \(p\) is the binary span of the
indicator vectors of the \(I_{\mathfrak P}\). Its fixed field
is the largest subextension unramified at every place above \(p\).
The span can be read directly from the translates of one indicator
under \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\).

**Theorem.** Suppose that \(a\) is divisible at exactly one prime of
\(F\) above \(p\), and that this prime has residue degree one.
Then the Frobenius splitting field over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(M\),
and
\[
 \operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)
   =(\mathbb Z/2)^g\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)
\]
in its paired-root representation. If \(N/L\) is a fixed finite
extension unramified at all \(\mathfrak P\mid p\), then
\(M\cap N=L\). All reductions of this type are absolutely simple,
and their Frobenius angles have rank \(g\).
For odd \(g\), \(M\cap L(\sqrt p)=L\) as well.

**Proof.** At each prime of \(L\) above \(p\) exactly one of the
\(g\) valuations of the \(a_i\) is positive. This counts embeddings
of the degree-one completion of the selected prime of \(F\).
Transitivity on the \(g\) embeddings makes every singleton occur.
The inertia exchanges therefore generate the full group of
coordinate sign changes. Equivalently the \(D_i\) are independent
in \(L^\times/L^{\times2}\), as seen by their singleton odd valuations.
If \(\mathbb Q(a)\) were a proper subfield of \(F\), then every
embedding of \(\mathbb Q(a)\) would have at least two extensions
and each positive \(v_{\mathfrak P}(a_i)\) would repeat. Hence
\(F=\mathbb Q(a)\). The roots of the rational Frobenius polynomial
recover all the \(a_i\) by \(\alpha_i+p/\alpha_i\), so its splitting
field contains \(L\) and is \(M\). Its permutation action lies in
the displayed semidirect product, and has the same order. Every
nontrivial subextension of \(M/L\) contains a quadratic subfield
\(L(\sqrt{\prod_{i\in J}D_i})\) for a nonempty \(J\). This subfield
is ramified at the \(\mathfrak P\) with singleton in \(J\), proving
the assertion about \(N\). Its relative discriminant norm has
\(p\)-valuation \([L:\mathbb Q]|J|/g\): ramification is tame
quadratic, the primes above \(p\) have equal residue degree, and
each singleton occurs equally often.

For the angles, put \(\beta_i=\alpha_i^2/p\), using one root from
each pair. None is a root of unity. For each \(i\) there is a prime
of \(L\) above \(p\) at which its component is ordinary, giving
nonzero valuation on \(\beta_i\) at any place above it. If
\(\prod\beta_i^{m_i}\) is torsion, apply the individual sign
change at index \(i\) and divide by the original relation.
This forces \(\beta_i^{2m_i}\) to be torsion and hence \(m_i=0\).
The same independence shows that ratios of distinct eigenvalues
are not roots of unity: within a pair the ratio is \(\beta_i\),
and between pairs its square is a product of two signed
\(\beta_i\)'s. The powers of the \(2g\) distinct
roots therefore stay distinct. Each powering is Galois equivariant
and the action stays transitive, so the Frobenius polynomial
remains irreducible after any finite residue extension.
This gives absolute simplicity.

If \(p\) became a square in \(M\), its class in \(L^\times/L^{\times2}\)
would be a product of the \(D_i\). The all-ones valuation vector of
\(p\) forces taking all the \(D_i\). Each \(D_i\) is totally negative,
since the relative Frobenius roots have absolute value \(\sqrt p\)
and equality \(a_i^2=4p\) is impossible by the odd valuation of
\(4p\). When \(g\) is odd, the product is totally negative, while
\(p\) is positive. This is a contradiction in the totally real
field \(L\). \(\square\)

In particular the one-failed-pair pattern for split real-cubic
RM threefolds forces full relative Weyl group \((\mathbb Z/2)^3\),
maximum angle rank, and the same disjointness over any fixed
auxiliary field for all sufficiently large \(p\). The generic
Weyl-group conclusion in Zywina's Theorem 1.5 and absolute
simplicity therefore cannot exclude this residual pattern.
The finite-field classification of Arango-Piñeros, Frengley,
and Vemulapalli already gives almost ordinary threefolds with maximum
angle rank, including the possibilities with both cyclic and
noncyclic cubic real parts. The fixed \(F\) and degree-one
ramification hypothesis here explains the precise inertia and
forces the maximal group on every instance of this pattern.
[Zywina, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0624)
· [Arango-Piñeros, Frengley and Vemulapalli, Theorem 6.1 and Table A.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.03358v2)
· [Dupuy, Kedlaya and Zureick-Brown, §3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-023-02633-7).

## A denominator sieve for a fixed variety

Now let \(A/K\) be fixed with full geometric endomorphism algebra
\(F\) of degree \(\dim A=g\), with its endomorphisms defined over
\(K\). Fix a finite set \(S\) of rational primes. Use either the
relative trace coordinates
\[
 (a_1,\ldots,a_g,p)\in\mathbb A^g\times\mathbb G_m
\]
or the labelled eigenvalue coordinates
\[
 (s,t_1,\ldots,t_g)\in\mathbb G_m^{g+1},\qquad
 s^2=p,\qquad t_i=\alpha_i/s.
\]
Each trace is algebraic in \(L\) and the eigenvalue coordinates may
be taken in \(M(\sqrt p)\). Allow both square roots \(s\) and both
roots \(\alpha_i\) at each \(i\).

**Proposition.** Let \(R=P/Q\) be a rational function over \(\mathbb Q\)
in one of these coordinate spaces, with \(P,Q\) coprime in its
coordinate ring. Suppose it has a pole on that space. For each
integer \(B\ge1\), on a density-one set of good absolute degree-one
primes \(v\) of \(K\), there are at least \(B\) rational primes
\(\ell\notin S\cup\{p\}\) at which the function takes a nonintegral
value at some place above \(\ell\). In the eigenvalue case this
means that for each such \(\ell\) some labelling of the roots has
a nonintegral value. Whenever all labels are Galois conjugate
over \(L\), each labelling has a nonintegral value at some place
above each such \(\ell\).

**Proof.** Take a geometrically irreducible component of \(Q=0\)
on which \(P\) is generically nonzero. There is one since \(P,Q\)
are coprime. This divisor and its nonempty open where \(P\ne0\)
are defined over a fixed number field. After excluding finitely
many primes, reduction at primes of a finite normal closure of this
field preserves its dimension \(g\) and geometric irreducibility.
For rational \(\ell\) split in that field and \(L\), Lang–Weil
gives at least \(c\ell^g\) points on the open, uniformly in \(\ell\)
for some \(c>0\). In the eigenvalue case push these points to
trace coordinates by
\[
 (s,t_i)\longmapsto (s(t_i+t_i^{-1}),s^2).
\]
This is a finite morphism with at most \(2^{g+1}\) points in
a fibre. In either case there are at least \(c'\ell^g\) distinct
trace-determinant tuples exhibiting the proposed pole.

For large split \(\ell\), the residual image is the group
\[
 G_\ell=\{(g_i)\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)^g:
                    \det g_i=\det g_j \text{ for all }i,j\}.
\]
Indeed apply Lombardo's surjectivity theorem over a finite
extension of \(K\) giving Serre's independence of the Tate
representations. Over that extension the individual images
at all large split \(\ell\) are full and their finite products
are full. Over \(K\) the finite products are also full, since
they are contained in the indicated groups and contain the
same image over the extension. The determinant condition
follows from the polarization.

Every fixed trace and nonzero determinant pair occurs in a
conjugacy class of at least
\(\lvert\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)\rvert/\ell^2\) matrices.
Take the companion matrix of the quadratic, whose matrix
centralizer ring has size \(\ell^2\). This also works for
repeated roots. The order of \(G_\ell\) is
\((\ell-1)\lvert\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)\rvert^g\).
Thus at least \(c''/\ell\) of the elements of \(G_\ell\)
have tuples on the listed pole images. Attach this condition to
one fixed place of \(L\) above \(\ell\) using the \(\ell\)-adic
labelling of the \(\sigma_i\). If the condition holds, the
specified numerator is a unit and the denominator reduces to
zero. In the eigenvalue case choose roots reducing to the
specified values \(s\) and \(s t_i\) in the chosen algebraic
closure of the local field. This is possible for each quadratic
independently, even at a repeated residual root. A change of
labelling that is a Galois conjugacy carries the nonintegral
value to a possibly different place over the same \(\ell\).

One may discard the density-zero set where a denominator
vanishes in characteristic zero for some labelling. In trace
coordinates that condition is a proper algebraic subset. For
eigenvalue coordinates take the image of \(Q=0\) in trace
coordinates under the finite morphism above. Its dimension is
at most \(g\), so it too is a proper closed set. The
trace-determinant morphism from the connected algebraic
monodromy group is dominant, by the common-determinant image
and the companion matrices. Algebraic Chebotarev applies.

For any finite collection of the auxiliary \(\ell\), the
pole events have the product distribution by finite
Chebotarev and the residual product image. The sum of their
probabilities is unbounded as the collection increases,
since the reciprocal primes split in a fixed number field
have divergent sum. The variance of the number of events
is at most its mean. For each fixed \(B\), the probability
of fewer than \(B\) events thus tends to zero. The degree-one
restriction and the exclusion of \(p=\ell\) remove density-zero
sets for each finite collection. Taking upper natural density
of primes with fewer than \(B\) denominator primes proves
the assertion. \(\square\)
[Lombardo, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.1703)
· [Serre, Theorem 1 and §3.1](https://www.college-de-france.fr/media/jean-pierre-serre/UPL8570957518086251784_independance.pdf)
· [Lang and Weil, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372655).

In the real-cubic one-failed-pair pattern all the labels are
Galois conjugate by the ramification theorem, including the
choice of \(s\). In particular, a proof of integrality outside
\(S\cup\{p\}\) for a fixed rational expression with a genuine
pole would imply that those primes have density zero. Such an
integrality implication must come from extra arithmetic of the
fixed variety. A pointwise assertion about the Newton polygon
alone cannot supply it, by the following variant of the
finite-field construction.

## Imposing a pole on finite-field models

Suppose \(g=3\), and fix the auxiliary rational torsion level
\(\ell_0^N\) in the [split polynomial construction](CM_NEWTON_SPLIT_POLYNOMIAL_OBSTRUCTION.md).
For any fixed rational function with a pole on either coordinate
space above, there is an auxiliary prime \(\ell\ne\ell_0\) and
an infinite sequence of one-failed-pair models for which its
values have a pole at a place above \(\ell\). The rational prime
\(\ell\) can be chosen outside any prescribed finite set.

To see this, choose \(\ell\) and a residue point on the open
pole divisor as in the sieve. The target trace-determinant
tuple is \((\bar a_1,\bar a_2,\bar a_3,\bar p)\), with
\(\bar p\ne0\). Require \(p\equiv\bar p\bmod\ell\), as well as
the prescribed splitting in \(F(i)\) and \(p\equiv1\bmod\ell_0^{2N}\).
For \(\ell\) outside ramification, this is compatible by
Chebotarev: \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)\) is totally ramified at
\(\ell\), so it is disjoint from the fixed normal closure of
\(F(i,\zeta_{\ell_0^{2N}})\). Impose
\(a_i\equiv \bar a_i\bmod\ell\) at the three split coefficient
places. This is a single residue condition on \(a\bmod\ell\mathcal O_F\).
In the trace lattice construction one can simply use the fixed
modulus \(\ell\ell_0^{2N}\) and the prescribed residue class
by the Chinese remainder theorem. Representatives within a
bounded grid still attain the bad coefficient valuation one,
the two ordinary valuations zero, and any chosen limiting
normalized archimedean trace vector in the open cube.

All characteristic-zero denominators can be kept nonzero.
For trace polynomials choose a normalized archimedean target
where the highest weighted part of \(Q(a_i,p)\), at \(p=1\),
is nonzero. For eigenvalue Laurent polynomials choose unit-circle
targets for the \(t_i\), using the leading power of \(s\).
One can avoid finitely many nonzero Laurent polynomials on any
product of open arcs. This handles every labelling at once,
by taking the product of the finitely many transforms of \(Q\).
The real traces then approximate \(2\operatorname{Re}(t_i)\).
The desired local labelling is possible by reduction of the
quadratics, and all labels are conjugate by the theorem.
Hence the rational expression cannot be universally integral
away from \(S\cup\{p\}\) on these models.

Consequently the only rational functions in \((s,t_1,t_2,t_3)\)
that are uniformly archimedean bounded and integral at all
places outside a fixed finite set of rational primes on every
one-failed-pair model are the constants satisfying that integrality
condition. A non-Laurent rational function fails by the auxiliary
pole construction. A Laurent polynomial fails by the
[eigenvalue obstruction](CM_NEWTON_EIGENVALUE_LAURENT_OBSTRUCTION.md),
at arbitrarily large residue primes if it is archimedean bounded.
For this conclusion one can use the common field
\(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_7+\zeta_7^{-1})\) and keep the prescribed
auxiliary torsion level.

The sieve uses the joint residual images of a fixed variety,
whereas the last construction concerns varying finite-field
varieties. The remaining split-ordinarity question concerns
how the residue prime itself interacts with the Frobenius
sequence of a fixed variety.
