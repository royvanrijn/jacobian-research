# Piecewise polynomial polyhedral laws and a fixed unit marker

A finite polyhedral polynomial-density law admits an all-order normalizer with one fixed Laurent unit marker. The theorem below assumes that every whole supporting hyperplane avoids the origin and that a requested finite geometric symmetry is Galois-stable. The construction handles signed extrapolated constants from cells away from the origin by a rational positive quadratic amplifier; it does not require continuity of the density across walls.

## 1. Precise hypotheses and conclusion

Fix integers r>=1 and b>=0. Let Omega be a bounded open subset of R^r partitioned, up to a set of Lebesgue measure zero, into finitely many disjoint full-dimensional open polyhedral cells Omega_s. Their closures are bounded polytopes. Assume0 lies in the interior of ONE cell Omega_0. Every codimension-one face of every cell lies in a specified finite family of REAL ALGEBRAIC AFFINE HYPERPLANES, and EVERY such supporting hyperplane avoids0. The last condition refers to whole hyperplanes, not merely their relatively open faces.

On Omega_s let rho=rho_s be a real polynomial of degree at most b, nonnegative on that cell, and assume

    integral_Omega rho=1,    C_0:=rho_0(0)>0.

No matching or continuity across partition faces is required. The coefficients of rho_s need not be rational or algebraic. Write their homogeneous decompositions about0 as

    rho_s(u)=sum_(j=0)^b rho_sj(u),    rho_s0=C_s=rho_s(0).

The constants C_s outside the origin cell can have either sign even though rho_s is nonnegative ON ITS OWN cell.

Let A be a commutative Q-algebra with normalized Q-linear functional lambda:A->Q and ACTUAL coordinates x_1,...,x_r satisfying

    lambda(P(x))=sum_s integral_(Omega_s) rho_s(u)P(u)du

for every P in Q[u_1,...,u_r]. Let Lambda on A[t,t^-1] be lambda followed by the constant term in t. Compatible native scalar extension is an additional hypothesis when asserting the same values over other characteristic-zero fields.

For a requested symmetry, let W be a FINITE subgroup of GL_r(Qbar), stable as a set under Gal(Qbar/Q), and assume a positive definite rational quadratic form q(u) is W-invariant. Thus this is a rational finite-group-scheme linear action; no rational or real representative of every geometric element is required. Without a symmetry requirement take W={1} and q=sum_i u_i^2. No invariance of Omega or its density under W is assumed.

**Theorem 1 (conditional analytic construction).** There are a W-invariant polynomial f in Q[u] with f(0)=1 and an integer L>=r+b such that

    T=1+t,    p=T*t^-1*f(Tx)^2,
    F=p^L(1+p),    H=t^r

are actual regular Laurent functions, Lambda(F^m)=0 and Lambda(HF^m)>0 in Q for EVERY integer m>=1. The SAME fixed UNIT marker H survives every order. If actual scalar-extended functionals satisfy lambda_K=lambda tensor_Q K, the identical zeros and nonzero rational values hold over every characteristic-zero K. Rational positivity is asserted before scalar extension, with no ordering of K assumed.

For the additional geometric conclusions suppose X=Spec A is a smooth geometrically integral affine Q-variety and the ACTUAL common fiber x_1=...=x_r=0 has a geometric point. Then F is absolutely not a unit times a proper power and has primitive actual Laurent support. Under an actual compatible W-action on these coordinates the constructed functions are invariant coefficientwise. Any descent to an actual quotient additionally needs its actual function-descent and trace-compatibility hypotheses.

## 2. Rational invariant boundary polynomial

Normalize a defining affine linear form of each original supporting hyperplane to ell(0)=1. This is possible exactly because it avoids0; all coefficients remain algebraic. Choose a finite Galois extension E/Q containing those coefficients and every entry of W. Galois stability of W ensures that the group generated by W and Gal(E/Q), acting semilinearly, is the FINITE semidirect product W semidirect Gal(E/Q).

Let Lset be the finite set of all DISTINCT normalized forms obtained from the original family by this finite action, using linear pullback ell(u)->ell(w^-1 u). Every form still has constant1, even if some new forms have nonreal coefficients. Put

    f_base(u)=product_(ell in Lset) ell(u).

Galois and W permute this finite set without scalar factors, since each constant is1. Hence f_base belongs to Q[u], is W-invariant and f_base(0)=1. It vanishes on EVERY face of EVERY actual cell, including the outer boundary. Additional real or nonreal hyperplanes cause no problem. The density law is used only on the original cells, and f_base is a real rational polynomial there.

The finite-group-scheme stability condition is substantive. A finite algebraic matrix group not Galois-stable need not have a finite simultaneous closure; Section8 gives an exact counterexample. When W acts over Q as an ordinary finite matrix group it automatically satisfies the condition. For a natural Q-defined Weyl group scheme its geometric matrix set is likewise Galois-stable.

## 3. Cellwise Euler zero flux and the exact coefficient identity

Let f be ANY rational polynomial with f(0)=1 vanishing on every cell face. For k>=1 define the polynomial in T

    J_skj(T)=T^(r+j) integral_(Omega_s) rho_sj(u) f(Tu)^(2k) du,
    nu_skj=J_skj(1).

For T>0, change of variables identifies J_skj(T) with the corresponding integral over T Omega_s. For other T we use only its displayed polynomial expression, so no sign error from a negatively dilated Lebesgue measure is involved.

Homogeneity of rho_sj and Euler's identity give

    J_skj'(T)=T^(r+j-1)
      * integral_(Omega_s) div_u[u rho_sj(u) f(Tu)^(2k)] du
    =T^(r+j-1)
      * integral_(boundary Omega_s) (u dot n)rho_sj(u)f(Tu)^(2k)dS.

The divergence identity for a bounded polytope follows by triangulation and the one-dimensional fundamental theorem on its faces; intersections of faces have measure zero. No smooth boundary assumption is needed.

On each face f(u)=0, so the polynomial f(Tu) is divisible by T-1 pointwise there. The boundary integral is therefore divisible by (T-1)^(2k), and

    J_skj(1+t)=nu_skj+O(t^(2k+1))                         (3.1)

as an EXACT polynomial Taylor statement. Each cell is handled separately; jumps of the density across faces create no additional term.

Apply the actual native moment law coefficientwise. Since T=1+t is a unit in R[[t]],

    lambda(p^k)=t^-k sum_sj T^(k-r-j)J_skj(T).

For every integer ell>=-k the requested coefficient degree k-ell is at most2k, so the remainder in (3.1) cannot contribute. Thus

    Lambda(t^ell p^k)
      =sum_(s,j) nu_skj binom(k-r-j,k-ell),                (3.2)

where binomial coefficients have their usual formal-power-series meaning for any integer upper index, and are0 for a negative lower index. Formula (3.2) is valid for EVERY k>=1 in its stated domain; it is not a bounded moment test.

Put a=r+b. When k>=a all upper indices k-r-j are nonnegative and at most k-r. The degree-k coefficient therefore vanishes, while the degree-(k-r) coefficient isolates j=0:

    Lambda(p^k)=0,
    Lambda(t^r p^k)=J_k,
    J_k=sum_s C_s integral_(Omega_s) f(u)^(2k)du,           (3.3)

for EVERY k>=a. Nonnegativity of the original rho does NOT by itself make this J_k positive, because the constants are extrapolated to0 from different cells.

## 4. Suppression of all negative constant cells

Use the rational positive W-invariant q from Section1. Choose a positive rational c so small that

    0<1-c q(u)<=1    on closure(Omega).

This is possible since the closure is compact. Let Neg be the finite set of cells with C_s<0. It does not contain0. Since a ball about0 is inside Omega_0 and the cell interiors are disjoint, every other cell closure has positive distance from0. Consequently, if Neg is nonempty,

    gamma=max_(s in Neg, u in closure Omega_s)(1-c q(u))<1.

Also gamma>0 under the strict choice of c. Set B=max_(closure Omega)|f_base|, a finite positive number. Choose an integer M>=0 with B gamma^M<1/4, and put

    f(u)=f_base(u)(1-c q(u))^M.                            (4.1)

Then f is rational, W-invariant, still vanishes on every cell face, f(0)=1, and |f|<1/4 on every negative-constant cell. If Neg is empty simply take M=0.

After this M is fixed, continuity provides a ball Ball contained in Omega_0 on which |f|>1/2. Set

    A_plus=C_0 volume(Ball)>0,
    A_minus=sum_(s in Neg)|C_s| volume(Omega_s)>=0.

Other nonnegative-constant cells only add positive terms. Hence for EVERY k>=1,

    J_k >= A_plus*4^-k - A_minus*16^-k
        =4^-k[A_plus-A_minus*4^-k].                       (4.2)

Choose K>=1 so that A_minus*4^-K<A_plus. Then J_k>0 for EVERY k>=K. The ball may depend on the already chosen M; K is chosen afterwards, so there is no circular choice of parameters. There is no requirement that f have a global maximum at0.

Only the existence of real bounds is used in this selection. With arbitrary real rho_s coefficients, no separate effective algebraic algorithm for computing these bounds is asserted. Native rationality of the COMPLETE marked values below needs no rationality of the individual nu_skj, constants or cell volumes.

## 5. The same fixed unit marker survives every order

Take an integer L>=max(a,K) and set F=p^L(1+p), H=t^r. For every m>=1,

    F^m=sum_(h=0)^m binom(m,h)p^(Lm+h).

Every k=Lm+h is at least a and K. By (3.3) each pure term vanishes and each t^r-marked term equals the positive J_k. Therefore

    Lambda(F^m)=0,
    Lambda(HF^m)=sum_(h=0)^m binom(m,h)J_(Lm+h)>0           (5.1)

at EVERY positive order. These exact numbers are rational because F,H and the actual native functional are rational. Compatible scalar extensions preserve their zeros and nonzeros. The coefficients in the analytic cell decomposition need not separately be rational.

There is also one rigorous necessary monomial bound. If ell<r is a fixed integer, then for all sufficiently large k, formula (3.2) applies and its lower index k-ell exceeds every nonnegative upper index k-r-j. Thus Lambda(t^ell p^k)=0 eventually. The expansion of F^m gives Lambda(t^ell F^m)=0 for all sufficiently large m. Since ell=r survives EVERY order, r is the LEAST unsafe integer Laurent-monomial exponent for the displayed phase. No assertion that every ell>r is unsafe, or of a sharp smallest L, is made.

## 6. Actual simple divisor and actual support

Assume the additional actual geometry. The nonempty common-zero fiber is of finite type over Q, so choose x0 over Qbar with x_i(x0)=0 for all i. Then

    p(x0,t)=T/t=1+t^-1,
    (1+p)(x0,t)=2+t^-1.

At t=-1/2 the modifying factor vanishes with t-derivative -4, while p=-1 is a unit. Smoothness of the ACTUAL product X times Gm gives a geometric prime divisor through this point on which ord(1+p)=1 and ord(p)=0. Hence ord(F)=1, excluding every unit-times-e-th-power factorization for e>=2. The same simple derivative argument works after every algebraically closed extension.

At the same ACTUAL point,

    F(x0,t)=(1+t^-1)^L(2+t^-1).

Its coefficients at exponents0 and-1 are2 and2L+1. Thus actual coefficient functions at adjacent exponents are nonzero, and their support differences have gcd1. This is a genuine downstairs geometric certificate whenever those actual functions and a smooth quotient model have separately been supplied.

## 7. What the symmetry conclusion does and does not supply

The formal W-invariance of f and the linear action imply f(Twu)=f(Tu), so p,F and the scalar Laurent unit H are invariant coefficientwise in any ACTUAL coordinate model transforming by this representation. This conclusion does not require invariance of an auxiliary square root, a real representative of each geometric group element, a W-invariant density or averaging a phase.

For an actual finite free quotient or homogeneous stabilizer extension, function descent and native trace restriction can then transport these GIVEN identities. The existence and smoothness of that quotient, its normalized trace, the coordinate transformation law and the actual common-zero fiber must be proved in the application. An abstract measure law alone supplies none of those geometric assertions.

A torus-normalizer application needs an actual rational coordinate map, this finite polyhedral law with a positive origin cell, the full supporting-wall condition, the genuine Galois-stable action and invariant positive rational form, and the actual fiber/trace/descent geometry. Those hypotheses are established separately in [the compact rational torus-normalizer theorem](COMPACT_RATIONAL_TORUS_NORMALIZER_PRODUCT_COUNTEREXAMPLES.md). They are not consequences of the conditional analytic theorem alone.

## 8. Two precise obstructions to weaker wall formulations

First, saying that0 lies in one cell and on no ACTUAL face is insufficient for the required boundary polynomial. For example take the two cells (-1,1)^2 and (0,1) times (2,3). The second cell has a relatively open facet on x=0 away from the origin. Any polynomial vanishing there vanishes identically on the entire hyperplane x=0, so it vanishes at0. Hence a polynomial with f(0)=1 cannot kill that face. The theorem requires every supporting hyperplane itself to avoid0.

Second, an arbitrary finite algebraic orthogonal W need not admit a rational invariant wall polynomial. In dimension2 let

    s=(1/3)[[1,-2sqrt(2)],[-2sqrt(2),-1]],    W={1,s}.

This is an order-two orthogonal group for the rational form x^2+y^2, but is not Galois-stable. The product of s with its Galois conjugate is a rotation with cosine -7/9. Its eigenvalue cannot be a root of unity: the rational number eigenvalue+inverse=-14/9 would then be an algebraic integer, which it is not.

Any rational W-invariant polynomial is also invariant under the Galois-conjugate reflection, hence under this infinite-order rotation. Over C, use coordinates z=x+iy and w=x-iy; invariance forces equal exponents of z and w in every monomial, so the polynomial is a polynomial in x^2+y^2. If it also vanishes on a relatively open facet in the line x=-1, it vanishes identically on that line, forcing this radial polynomial to be zero. It cannot have value1 at0. Thus finite Galois-stable closure is a real hypothesis, not automatic from finite order of the original algebraic matrices.

Both obstructions concern weakened formulations. These are the hypotheses used here to construct a polynomial vanishing on EVERY face. The examples do not assert that supporting walls through the origin preclude a different Euler-flux construction or an all-order counterexample.

Its bounded-cell zero-flux input can equally be proved coefficientwise by [the elementary bounded-open Fubini lemma](UNIFORM_BOUNDED_DOMAIN_NORMALIZER_CRITERION.md), Section2. The normalizer method retains the classical endpoint attribution recorded there; the complete calculation and new suppression bound are written above.
