# A two-term angular face for univariate negative winding

Let K be any field of characteristic zero. On
\[
 A_K=K[x,y,t]/(t^2+2xy-1)
\]
use the normalized polynomial functional
\[
 \Lambda(f)=\operatorname{CT}_x\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}
       f(x,(1-t^2)/(2x),t)\,dt.                       \tag{1}
\]
The integral means the rational polynomial rule. A phase is pure when all positive moments vanish, eventually pure when all sufficiently large moments vanish, and safe when every fixed marker in A_K has eventual mixed vanishing.

## 1. Exact classification of the phase class

**Theorem.** Let
\[
                 f=P(y)+xS(t),\qquad P\in K[y],\ S\in K[t].   \tag{2}
\]
The following conditions are equivalent:

1. f is pure.
2. f is eventually pure.
3. f is safe for every fixed marker in the full sphere algebra A_K.
4. P(0)=0 and either S=0 or P=0.

Every safe member satisfies the uniform sufficient cutoff
\[
              \Lambda(Qf^m)=0\quad\text{for }m>\deg Q         \tag{3}
\]
for every sphere marker Q, where degree is the sphere filtration degree. The zero phase is included. This is a classification of the specified phase class, not a statement that the class is an algebra or that the full sphere functional is Mathieu.

The substantive exclusion is stronger than a finite purity test: if P is nonconstant and S is nonzero, there are infinitely many nonzero positive moments, regardless of P(0).

## 2. A unique exposed two-term face

Assume first that the coefficients lie in a number field. Write
\[
 j=\deg P\ge1,\qquad v=\deg S\ge0,
 \qquad a=[y^j]P\ne0,\quad b=[t^v]S\ne0,
 \qquad L=j(v+2).                                            \tag{4}
\]
After eliminating y, let F(x,t) be the literal Laurent polynomial representing f. Assign to a monomial x^i t^h the integer weight
\[
                    W(i,h)=(j+1)h+(2j-v)i.                   \tag{5}
\]
A term of P with y-degree r expands into monomials x^{-r}t^{2s}, 0<=s<=r. Each has
\[
 W(-r,2s)\le (j+1)2r-(2j-v)r=(v+2)r\le L.
\]
Equality requires r=j and s=j. A term x t^h of xS(t) has
\[
 W(1,h)\le (j+1)v+2j-v=L,
\]
with equality only for h=v. Consequently the EXACT maximal face is
\[
               a(-1/2)^j x^{-j}t^{2j}+bxt^v.                \tag{6}
\]
No other term, including the constant term of P, has maximal weight. This argument allows v=0 and j=1 and does not require the coefficient 2j-v in (5) to be positive.

For any integer k>=1 put m=(j+1)k. In F^m, weight is at most mL. At angular exponent zero this gives t-height at most
\[
                          \frac{mL}{j+1}=kL.                 \tag{7}
\]
The coefficient at that height must use only (6). Balancing the angular exponent requires exactly k negative terms and jk positive terms, so
\[
 [x^0t^{kL}]F^m
   =\binom{(j+1)k}{k}\bigl(a(-1/2)^j\bigr)^k b^{jk}.          \tag{8}
\]
This is an exact coefficient identity, not an asymptotic or a positivity assertion.

## 3. A good-prime obstruction at infinitely many orders

Choose an odd rational prime p=1 modulo L outside the finite set at which a,b or any coefficient denominator fails to be integral with a,b units. Put
\[
                 k=(p-1)/L,\qquad m=(j+1)k.                  \tag{9}
\]
Because
\[
          L-(j+1)=j(v+1)-1\ge0,
\]
one has m<=p-1. All three factorial indices in the binomial coefficient of (8) are therefore below p. Its coefficient is a unit at every retained prime ideal above p.

By (7), the angular constant term of F^m is a polynomial of t-degree at most p-1. In the normalized interval rule, t^h has value zero for h odd and 1/(h+1) for h even. The ONLY denominator divisible by p is the one at h=p-1, which is even. Thus at any retained prime ideal above p,
\[
 p\Lambda(f^m)\equiv
 \binom{(j+1)k}{k}\bigl(a(-1/2)^j\bigr)^k b^{jk}
                   \not\equiv0.                             \tag{10}
\]
In particular the original characteristic-zero moment is nonzero.

There are infinitely many primes 1 modulo the fixed integer L>=2. For completeness, if they were all in a finite set, take a sufficiently large positive integer n divisible by L, those primes and all finite exclusions. A prime ell dividing Phi_L(n) divides neither n nor L, since Phi_L(0)=1, and is not in the prescribed set. The distinct cyclotomic factors of X^L-1 modulo ell show that n has exact multiplicative order L modulo ell. Hence ell=1 modulo L, a contradiction. Taking n large makes Phi_L(n)>1. This elementary argument also permits all finite bad-prime exclusions used above.

The orders (9) are unbounded, proving the stated infinite nonvanishing. Nothing is inferred from a finite prefix of moments.

## 4. Every characteristic-zero coefficient field and the remaining branches

For arbitrary K, form the finitely generated Q-subalgebra generated by the finitely many coefficients of P,S and by a^{-1},b^{-1}. A maximal-ideal specialization has a number-field residue field and preserves the exact degrees j,v. Moments specialize by the rational rule (1). ONE such specialization has infinitely many nonzero moments by Sections2--3. At each of those orders the original moment must be nonzero as well. No embedding of all of K into C and no order-dependent specialization is needed.

If P is constant, write it as c. Then \Lambda((c+xS(t))^m)=c^m, since every term using x has positive angular weight. If S=0, the same identity holds with c=P(0), since all other terms of P(y) have negative angular weight. Thus a nonzero constant prevents even eventual purity in either branch. Every remaining pure member is either P(y) with P(0)=0 or xS(t), or zero.

These members are safe in the FULL ambient algebra. A marker of filtration degree d has a finite Laurent expansion whose angular weights belong to [-d,d]. Positive powers of xS(t) have weight exactly m. If P(0)=0, every term of P(y)^m has angular weight at most -m. For m>d no product with that fixed marker has weight zero. This proves (3). Safety implies eventual purity by taking marker1, and purity trivially implies eventual purity. Together with the preceding exclusions, this proves all four equivalences.

## 5. The actual stripped degree-five consequence

Let q=xt^2. The specified stripped-surface slice
\[
 f=P(y)+qQ(t),\qquad \deg P\le5,\quad\deg Q\le2               \tag{11}
\]
has actual sphere degree at most five and is covered by the theorem, by taking S=t^2Q. Hence it contains no pure phase having both a nonconstant negative-winding polynomial P and a nonzero q term. Its entire pure locus is
\[
 \{P(y):P(0)=0\}\ \cup\ \{qQ(t)\},                           \tag{12}
\]
with the degree bounds understood. All of these phases are safe for full sphere markers.

This permits arbitrary lower coefficients and mixed t parity in Q. In particular it is not restricted to a monomial negative-winding factor or to the previously treated linear P. The class (11) has dimension nine, including the constant term: six coefficients of P and three of Q. Its even-t subspace has dimension eight. It does NOT include arbitrary coefficients y^a t^b, a nonconstant angular-zero polynomial in t, or the whole fourteen-dimensional even or twenty-four-dimensional stripped degree-five space. No global degree-five or full S24 conclusion follows.

## 6. Method and antecedent scope

The argument is an exposed-face prime-denominator obstruction of the same elementary kind as the retained three-height and leading-binary-form arguments. Its unrestricted coefficient identity (8), moment bound and specialization proof are written in full here; no rational one-variable moment classification is imported.

The existing quadratic-height classification already covers some low-height overlaps after swapping x,y. Its numerator has literal height at most two; it is not a theorem for all degrees of S in (2). The three-height quintic classification has a specified globally t-odd box, whereas (11) permits both parities and arbitrary P(y). These comparisons concern exact hypotheses only and make no historical-priority assertion.

Pakovich's fixed-rational-phase, fixed-contour moment theorems remain relevant context. They do not automatically apply fibrewise to the height average in (1). No extra Laurent monodromy input or ordinary-reduction theorem is needed for the exposed two-term face used here.
