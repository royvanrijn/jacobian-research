# Abelian realizations of split real-multiplication tensor systems

Let \(A/K\) be an abelian variety over a number field, with geometric
rational endomorphism algebra a totally real field \(F\) of degree
\(g=\dim A\). Write \(V_\ell(A)=H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}
(A_{\overline K},\mathbb Q_\ell)\), with Hodge–Tate weights numbered \(0,1\).
Assume that the connected algebraic monodromy group, over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\), is
\[
G_A^\circ=\{(g_i)_{i=1}^g\in\mathrm{GL}_2^g:
                 \det g_i=\det g_j\text{ for all }i,j\}.
\]
This hypothesis holds for all sufficiently large \(\ell\) by Lombardo once
the endomorphisms are defined. Its \(g\) standard summands \(V_i\) are
indexed by the embeddings of \(F\).
[Lombardo, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.1703).

The [rational Hodge argument](CM_NEWTON_DISCONNECTED_RM_ORDINARITY.md#weight-one-tensor-constructions-from-the-independent-system)
applies to tensor constructions with CM Hodge structures. The arithmetic
version below allows arbitrary continuous auxiliary representations with
toric connected monodromy.

## Arithmetic tensor category

**Proposition.** Let \(T\) be a continuous \(\ell\)-adic representation of
\(G_K\) whose algebraic monodromy has toric identity component. Suppose
\(V_\ell(B)\), for an abelian variety \(B/K\), belongs to the tensor
category generated by \(V_\ell(A)\) and \(T\), possibly after a finite
extension of \(K\). This includes direct sums, tensor products, duals,
and subquotients. Then \(B\) is potentially isogenous to \(A^m\times C\)
with \(m\ge0\) and \(C\) of CM type. If \(V_\ell(A)\) alone generates
the category, then \(C\) has dimension zero.

A finite sum of CM representations, Tate twists, and finite-image
representations is allowed as \(T\). At common good places over a
field where the isogeny is defined, the \(p\)-rank defect of \(B\) is
\(m\) times that of \(A\) plus the defect of \(C\). In particular, when
\(m>0\), a split one-failure reduction of \(A\) contributes defect \(m\)
to that of \(B\).

**Proof.** After passing to a finite extension, work with the connected
joint monodromy \(G\subset G_A^\circ\times G_T^\circ\) with surjective
projections to both factors. Its derived group over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) is \(\mathrm{SL}_2^g\times 1\). Indeed,
commutators have trivial \(T\)-projection and project onto the full
derived group of \(G_A^\circ\). The unipotent radical of \(G\) projects
trivially to both reductive groups, so \(G\) is reductive. Objects in
the tensor category are algebraic representations of \(G\).

An irreducible representation of \(G\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\)
has form
\[
W=\xi\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^g\operatorname{Sym}^{n_i}V_i,\qquad n_i\ge0,
\]
for a character \(\xi\) of \(G\). The center acts by scalars on an
irreducible representation, so restriction to the derived group is
irreducible. Its highest weights give the \(n_i\); the one-dimensional
intertwiner between \(W\) and the displayed tensor on the derived group
gives the character. In particular, for a constituent \(W\) of \(V_\ell(B)\),
the character \(\xi\)
is a subrepresentation of \(W\) tensored with the dual of that tensor
of the \(V_i\). This ensures the local and finite-ramification properties
of \(\xi\) even when the original auxiliary \(T\) was arbitrary.

At each \(\ell\)-adic place and each labelled embedding of the base,
every \(V_i\) has Hodge–Tate weights \(0,1\). The comparison is compatible
with the \(F\)-action. The Hodge space in each \(F\)-component has
dimension one, since the component has dimension two and complex
conjugation exchanges Hodge types while preserving the real embedding
of \(F\). Therefore \(W\) has Hodge–Tate width \(\sum_i n_i\).
For a constituent of \(V_\ell(B)\) this width is at most one.
Width zero gives a character. Width one gives \(W=V_i\otimes\xi\)
with \(\xi\) Hodge–Tate of weight zero at every labelled embedding at
every place over \(\ell\).
[Scholze, *p-adic Hodge theory for rigid-analytic varieties*, Theorem 1.6 and Corollary 1.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.3463).

Such a \(\xi\) has finite image. At \(\ell\) it is locally algebraic
by Tate's rank-one theorem, with all algebraic exponents zero, so its
inertia image is finite. At a finite place away from \(\ell\) the
abelian inertia image is finite by local reciprocity, since local
units have an open pro-residue-characteristic subgroup. The
character is unramified outside a finite set because \(W\) and the
\(V_i\) are. Restricting to the inverse image of a torsion-free open
subgroup of the units in its coefficient field kills every finite
inertia image. Global class field theory then makes the restricted
unramified character finite. On the connected \(G\) the algebraic
character \(\xi\) is thus trivial.
[Conrad, Appendix B](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/locchar.pdf).

Any occurrence of \(V_i\) in \(V_\ell(B)\otimes\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\)
gives a nonzero \(\mathbb Q_\ell\)-linear Galois intertwiner with
\(V_\ell(A)\), by scalar extension of the intertwiner space. Faltings'
isogeny theorem and absolute simplicity of \(A\) split off a copy
of \(A\) up to isogeny. Iterating leaves an abelian variety \(C\) with
toric monodromy. To see that this implies CM, write its commutant over
\(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) as \(\prod_\eta M_{m_\eta}\), where
\(\eta\) runs through its distinct characters and \(\sum_\eta m_\eta
=2\dim C\). Faltings identifies this commutant with the scalar extension
of the rational endomorphism algebra. In a simple factor \(M_d(D)\)
of that algebra, with center \(Z\) and \([D:Z]=e^2\), take \(d\)
diagonal copies of a maximal subfield of \(D\). They form a commutative
semisimple algebra of dimension \([Z:\mathbb Q]de\). Summing over the
factors gives a commutative semisimple algebra of dimension \(2\dim C\),
as required for CM. Without \(T\), all characters of \(G_A^\circ\) are
integer powers of the common determinant and have even Weil weight.
Purity excludes them from \(V_\ell(B)\).
[Faltings, §5, Theorems 3–4](https://www-fourier.univ-grenoble-alpes.fr/~lefourns/contenu/Zywina/Faltings.pdf).

Isogenies preserve Newton slopes, and \(p\)-ranks add under products.
This proves the assertion on the defect. \(\square\)

**Corollary.** Suppose a two-dimensional constituent \(W\) of \(V_\ell(B)\)
becomes projectively isomorphic to some \(V_i\) over a finite extension
of \(K\). Then \(A\) is potentially an isogeny factor of \(B\).

After conjugating the projective representations, the scalar ratio of
their linear lifts is a character \(\xi\) with \(W=V_i\otimes\xi\).
The Hodge–Tate and finite-ramification argument above makes \(\xi\)
finite. Restricting further kills it, and the resulting common
constituent gives an isogeny factor by Faltings. This argument requires
only the indicated projective constituent of \(V_\ell(B)\).

## Arithmetic information in the adjoint

Let \(v\) be a good degree-one place over \(p\), and suppose \(p\) splits in
\(F\). For a coefficient prime \(\mathfrak q\mid p\), let
\(X^2-a X+p\) be the relative quadratic in \(F_{\mathfrak q}\).
If its roots are \(\alpha,\beta\), the weight-zero trace of the
trace-free adjoint is
\[
 1+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}
 = \frac{a^2}{p}-1.
\]
This trace is unchanged by any scalar twist. Its valuation is \(-1\)
at an ordinary factor. At a failed factor it is integral and congruent
to \(-1\) modulo \(\mathfrak q\). Thus the number of integral adjoint
traces at the matching coefficient primes is exactly the \(p\)-rank
defect. The trace-free adjoint has Hodge–Tate weights \(-1,0,1\), and
every torus twist preserves their width two. The split one-failure
distribution remains a problem for this weight-zero system.
[Projective trace criterion](CM_NEWTON_PROJECTIVE_TRACE_FIELD.md).
