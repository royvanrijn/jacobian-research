# Coordinate degrees and complete hyperbolic Hopf-orbit spectra

Let \(K\) be any field of characteristic zero, and put
\[
 A_K=K[x,y,t]/(t^2+2xy-1).
\]
The degree of a nonzero element is the least total degree of a polynomial
representative. Automorphisms mean polynomial \(K\)-algebra automorphisms.
All statements below remain valid over every extension of \(K\), including
for automorphisms defined only over that extension.

**Theorem QuadricCoordinateDegreeTriples.** The set of triples
\[
 \bigl(\deg\sigma(x),\deg\sigma(y),\deg\sigma(t)\bigr),
 \qquad \sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_K(A_K),
\]
is exactly
\[
 \boxed{\bigl\{(r,(2q-1)r,qr),\ ((2q-1)r,r,qr):
                   r\ge1\text{ odd},\ q\ge1\bigr\}.}                 \tag{C.1}
\]
Every triple is realized by explicit automorphisms with rational
coefficients. In particular, both isotropic coordinate degrees are odd;
the three coordinates cannot be assigned independent degrees.

The proof uses the classical generator theorem for this split affine
quadric, stated precisely in Section 2. The degree induction and all
realizations are given here. The bounded checker is supporting evidence,
not a proof of exhaustion of the automorphism group.

## 1. The filtration and the elementary maps

The associated graded ring is
\[
 \operatorname{gr} A_K=K[x,y,t]/(t^2+2xy).
\]
The homogeneous quadratic has rank three. A product of linear forms has
rank at most two, so this graded ring is a domain even after extending
the coefficient field. Therefore
\[
 \deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g\quad(f,g\ne0),\qquad
 \deg P(f)=(\deg P)(\deg f)                           \tag{C.2}
\]
for every nonconstant \(f\) and nonzero univariate \(P\). Terms of distinct
degrees do not cancel. Automorphisms send each of \(x,y,t\) to a
nonconstant element. If their degrees are \((a,c,b)\), the relation gives
\[
 a+c=2b.                                             \tag{C.3}
\]
Indeed, two nonzero positive-degree terms in
\(\sigma(t)^2+2\sigma(x)\sigma(y)=1\) must have equal degree.

For \(Q\in K[x]\), define
\[
 \tau_Q(x,y,t)=
 \left(x,\ y-tQ(x)-\frac{xQ(x)^2}{2},\ t+xQ(x)\right),
 \qquad s(x,y,t)=(y,x,t).                              \tag{C.4}
\]
They preserve \(t^2+2xy\), with
\(\tau_Q^{-1}=\tau_{-Q}\) and \(s^{-1}=s\).
These identities hold in the ambient polynomial ring. In formulas for
compositions, maps act on coordinate triples from right to left.

Let \(\mathcal L\) be the group of invertible linear transformations
preserving \(t^2+2xy\). Let \(\mathcal J\) consist of the maps
\[
 \left(a x,\ a^{-1}\left(y-\varepsilon t Q(x)
                         -\frac{xQ(x)^2}{2}\right),\
              \varepsilon t+xQ(x)\right),
 \qquad a\in K^*,\quad\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\},\quad Q\in K[x].       \tag{C.5}
\]
Their intersection \(\mathcal B=\mathcal L\cap\mathcal J\) consists
exactly of (C.5) with constant \(Q\).

For later use, \(\mathcal B\) is also the subgroup of \(\mathcal L\)
preserving the line \(Kx\) in the linear coordinate space. To check
this, an isotropic linear coordinate \(u x+v y+w t\) satisfies
\(2uv+w^2=0\). If \(v=0\), then \(w=0\), so the first coordinate is
\(a x\). Preservation of the quadratic then forces the third coordinate
to be \(\varepsilon t+cx\), with \(\varepsilon^2=1\), and forces the
second coordinate to have exactly the form (C.5).
Consequently,
\[
 \ell\in\mathcal L\setminus\mathcal B
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{the coefficient of }y\text{ in }\ell(x)\text{ is nonzero}.   \tag{C.6}
\]

## 2. The precise classical input and a useful word form

**Imported generator theorem.** The polynomial automorphism group of
\(xy=(1-t^2)/2\) is generated by \(\mathcal L\) and \(\mathcal J\).

This is the specialization of Blanc and Dubouloz,
*Automorphisms of A1-fibered affine surfaces*, Transactions of the AMS
363 (2011), 5887–5924, Theorem 5.4.5(7),(8), printed pages 5915–5919.
Their field convention is on printed page 5889. Here the polynomial has
degree two and two distinct roots \(1,-1\) in \(K\). Their affine-factor
generators \(H,S_p,I,T_0\) are linear in these coordinates, and their
fibered factor has form (C.5). Thus their theorem supplies the generation
assertion over each field under consideration. We do not need uniqueness
of their amalgamated-product normal form.

The [retained primary PDF](sources/blanc-dubouloz-a1-fibered-surfaces-2011.pdf)
was read at these locations, including its factorization proof. The
printed formulas for \(T,T_0\) in part (8) omit the original \(v\) summand
in their second coordinate. The correct shear is
\(v+(P(w+uQ(u))-P(w))/u\), as follows directly from \(uv=P(w)\) and
from the plane shear used in their proof on page 5919. We use the directly
verified formula (C.4), not the malformed literal formula. This is a
scoped use of a classical theorem, with no historical-priority claim.

Generation permits a word in alternating \(\mathcal L\) and
\(\mathcal J\) factors. Merge adjacent factors in the same subgroup;
absorb every interior \(\mathcal B\) factor into its neighbors. Each
remaining \(\mathcal J\setminus\mathcal B\) factor is a member of
\(\mathcal B\) times a shear \(\tau_Q\) with \(\deg Q\ge1\). Absorb that
linear factor into the adjacent \(\mathcal L\) factor. Multiplication by
\(\mathcal B\) cannot turn an element of
\(\mathcal L\setminus\mathcal B\) into one of \(\mathcal B\).

Hence every nonlinear automorphism has a representation
\[
 \ell_m\tau_{Q_m}\ell_{m-1}\cdots\ell_1\tau_{Q_1}\ell_0,
 \quad m\ge1,\quad\deg Q_i\ge1,                     \tag{C.7}
\]
where \(\ell_0,\ell_m\in\mathcal L\), and every interior
\(\ell_i\) lies in \(\mathcal L\setminus\mathcal B\). Only existence
of this word form is used. All cancellations in an arbitrary unreduced
word have already been accounted for by these subgroup operations.

## 3. Necessity: strict growth and the final linear map

Start with the linear triple \(\ell_0(x,y,t)\), whose three degrees are
one. If \(q_1=1+\deg Q_1\ge2\), the term
\(XQ_1(X)\) strictly dominates the old third coordinate in (C.4).
The triple after the first shear therefore has degrees
\[
 (1,2q_1-1,q_1),
\]
where the second degree follows from (C.3).

Suppose a shear has produced degrees
\[
 (r,(2q-1)r,qr),\qquad r\text{ odd},\quad q\ge2.      \tag{C.8}
\]
The second coordinate has unique largest degree
\(d=(2q-1)r\). By (C.6), the next interior linear map makes the first
coordinate have degree exactly \(d\); every other coordinate has degree
at most \(d\). If the next shear has \(q'=1+\deg Q'\ge2\), its added
term has degree \(q'd>d\). The new triple has degrees
\[
 (d,(2q'-1)d,q'd).
\]
The new first degree \(d\) is again odd. This proves (C.8) inductively
immediately before the last linear factor.

There are exactly three possibilities for that last factor \(\ell_m\).

* If \(\ell_m\in\mathcal B\), its first coordinate is a nonzero
  multiple of \(X\), and its third is \(\varepsilon T+cX\). The strict
  degree ordering in (C.8) preserves the triple (C.8).
* If \(\ell_m\notin\mathcal B\) and its third-coordinate coefficient
  of \(Y\) is zero, its third coordinate is \(uX+wT\). Its quadratic
  norm is one, so \(w^2=1\). The first coordinate has degree
  \((2q-1)r\), the third has degree \(qr\), and (C.3) gives second
  degree \(r\). This is the swapped triple.
* If that third-coordinate coefficient of \(Y\) is nonzero, both the
  first and third degrees are \(d=(2q-1)r\), and (C.3) gives second
  degree \(d\). This equal triple occurs in (C.1) with parameters
  \((r,q)=(d,1)\).

If there was no nonlinear factor, the triple is \((1,1,1)\).
This proves necessity without any assumption that coordinate degrees
can be chosen independently.

## 4. Sufficiency and automorphism degree

For odd \(r\ge1\) and \(q\ge1\), put
\[
 \theta_{r,q}=	au_{x^{q-1}}\,s\,\tau_{x^{(r-1)/2}}.                \tag{C.9}
\]
After the rightmost shear and the swap, the degrees are
\((r,1,(r+1)/2)\). If \(r>1\), the last added term has degree
\(qr>(r+1)/2\). The same strict inequality holds for \(r=1,q>1\).
Thus \(\theta_{r,q}\) realizes
\((r,(2q-1)r,qr)\). When \(r=q=1\), the map is invertible and linear,
so its three degrees are one. Finally \(s\theta_{r,q}\) realizes the
swapped triple. Every map here is defined over \(\mathbb Q\).

**Corollary OddAutomorphismDegrees.** Define the degree of an
automorphism as the largest of its three coordinate degrees. Its
possible values are exactly the positive odd integers, and
\[
 \deg\sigma=\deg\sigma^{-1}.                         \tag{C.10}
\]
For (C.7), the degree induction gives
\(\deg\sigma=\prod_i(2\deg Q_i+1)\); an invertible linear final factor
preserves the largest coordinate degree. Reversing the word gives its
inverse, with the shears replaced by \(\tau_{-Q_i}\), the same shear
degrees, and interior linear factors still outside \(\mathcal B\).
The same product gives (C.10). Formula (C.9) realizes every odd maximum.

## 5. Full spectra of the retained higher-start hyperbolic seeds

For odd \(h\ge1\), \(n\ge1\), and \(\lambda\in K^*\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C(x)&=(1+\lambda x)^{n+1},\\
 U(x)&=\frac{1+\lambda x}{2n}
       \sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}\binom{2n}{j+1}(\lambda x)^j,\\
 p_{h,n,\lambda}&=\frac{t^hC(x)U(x)^{h-1}}x
                         (1-t^2U(x)^2),\\
 \alpha&=(2h+3)n,\qquad\beta=h+2.
\end{aligned}                                                        \tag{C.11}
\]
Since \(U(0)=1\), the quotient in (C.11) is regular: replace
\((1-t^2)/x\) by \(2y\). The exponent \(h\) is the starting exponent;
the actual \(t\)-height on \(x\ne0\) is \(h+2\).

**Theorem HyperbolicOrbitFullDegreeSpectrum.** For each fixed triple
\((h,n,\lambda)\), the complete degree spectrum of its automorphism
orbit is
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal D_{h,n}=
 \{r(\alpha+\beta q):r\ge1\text{ odd},q\ge1\}
 \ \cup\
 \{r((2\alpha+\beta)q-\alpha):r\ge1\text{ odd},q\ge1\}.}          \tag{C.12}
\]
The spectrum is independent of \(\lambda\) and the characteristic-zero
coefficient field.

For completeness, put \(a=\deg\sigma(x)\), \(b=\deg\sigma(t)\).
The degrees of \(C(\sigma(x))\) and \(U(\sigma(x))\) are
\((n+1)a\) and \(2na\). The last factor in (C.11) has degree
\(2b+4na\), with a positive-degree term strictly dominating its constant
term. Applying (C.2) to \(xp_{h,n,\lambda}\) and subtracting \(a\) gives
\[
 \deg\sigma(p_{h,n,\lambda})=\alpha a+\beta b.        \tag{C.13}
\]
Substitution of the two triples in (C.1) proves (C.12), in both directions.
The realizing maps are (C.9) and their swaps.

**Corollary HyperbolicSpectrumDivisorTest.** A positive integer \(D\)
belongs to \(\mathcal D_{h,n}\) if and only if some positive odd divisor
\(r\mid D\) has quotient \(v=D/r\ge\alpha+\beta\) satisfying
\[
 v\equiv\alpha\pmod\beta
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 v\equiv-\alpha\pmod{2\alpha+\beta}.                 \tag{C.14}
\]
This is a finite, exact arithmetic decision criterion. It does not require
enumerating automorphisms.

For a prime \(\ell\), the only possible divisor in (C.14) is \(r=1\),
because \(r=\ell\) would give \(v=1<\alpha+\beta\). Thus its membership
is given by the same two congruences and \(\ell\ge\alpha+\beta\).
Also every degree in this orbit is divisible by
\[
 \gcd(\alpha,\beta)=\gcd(n,h+2).
\]
If this gcd exceeds one, the orbit contains no prime degree: its minimum
\(\alpha+\beta\) exceeds the gcd. No prime-density theorem is imported.

For the smallest hyperbolic orbit \((h,n)=(1,1)\), the two rays before
taking odd multiples are \(5+3q\) and \(13q-5\). Its prime degrees are
exactly primes \(\ell\ge8\) with
\(\ell\equiv2\pmod3\) or \(\ell\equiv8\pmod{13}\).
For example, degrees 13 and 19 are excluded from this entire orbit,
whereas the swapped construction at \(q=6\) gives degree 73.
These are statements about this orbit, not restrictions on other seeds.

## 6. Scope and exact support

The [existing orbit proof](HOPF_AUTOMORPHISM_ORBIT_SPECTRA.md), Section 8,
separately establishes scalar-and-automorphism classification by \((h,n)\),
absolute nonpower, and the primitive unsafe Image interpretation with its
retained moment and trace inputs. Those statements are not premises for
the coordinate-degree theorem or the algebraic spectrum calculation here.
In particular, this result supplies no new starting witness in primitive
degree five or seven and does not classify every pure unsafe phase.

The standard-library checker
[verify_hopf_coordinate_degree_spectra.py](verify_hopf_coordinate_degree_spectra.py)
uses exact rational quotient arithmetic, two independent normal forms,
literal elementary maps and inverse words. It checks bounded degree
triples, final-linear cases, cancellations, hyperbolic degree examples,
the divisor criterion, and corrupted negative controls. Its actual
coverage is recorded in
[the receipt](hopf_coordinate_degree_spectra_verification.json).
The [source ledger](hopf_coordinate_degree_spectra_source_ledger.json)
pins the proof, checker, arithmetic helper, and exact primary PDF.

The generator theorem is an imported mathematical premise. The all-word
degree induction, exhaustion of the final-linear cases, explicit
realizations, and arithmetic corollaries are written arguments. Finite
controls do not establish those universal quantifiers. No formal
verification, external review, or worldwide novelty is claimed.
