# Projective trace fields and ordinary reduction in relative rank two

Let \(A/K\) be a polarized abelian variety of dimension \(g=2e\) over a
number field, with a CM field action by \(E\) of degree \(2e\).
Assume Rosati acts on \(E\) by conjugation. Put \(F=E^+\).
Write \(b\) for the number of conjugate Hodge pairs of type \((1,1)\).

At a good prime \(v\) of absolute residue degree one, with residue
characteristic \(p\), write \(\tau_v,\delta_v\in E\) for the relative
Frobenius trace and determinant. These coefficients are compatible
across cohomology theories by applying the degree-polynomial and
field-trace comparison to \(a\pi_v\) and \(a\pi_v^2\), where \(a\)
ranges over a basis of the integral endomorphism order, then using
Newton identities. Polarization gives
\[
 \bar\delta_v=p^2/\delta_v,\qquad
 \bar\tau_v=p\tau_v/\delta_v,\qquad
 T_v=\tau_v^2/\delta_v=\tau_v\bar\tau_v/p\in F .
\]
The [simultaneous norm proof](../../../unpublished/cm-newton-polygon-constraints/main.tex)
gives the strict trace norm bound at the split degree-one primes
used below.

## The projective trace field theorem

**Theorem.** After extending the base to define the action and
polarization, contain a normal closure of \(E\), and have connected
auxiliary \(\ell\)-adic monodromy, define \(F_0\subset F\) as the
field generated by \(T_v\) at good absolute degree-one primes.
Put \(d=[F:F_0]\). The projective trace classes of conjugate pairs
are the fibres of restriction from the embeddings of \(F\) to those
of \(F_0\). Each class has size \(d\) and is entirely balanced or
entirely definite. If \(b>0\), then \(d\mid b\) and after finite
extension, on a set of density one,
\[
 r\le d\left\lfloor\frac{b/d-1}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 f_p(A)\ge g-2d\left\lfloor\frac{b/d-1}{2}\right\rfloor .
\]
Here \(r\) counts nonordinary balanced pairs. Thus \(b/d\le2\)
forces density-one ordinary reduction after finite extension.
If \(b=0\), the definite signatures give split ordinarity directly.

**Proof.** Over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell\) the embeddings of \(E\)
give rank-two summands \(V_\sigma\) of the first cohomology.
The class function
\[
 B_\sigma(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g|V_\sigma)^2/\det(g|V_\sigma)
\]
has Frobenius value \(\sigma(T_v)\). Agreement of two class
functions on algebraic monodromy is equivalent to agreement at
the good degree-one primes by Chebotarev, since absolute
higher-degree primes have density zero. Conjugate embeddings
give the same function by polarization. Two embeddings of \(F\)
agree on the indicated Frobenius values precisely when their
restrictions to \(F_0\) agree. Each embedding downstairs has
\(d\) extensions upstairs. Further finite extensions preserve
the group once connected, since the resulting subgroup of
finite index is Zariski dense, so preserve these restriction
fibres and the field they determine.

If all pairs are definite, the signature homogeneity is automatic,
and the split slopes are zero and one with the ordinary multiplicities.
For the remainder of the proof suppose \(b>0\). At a split good
degree-one prime, the determinant in a component
of Hodge dimension \(m\) has valuation \(m\in\{0,1,2\}\).
The strict trace norm bound gives nonzero trace, and the local
trace valuation is integral. Hence
\[
 v_p(\sigma(T_v))=2v_p(\sigma(\tau_v))-m
\]
is odd in a balanced pair and even in a definite pair.
Since it is constant within each projective class, each class
has just one of these types. For a balanced class its valuation
is \(-1\) when ordinary and a positive odd integer otherwise:
the two slopes of sum one include zero precisely when the
trace is a unit. For definite classes the projective valuation
is nonnegative and even. Define a nonnegative integer \(u_C\)
by \(v_p(T_{v,C})=-1+2u_C\) on balanced classes and
\(v_p(T_{v,C})=2u_C\) on definite classes.

The norm identity
\[
 N_{F/\mathbb Q}(T_v)=p^{-e}N_{E/\mathbb Q}(\tau_v)
\]
and the strict trace bound give
\[
 -b+2d\sum_C u_C=v_p(N_{F/\mathbb Q}(T_v))<0 .
\]
Thus \(\sum_Cu_C\le\lfloor(b/d-1)/2\rfloor\).
A nonordinary class accounts for \(d\) balanced pairs and
uses at least one unit of this budget. Each nonordinary
pair removes two slope-zero eigenvalues.
\(\square\)

This gives a bound on the valuation levels, including extra
valuation on definite classes. If \(t=b/d\) is even, the
improvement over the ungrouped \(p\)-rank bound is \(2(d-1)\).
If \(t\) is odd, it is \(2\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor\). In
particular prime \(b\) gives potential density-one ordinarity
whenever a linkage crosses distinct conjugate pairs.

One can also use \(F_v=\mathbb Q(T_v)\) instead of \(F_0\)
in the calculation at a single prime, obtaining the same
inequality with \(d_v=[F:F_v]\). For three balanced pairs,
every remaining nonordinary prime must have \(F_v=F\).
Conversely on a density-one set with connected monodromy,
\(F_v=F_0\). Indeed for each proper subfield of \(F_0\),
membership imposes equality of two functions that differ
on the connected group. There are finitely many subfields
to test. The field is also independent of the auxiliary
prime, by taking a common connected extension and using
its description by compatible algebraic Frobenius values.

## Density over the field of definition of the action

If \(A\) is not potentially CM and \(K\) defines the action
and polarization, the same bound holds on a density-one
set **over \(K\)**. The good absolute degree-one values \(T_v\)
over \(K\) already generate \(F_0\). No splitting hypothesis
on \(K\) is needed for this assertion.

First the projective links persist on disconnected components.
By Faltings each \(V_\sigma\) is irreducible under connected
monodromy in the non-potentially-CM case. For this use relative
rank two: projection onto each geometric isotypic factor
retains a faithful \(E\)-action. Two such factors would each
have rank one over \(E\), giving potential CM. In the single
isotypic case the split matrix blocks of the geometric
endomorphism algebra give irreducible constituents of one
common dimension by the uniform Betti rank over its centre.
This dimension divides two. If it is one the commutant has
a commutative étale subalgebra of degree \(2g\), again giving
potential CM. The connected derived projections in the
remaining case are all \(\mathrm{SL}_2\).

For two linked summands the connected projective image in
\(\mathrm{PGL}_2\times\mathrm{PGL}_2\) projects onto each
factor. It is the product or the graph of an automorphism
by the subdirect product argument for the centreless simple
group \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\). Equality of the two nonconstant
projective trace functions rules out the product. Over
an algebraic closure the graph automorphism is inner.
Identify the two projective representations on the
connected subgroup by this conjugacy. Every element of
the full algebraic monodromy normalizes the connected
subgroup. The quotient of its two projective images
centralizes the connected \(\mathrm{PGL}_2\) image and is
trivial. Thus projective linkage holds on the full group,
which proves the field assertion by Chebotarev. In fact
\(F_v=F_0\) on a density-one set over \(K\) as well. For pairs
in different classes the connected projective image is the
product, so the image of each component is also the product.
Equality of their projective traces cuts out a proper closed
subset in every component. Apply algebraic Chebotarev to the
finitely many pairs of embeddings detecting proper subfields.

Next, strict norm divisibility fails at most on a density-zero
set. On the full algebraic monodromy consider
\[
 H(g)=\nu(g)^{-e}\prod_\sigma\operatorname{tr}(g\mid V_\sigma),
\]
where \(\nu\) is the polarization similitude. At a degree-one
Frobenius this is \(p^{-e}N_E(\tau_v)\), a rational number of
magnitude at most \(4^e\). The function is nonconstant on
every connected component. Choose a cocharacter of the
connected derived group whose projection to each
\(\mathrm{SL}_2\) has nonzero weights, by avoiding finitely
many proper hyperplanes of a maximal torus. In eigenbases,
each leading diagonal entry of a matrix in a fixed
component is a nonzero regular function: the connected
projection contains \(\mathrm{SL}_2\), whose matrices span
the full matrix algebra, and multiplication by a fixed
invertible matrix preserves that span. Some point of the
component makes all the leading entries nonzero
simultaneously. On the translate of the cocharacter by
that point the product has a nonzero leading term of
positive degree, with constant \(\nu\). Hence the integral
levels of \(H\) form a finite union of closed subsets
containing no component. Algebraic Chebotarev gives density
zero for them, including the zero-trace level.

At a good nonsplit degree-one prime outside ramification
and the order index, the crystalline idempotents split
by coefficient places of \(E\). On an \(E_\lambda\)-linear
component the determinant valuation is the common Hodge
dimension at the local embeddings of \(E_\lambda\). This
follows by dividing the \(\mathbb Q_p\)-determinant valuation,
the dimension of the component, by \([E_\lambda:\mathbb Q_p]\).
The dimensions coincide on its local embeddings because
the completion of the base is \(\mathbb Q_p\). Relative
coefficient valuations are integral in these unramified
coefficient fields. Counting them once for each embedding
gives the norm valuation. The parity and level computation
therefore applies unchanged outside the density-zero
exception above.

The non-potentially-CM hypothesis matters for this assertion
over \(K\). For example, the square of the elliptic curve
\(y^2=x^3-x\) over \(\mathbb Q\) carries the rational rotation
action of \(E=\mathbb Q(i)\) on the copies. It has \(b=d=1\),
whereas its ordinary primes over \(\mathbb Q\) have density
one half. The field of definition of this action does not
define its elliptic curve's CM endomorphisms.

Frobenius of \(KN/K\), for \(N\) a normal closure of \(E\),
permutes the balanced projective classes. At a degree-one
prime the failed classes are a union of these cycles,
by constancy of the Newton slopes on each coefficient
place. The total size of those cycles is at most
\(\lfloor(b/d-1)/2\rfloor\). Thus the conjugacy classes
whose cycles on the balanced projective classes all
exceed that budget give ordinary primes outside a
density-zero set. Their Chebotarev proportions give lower
bounds over \(K\). For \(d=1,b=3\) this specializes to the
[three-cycle criterion](CM_NEWTON_THREE_BALANCED_GLOBAL.md).

## Comparison and scope

The rank-two adjoint trace is \(T_v-1\). Trace fields of
adjoint or projective compatible systems are established:
Ribet's Theorem 5.3 obtains the endomorphism centre from
the corresponding normalized traces for the simple
modular \(\mathrm{GL}_2\)-type case over \(\mathbb Q\).
Here the relative Frobenius norm and Hodge parity turn
the projective field degree into a reduction bound.
Fité and Wang–Zhang use different splitting conditions
in the cubic RM case. Cantoral Farfán and coauthors assume
a full CM endomorphism field and simple signature in
their eventual ordinary criterion. Their results on
\(\mu\)-ordinary primes and original-field density address
additional cases with their own hypotheses.
[Ribet, Theorem 5.3](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ribet/Articles/korea.pdf)
· [Fité, Theorems 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.10185v3)
· [Wang–Zhang, Theorems 1.3, 1.7–1.8](https://doi.org/10.1112/mtk.70081)
· [Cantoral Farfán et al., Theorems 4.18–4.19](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11174v1)
· [Sawin, density from normalized trace levels](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.crma.2016.01.025.pdf).

Suh gives a close antecedent in parallel weight-two Hilbert
modular systems. There the determinant becomes cyclotomic
after finite extension. On the connected group, equality of
projective traces is then equality of squared traces, which
is equality of traces since the sum of the traces is nonzero
at the identity and the coordinate ring is a domain. The
projective field in that setting is Suh's smallest Frobenius
field. Theorem 4.1.1(3) proves potential ordinarity for degree
at most two. Theorem 4.1.1(5) gives the same strict coefficient
count in even degree with a Sato–Tate hypothesis. The parity
argument here permits different determinant weights for mixed
and definite pairs, and the disconnected-component argument
gives a density-one bound directly over the action field.
[Suh, §3.2 and Theorem 4.1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01182v2).

### Unconditional coefficient count in Suh's setting

Let \(f\) be a new normalized non-CM Hilbert cuspidal eigenform
of parallel weight two with coefficient field \(K_f\). Let \(n=[K_f^\circ:\mathbb Q]\)
be the degree of its smallest Frobenius field and put
\(c=[K_f:K_f^\circ]\). Pass to a number field over which the
connected joint monodromy and determinant are as in Suh's
Proposition 3.2.1 and Definition 3.2.2. At a density-one set
of its degree-one primes, the number of nonordinary rank-two
constituents indexed by embeddings of \(K_f\) is at most
\(c\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\), without a Sato–Tate hypothesis.

Indeed let \(a\in K_f^\circ\) be the Frobenius trace at a
degree-one prime over \(p\). With the common determinant
\(\nu\), the regular class function
\[
 \nu^{-n}\prod_{\sigma:K_f^\circ\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb Q}_\ell}
                  \operatorname{tr}(g_\sigma)^2
\]
takes the value \(N_{K_f^\circ/\mathbb Q}(a)^2/p^n\). Suh's
monodromy description contains the product of the \(n\)
determinant-one groups, so the function is nonconstant by
varying the trace in one factor with the others fixed at
the identity. At a degree-one prime the displayed number
lies in \([0,4^n]\) by the Weil bound. If its \(p\)-valuation
is nonnegative, it is one of finitely many integers, since its
denominator is a power of \(p\). Every level is a proper closed
subset of the connected monodromy group. Algebraic Chebotarev gives \(2v_p(N(a))<n\) on a density-one set. At primes
unramified in the coefficient field, each nonordinary
\(K_f^\circ\)-embedding contributes at least one to \(v_p(N(a))\),
and has \(c\) extensions to \(K_f\). This proves the count.
It concerns the individual rank-two systems and applies in
particular at degree-one places of the extension above the split
rational primes used in Suh's count.
[Suh, Proposition 3.2.1, Definition 3.2.2 and Lemma 4.1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01182v2).

The [geometric comparison](CM_NEWTON_PROJECTIVE_GEOMETRIC_CASES.md)
distinguishes projective linkage from the isotypic decomposition
of the weight-one representation. The independent case \(d=1,b=3\),
including the split real-cubic RM threefold problem, remains outside
the ordinary density-one conclusion.
