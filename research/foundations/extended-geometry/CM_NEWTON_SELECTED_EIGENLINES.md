# Selected coherent Frobenius maps and sphere moments

Ordinarity makes every coherent Frobenius map invertible. A sphere
moment needs much less. It can detect one map to a one-dimensional
coherent eigenspace, and a nonzero value of that map can exclude purity
even if the return map on the eigenspace itself is zero. The two issues
are the arithmetic of the selected eigenspace and which Frobenius map
the moment actually sees.

## Split normalizers and the contribution of an orbit

Let \(E\) be the acting CM field of relative rank \(h\). At a good
split prime let \(s_1\le\cdots\le s_h\) be the slopes on one side of
a conjugate pair, and put
\[
 D_j(s)=\left\lfloor\sum_{k=h-j+1}^h s_k\right\rfloor
       -\left\lceil\sum_{k=1}^j s_k\right\rceil .
\]
This is independent of the choice of side by complementary slopes.
The [simultaneous CM theorem](../../../unpublished/cm-newton-polygon-constraints/main.tex)
gives \(\sum D_j\ge1\) over the mixed pairs, for every \(1\le j<h\),
at its absolute degree-one primes after finite extension.

Suppose invertible geometric quasi-endomorphisms normalize \(E\)
and induce a finite subgroup \(\Gamma\) of
\(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf Q}(E)\). Take a finite extension
defining a set of representatives. At good split primes away from
their denominators, the representatives commute with Frobenius and
identify the slopes of components in the same orbit. The action on
embeddings is free, since an embedding is injective. The stabilizer of
a conjugate pair is precisely
\(\Gamma\cap\{1,\text{complex conjugation}\}\). Thus all pair orbits
have the same size \(d\), even when \(E/\mathbf Q\) is not Galois.
The inequalities become
\[
 \sum_{\text{mixed pair orbits}}D_j\ge1 ,
\]
because \(d\) multiplies the integer sum. Conversely, any assignment
of numerical polygons to the orbits satisfying these inequalities
lifts to an assignment satisfying the original bounds. This
converse concerns the numerical tests, regardless of realizability.

In rank two, if there are \(c\) balanced pair orbits, at most
\(\lfloor(c-1)/2\rfloor\) are nonordinary for \(c>0\).
In particular a single untested orbit becomes ordinary as soon as
other certified bad orbits exhaust that allowance. Without any
such local information, the normalizer and the numerical inequalities
force a selected balanced orbit ordinary exactly in the cases
\(c\le2\), where they force every balanced orbit ordinary.

An analogous limitation holds for a selected pair of coherent
dimensions \((1,h-1)\) when \(h\ge3\). In that pair the modified slopes
\(0^{h-2},(1/2)^2\) on its slope-sum-one side have \(D_1=0\) and
\(D_j\ge0\) for all \(j\). Indeed \(D_{h-j}=D_j\), and for
\(j\le h/2\) its sum of the smallest slopes is zero.
Another mixed orbit left ordinary contributes at least one for all
\(j\). Hence the selected bad orbit is compatible with the numerical
bounds whenever another mixed orbit remains. If local information
instead shows \(D_1\le0\) on every other mixed orbit, then the
selected orbit must have \(D_1=1\). A mixed pair of type \((1,h-1)\)
can have both slope endpoints on its slope-sum-one side only when
it has one slope one and all other slopes zero. Thus the selected
orbit is ordinary in that case. For a nonsingleton mixed pair,
nonordinarity alone does not imply \(D_1\le0\).

For a cyclic cover, a base change of coordinate \(M\) with
branch congruences \(a_{M(Q)}\equiv u a_Q\bmod n\) lifts to
\((z,w)\mapsto(M(z),R(z)w^u)\) for a unit \(u\bmod n\), after a
finite extension. The divisor of \(F(M(z))/F(z)^u\) is divisible
by \(n\) on \(\mathbb P^1\), so it is an \(n\)th power up to a
constant. Pullback transports character \(i\) to character \(ui\).
If the automorphism is defined over the residue field it
intertwines Frobenius even when Frobenius permutes the deck characters.
For primitive characters the multiplicative action on the set of
Frobenius orbits is the action on a quotient of
\((\mathbb Z/n)^\times\) by the group generated by the residue
Frobenius multiplier. The split norm comparison above requires the
split-prime hypothesis. The transport of individual maps does not.

When geometric curve automorphisms supply an actual group
section of \(\Gamma\), their algebra with \(E\) contains the
split crossed product \(E\rtimes\Gamma\simeq M_{|\Gamma|}(E^\Gamma)\).
This follows from the regular action on \(E\) over its fixed field
and linear independence of distinct field automorphisms. The
resulting isogeny to a power can account for some orbit arguments
by ordinary reduction of a smaller-dimensional factor. For example,
the order-nine example with a 3-cycle of characters in the
[earlier note](CM_NEWTON_POLYGONS_AND_MOMENT_OBSTRUCTIONS.md) reduces
to an abelian surface. The orbit-compressed inequalities themselves
need no group-section assumption.

The local split-prime argument can also use the rank-two trace
norm directly. At \(p=73\), for
\(w^9=z^5(z-1)^2(z-5)^3\), the primitive character-two scalar
vanishes and the character-four scalar is \(22\). Hence the
trace norm is nonzero. Two bad pairs would make it at least \(p^4\)
in absolute value, contradicting the bound \(64p^3\). Consequently
the selected pair \((1,8)\) is ordinary while \((2,7)\) is not.
Knowing the bad orbit matters. For the same cover family with
branch parameter 6 instead of 5, the order-nine character-one
scalar vanishes at 73 and the trace norm valuation is exactly two,
strictly below three. That calculation and the prime-power
propagation of the selective example are in the
[infinite-family note](CM_NEWTON_INFINITE_MOMENT_FAMILIES.md).

## A moment detecting a transition rather than a return

Use the literal Laurent chart of \(t^2+2xy=1\). Suppose \(q,u,n\) are
positive integers, with \(u/q>1\), and the largest value of
\(qj+ui\) on literal monomials \(x^it^j\) of a phase \(f\) is \(n\).
Suppose its equality angular polynomial is
\(z^{-b}P(z^q)\), with positive extreme angles \(a,b\) on the two
sides, so that \(\deg P=(a+b)/q\) and \(P(0)\ne0\).
On the equality line the coefficient of \(z^i\) means
the coefficient of \(x^it^{(n-ui)/q}\).

Fix an odd positive character index \(s\), coprime to \(q\).
Impose a congruence on all literal support,
\[
                   qj+ui\equiv n\pmod s .
\]
Let \(p\) be an odd prime at which the coefficients are integral,
with \(p\nmid s\), and choose \(r\ge1\) with
\(s p^r\equiv1\bmod n\). Put \(k=(s p^r-1)/n\).
In the angular constant term of \(f^{qk}\) the largest possible
height is \(nk=s p^r-1\). Every angular-constant height \(j\)
satisfies \(j+1\equiv0\bmod s\), by adding the monomial congruences.
Among the denominators \(j+1\) divisible by \(p^r\) up to
\(s p^r\), the only one divisible by \(s\) is \(s p^r\).
The upper height is even since \(s,p\) are odd. Integrating in
\(t\) on the sphere and multiplying by \(s p^r\) therefore yields
\[
 s p^r\mathcal A(f^{qk})
 \equiv [z^{bk}]P(z)^{qk}
 = [z^{s p^r-1}](z^{n-b}P(z)^q)^k \pmod p.
\]
This isolates a leading coefficient even though its interval
denominator exceeds \(p^r\).

For a good reduced branch configuration, write
\(w^n=F(z)=z^{n-b}P(z)^q\) for the associated normalized
connected cover, with all endpoint and interior exponents in
\(\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\). Its character-one line has Čech generator
\(w/z\). The overlap expression \(w^s/z^s\) is regular on the
overlap and defines a class of character \(s\). Its \(r\)-fold
Frobenius image is \(w F(z)^k/z^{s p^r}\). The selected
coefficient is thus the image of that class in the character-one
line. The class can be zero in some branch configurations.
Any nonzero coefficient proves that both the class and its image
are nonzero. This is a standard cyclic-cover Hasse–Witt entry
used with a different moment denominator.
[Bouw, §§4–5](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6D1BEC9200F6849339665AC4371AB7AF/S0010437X01000343a.pdf),
[Lin–Mantovan–Singhal, §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09632v1#S2.SS4)

If \(s\) times each reduced interior exponent is less than \(n\),
then the overlap input is a single element of the usual basis.
Writing \(u_s=\lfloor s(n-b)/n\rfloor\), the normalized finite
generator is \(w^s/z^{u_s}\), and the chosen class is its basis
vector with denominator \(z^{s-u_s}\). The coherent dimension is
\(2s-1-u_s-\lfloor s(n-a)/n\rfloor\), at least \(s-u_s\).
The more general calculation of the overlap class does not need
this interior-exponent restriction.

## Fixed moments and growing connected conductors

Choose \(p,s\) as above, \(m_0\ge1\), positive \(a,b\), and a
\(p\)-integral polynomial \(P\) of degree \(a+b\) with nonzero
constant and leading terms modulo \(p\). Assume its distinct
nonzero roots, with multiplicities \(m_\nu\), remain distinct at
the chosen reduction. Suppose
\[
             [z^{b m_0}]P(z)^{m_0}\not\equiv0\pmod p .
\]
For any \(r\) with \(n_r=(s p^r-1)/m_0\) integral and sufficiently
large, take the face \(j+2si=n_r\) with angular polynomial
\(z^{-b}P(z)\), and permit smaller terms with weight congruent
to \(n_r\bmod s\) and coefficients integral at \(p\).
For example, a negative angle \(-v\) is lifted by
\[
 (-1)^v x^{-v} t^{n_r}(1-t^{2s})^v
 =
 (-2)^v y^v t^{n_r}(1+t^2+\cdots+t^{2s-2})^v .
\]
This is regular, has the correct leading coefficient, and all
its terms have the required weight congruence. The positive
angles have nonnegative literal heights for \(n_r\ge2sa\).
The least degree of the lift is \(n_r+(2s-1)b\); terms of
smaller weight may be independently included. The \(m_0\)-th
moment is nonzero by the transition test.

If \(\gcd(a,b,m_1,\ldots,m_\nu)=1\), the auxiliary covers with
branch exponents \(n_r-b,m_1,\ldots,m_\nu,n_r-a\) are connected.
If a single exponent \(r\) gives an integral \(n_r\), then
\(p,s\) are prime to \(m_0\) and that congruence recurs along a
progression of exponents. Thus this gives unbounded connected
conductor. The coherent dimension at a primitive character \(i\)
of the cover of order \(n_r\) is \(D(i/n_r)\) where
\[
 D(x)=1+\lfloor bx\rfloor+\lfloor ax\rfloor
                   -\sum_\nu\lfloor m_\nu x\rfloor .
\]
Nonordinary examples can be certified from differing dimensions in
one primitive Frobenius orbit. If \(D\) is constant with value
different from one in a neighborhood of \(m_0/p^j\) in \((0,1)\),
for some fixed \(j\ge1\), then the character \(s p^{r-j}\)
has that dimension for large \(r\), because its ratio to \(n_r\)
tends to \(m_0/p^j\). That character is in the same Frobenius orbit
as character one, whose dimension is one. Hence that primitive
factor cannot have bijective coherent Frobenius.

Dimension mismatch on a different Frobenius orbit can be certified
by another rational interior test. Fix a reduced rational
\(\alpha=A/B\) in \((0,1)\) with \(p\equiv1\bmod B\) and restrict
to \(B^2\mid n_r\). For \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) the character
\(\alpha n_r+\epsilon\) is a unit for large \(r\). Indeed it is
prime to \(n_r/B\) and to \(B\). Its ratio approaches \(\alpha\)
from the \(\epsilon\) side. The character reached after \(r-j\)
Frobenius steps has ratio approaching
\[
          \{\alpha+\epsilon m_0/(s p^j)\}.
\]
If the latter is interior to an interval of \(D\) with value
different from the one-sided value at \(\alpha\), the primitive
factor is nonordinary. The progression \(B^2\mid n_r\) is
infinite whenever it has an initial solution, by working modulo
\(m_0 B^2\). The earlier \(p=37\) example is recovered with
\(s=1\), \(m_0=4\), \(\alpha=1/3\), \(\epsilon=-1\), \(j=1\).

## A nonzero transition with vanishing character-one return

Take \(p=5\), \(s=3\), \(a=1\), \(b=6\), \(m_0=2\) and
\[
 P(z)=(z-1)(z-2)^2(z-3)^2(z-4)^2 .
\]
Modulo 5 this is \((z^4-1)^2/(z-1)\), and hence
\([z^{12}]P(z)^2=3\) modulo 5. The family has connected
orders \(n_r=(3\cdot5^r-1)/2\), and normal degrees \(n_r+30\)
when the smaller terms obey that degree bound. The second moment
of each specified phase is nonzero. The relevant \(r\)-fold
Frobenius map sends the selected class in character three to the
character-one line with coefficient \(3\).

Here \(D(x)=1+\lfloor6x\rfloor-3\lfloor2x\rfloor\).
The ratio \(3\cdot5^{r-1}/n_r\) is strictly between \(1/3\)
and \(1/2\), so that character has dimension three. It belongs
to the same Frobenius orbit as character one, of dimension one.
The primitive factor is nonordinary for every \(r\ge1\).

In the first member \(n_1=7\), the character-one return map itself
vanishes. With the normalized Čech bases, the map from character
one to five sends its generator to the column \((0,1)^T\).
The next map from five to four has row \((1,0)\).
Thus the full return \(F_5^6\) on character one is zero.
In contrast, the row from character three to one is \((3,2,3)\)
and sends the selected third basis vector to \(3\).
This verifies at the same prime that the transition detects a
nonzero moment coefficient which the return-map test cannot detect.
The novelty here is in the sphere support congruence and the
coefficient it isolates. The coherent Frobenius matrix is the
classical Kummer calculation.

The character-one return also vanishes throughout the unbounded
progression \(r\equiv4\bmod6\). Write \(n=n_r\). Then
\(5^r\equiv4\bmod9\), so \(n\equiv19\bmod27\), and the character
\[
                         i=(17n+1)/27
\]
is in the orbit of character one. In fact \(27i\equiv1\bmod n\)
and \(27\cdot5^{3r}\equiv1\bmod n\). Its ratio \(x=i/n\) lies
strictly between \(3/5\) and \(19/30\). On this interval both
source and target have coherent dimension one:
\(D(x)=1\) and \(D(5x-3)=1\).
The Kummer Frobenius coefficient is the coefficient at degree
\(5-1=4\) of the polynomial with source exponents
\(\lfloor5\{-6x\}\rfloor=1\), \(\lfloor5\{x\}\rfloor=3\),
and \(\lfloor5\{2x\}\rfloor=1\) at each of the other three
finite points. In \(\mathbb F_5[z]\) this polynomial is
\[
  z(z-1)^3(z-2)(z-3)(z-4)=z(z-1)^2(z^4-1).
\]
Its degree-four coefficient vanishes. One map in the return
orbit is thus zero for every \(r\equiv4\bmod6\), even though
the selected transition into character one still has coefficient
\(3\). This gives unbounded connected covers where failure of the
return-map test persists.

The [standard-library checker](verify_cm_newton_selected_eigenlines.py)
replays the three order-seven matrices and rational second moments
for orders \(7,37,187,937\). All four normalized residues are
\(3\bmod5\). It also checks the zero orbit map for the first three
orders in the progression \(r\equiv4\bmod6\). As a negative control,
adding the smaller term
\(2t^2\) to the order-seven lift violates the weight congruence.
Its square contributes a denominator \(5\), and the computed
normalized residue becomes zero. This confirms the role of the
support restriction in the local test.
