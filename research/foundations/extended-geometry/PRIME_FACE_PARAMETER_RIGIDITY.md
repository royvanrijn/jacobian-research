# Prime-face parameter rigidity for spherical polynomial moments

Let K be any field of characteristic zero. Put

    A_K=K[x,y,t]/(t^2+2xy-1),
    Lambda(f)=CT_x (1/2) integral[-1,1]
                     f(x,(1-t^2)/(2x),t) dt.                (1)

The integral denotes the rational polynomial rule: Lambda(t^h)=0 for odd h and 1/(h+1) for even h. A phase is pure if Lambda(f^m)=0 for every positive integer m, and eventually pure if this holds for all sufficiently large m. All conclusions below concern moment vanishing; no all-marker classification or global quintic classification is asserted.

The principle is elementary: a nonzero residue polynomial in normalized face parameters prevents a pure coefficient family from dominating the parameter space. For one parameter this gives a finite exceptional set. The proof uses finite-type integral models, not an unproved ordinary-prime assertion for a Jacobian.

## 1. An abstract residue-parameter theorem

Let V=Spec C be an affine scheme of finite type over Q, parametrizing phases by finitely many coefficients. Let M_m in C be their rational moment functions, m>=1, and let tau_1,...,tau_r in C, r>=1, be regular parameter functions. Allow a fixed finite number of coefficient inversions in C. Choose a finitely generated Z-subalgebra C_0 of C containing the parameters and all coefficient generators, with C_0 tensor Q=C.

Assume there are an infinite set Sigma of rational primes, orders m_p tending to infinity along Sigma, and NONZERO polynomials

    H_p in F_p[T_1,...,T_r]

such that, outside finitely many p in Sigma,

    p M_{m_p} belongs to (C_0)_(p),
    (p M_{m_p}) mod p = H_p(tau_1,...,tau_r).                (2)

Here (C_0)_(p)=C_0 tensor_Z Z_(p); reducing it modulo p is the same as reducing C_0. Condition(2) only asks for integrality at the chosen critical order, not for all moment denominators simultaneously.

**Theorem 1.**

(a) For any eventually pure K-point of V, its r parameter values are algebraically dependent over Q. Their transcendence degree over Q is at most r-1.

(b) There is a nonzero G in Q[T_1,...,T_r] that vanishes on the parameter values of EVERY pure K-point, for EVERY characteristic-zero K. Thus the Zariski closure of the pure-locus parameter image is a proper closed subset.

(c) For EACH FIXED integer m0>=1 there is such a nonzero polynomial G_m0 for the locus defined by M_m=0 for all m>=m0. The polynomial may depend on m0. No single proper closed subset for the union over unbounded eventual thresholds is asserted.

If r=1, the possible values for pure phases, and for each fixed tail threshold, form finite sets of algebraic numbers, stable under automorphisms over Q. Part(a) by itself says only that an individual eventually pure phase has algebraic parameter.

### Proof of the individual assertion

Suppose the evaluated parameters were algebraically independent. Let R be the Z-subalgebra of K generated by the finitely many evaluated generators of C_0. It is a domain, finite type over Z, and the map

    Z[T_1,...,T_r] -> R,   T_i -> tau_i,

is injective. This map is finite presentation: its source is Noetherian, and R is finite type over it. It induces a dominant morphism on spectra. Chevalley's theorem makes the image constructible; it consequently contains a nonempty open D(F), with 0!=F in Z[T_1,...,T_r]. For all sufficiently large rational p, F modp is a nonzero polynomial, and the geometric image of Spec(R/pR) contains the dense open D(F modp) in A^r_Fbar_p. The last assertion follows by base change of the surjective morphism over D(F); a nonempty finite-type field fiber stays nonempty after field extension.

For a large p in Sigma, the order m_p exceeds the eventual threshold. The exact moment identity says p M_{m_p}=0 after evaluation in the characteristic-zero field K. By(2) this element belongs to R_(p), which injects into K, so it is zero in R_(p). On reduction, EVERY point of Spec(R/pR) therefore satisfies H_p(tau)=0. Its image cannot at the same time contain a dense open subset of affine r-space: H_p is nonzero. This contradiction proves(a). Notice that only ONE finite-type integral coefficient domain was used. There was no order-dependent choice of a number-field specialization and no inversion of all rational primes.

### Proof of the uniform pure and fixed-tail assertions

For fixed m0 the moment ideal

    I_m0=(M_m : m>=m0) subset C

is finitely generated, because C is Noetherian. Its zero scheme has finitely many irreducible components. On the function field of each component the universal phase has the specified eventual vanishing. By(a), its parameters satisfy a nonzero polynomial G_i over Q. This polynomial vanishes on the entire reduced component. The product of these finitely many G_i is nonzero and vanishes on all the parameter images. If the locus is empty use 1. This proves(c), and m0=1 proves(b). Hilbert basis DOES imply that some non-effective finite moment prefix defines each fixed-tail locus: each of finitely many ideal generators uses only finitely many M_m. The proof neither computes the parameter polynomials nor bounds that prefix, so it supplies no explicit cutoff or computed prefix certificate. The tail ideals descend as m0 increases, and no unsupported stabilization of that descending sequence is used.

## 3. Two residual faces with a common quadratic residue polynomial

Consider the following two finite coefficient spaces inside A_K:

(I)

    f=P(y,t)+Bxt^2,
    P=sum_{j+r<=5, j+2r<=7} p_jr y^j t^r,
    A=p_13, C=p_32, BC!=0;                                (3)

(II)

    f=P(y,t)+xt^2 Q(t), degQ<=2,
    P=sum_{j+r<=4, 3j+2r<=9} p_jr y^j t^r,
    A=p_13, C=p_30, B=[t^2]Q, BC!=0.                       (4)

Only the two specified supports are used. There is no claim here that they contain all pure quintics. The spaces include arbitrary coefficients below their supporting faces.

Eliminating y gives upper monomials

    p_jr(-1/2)^j x^{-j}t^{2j+r};                           (5)

the other terms in each expansion have smaller t height. In(I), the weight W=2h+3i of x^i t^h is at most 7, with equality exactly for the possible monomials of

    xt^2 [ B-(A/2)z-(C/8)z^2 ],  z=t^3/x^2.               (6)

Indeed 2(2j+r)-3j=j+2r<=7, whose equality cases within degree five are (j,r)=(1,3),(3,2). In(II), use W=2h+i<=9. Its equality face is

    xt^4 [ B-(A/2)z-(C/8)z^2 ],  z=t/x^2,                 (7)

because 2(2j+r)-j=3j+2r<=9. Its equality cases within degree four are (1,3),(3,0). The lower positive terms xt^2,xt^3 in(II) have strictly smaller weight.

Overall multiplication by mu and the ACTUAL angular automorphism x->rho*x, y->rho^-1*y preserve all moment-zero conditions, and act on the three face coefficients as

    (A,B,C) -> (mu rho^-1 A, mu rho B, mu rho^-3 C).

Over an algebraic extension choose rho^4=C/B and mu=1/(rho B). This normalizes B=C=1 and gives

    lambda=A/(B rho^2),   lambda^2=A^2/(BC).                (8)

Purity over K is preserved by this scalar extension. No ordering or complex embedding is required.

At order m=2k, angular balance in either normalized face requires exactly degree k in z. In(I) the top t height is 7k; in(II) it is 9k. Choose p=ell*k+1 with ell=7 in(I) and ell=9 in(II). The maximum angular-constant height is p-1. Every denominator in p times the moment is prime to p, and only the height p-1 term remains on reduction. Thus

    p Lambda(f^(2k)) modp = H_p(lambda),
    H_p(T)=[z^k](1-(T/2)z-z^2/8)^(2k).                   (9)

This is an identity in the normalized universal coefficient ring modulo p. Its degree in T is exactly k, with leading coefficient

    (-1/2)^k binom(2k,k),                                  (10)

a unit because 2k<p. All lower coefficients of f are unrestricted and disappear from this residue by the weight inequality, not by a numerical cancellation assumption. There are infinitely many primes 1 modulo either fixed ell; a cyclotomic Euclidean proof permits deletion of any finite excluded set.

**Corollary 2.** In either space(3)--(4), every eventually pure phase has A^2/(BC) algebraic over Q. For PURE phases there is a FINITE exceptional set of possible values of A^2/(BC), independent of the phase and the characteristic-zero coefficient field. The same holds separately for each fixed eventual threshold. No exceptional list is supplied.

Apply Theorem1 to the normalized one-parameter spaces and then square the finite lambda sets. If g(T) is a polynomial annihilating lambda, the nonzero resultant Res_T(g(T),T^2-X) annihilates A^2/(BC). This also makes the finite-set assertion independent of the choice of fourth root in(8).

There are elementary exclusions from these possible sets. If lambda=0, take k even, using primes 1 modulo 2ell. Then H_p(0)=binom(2k,k/2)(-1/8)^(k/2) is a unit. If lambda^2=-2, the quadratic in(9) is (1-lambda*z/4)^2, so H_p=binom(4k,k)(-lambda/4)^k is a unit because 4k<p. Therefore 0 and -2 are NOT possible values of A^2/(BC) for an eventually pure phase in these spaces. This last assertion uses only the residue formula, not ordinary reduction or a potential-CM theorem.

## 4. The full even stripped leading face

The entire even stripped degree-at-most-five space is

    f=P(y,t)+xt^2(q0+q2 t^2),
    P=sum_{j+r<=5, r even} p_jr y^j t^r.                  (11)

Write its four possible degree-five coefficients as

    B=[xt^4]f, A=[yt^4]P, C=[y^3t^2]P, D=[y^5]P.

The ordinary weight W=h+i on the literal Laurent chart is at most 5, because a top monomial of y^j t^r has h+i=j+r. Equality gives the EXACT leading face

    xt^4 [ B-(A/2)z-(C/8)z^2-(D/32)z^3 ], z=t^2/x^2.     (12)

In particular lower-degree terms do not affect the selected residue. At p=10k+1, m=2k, angular balance selects z-degree k and the maximum constant-term t height is 10k=p-1.

### A proper algebraic condition when B,D are nonzero

Assume BD!=0. Normalize them by rho^6=D/B, mu=1/(rho B), obtaining B=D=1 and two remaining parameters

    alpha=A/(B rho^2), beta=C/(B rho^4).                   (13)

The residue polynomial is

    H_p(alpha,beta)
      =[z^k](1-(alpha/2)z-(beta/8)z^2-z^3/32)^(2k).       (14)

It is NONZERO: its alpha^k coefficient is again the unit(10). Theorem1 with r=2 applies.

**Corollary 3.** Every eventually pure phase of(11) with BD!=0 has alpha,beta algebraically dependent over Q, for every choice of the normalization(13). There is some nonzero polynomial G in Q[U,V], independent of the phase, which vanishes at the normalized parameters of every PURE such phase. The same proper-algebraic-subset conclusion holds separately for each fixed eventual threshold. Neither G nor an explicit curve containing the image is computed; the statement means that the Zariski closure is proper, and allows it to be empty or zero-dimensional.

For comparison with rational invariants, alpha^3=A^3/(B^2D), beta^3=C^3/(BD^2), and alpha*beta=AC/(BD). The residual finite choices of rho do not change the transcendence degree. No global statement that leading coefficients with these dependences are realized by pure phases is implied.

### A finite set when D=0 and BC is nonzero

If D=0 and BC!=0, normalize B=C=1 exactly as in(8). Equation(12) has the SAME quadratic polynomial as(9), now with ell=10. The proof of(9)--(10) and the two elementary exclusions are unchanged, with p=10k+1.

**Corollary 4.** In the subfamily(11) with D=0, BC!=0, an eventually pure phase has A^2/(BC) algebraic over Q. The PURE locus, and separately every fixed-tail locus, has only finitely many possible such ratios, with 0 and -2 excluded. This assertion uses no ordinary-prime theorem. It does not settle any of the remaining algebraic values.

## 5. What is and is not effective

These are all-order necessary conditions, valid over arbitrary characteristic-zero fields. A finite exceptional polynomial or proper-image polynomial EXISTS by the Noetherian component argument, but this proof does not exhibit its coefficients, a generating moment prefix, or a complexity bound. No finite moment computation is offered as a proof of purity. An unrestricted union over eventual thresholds need not lie in one uniform proper algebraic subset; only the individual algebraic-dependence assertion and each fixed-threshold conclusion have been proved.

The face derivations above are self-contained. They require no unregistered Newton-face classification, no genus-six ordinary-reduction claim, and no fixed-contour Laurent-moment theorem. Global degree-five existence remains undecided by these results. In particular, this is not an all-marker safety criterion and not an assertion that every phase outside the displayed parameter loci has a specified numerical nonzero-moment cutoff.

## 6. Reverse implication: a hypothetical residual phase forces ordinary-prime density zero

There is an arithmetic consequence stronger than mere algebraicity of the residual parameter, but it is still a conditional obstruction rather than a proof of nonexistence.

**Theorem 5.** If an eventually pure phase exists in either residual space(I) or(II), then there is an explicitly specified abelian SIXFOLD over a number field whose ordinary primes have density ZERO, and retain density zero after every finite extension. For a pure phase, or a fixed-tail locus, only the finitely many parameter values of Corollary2 need be considered. No assertion that such phases or sixfold counterexamples actually exist is made.

### The fixed algebraic curve attached to the top parameter

By Corollary2 its normalized lambda is algebraic. The two elementary exclusions show lambda!=0 and lambda^2+2!=0. Define

    R_lambda(z)=1-(lambda/2)z-z^2/8,
    C_7: w^7=z^6 R_lambda(z)^2,
    C_9: w^9=z^8 R_lambda(z)^2,                             (16)

always taking the smooth projective normalization. The roots of R_lambda are distinct and nonzero. In homogeneous degree 2ell, the raw multiplicities of the two covers are respectively

    ell=7: (6,2,2,4),    ell=9: (8,2,2,6).

Every displayed multiplicity is between 1 and ell-1, and the multiplicity at 0 is coprime to ell, so the cover is connected. Riemann--Hurwitz gives genera 6 and 7. Take a number field F containing lambda, zeta_ell, and, if desired, the two branch roots. Its finite further enlargement does not affect the argument below.

Here the normalized character-one eigensheaf is exactly O(-2). Locally the character-i integral generator is w^i divided by a branch parameter to floor(i*a/ell), so at i=1 no branch correction occurs. A Cech representative of its one-dimensional H^1 is w/z. At a good absolute-degree-one prime mathfrak p of F, p=1 mod ell, write k=(p-1)/ell. Frobenius on this line is

    w/z -> z^{-p}w h(z)^k,

where h(z)=z^(ell-1) R_lambda(z)^2. Its scalar in H^1(O(-2)) is therefore

    [z^{p-1}]h(z)^k=[z^k]R_lambda(z)^(2k)=H_p(lambda).      (17)

This is exactly the residue polynomial in(9), not a different Hasse entry. The normalized-cover calculation remains valid at every good prime outside the fixed finite branch/discriminant exclusions.

For ell=7 take J=Jac(C_7), of dimension 6. For ell=9 the deck-character dimensions on H^1(O_C) for i=1,...,8 are

    (1,1,1,2,0,0,1,1).

The characters of order three have dimensions 1 and 0; all primitive order-nine characters have total dimension 6. The rational idempotents of Q[mu_9] thus split the Jacobian, up to isogeny, as an elliptic factor and a dimension-six primitive-order-nine factor J. One may realize the factors as images of integral multiples of the idempotent endomorphisms. The character-one line in(17) belongs to J. All data can be defined over F by a finite extension. No endomorphism-field classification is required.

### All but finitely many base primes admit a coefficient lift

The lower coefficients of the phase need not be algebraic. Extend the characteristic-zero coefficient field algebraically to contain F and the chosen normalization, preserving eventual vanishing. Choose s!=0 in O_F so that the normalized face coefficients, branch discriminant and all fixed rational denominators are integral/units where needed. Let R be the O_F[1/s]-subalgebra generated by the finitely many normalized lower coefficients. It is a finite-type DOMAIN, contained in the coefficient field, and contains the base ring injectively.

The map Spec R -> Spec O_F[1/s] is finite presentation and dominant. Chevalley's theorem implies that its image contains a nonempty open subset. Since the base is a Dedekind domain of dimension one, its complement consists of only FINITELY MANY prime ideals. In particular, every sufficiently large good absolute-degree-one base prime mathfrak p has some prime mathfrak q of R above it. The inclusion of residue fields

    kappa(mathfrak p) -> kappa(mathfrak q)

is injective. This is ONE fixed coefficient domain over ONE fixed number field, not a new lift selected by changing the phase or its arithmetic model.

At any such prime, let p be its rational characteristic and take m=2(p-1)/ell. It exceeds the eventual threshold for large p. As in the proof of Theorem1, p*Lambda(f^m)=0 is an identity in the localization of R at prime-to-p integers. On reduction at mathfrak q, formula(9) gives H_p(lambda)=0. But this element already belongs to the BASE residue field. Its injection into kappa(mathfrak q) forces

    H_p(lambda)=0 in kappa(mathfrak p)                     (18)

at EVERY sufficiently large good absolute-degree-one prime of F. The lift need not be an F-point or have finite residue degree over the base; field injectivity is enough.

### From the zero line to nonordinarity

For a smooth proper curve in characteristic p, ordinarity of its Jacobian implies Frobenius is bijective on H^1(O_C). More generally the p-rank equals the stable rank of this semilinear Frobenius; this follows from the Artin--Schreier sequence, whose fixed vectors compute etale H^1 with F_p coefficients. Thus a zero Frobenius map on a nonzero invariant line forces nonordinarity. The same criterion applies to an isogeny factor, away from the finite primes dividing a chosen splitting isogeny.

Equations(17)--(18) give exactly such a zero line inside J. Hence J is nonordinary at all sufficiently large absolute-degree-one primes of F. Those prime ideals have density one; prime ideals of larger absolute residue degree have density zero. The ordinary-prime density of J is therefore zero.

This remains true after any finite extension F'/F: every absolute-degree-one prime of F' lies over an absolute-degree-one prime of F, and ordinarity is unchanged by extending the residue field. Finitely many excluded primes do not change density. This proves Theorem5.

The conjectural context is explicit. The assertion that every abelian variety over a number field has a positive-density set of ordinary primes is often attributed to Serre; see Fite, arXiv2005.10185v3, Introduction preceding Theorem1. It is used ONLY as a stated CONJECTURE: any phase hypothesized in Theorem5 would contradict that assertion for the concrete sixfold J. Conversely, proving positive-density ordinary primes for every algebraic member of these two particular families would rule out the corresponding eventual phases. The proof has not established that ordinary-prime result.

For PURE phases, Corollary2 gives only finitely many candidate algebraic lambda values (up to its finite normalization choices), and thus finitely many such curves/factors to investigate. It does not compute the candidates, assert that every candidate is realized, or give a uniform finite list for all variable eventual thresholds. No reverse ordinary-density claim is made here for the two-parameter even family of Corollary3 or for its separate quadratic Corollary4.
