# Extensions of the Mathieu classifications

Use normalized invariant integration on an affine homogeneous space $X=G/H$ in characteristic zero. A function $P$ is **pure** if $\Lambda(P^m)=0$ at every positive order. It is **safe** if $\Lambda(QP^m)=0$ eventually for every fixed regular function $Q$. Thus the Mathieu–Zhao property says that every pure function is safe. The geometric nullcone consists of the functions whose orbit closure over the algebraic closure contains zero.

The [complex classification](../../../results/mathieu-reductive-homogeneous-spaces/main.tex) supplies the moment identities and the homogeneous-space reductions used below. Mathieu's original compact-group and rational-algebra formulations are in §§1.1–1.7 of [Some conjectures about invariant theory](https://smf.emath.fr/sites/default/files/2018-11/smf_sem-cong_2_263-279__sample.pdf). [Long's Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.16178v1) classify the compact connected group case and supply the original radial counterexamples.

## Algebraically closed fields

**Theorem.** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and let $G$ and $H\subseteq G$ be linear algebraic groups with reductive identity components. On $k[G/H]$ the normalized Reynolds kernel is Mathieu–Zhao exactly when $D=[G^\circ,G^\circ]\subseteq H$. If containment fails, a pure $P$ and one fixed $Q$ have nonzero marked moments at infinitely many orders.

When $G$ is connected, equality of the pure set and the geometric nullcone has the same criterion. In general this equality also requires injectivity of
$$
\bigoplus_i X^*(G^\circ/H_i)\ \longrightarrow\ X^*(G^\circ/D),
\qquad (\chi_i)_i\longmapsto\sum_i\chi_i,
$$
where $G^\circ/H_i$ are the components and the map is used when $D\subseteq H$.

### Transferring the radial witness

The case that requires field transfer is the line-and-doublet proposition of the complex classification. Suppose for connected $G$ that an irreducible module $V$ has an $H$-stable line $k\xi$ with character $\chi$, and a homomorphism $S:\mathrm{SL}_2\to G$ has a defining doublet in $V$. Fix a nonzero intertwiner $j:\,k^2\to V$ for that doublet.

All these data, including faithful matrix coordinates, descend to an algebraically closed subfield of $k$ algebraic over a finitely generated extension of $\mathbb Q$. Embed that subfield in $\mathbb C$ by sending a transcendence basis to algebraically independent real numbers. Its image $F$ is the algebraic closure inside $\mathbb C$ of the field generated by these real numbers. It is stable under conjugation, and $R=F\cap\mathbb R$ is real closed. Indeed positive elements have square roots in $R$, and odd-degree polynomials over $R$ have real roots in $F$. Also $F=R(i)$. Reductivity, irreducibility, and the intertwining and character identities here are geometric properties or polynomial identities, so the models have the same properties after these scalar extensions.

Embed $G$ faithfully on a direct sum $E$ containing $V$ as a summand. Write $B$ for a block-diagonal positive definite Hermitian matrix on $E$ and $\dagger$ for the corresponding adjoint, using the standard form on the doublet. Seek $B$ and $s\in G$ with the following properties.

1. The matrix Lie algebra of $G$ is stable under adjoint.
2. The three generators $iH_0,E_0-F_0,i(E_0+F_0)$ of the compact Lie algebra of $\mathrm{SU}_2$ have skew-adjoint images under the conjugate $S_s=\operatorname{Int}(s)\circ S$. Here $H_0,E_0,F_0$ are the usual $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ matrices.
3. The row $\xi^\dagger$ belongs to the $\chi^{-1}$-eigenspace of $H$ in $V^*$.

These are finite polynomial conditions and strict inequalities on real and imaginary parts over $R$. For the first condition introduce coefficients expressing the adjoints of a fixed Lie algebra basis in that basis. For the third use a basis over $F$ of the specified eigenspace. This eigenspace is the kernel of a coaction equation and commutes with base change. Positive definiteness is expressed by the leading principal minors.

A complex solution exists. Take a compact form $L$ of $H$ and a compact form $C$ of $G$ containing $L$. Conjugate the image of $\mathrm{SU}_2$ into $C$ and average the Hermitian forms on the summands over $C$. The action of $L$ on the line is unitary, so the dual row transforms by $\chi^{-1}$ on $L$ and hence on $H$ by Zariski density. The existence and conjugacy of compact forms used here are [Adams–Taïbi, Theorems 3.7–3.8](https://otaibi.perso.math.cnrs.fr/galois.pdf). Quantifier elimination for real closed fields therefore gives a solution with entries in $F$. [Cohen and Mahboubi, §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.3731)

For this solution let $C_B$ be the subgroup of $G(\mathbb C)$ preserving $B$. It is compact. Its skew-adjoint Lie algebra complexifies to the Lie algebra of $G$ by condition 1, so $C_B$ is Zariski dense in connected $G$. Condition 2 puts $S_s(\mathrm{SU}_2)$ in $C_B$. Define $j_s=\rho(s)j$, where $\rho$ acts on $V$, and the regular column and row
$$
z(g)=j_s^\dagger\rho(g)\xi,\qquad
w(g)=\xi^\dagger\rho(g)^{-1}j_s.
$$
On $C_B$, $w(g)=\overline{z(g)}^{\mathsf T}$. The column map $z$ is equivariant for $S_s(\mathrm{SU}_2)$ by unitarity and the intertwining identity for $j_s$. It is nonzero somewhere on $C_B$ because the span of $C_B\xi$ is all of the irreducible $V$ and $j_s^\dagger\ne0$.

On right multiplication by $h\in H$ the column $z$ scales by $\chi(h)$ and the row $w$ by its inverse. Evaluate the two polynomials $P_4,Q_4$ of the complex paper's normalizer witness on the matrix with columns $z(g)$ and $(-w_1(g),w_0(g))^{\mathsf T}$. These polynomials have right diagonal weight zero on the whole matrix space. Therefore this gives a regular pair over $F$ on $G/H$. Normalized integration on $C_B$ is the Reynolds functional after extension to $\mathbb C$. The radial proposition in §5 of the complex paper, applied to the pushforward under $z$, gives purity and strict positivity of every positive marked moment. Injectivity of $F\to\mathbb C$ and base change of the Reynolds projection give the asserted witness over $k$.

Only the finite matrix conditions were transferred between real closed fields. Positivity after that transfer proves the whole infinite sequence of marked inequalities in one step.

### Completing the homogeneous-space classification

For connected $G$, make the central cover used in the complex paper. Its higher-rank root-doublet and diagonal-coupling reductions are algebraic over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. The doublet condition can also be tested after embedding the algebraic closure of a finitely generated field of definition into $\mathbb C$. The intertwiner space is a kernel of a coaction equation and commutes with scalar extension. An active higher-rank simple factor gives the line through evaluation at $eH$ in the dual of an irreducible submodule of $k[G/H]$. When the remaining rank-one stabilizer projections are all surjective, a nonsingleton diagonal block gives the alternating line. Apply the preceding transfer in these two cases. A proper rank-one projection is covered by the [split rank-one proof](SPLIT_RANK_ONE_HOMOGENEOUS_SPACE_MATHIEU_CLASSIFICATION.md). Equivariant quotient pullback preserves the normalized trace.

The arguments of §7 of the complex paper apply over $k$. A connected-component witness extends by zero to the whole space. If $D\subseteq H$, the components are tori, the residual trace is the average of their constant terms, and finite-trace separation applies in characteristic zero. Apply Duistermaat–van der Kallen to each component. Its support then has a strictly separating functional, which proves safety. The lattice-injectivity condition lets the separate functionals extend to a single geometric cocharacter for each pure tuple. For noninjectivity the relation of component characters in the complex paper gives a pure tuple outside the nullcone. On the negative branch the fixed marked witness excludes geometric instability by the weight-bound argument in [Long's instability lemma](https://github.com/long-mathematics/mathieu-second-conjecture-spherical-pairs/blob/e9edac262b4b64eb68feb0e6e80496cefe4a1dee/mathieu_second_conjecture_spherical_pairs.tex).

For any characteristic-zero field $K$ and such a pair over $K$, the same containment criterion classifies the Mathieu–Zhao property **after every field extension** of $K$. In the positive case apply the algebraically closed result to the algebraic closure of each extension and restrict the trace. In the negative case the geometric witness is defined over a finite extension, since only finitely many coefficients of two regular functions have to be adjoined and all its moment identities commute with base change.

## Split groups over the ground field

**Group case.** For every characteristic-zero field $K$ and every split connected reductive $K$-group $G$, the normalized trace on $K[G]$ has Mathieu–Zhao kernel exactly when $G$ is a torus.

For the negative direction use a split adjoint simple quotient. Start on the simply connected split group of that type over $\mathbb Q$. In the representation of fundamental highest weight corresponding to a simple root, the highest-weight vector and its first lowering give the defining doublet for that root. These data are rational. The compact real Lie algebra for a split Chevalley basis is spanned over $\mathbb R$ by $iH_j$, $E_\alpha-E_{-\alpha}$, $i(E_\alpha+E_{-\alpha})$, with the usual compact conjugation of root vectors. Its compact group is stable under ordinary conjugation in the rational model and contains the root $\mathrm{SU}_2$. The Chevalley compact form is also described in [the rational coadjoint construction](PIECEWISE_DENSITY_AND_COMPACT_COADJOINT_ALL_ORDER_COUNTEREXAMPLES.md).

There is a rational positive definite symmetric Gram matrix on $V$ making this Lie algebra skew-adjoint. Averaging over the compact group gives a positive definite Hermitian solution. Averaging that matrix with its entrywise conjugate makes it real. The resulting real-symmetric skew-adjointness constraints are linear with rational coefficients because each generator is rational or $i$ times rational. Rational points are dense in the real solution space, and positive definiteness is open there. Use such a rational matrix, the rational intertwiner and a nonzero rational vector on the highest-weight line in the preceding radial construction. The central character on that line and its inverse on the dual row cancel, so both regular functions descend to the adjoint group over $\mathbb Q$. Their moments are zero and nonzero, respectively, at every positive order by the compact calculation. They remain so after base change to $K$ and pullback to $G$. The positive direction is scalar Laurent constant-term separation.

**Real closed fields.** For a real closed field $K$ and a homogeneous pair as in the first theorem with $G^\circ$ split, the criterion $[G^\circ,G^\circ]\subseteq H$ holds for the trace on $K[G/H]$ itself.

First take $K=\mathbb R$ and $G$ connected and split. The preceding reduction to a rank-one projection or to a line and a defining doublet works over the field. Split irreducible modules are absolutely irreducible, so the evaluation line and a root doublet can be taken over the field in the higher-rank case. In the subdirect case, a coupling is a graph of automorphisms between split $\mathrm{SL}_2$ factors. An automorphism is conjugation by a matrix in $\mathrm{GL}_2(K)$, so the alternating line after this identification is defined over $K$. The rank-one projection uses the ground-field rank-one proof.

For the line-and-doublet case choose a compact conjugation of $G_\mathbb C$ compatible both with the given real conjugation and with a compact conjugation of $H_\mathbb C$. This is [Adams–Taïbi, Theorem 3.13 and Lemma 3.14](https://otaibi.perso.math.cnrs.fr/galois.pdf), including disconnected $H$. The compact group is stable under ordinary conjugation, so the Hermitian matrices can be taken real symmetric by averaging each with its conjugate. For the fixed root subgroup use the compact conjugation of the split Chevalley model, whose compact form contains its $\mathrm{SU}_2$. The uniqueness statement in Theorem 3.13 conjugates this compact form to the desired one by $s\in G(\mathbb R)^\circ$. The same radial proof now uses real matrices and gives real witnesses.

Here is the transfer to arbitrary real closed $K$. Over a finitely generated subfield $E\subset K$ containing the pair, its split-model isomorphism and the split representation data, the compact-matrix conditions are a finite existential formula with $B$ real symmetric and $s$ real. Every embedding $E\to\mathbb R$ admits a solution by the preceding argument. Any finite list of strict sign conditions on elements of $E$ that holds in $K$ can be preserved by such an embedding. To see this, write $E=\mathbb Q(t_1,\ldots,t_d)[\alpha]$ with $\alpha$ separable, and express the signs, nonzero denominators and nonvanishing derivative of the minimal polynomial as a finite real formula over $\mathbb Q$. It has a solution over $\mathbb R$ by Tarski. By the real implicit function theorem the solution branch persists on an open set of parameter values. Pick algebraically independent real parameter values in that set to obtain a field embedding. For $d=0$ use the real root from the same formula.

Quantifier elimination now gives the compact-matrix solution over $K$. Otherwise finitely many signs would force failure over $\mathbb R$ along an embedding preserving those signs. To verify the moments, adjoin the selected matrix entries to the finitely generated field and embed it in $\mathbb R$ preserving the positivity of the leading principal minors. This single embedding verifies every moment of the pair via the radial proof and injectivity. Thus the witness is defined over $K$. For disconnected $G$ use the open-and-closed $K$-subspace $G^\circ H/H$ for the negative branch and extend the pair by zero. For the positive branch the criterion after algebraic closure already implies the ground-field property.

### A split homogeneous example over every characteristic-zero field

For $n\ge3$, let $G=\mathrm{SL}_n$ and let $H=S(\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}\times\mathrm{GL}_1)$ be block diagonal. Over $\mathbb Q$ take $z$ to be the first two entries of $g e_n$ and $w$ the first two entries of $e_n^{\mathsf T}g^{-1}$. The last coordinate character of $H$ and its inverse cancel in the radial polynomials. On $\mathrm{SU}(n)$, $w=\overline z^{\mathsf T}$, and the squared norm of $z$ is the sum of the first two squared moduli of a uniformly distributed unit vector in $\mathbb C^n$. Those moduli have the Dirichlet law with parameters all one. This follows by normalizing independent standard complex Gaussians, whose squared moduli are independent exponentials. Hence $r=\|z\|^2$ has the beta law with parameters $(2,n-2)$. For the same fixed pair as in the radial construction,
$$
 \Lambda(P^m)=0,\qquad
 \Lambda(QP^m)=\beta_m\frac{(2)_{6m-2}}{(n)_{6m-2}},
 \qquad
 \beta_m=\frac{2m}{3m+1}\frac{(2m)!(4m)!}{(6m)!}.
$$
Here $(a)_j=a(a+1)\cdots(a+j-1)$. The formulas and the fixed pair extend from $\mathbb Q$ to every characteristic-zero field. For $\mathrm{SL}_3/S(\mathrm{GL}_2\times\mathrm{GL}_1)$ the first marked moment is $1/90$.

## The universal algebra formulation of Mathieu I

Let $K$ be a characteristic-zero field and $G$ a linear algebraic $K$-group with reductive identity component. The following are equivalent.

- $G^\circ$ is a torus.
- For every commutative rational $G$-algebra $A$, the canonical Reynolds projection $R_G:A\to A^G$ has a Mathieu–Zhao kernel.
- For every such $A$ and every $f\in A$ with $R_G(f^m)=0$ for every $m\ge1$, each fixed geometric isotypic component of $f^m$ vanishes eventually.

A geometric isotypic component is taken after scalar extension to an algebraic closure. This is the isotypic condition in Mathieu's Corollary 1.7. To prove the positive direction, pass to the algebraic closure and take the finitely generated $G$-stable subalgebra $B$ generated by a finite-dimensional submodule containing $f$. Put $T=G^\circ$, choose component representatives $g_1=1,g_2,\ldots,g_r$, and let $L_i$ be the Laurent polynomial that records the $T$-weights of $g_i f$ with their coefficients in $B$. Tori act by a grading, so taking the constant coefficient of $L_i^m$ gives $R_T((g_i f)^m)$. Averaging over the components gives
$$
 \sum_{i=1}^r \operatorname{CT}(L_i^m)=0\qquad(m\ge1).
$$
Take a primary decomposition $0=\bigcap_j\mathfrak q_j$ of $B$. For each $j$, put $\mathfrak p_j=\sqrt{\mathfrak q_j}$ and choose $e_j$ with $\mathfrak p_j^{e_j}\subseteq\mathfrak q_j$. In the fraction field of $B/\mathfrak p_j$, the finite-trace lemma of the complex paper applies to the Laurent polynomials $L_i$ and gives zero pure constant terms for each separately.

Write $L_1=U+V$ by putting the coefficients in $\mathfrak p_j$ in $V$. If $U$ is nonzero, the theorem of Duistermaat–van der Kallen gives a functional strictly positive on its support. The theorem applies in characteristic zero by embedding the finitely generated field of the coefficients into $\mathbb C$. For a fixed weight, the terms of $(U+V)^m$ with fewer than $e_j$ factors of $V$ eventually cannot reach that weight. Terms with at least $e_j$ such factors have coefficients in $\mathfrak q_j$. If $U=0$, all coefficients are in $\mathfrak q_j$ for $m\ge e_j$. Since the number of primary components is finite, each fixed weight coefficient of $f^m$ is eventually in their intersection, hence zero. A simple $G$-module has finitely many $T$-weights. Vanishing of these weight components implies vanishing of its whole isotypic projection. Likewise a fixed multiplier has finitely many weights, so eventually the product with $f^m$ has zero weight-zero component and therefore zero Reynolds projection.

If $G^\circ$ is non-toral the homogeneous theorem gives a witness on $\overline K[G_{\overline K}]$. The pair lives on $G_E$ over some finite extension $E/K$. Regard $E[G_E]$ as a rational $G$-algebra over $K$. Its invariants are $E$, and the $G$-projection is the normalized trace with values in $E$. The pair contradicts the universal kernel condition. It also contradicts the universal isotypic condition, since trace of a product pairs only dual types and a fixed multiplier has finitely many types. This argument permits disconnected $G$ and arbitrary commutative rational $G$-algebras, including nonreduced ones.

## Compact groups and compact homogeneous spaces

Let $C$ be any compact Hausdorff group, $L$ a closed subgroup, and use the complex-valued $C$-finite functions on $C/L$. Then the normalized invariant integral has Mathieu–Zhao kernel exactly when
$$
 \overline{[C^\circ,C^\circ]}\ \subseteq\ L.
$$
In particular the group case holds exactly when $C^\circ$ is abelian.

For a compact Lie quotient $J$ of $C$, write $L_J$ for the image of $L$. The compact Lie pair $J/L_J$ has the complexification given by the Zariski closures of a faithful unitary model of $J$ and its closed subgroup $L_J$. These closures are reductive on identity components. Representative functions on $J$ are the restrictions of regular functions on the closure. One can see this by using the matrix-entry algebra, which contains conjugate entries since the model is unitary. It is dense by Stone–Weierstrass, so projection onto each finite-dimensional Peter–Weyl isotype is onto. Right invariance is equivalent to invariance under the closure of $L_J$. The intersection of that closure with $J$ is $L_J$, since averaging matrix-entry polynomials over $L_J$ separates distinct right cosets. The identity component $J^\circ$ is Zariski dense in the identity component of its complexification. Consequently the preprint applies to $J/L_J$, with positive criterion $[J^\circ,J^\circ]\subseteq L_J$.

Any two representative functions on $C/L$ factor together through a compact Lie quotient $J/L_J$ by taking the direct sum of their finite-dimensional representations. The image of $C^\circ$ in $J$ is $J^\circ$. Indeed the quotient by the image is a quotient of the profinite group $C/C^\circ$ and is totally disconnected. Haar measure pushes forward to the normalized invariant measure on the quotient. Thus containment in $L$ implies safety by the Lie quotient criterion. Conversely if an element of $[C^\circ,C^\circ]$ lies outside $L$, a finite-dimensional representation separates that element from the compact set $L$. To choose it, cover $L$ by finitely many point-separating Peter–Weyl neighborhoods and take the direct sum. The resulting compact Lie quotient has a bad pair, which pulls back with its marked moments intact. [Peter–Weyl and compact Lie quotients](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/fausk.pdf)

For the corresponding geometric nullcone condition on compact homogeneous spaces, use the Zariski closure of the $C$-orbit of a representative function in its finite-dimensional complex orbit span. Equality with the pure set holds exactly when every compact Lie quotient pair $J/L_J$ satisfies the lattice criterion of the complex classification. Both the pure condition and that orbit closure are computed in a common Lie quotient for each individual function. When $C$ is connected every Lie quotient is connected, so this criterion is again $\overline{[C,C]}\subseteq L$.

For $C=\mathrm O(2)$ and $L=\{1\}$ the Mathieu–Zhao property holds. In coordinates $z$ on the rotation circle and on its translate by a reflection, the representative function $(z,z^{-1})$ is pure and safe. Its two nonzero components have opposite weights for the same identity torus. Reflection interchanges the two spanning functions. The product of the coordinate functions on their span is therefore an invariant equal to one at this function, so its orbit closure excludes zero.

## Ground-field witnesses on Weil restrictions

Let $E/K$ be finite separable in characteristic zero and let $Y=G/H$ be an affine reductive homogeneous space over $E$. If $(P,Q)$ is a fixed witness on $Y$, the Weil restriction $\operatorname{Res}_{E/K}Y$ has a fixed witness over $K$ with exactly the same surviving marked orders. The two functions are the norms of the pullbacks of $P,Q$ to the base extension of the restriction to $E$. After scalar extension to $\overline K$, the restriction is the product of $Y^\sigma$ over all $K$-embeddings $\sigma:E\hookrightarrow\overline K$, and each norm is the product of the corresponding conjugate functions. This product descends to $K$ by Galois invariance. The restriction is homogeneous under $\operatorname{Res}_{E/K}G$ with stabilizer $\operatorname{Res}_{E/K}H$, as can be checked on this product after faithfully flat extension. Normalized traces multiply on products, hence
$$
 \Lambda_{\operatorname{Res}_{E/K}Y}(Q_{\rm N}P_{\rm N}^{m})
 =N_{E/K}\bigl(\Lambda_Y(QP^m)\bigr).
$$
The same formula for $Q=1$ proves purity. For example, a non-toral split connected group over $E$ gives a ground-field counterexample on its restriction of scalars over $K$.

## Ground-field boundary

The noncompact split and compact real forms already give different answers for group traces with real coefficients. The real quaternion norm-one group has positive square trace on every nonzero real regular function, so only zero is pure. Its complexification has a fixed witness. For arbitrary characteristic-zero fields a ground-field classification of all individual nonsplit homogeneous spaces still requires information about the field and form.
