# Full automorphism-orbit degrees of three known Hopf seeds

Let K be any characteristic-zero field and

    A=K[x,y,t]/(t^2+2xy-1).

The degree of a nonzero element is its least total polynomial degree in
this quotient. Consider

    f4=(1+x)y-(1+x)(2+x)t^2/2
       =(1+x)^3 y-(x+1)(x+2)/2,

and

    f8=t(C(x)+E(x)t^2)/x,
    C(x)=4(1+x)^2,  E(x)=-(1+x)^4(x+2)^2.

The latter is regular since C(0)+E(0)=0. An explicit polynomial is

    f8=8yt+8t+4xt
       -(x^5+8x^4+26x^3+44x^2+41x+20)t^3.

Also put

    f6=t*((1+x)^2-(1+x)^4*t^2)/x
       =2yt+2t+xt-(x^3+4x^2+6x+4)t^3.

This is regular and has least degree6.

**Theorem OrbitDegreeSpectra.** Over K and every field extension:

1. The degrees in Aut_K(A).f4 are exactly the even integers at least4.
   In particular no automorphism sends f4 to degree5 or7, or to any
   prime degree.
2. The degrees in Aut_K(A).f8 are exactly8 and the integers

       s*(8+nu*k),
       s odd>=1, nu in {3,13,16}, k>=1.

   Equivalently s can be a possibly empty product of odd integers>=3. Thus this orbit has minimum degree8,
   and its first prime degree is11. More precisely its prime degrees
   are exactly the primes p with p>=11 and

       p=2 mod3 OR p=8 mod13.

3. The degrees in Aut_K(A).f6 are exactly the positive multiples of3
   at least6. This orbit contains no prime degree.

These are statements about these three specified automorphism orbits,
not about all unsafe phases, all quartic phases, all degree-eight phases,
or all primitive balanced Image witnesses. The degree arguments do not
assume purity or safety. Trace invariance supplies the usual unsafe-orbit
interpretation separately and is not used to prove the degree formulas.

## 1. The applicable classical group theorem

Use U=x,V=-2y,W=t to identify the surface with UV=W^2-1.
Blanc--Bot--Poloni, *Real forms of some Gizatullin surfaces and
Koras--Russell threefolds*, Publ. Mat.67(2023),851--890,
Theorems3.5--3.6, printed860--862, give affine and triangular generators
and their amalgam for degree at least2 over any field. Their Lemma3.9,
printed863--864, treats W^2-1 explicitly. Thus the quadratic case here
is included, rather than inferred from a degree-at-least-three theorem.
Primary author PDF:

    https://algebra.dmi.unibas.ch/bot/publmat.pdf

Only the resulting generation by the affine group O and the triangular
group B, and their intersection, are needed below. Write

    J=x partial_t-t partial_y,
    beta_H=exp(H(x)J):
      x -> x, t -> t+xH(x), y -> y-H(x)t-xH(x)^2/2.

Here B is generated by these beta_H and affine elements that preserve
the line Kx. Put T=O intersect B. Its shears have constant H; arbitrary
nonconstant H has degree k>=1. Every automorphism can be expressed as

    alpha_r beta_(H_r) alpha_(r-1) ... beta_(H_1) alpha_0,       (O1)

where the H_i are nonconstant, each alpha_i belongs to O, and each
interior alpha_i is outside T. The cases r=0 and empty end factors
are allowed. Indeed split off constant shears into O and repeatedly
absorb an interior factor in T into its adjacent B factors; each such
operation reduces the number of factors. The classical amalgam theorem
also supplies this expression, but uniqueness is not needed here.

For completeness, every ambient affine automorphism preserving this
quadric is linear orthogonal: writing it Lz+b, comparison with a scalar
multiple of the defining equation gives L^T Q b=0. Invertibility forces
b=0, and comparison of constants gives that scalar1.

If alpha in O satisfies alpha(x)=u x+v t+w y, then

    v^2+2uw=0.                                             (O2)

In particular w=0 implies v=0 and alpha(x) is a nonzero multiple of x.
Conversely preservation of that line puts alpha in T: preservation of
the quadratic equation forces alpha(t)=epsilon t+c x, epsilon^2=1,
and the corresponding affine triangular formula for alpha(y).
Thus alpha outside T is equivalent to a nonzero y coefficient of
alpha(x).

## 2. LND height and the first nonlinear degree

For nonzero g define h_J(g)=max{j:J^j g!=0}. This finite degree is
additive on products: the top Leibniz coefficient is a nonzero binomial
coefficient times two nonzero factors in a domain. Characteristic zero
and the domain assumption are used here. Every linear form has height
at most2, and every element of ordinary degree at most D has height
at most2D.

The ordinary filtration has associated graded domain

    gr A=K[x,y,t]/(t^2+2xy).

Indeed the leading term of any nonzero multiple of t^2+2xy-1 is
the leading homogeneous factor times t^2+2xy. Thus the principal
relation gives the stated initial ideal. The rank-three quadratic
t^2+2xy is absolutely irreducible: a product of two linear forms has
quadratic rank at most2 in characteristic not2. The graded quotient
is consequently a domain over K and every extension.

The derivation J preserves the filtration. If g has degree D, actual
height nu, and its leading homogeneous form also has height nu, then
J^nu g has degree D. Moreover ker_A J=K[x]: after inverting x,
J=x partial_t on K[x,x^-1,t], and a negative power of x cannot be
regular in A. Explicitly, if x^-r P(x) is regular with r>=1 and
P(0)!=0, then x^r f=P(x) in A reduces modulo x to0=P(0),
which is impossible in the nonzero quotient A/(x).
Consequently

    J^nu g=c x^D+lower x powers, c!=0.

For a nonconstant H of degree k, expansion of beta_H g gives

    deg(beta_H g)=D+k nu,                                 (O3)

and its leading form is a nonzero scalar times x^(D+k nu).
The nu-th summand has this degree; every smaller j has degree at most
D+kj<D+k nu. There is no cancellation or generic-coefficient caveat.

## 3. First heights for every linear conjugate of f4

Let ell=alpha(x), m=alpha(y) for an arbitrary alpha in O. Both are
nonzero isotropic linear forms. Each has height0 or2 by(O2), and they
cannot both have height0, since they are linearly independent. Hence
their height pairs are exactly the three possible types

    (0,2), (2,0), (2,2).

The element is

    alpha(f4)=(1+ell)^3 m-(1+ell)(2+ell)/2.

Its respective heights are2,6,8. For the first type the last derivative
is (1+ell)^3 J^2 m, a nonzero polynomial of degree4. In the other
two types the unique highest-height term is ell^3 m, of heights6,8;
the lower terms cannot cancel it. Its homogeneous degree-four form
is ell^3 m and has the same height. Thus(O3) applies with D=4 and

    nu in {2,6,8}.                                        (O4)

In particular EVERY first nonlinear factor in(O1) produces an even
degree at least6 with unique pure-x leading form. This controls all
affine orientations, not just the original x-axis shear.

## 4. First heights for every linear conjugate of f8

Let ell=alpha(x), n=alpha(t), and g=alpha(f8). The polynomial identity

    ell*g=n*C(ell)+n^3*E(ell)                             (O5)

is valid in A. If h_J(ell)=0, orthogonality forces n=c t+d x with
c!=0, so h_J(n)=1. The right side has height3, hence g has height3.
If h_J(ell)=2, the nonisotropic form n has height1 or2: height0 would
make it a multiple of the isotropic x, which is impossible. Since C
has degree2 and E degree6, the second term of(O5) has height
12+3h_J(n), strictly exceeding the first term's4+h_J(n). Additivity
and cancellation of the ell factor show

    h_J(g)=10+3h_J(n)=13 or16.

The leading homogeneous form of f8 is -x^5 t^3; after alpha it is
-ell^5 n^3. Its height is exactly the computed3,13 or16. Therefore
(O3) applies with D=8 and

    nu in {3,13,16}.                                      (O6)

This uses the exact regular element and product identity(O5), so it
does not treat a formal rational expression as an independent phase.

## 5. Every later reduced factor multiplies degree by an odd integer

Suppose a stage has degree D and leading form c x^D, c!=0. An interior
alpha outside T sends its leading form to c ell^D with ell=alpha(x)
of height2. This leading form has height2D. Every lower-degree part
has height at most2D-2, so the full element has height2D and its last
derivative has degree D. Equation(O3) gives after beta_H

    D -> D+2D*k=(2k+1)D,                                 (O7)

again with unique pure-x leading form. End affine factors preserve
least degree. Applying(O7) through(O1) proves all the degree exclusions
and the stated containment in the two spectra.

## 6. Realizations and prime consequences

For f4 use the unrotated height2 and H=x^k. This realizes4+2k for
every k>=1; identity realizes4. These are all even integers at least4.

For f8, the three height types are realized over K itself by alpha
equal to identity, the exchange x<->y with t fixed, and exp(J_y),
where J_y=y partial_t-t partial_x. The latter has

    ell=x-t-y/2, n=t+y,

both of height2. Thus every8+nu*k in(O6) occurs. Between successive
nonlinear factors choose the exchange x<->y, which is outside T; then
every multiplier2k_i+1 occurs by(O7). This proves equality of spectra.

Every later factor2k_i+1>=3 makes the final degree composite. A prime
in the f8 orbit must therefore be8+3k,8+13k or8+16k. The last is even
and at least24; the first two give exactly the prime congruences in
the theorem. Identity has degree8, and H=x produces degree11.

The conclusions hold after every field extension, since the group
theorem applies over that extension and the argument is algebraic.
They obstruct obtaining degree5/7 from these known unsafe orbits;
they do not prove that every unsafe phase belongs to one of them.
No historical-priority claim is made for these orbit consequences.

## 7. The degree-six comparison orbit

For f6 the leading form is -x^3 t^3 and the analogue of(O5) is

    ell*alpha(f6)=n*(1+ell)^2-n^3*(1+ell)^4.

If ell has J-height0, n has height1 and alpha(f6) has height3.
Otherwise ell has height2 and n has height1 or2. The second term
has height8+3h_J(n), strictly greater than the first term; subtracting
the ell factor gives heights9 or12. The leading form -ell^3 n^3
has the same respective heights3,9,12 and last derivative degree6.
Thus the first nonlinear factor has degree6+nu*k for nu in{3,9,12},
k>=1. Every later reduced factor multiplies by an odd integer. All
degrees are multiples of3 and at least6; identity realizes6 and the
native height3 shears realize6+3k for every k>=1. This proves item3.

An arbitrary product of odd integers>=3 is odd, and conversely every
odd s>=3 is itself the one allowed factor2*((s-1)/2)+1. This proves
the compact s-form of the degree-eight spectrum. Prime degrees13 and19
are not in that one orbit, although other known constructions supply
them. No density claim for its prime-degree set is made here.
