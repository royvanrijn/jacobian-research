# A polynomial Keller map with a dense multiplicative family of Hasse-principle failures

## 1. The fixed map and target line

Use the single polynomial map
\[
 F:\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb Q},
 \qquad \det DF=-2,
\]
defined in
[INFINITE_HASSE_KELLER_FIBERS.md](INFINITE_HASSE_KELLER_FIBERS.md).
For every rational parameter \(a\), its target
\[
 y_a=\left(1,\frac{32a}{9},\frac{8a+1}{3}\right)
 \tag{1.1}
\]
has complete inverse polynomial
\[
 (X^3-a)(X^2+X+1). \tag{1.2}
\]
The determinant and factor identity are replayed by
[`verify_infinite_hasse_keller_fibers.py`](../scripts/verify_infinite_hasse_keller_fibers.py).

## 2. The multiplicative local criterion

### Theorem 2.1

Let \(a>1\) be an integer such that
\[
 a\equiv1\pmod9,\qquad a\notin\mathbb Q^3,
 \qquad p\mid a\Longrightarrow p\equiv1\pmod3. \tag{2.1}
\]
Then \(F^{-1}(y_a)\) is a reduced degree-five scheme with a point over
every completion of \(\mathbb Q\) and no rational point.

### Proof

The cubic \(X^3-a\) has no rational root because \(a\notin\mathbb Q^3\);
the quadratic \(X^2+X+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\).  Their
resultant is \((a-1)^2\), so (1.2) is squarefree for \(a>1\).  The complete
fiber therefore has no rational point.

Over \(\mathbb R\), the cubic has a root.  Since every prime divisor of
\(a\) is \(1\pmod3\), the integer \(a\) is odd.  Thus \(X=1\) is a simple
root of \(X^3-a\) modulo \(2\), and ordinary Hensel lifting gives a root
over \(\mathbb Q_2\).

At \(3\), write \(a=1+9k\) and put \(X=1+3T\).  Then
\[
 X^3-a=9h(T),\qquad h(T)=T+3T^2+3T^3-k.
\]
Modulo \(3\), one has \(h(T)\equiv T-k\) and \(h'(T)\equiv1\).  The root
\(T\equiv k\pmod3\) therefore lifts uniquely to \(\mathbb Z_3\).

Let \(p\ne2,3\).  If \(p\equiv1\pmod3\), the fixed quadratic has two
distinct roots modulo \(p\), hence a root over \(\mathbb Q_p\).  This
includes every prime dividing \(a\).  If \(p\equiv2\pmod3\), then
\(p\nmid a\) by (2.1), cubing is a bijection on
\(\mathbb F_p^\times\), and the cubic has a simple root modulo \(p\).
This covers every place.

## 3. The full family and a clean subfamily

Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A={}&\{a>1:a\equiv1\pmod9,\ a\notin\mathbb Q^3,\
                   p\mid a\Rightarrow p\equiv1\pmod3\},\\
\mathcal A_9={}&\{a>1:a\notin\mathbb Q^3,\
                   p\mid a\Rightarrow p\equiv1\pmod9\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(\mathcal A_9\subset\mathcal A\).  The second family is especially
transparent: it is the multiplicative semigroup generated by primes
\(1\pmod9\), with \(1\) and the perfect cubes removed.

For every \(a\in\mathcal A\), (1.1) has primitive projective coordinates
\[
 [9:9:32a:24a+3] \tag{3.1}
\]
and
\[
 H(y_a)=32a. \tag{3.2}
\]
Thus the targets are pairwise distinct.

## 4. Selberg--Delange count for the full family

First ignore the congruence \(a\equiv1\pmod9\) and the cube exclusion.
The Dirichlet series for integers supported on primes \(1\pmod3\) is
\[
 \mathcal F_3(s)
 =\prod_{p\equiv1(3)}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
 =\zeta(s)^{1/2}G_3(s). \tag{4.1}
\]
If \(\chi_{-3}\) is the nonprincipal character modulo \(3\), then the
Euler factors give
\[
 G_3(1)=
 \left[
  \frac23L(1,\chi_{-3})
  \prod_{p\equiv2(3)}(1-p^{-2})
 \right]^{1/2}>0. \tag{4.2}
\]

The supported integers lie in the three residue classes \(1,4,7\pmod9\).
Character orthogonality on this order-three group and the
character-twisted Selberg--Delange theorem show that the three classes
have equal main terms.  Indeed, the untwisted series has pole exponent
\(1/2\), while for either nontrivial character the mean of its values on
\(1,4,7\) is zero, so the corresponding twist contributes
\(o(X/\sqrt{\log X})\).  Removing cubes changes the count by at most
\(O(X^{1/3})\).

Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 \#\{a\in\mathcal A:a\le X\}
 \sim
 \frac{G_3(1)}{3\sqrt\pi}\,
 \frac{X}{\sqrt{\log X}}.
 } \tag{4.3}
\]
Numerically, the leading constant in (4.3) is approximately
\[
 0.1004055.
\]
By (3.2), the corresponding target count is
\[
 \boxed{
 \#\{a\in\mathcal A:H(y_a)\le B\}
 \sim
 \frac{G_3(1)}{96\sqrt\pi}\,
 \frac{B}{\sqrt{\log B}}.
 } \tag{4.4}
\]

This is larger by a factor of order \(\sqrt{\log B}\) than the prime
subfamily count \(B/(192\log B)\).

## 5. The clean Euler product

For the clean subfamily,
\[
 \mathcal F_9(s)
 =\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\p\mid n\Rightarrow p\equiv1(9)}}n^{-s}
 =\prod_{p\equiv1(9)}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}. \tag{5.1}
\]
The primes \(1\pmod9\) have Dirichlet density \(1/6\), and the six
Dirichlet characters modulo \(9\) factor (5.1) as
\[
 \mathcal F_9(s)=\zeta(s)^{1/6}G_9(s), \tag{5.2}
\]
where \(G_9\) is holomorphic and nonzero near \(s=1\).  Equivalently,
\[
 G_9(1)=
 \lim_{s\to1^+}(s-1)^{1/6}
 \prod_{p\equiv1(9)}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}>0. \tag{5.3}
\]
Selberg--Delange and the \(O(X^{1/3})\) cube exclusion give
\[
 \boxed{
 \#\{a\in\mathcal A_9:a\le X\}
 \sim
 \frac{G_9(1)}{\Gamma(1/6)}
 \frac{X}{(\log X)^{5/6}}.
 } \tag{5.4}
\]

Already the products of two primes \(1\pmod9\) contribute order
\[
 \frac1{36}\frac{X\log\log X}{\log X}, \tag{5.5}
\]
so their gain over the one-prime family is a \(\log\log X\) factor.
Allowing arbitrarily many factors produces the stronger exponent in
(5.4).

## 6. Exact finite experiment

The dependency-free enumerator
[`count_multiplicative_hasse_parameters.py`](../scripts/count_multiplicative_hasse_parameters.py)
checks the support conditions, cube exclusion, target primitivity,
distinctness, height formula, and the one- and two-prime subcounts.
At \(X=10^6\) it finds

| family | count | scaled count |
|---|---:|---:|
| full \(\mathcal A\) | \(26846\) | \(A(X)\sqrt{\log X}/X=0.0997845\) |
| clean \(\mathcal A_9\) | \(16107\) | \(A_9(X)(\log X)^{5/6}/X=0.143655\) |
| primes \(1\pmod9\) | \(13063\) | — |
| products of two such primes | \(2853\) | — |

[`multiplicative_hasse_parameters_1000000.json`](../artifacts/generated-results/multiplicative_hasse_parameters_1000000.json).

The analytic input is the classical Selberg--Delange theorem in the

G. Tenenbaum,

Acta Arith. **200** (2021), 349--369.

## 7. Scope

The local theorem applies to every parameter in \(\mathcal A\).  Equations
(4.3)--(4.4) count that full sufficient family, not all Hasse-failing
targets of the fixed map.  The finite enumeration is a regression and
numerical convergence check; the asymptotics come from the Euler products
and Selberg--Delange, not from the bounded search.
