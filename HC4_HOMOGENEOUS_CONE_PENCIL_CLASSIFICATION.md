# Homogeneous cone-pencil classification for the moving `HC4` frontier

## Status and scope

This note isolates the homogeneous geometry behind polynomially moving kernel
flags in `HC4RSD17`.  It is independent of total degree.

> **Theorem HC4RSD55 — rank-three quaternary cone-pencil classification.**
> Let `K` have characteristic zero and let
>
> \[
> F,G\in K[x,y,z,w]
> \]
>
> be homogeneous of the same degree `d>=2`.  Assume the generic member
>
> \[
> F+sG
> \]
>
> has Hessian rank three and
>
> \[
> \det\operatorname{Hess}(F+sG)=0
> \qquad\text{identically in }(x,s).
> \tag{0.1}
> \]
>
> Then exactly one of the following holds after a constant linear change of
> the four variables.
>
> 1. **Fixed vertex.** There is a nonzero constant vector `v` such that
>    \[
>    D_vF=D_vG=0.
>    \tag{0.2}
>    \]
>
> 2. **Minimal moving vertex.** There are homogeneous binary forms
>    \(P,Q,R\) with
>    \[
>    \deg P=d-1,\qquad \deg Q=\deg R=d,
>    \]
>    such that
>    \[
>    F=zP(x,y)+Q(x,y),
>    \qquad
>    G=-wP(x,y)+R(x,y).
>    \tag{0.3}
>    \]
>    The generic kernel line is generated by
>    \[
>    v(s)=e_w+s e_z.
>    \tag{0.4}
>    \]
>
> In particular every genuinely moving generic rank-three homogeneous
> Hessian pencil has Kronecker minimal index one.  Minimal indices two and
> three force Hessian rank at most two and one respectively.

The moving normal form (0.3) is a two-base-variable scroll/cotangent-type
configuration.  Thus the highest homogeneous part of a moving `HC4` pencil
cannot carry an arbitrary curved kernel flag.

## 1. Gordan--Noether converts singular Hessians to vertices

For each generic scalar `s`, the homogeneous quaternary form `F+sG` has zero
Hessian determinant.  The Gordan--Noether theorem in projective dimension
three says that such a hypersurface is a cone.  Equivalently there is a
nonzero constant vector over `K(s)` in the kernel of its Hessian.

For a homogeneous form of degree at least two,

\[
\operatorname{Hess}(F+sG)v=0
\]

is equivalent to

\[
D_v(F+sG)=0,
\tag{1.1}
\]

because the derivative is homogeneous and its gradient is the Hessian-vector
product.

Define the linear matrix pencil

\[
L(s):K(s)^4\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Sym}^{d-1}(K^4)^*,
\qquad
v\longmapsto D_v(F+sG).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Its normal rank is three.  Hence its right nullspace over `K(s)` is
one-dimensional.

## 2. Kronecker minimal index

The Kronecker canonical form of a linear matrix pencil gives one right
singular block.  Since `L(s)` has only four columns, its right minimal index
satisfies

\[
0\le\varepsilon\le3.
\tag{2.1}
\]

After constant changes in the source coordinates and an irrelevant change of
row basis, the primitive kernel chain can be written

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\varepsilon&v(s)\\ \hline
0&e_w\\
1&e_w+s e_z\\
2&e_w+s e_z+s^2e_y\\
3&e_w+s e_z+s^2e_y+s^3e_x.
\end{array}
\tag{2.2}
\]

We now impose that `L(s)` comes from directional derivatives of **two
potentials** `F,G`.  This integrability eliminates the long chains.

## 3. Minimal index zero: fixed vertex

For `v=e_w`, equation (1.1) gives

\[
F_w=G_w=0,
\]

which is (0.2).

## 4. Minimal index one: the unique moving scroll

Put

\[
v(s)=e_w+s e_z.
\]

Expanding

\[
D_{v(s)}(F+sG)=0
\]

in `s` gives

\[
F_w=0,
\qquad
F_z+G_w=0,
\qquad
G_z=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus `F` is independent of `w`, `G` is independent of `z`, and

\[
F_z(x,y,z)=-G_w(x,y,w).
\]

The two sides can depend only on their common variables `x,y`; call the
common form `P(x,y)`.  Integration gives

\[
F=zP(x,y)+Q(x,y),
\qquad
G=-wP(x,y)+R(x,y),
\]

which is (0.3).

Conversely (0.3) satisfies

\[
D_{e_w+s e_z}(F+sG)=0
\]

identically, so the normal form is exact rather than merely necessary.

## 5. Minimal index two forces rank at most two

Now

\[
v(s)=e_w+s e_z+s^2e_y.
\]

Coefficient comparison gives

\[
F_w=0,
\quad F_z+G_w=0,
\quad F_y+G_z=0,
\quad G_y=0.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The first and last equations remove `w` from `F` and `y` from `G`.
From `F_z=-G_w`, differentiating with respect to `y` and `w` shows that
both sides are a form `P(x)` depending only on `x`.  Hence

\[
F=zP(x)+Q(x,y),
\qquad
G=-wP(x)+\widetilde R(x,z).
\]

The middle equation then forces

\[
Q_y(x,y)=-\widetilde R_z(x,z)=L(x).
\]

Therefore

\[
F=zP(x)+yL(x)+Q_0(x),
\qquad
G=-wP(x)-zL(x)+R_0(x).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Every Hessian in this pencil has rank at most two: apart from the `x` row
and column, all nonzero entries lie in that single row/column.  This
contradicts the rank-three hypothesis.

## 6. Minimal index three forces rank at most one

Finally let

\[
v(s)=e_w+s e_z+s^2e_y+s^3e_x.
\]

We obtain

\[
F_w=0,
\quad F_z+G_w=0,
\quad F_y+G_z=0,
\quad F_x+G_y=0,
\quad G_x=0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Since `F` is independent of `w` and `G` of `x`, the last coupled equation
makes `F_x=-G_y` independent of both `x` and `w`.  Compatibility with the
preceding equations successively removes its `y,z` dependence as well.
The same argument then makes `F_z=-G_w` and `F_y=-G_z` constants.
Consequently the nonlinear parts of `F` and `G` are each functions of at most
one linear form, and their Hessians have rank at most one.

Thus minimal index three is also incompatible with generic Hessian rank
three.  Sections 3--6 prove `HC4RSD55`.

## 7. `HC4` consequence and next obstruction

For a constant-Hessian pencil

\[
\operatorname{Hess}(\psi+sA),
\]

every nonconstant highest homogeneous face has singular Hessian.  Whenever
the leading pencil has generic rank three and both leading forms occur in the
same degree, `HC4RSD55` leaves only:

1. a common constant vertex, which is the desired flag stabilization; or
2. the moving scroll (0.3).

So the next genuinely new calculation is no longer a general moving-kernel
search.  It is the single all-degree scroll packet

\[
F=zP+Q,
\qquad G=-wP+R.
\]

The natural target is to insert the next lower homogeneous faces into the
constant-Hessian determinant and prove that either the vertex synchronizes or
the packet becomes the `JC2` cotangent geometry.

## 8. External input

The only classification theorem used here is the classical Gordan--Noether
result: a homogeneous polynomial in four variables with identically vanishing
Hessian determinant is a cone.  A modern proof is given by Bricalli, Favale
and Pirola, *A theorem of Gordan and Noether via Gorenstein rings*, Selecta
Mathematica 29 (2023), Article 74.

The kernel-chain reduction is the standard Kronecker canonical form for a
linear matrix pencil; all subsequent eliminations are elementary mixed-partial
compatibility.
